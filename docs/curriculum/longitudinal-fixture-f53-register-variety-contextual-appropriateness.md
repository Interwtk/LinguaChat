# F53 — Register and legitimate language variation must not be confused with CEFR incompetence

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped register evaluator, validated dialect model, CEFR certification or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F52 separates group participation from each learner's independently attributable competence. F53 isolates **context-appropriate register and communicative effect** from **unjustified penalties for legitimate regional, social or interactional language variation**. A learner can be linguistically accurate yet choose an unsuitable register for a source-defined task, or communicate effectively with a legitimate variant that differs from the evaluator's preferred example. Neither every variant nor every register is automatically interchangeable: the actual task, audience, source-defined communicative objective and validated rubric determine the construct. Never promote a single prestige variety to an unstated universal CEFR requirement.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a real, versioned CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, task, target-language variety scope and evaluator/rubric before scoring. Freeze communicative intent, audience relationship, channel, genre, expected formality, pragmatic moves, linguistic difficulty, allowed alternatives, independence requirements, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, review debt and exit gates. Where a rubric legitimately tests register choice or a specified standard form, apply that **explicit** criterion without generalizing it to unrelated tasks or learners. Where the source has no validated rule for a disputed variety, dialect form or culturally contingent pragmatic judgment, preserve the observation and mark the disputed credit **UNSUPPORTED**; do not invent dialect rules, regional stereotypes, penalty weights or CEFR cutoffs.

Use deterministic synthetic pseudonymous learners, injected clock/seed and immutable event histories across genuinely separate sessions. Record level-qualified objective, blueprint/rubric/evaluator versions, task audience/role/power relationship, purpose, locale/variety scope *only when task-relevant and explicitly known*, genre/channel, task novelty, learner-authored first attempt, final submission, specific lexical/grammatical/pragmatic features, any feedback/model/peer hints, evaluator reasons and alternative-validity evidence. Keep **linguistic form**, **contextual appropriateness**, **comprehensibility**, **task completion** and **independence** separate; an aggregate “sounds natural” score cannot substitute for source-qualified rationale. Do not infer identity, ethnicity, nationality, disability or language proficiency from accent, spelling, vocabulary or presumed dialect. Minimize unnecessary voice and demographic data.

Predeclare `SOURCE_VALID_VARIANT`, `REGISTER_APPROPRIATE`, `REGISTER_MISMATCH`, `PRAGMATIC_EFFECT_UNCERTAIN`, `VARIETY_SCOPE_UNKNOWN`, `UNJUSTIFIED_VARIANT_PENALTY`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `INDEPENDENT_PROBE_PENDING`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Absence from one model answer is not proof of error; a disputed source criterion is not silently relaxed.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Legitimate variant, equivalent task success.** Two learners independently complete the same source-valid everyday request in comparable settings. Their expressions differ in vocabulary or grammatical form recognized as legitimate in the specified target-language scope, while both satisfy source-required intent, clarity and register. The evaluator's single reference answer matches only one. Do not mark the other wrong solely for diverging from model wording. Preserve evidence supporting equivalence; if not validated, report `UNSUPPORTED`, not a guessed pass.
- **B — Grammatical answer, inappropriate register.** A learner writes a linguistically well-formed but excessively casual message for a source-defined formal institutional request; another uses an appropriately polite formulation that fulfills the same task. Score any explicitly assessed audience/register choice independently from grammar and semantic completion. Explain the specific contextual mismatch rather than calling an entire dialect or everyday speaking style “incorrect.” A casual version may be appropriate in a different source-defined peer context.
- **C — Same expression, different audience and genre.** The same learner uses a concise informal request appropriately in peer chat, then copies it unchanged into a formal complaint letter where the source rubric requires a different register. A later learner-authored revision adapts the wording, but only after a targeted tutor hint. Preserve both original observations and assistance chronology; do not count the prompted revision as unaided initial register control or retroactively invalidate legitimate peer-chat use.
- **D — Variety coverage and evaluator disagreement.** An independently produced regional lexical or pronunciation variant is accepted by a validated source for the target task, but one evaluator marks it wrong while another accepts it. Keep original language evidence, source citations/versions and both evaluator decisions. Escalate an inconsistent evaluator rule as a reproducible **fixture-level disagreement** only if executable traces establish it; do not claim an engine defect from this design scenario. If the task specifies a different variety or orthographic convention, evaluate the explicitly required feature only within that task. Missing validated equivalence is `UNSUPPORTED`.
- **E — Context-sensitive learning without fixed speaker labels.** Two learners share a versioned CEFR can-do, unchanged rubric and comparable difficulty. Learner A shows better later unaided formal-request transfer after contrastive formal/informal examples; learner B shows better later transfer after role-play with gradually faded prompts. Counterbalance contexts, interlocutor roles, variety familiarity and method order; sample both options. When subsequent delayed independent results contradict the earlier ordering, lower confidence and revise the instructional hypothesis. Preserve source-required skill and unfamiliar-context opportunities only where implemented; never label either learner as permanently “formal,” “regional” or “auditory.”
- **F — Late rubric update, offline replay and contextual leakage.** A peer-chat answer and formal-email answer share a session-local item label; an older rubric scores the peer-chat form as universally wrong; a newer source-qualified rubric accepts it in peer chat but not formal email. A tutor hint is delivered before a revision but logged late; final submission is uploaded twice and a cached evaluation arrives after the new one. Replay two receipt orders with trusted occurrence/delivery chronology, session-qualified IDs, immutable rubric versions and explicit migration rules. Do not overwrite original judgments or silently reinterpret past credit. Source-qualified evidence, assistance flags, pending reviews and provisional teaching choices must converge, or report `UNSUPPORTED` where migration is unjustified.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-defined communicative construct.** For A–F emit exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task/item, audience, purpose, channel, variety scope, evaluator/rubric and source rules for register, acceptable variation, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, transfer and exit. Unknown criteria yield `UNSUPPORTED`, not invented norms or thresholds.
2. **Model answer is not the entire language.** In A, a source-validated legitimate alternative satisfying the same objective cannot fail merely for not matching one preferred example. Report feature-level rationale and the source supporting acceptability; do not accept unverified variants by assumption.
3. **Register is context-dependent.** In B, distinguish grammaticality, comprehensibility, task completion and explicitly required audience/register appropriateness. A genuine register mismatch may affect the relevant criterion without globally stigmatizing a form or speaker.
4. **Same form may score differently across valid contexts.** In C, respect peer versus institutional demands when the rubric requires them; retain original attempts and targeted-hint exposure. A corrected assisted formal response does not erase first-attempt evidence or become unaided transfer.
5. **No prestige-variety or accent shortcut.** In D, do not infer low competence from a recognized regional form, accent, non-model wording or identity proxy. Reconcile evaluator disagreements against versioned, task-specific source rules. An explicit assessed standard applies only within its legitimate scope; missing validation is `UNSUPPORTED`.
6. **Adaptation is reversible and outcome-based.** In E, log available/selected/delivered explanations, contrastive examples, role-play and scaffold options, uncertainty, topic/variety/context familiarity and comparable later unaided outcomes. Explore alternatives, reverse hypotheses on contradictory evidence and maintain required CEFR skill/objective coverage; no permanent learner-type classification.
7. **Interests remain a separate soft layer.** Across A–F, onboarding and demonstrated interests may probabilistically change examples, never restrict tasks to favorite topics, suppress adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar settings, violate explicit topic boundaries, lower register difficulty or waive required CEFR objectives and reviews. Interests or presumed background do not authorize dialect stereotypes.
8. **Deterministic, source-qualified replay.** In F, deduplicate stable events, preserve original task/audience and cue-delivery chronology, version evaluator decisions, quarantine unsupported rubric migrations and use session-qualified identifiers. Both arrival orders yield identical independent/assisted evidence, variant/register classification, review debt and provisional method hypotheses; missing provenance fails closed for disputed credit.
9. **Honest evidence and release boundary.** For every history output first/final independent quality, linguistic versus pragmatic/register reasons, validated variants, disagreements, delivered hints, method alternatives/confidence, topic/skill balance, eligible/completed/missing delayed independent probes, unseen-context transfer, review/exit obligations and per-assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED. Documentation or CI success alone cannot establish a shipped evaluator, CEFR mastery, fair outcomes or causal improvement in durable learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, injected clock/seed, immutable event streams in both receipt orders, versioned CEFR blueprint/rubric/evaluator and variety policy, explicit audience/genre/channel, learner-authored first and final responses, source-grounded alternative-form judgments, delivered cue provenance, contextual register classification, evaluator disagreements, independently completed delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer with eligible/completed/missing denominators, balanced instructional/topic alternatives, unchanged review/exit gates, and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. If a source-valid variant, rubric criterion, evaluator agreement or modality is unverified, expose the limitation; never manufacture an accepted form or award disputed CEFR credit.

A claim that contrastive register instruction or variety-sensitive feedback improves **durable independent** learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal evidence with comparable contexts, balanced instructional comparators and ethically reviewed real-learner evaluation. **The tutor should teach language appropriate to a situation without mistaking one preferred wording or prestige variety for the whole language.**
