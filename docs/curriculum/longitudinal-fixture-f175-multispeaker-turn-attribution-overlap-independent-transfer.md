**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable exact-head tests bind independently reviewed source-world and participant-role oracles, target-language/variety-specific **actually delivered multi-speaker audio**, accessibility/help chronology and later first-unaided unseen recipient-facing transfer to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a shipped speaker-diarization system, universal overlap recognizer, new CEFR gate, adaptive-tutor implementation or evidence of human learning gains. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F169 separates offers from commitments; F171 treats prosodic uncertainty; F173 treats phrase grouping; F174 treats **one speaker's** abandoned fragment and self-repair. **Distinct construct:** across two or more interlocutors, can the learner attribute a proposal, correction, question, agreement and eventual responsibility to the **right speaker**, distinguish an interruption from a completed turn, avoid turning overlapping or conflicting voices into a single invented statement, and ask for clarification when authority or speaker identity is unresolved? Last voice heard is not automatically the authoritative voice. Accent, speech rate, disability and natural conversational overlap are not deficits.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Two speakers, two distinct responsibilities.** In actually delivered, independently reviewed two-voice audio, Mina says, “I can bring the keys,” and Theo says, “I'll bring the maps.” The source-world records Mina's **offer**, not an accepted key commitment; Theo's expressed map commitment, not evidence of completed delivery. Asked who said they would bring the maps, a learner independently answers Theo; asked who is *confirmed* to bring keys, the learner says this has not been confirmed. A name-tagged caption or visually highlighted speaker shown before the first attempt contaminates an audio-specific claim.

**B — Overlapping contradiction is not consensus.** At a fictional study-club meeting, Mina begins “The room is free—” while Theo overlaps “I haven't checked it yet.” The participant-world establishes Mina's assertion and Theo's uncertainty, **not** a verified reservation or shared agreement. A learner relays “Mina thinks it's free; Theo hasn't checked” or asks the organizer to verify. If overlapping audio masks Theo's negation, the learner can ask “Did Theo say he checked?” rather than guessing. A diarization model that fuses both into “We checked; it's free” fails the source-world oracle.

**C — Interruption, uptake and actual owner of an action.** Lina starts “I'll email the—” but is interrupted by Omar: “I can email the teacher.” Lina then says “Great, you do that.” The participant-world binds the teacher email to Omar **after Lina's acceptance**. Lina's truncated phrase is not a completed promise; Omar's initial offer alone is not proof the email was sent. A new volunteer asks who should send the email; the learner independently names Omar and distinguishes assigned work from completed work. If the acceptance is clipped, confirmation remains unresolved.

**D — Conflicting directions and role-based authority.** In a fictional event, one helper says “Meet by the west gate”; another says “No, the east gate.” Their roles and authority are **not established** in the source-world. A learner must not adopt the later direction solely because it was said last or louder; “Which gate did the organizer confirm?” is appropriate. A separate reviewed variant explicitly identifies the organizer and confirms the east gate; only then can the learner relay it confidently. Do not infer authority from gender, accent, volume or synthetic voice style.

**E — First-authorship and assistance chronology.** At 09:02 a two-speaker exchange renders without speaker-labeled captions; the learner's first unaided response is “Was it Mina or Theo who offered the keys?” Preserve FIRST_UNAIDED_LISTENING and TARGETED_SPEAKER_CLARIFICATION. At 09:03 a color-coded transcript and isolated-voice replay **actually render**; at 09:04 the learner answers Mina. That answer is ASSISTED and ANSWER_EXPOSURE and cannot retroactively rewrite 09:02. A help control merely displayed but not activated is not rendered exposure. Deduplicate a retried 09:04 submission by stable session-qualified ID. Transcript-first accessibility is valid but measured under its actual construct.

**F — Outcome-driven, reversible and confound-aware adaptation.** Learner A accurately maps clean written dialogue but misattributes speakers in later unaided natural overlapping audio. After several matched sessions with **actually delivered** SPEAKER_TURN_MAPPING followed by progressively natural audio, A shows improved later first-unaided recipient actions with **new voices**, neutral and unfamiliar contexts, and distinct interruptions. Cautiously raise that option's probability, retain uncertainty and continue sampling alternatives. Learner B initially improves with CONTROLLED_OVERLAP_TO_NATURAL_FADING but later incorrectly treats a second speaker's denial as agreement; lower confidence and explore TARGETED_WHO_SAID_WHAT_CLARIFICATION. If method groups differ in overlap severity, number of speakers, caption exposure, voice quality, role authority, familiarity or follow-up availability, declare **no causal winner**. Same audio, next-day boundary, or an assisted re-answer is not unseen delayed transfer. Missing follow-up is missing, not failure.

1. **Fixed CEFR objective and independent oracles.** A–F bind an existing relevant versioned can-do, reviewed participant-world and speaker-role oracle, independently reviewed language/variety and actual acoustic delivery, unchanged rubric and due/delayed/unseen probes; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED. No new level gates.
2. **Speaker-specific responsibility without invented confirmation.** A attributes the map commitment to Theo, treats Mina's keys as an offer until accepted and never infers that either item was delivered; caption-based correctness cannot claim unaided listening.
3. **Overlapping contradiction and masking.** B retains Mina's assertion versus Theo's uncertainty, rejects fused or ASR-hallucinated consensus, validates actual overlap and accepts targeted clarification when negation is masked.
4. **Interruption and acceptance scope.** C does not complete Lina's abandoned phrase, attributes the accepted email task to Omar, separates commitment from execution and withholds certainty when Lina's acceptance is inaudible.
5. **Authority and ambiguity.** D distinguishes confirmed organizer instructions from conflicting unverified helpers, accepts role-targeted clarification and never infers authority from speaker order, loudness, accent, disability or gender.
6. **First authorship, rendered help and accessibility.** E preserves earliest unaided response; only actually rendered speaker labels, replay, transcript or hints contaminate later attempts; deduplicate replayed event IDs and report accessible text routes honestly.
