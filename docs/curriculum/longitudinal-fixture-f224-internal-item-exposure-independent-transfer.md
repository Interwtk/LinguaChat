# F224 — Internal item exposure, semantic near-duplicates and independent transfer

**Status: DESIGN-ONLY / UNSUPPORTED.** This is a synthetic longitudinal curriculum acceptance fixture, not an implemented assessment engine, CEFR certification, evidence of human-learning gains, or permission to experiment with learners. Pre-A1 is frozen and A1–C2 remain unavailable. No Auth, cloud, infrastructure, production, Supabase, paid Claude, EvoLabs or auto-merge changes.

**Governing contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F221 covers *external* assistance and tools; F222 covers method-selection confounding; F223 covers evidence freshness and late arrivals. **Distinct failure under test:** a tutor itself may expose answers, reuse memorized prompts, or choose semantically equivalent items and then mistake the resulting correct responses for new, delayed, independent language transfer.

## Fixed objective, item lineage and pre-choice record

Bind every case to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do**, target language and accepted varieties, independently reviewed meaning/function rubric, prerequisites, objective difficulty, required receptive/productive skills, accessible channels, independent exit criteria and review obligations. Example items are illustrative only, not new published can-dos. Text-only success never certifies speaking/listening. This fixture does not decide the B2 Arc 6 recognition/open-retrieval disagreement.

A deterministic history must preserve the following *before* any instructional choice or assessment verdict:

- Stable pseudonymous learner and objective, task, item, item-family, semantic-equivalence-cluster, rubric, channel, content and policy versions; immutable attempt, session, run and event identities.
- Item provenance: exact prompt and answer-key identity where allowed; semantic intent/slots, near-duplicate or translation lineage, prior exposures and reviewed equivalence verdict. A changed surface string, topic or hash does **not** automatically create an unseen task.
- Trusted occurrence, arrival, prompt-render, answer-bearing-render, hint, model, transcript, caption, feedback, replay and submission chronology. Distinguish *available in code* from *actually delivered or exposed*. If exposure cannot be established, mark it UNKNOWN rather than assume no exposure.
- First valid unaided attempt and reviewed meaning/function quality, retries, scaffold dependence, reliable latency if available, recurring error type, distinct learning-day/session provenance, meaningful-delay review and new-context independent transfer.
- Pre-choice feasible instructional alternatives, learner consent and accessibility, current skill debt, interest/topic family, selection reason, uncertainty, item-pool eligibility, method **actually rendered** and dose; due/offered/rendered/completed/missing counts.
- Independent human or reviewed linguistic oracle for semantic equivalence and task comparability. Text overlap, embeddings, item hashes and lexical novelty are *signals for review*, not sufficient proof of independent transfer or equivalence.

**Three different outcomes must stay separate:** (1) valid unaided first attempt on a previously seen item; (2) independent retrieval after meaningful delay on a known item, with no answer-bearing exposure in the relevant interval; (3) independent **unseen transfer** to a reviewed non-equivalent realization/context of the same can-do. The first can be diagnostically useful; the second may support retention; neither automatically satisfies the third. A clean historical attempt must not be retroactively invalidated by feedback displayed *after* submission, but later attempts can be contaminated by that feedback.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories with matched controls

**A — Same item on another day is not unseen transfer.** A0 shows a request-for-information prompt, A1 is an independently reviewed first attempt, and A2 supplies corrective feedback. A3, ten elapsed days later in a distinct real learning session, repeats the exact prompt and the learner responds correctly without a new hint. Unsafe policy counts A3 as unseen cross-context transfer and promotes mastery. **Expected:** preserve A1's genuine first-attempt evidence; A3 is *seen-item retrieval*, not unseen transfer. Its delayed-retention eligibility requires review of intervening exposure and meaningful delay. An unseen transfer probe remains due. **Matched control:** replace only A3 with a genuinely unseen, reviewer-approved new situation preserving the can-do, meaning burden, difficulty, channel, accessibility, no answer exposure and independent scoring. Only the control may become eligible as unseen transfer.

**B — Lexical novelty conceals semantic answer reuse.** B0/B1 teach and display a complete model for politely requesting a change of appointment. B2 changes a person's name, venue and date, and paraphrases the prompt, but preserves the identical communicative move, slot sequence and answer template. B3 succeeds immediately. Unsafe policy declares a new task because the surface text and content hash differ. **Expected:** item-family and reviewed semantic-equivalence lineage classify B2 as *near-duplicate/answer-template exposed*, not clean transfer; the success may still guide scaffolding. **Matched control:** an independently reviewed scenario demands the same fixed can-do through a genuinely different realization and pragmatic context, with comparable difficulty and no exposed answer template. If reviewers cannot agree on equivalence or novelty, return NOT_COMPARABLE/UNSUPPORTED, not a fabricated success.

**C — Feedback order and assisted repair.** C0 presents a valid independent task; C1 is a reviewed incorrect first submission; C2 reveals a model and specific repair; C3 copies the correction successfully in the same session; C4 later independently succeeds on a reviewed unseen probe. Unsafe policy upgrades C3 to unaided mastery or erases C1, while a second unsafe policy marks C1 assisted because feedback exists later in the event log. **Expected:** C1 remains an authentic unaided failure, C3 is assisted repair, and only C4 may contribute new unseen transfer if its provenance, meaningful delay and answer isolation are verified. **Matched control:** move the answer-bearing model *before* C1's submission while keeping surface responses unchanged. C1 now loses unaided eligibility; event ordering, not final correctness, changes the evidence verdict.

**D — Channel-specific exposure, transcripts and delivery uncertainty.** D0 schedules an independent listening probe. D1 renders audio but also exposes a full answer-bearing transcript before response; D2 is correct. A later D3 has playback failure and UNKNOWN transcript visibility. A separate D4 text task is valid and independently correct. Unsafe policy treats D2 as clean listening transfer, D3 as a listening failure, and D4 as oral mastery. **Expected:** D2's listening *independence* is contaminated by the answer-bearing transcript, D3 is UNKNOWN/NOT_ASSESSED rather than failed, and D4 cannot discharge listening or speaking debt. Respect captions, accessibility accommodations and learner choice: the problem is answer leakage relative to the **specific** independent construct, not use of accessibility technology itself. **Matched control:** deliver an accessible, verified non-answer-bearing audio task with reviewed listening evidence, no prior answer exposure and an independent response; only that new result can support listening mastery.

**E — Familiar-item selection bias creates a false method winner.** A worked-example policy draws from 20 heavily rehearsed prompts while retrieval-first uses 20 unseen but equivalently difficult prompts. The worked-example group scores higher immediately and after a calendar-day boundary. Unsafe policy declares worked examples universally superior and narrows the pool to memorized items. **Expected:** audit selection, exposure, item families, true delay, baseline proficiency, learner choice, completion and missingness; refuse the naive method comparison as NOT_COMPARABLE. Preserve balanced exposure to unfamiliar contexts and required productive/receptive skills. **Matched control:** counterbalance independently reviewed seen/unseen item families and comparable difficulty across feasible methods; measure later independent retrieval and truly unseen transfer in distinct sessions, including non-completers. Cautious observational comparison may then be eligible, never automatically causal.

**F — Cached models, replay and out-of-order delivery.** F0 freezes a pre-choice record with due listening and writing reviews and two feasible methods. F1 is an independently reviewed unseen success with stable attempt ID F-T1. F2 replays F-T1; F3 has a distinct ID but reused semantic answer template from a cached model, exposure UNKNOWN; F4 is a late-arriving answer-bearing render event whose trusted occurrence predates F3 but arrived after its score was displayed; F5 queues a listening review without rendering it. F6 actually renders a balanced accessible writing activity; its independent outcome remains pending. Unsafe policy counts F1 twice, credits F3 as unseen, retroactively rewrites the F1 decision using F4, calls queued listening complete and locks a method. **Expected:** deduplicate F-T1, quarantine F3 pending trustworthy render chronology, keep F1's historical pre-choice snapshot immutable, retain due/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators and listening debt, and record the actual F6 dose without invented independent success. **Matched control:** replace only F3 with a verified non-exposed, reviewed unseen item while preserving event identity, difficulty, opportunity, accessibility and timing; the later evidence/hypothesis may change, but F1 must not be backdated and F5 remains unrendered.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR/release contract:** A–F preserve versioned existing can-dos, reviewed communicative rubrics, prerequisite and required skill coverage, independent exit standards and closed A1–C2 gates; no Pre-A1 changes or invented can-do.
2. **Item-family and semantic lineage:** exact repeats, translations, slot swaps and paraphrased model templates are not automatically unseen merely because their text, hash or topic differs. A reviewed equivalence/novelty oracle is required; ambiguity returns UNSUPPORTED.
3. **Separate retention from transfer:** first independent attempt, delayed seen-item retrieval and delayed unseen transfer are independently recorded; no repeat, calendar boundary or assisted success silently satisfies all three.
4. **Actual answer-bearing exposure:** distinguish prompt, model, hint, feedback, transcript, captions, answer-key and cached content delivery before/after submission. UNKNOWN exposure is not clean evidence. Do not retroactively contaminate an earlier valid attempt with later feedback.
5. **Assistance and accessibility fairness:** assisted repair cannot earn independent mastery; necessary accommodations are not inherently cheating; only independently reviewed construct-specific answer leakage invalidates the relevant independence claim. Text-only evidence cannot certify oral/listening skills.
6. **Method comparability and interest separation:** method contrasts control item familiarity, semantic family, task difficulty, baseline ability, opportunity, learner choice, context interest, delay and missingness. Interest weights remain soft and never exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar topics.
7. **Chronology, deduplication and missingness:** stable IDs, trusted occurrence versus arrival, actual render history and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing counts survive replay and late events; queued, failed or UNKNOWN probes create neither successes nor failures.
8. **Reversible *delivered* adaptation:** A–F preserve competing uncertain hypotheses and feasible accessible alternatives. Matched controls may alter a later actually rendered instructional choice or justify a balanced fallback; they cannot rewrite prior choices or freeze learner-style labels.
9. **Executable/efficacy boundary:** future machine-readable histories and matched controls must specify deterministic seed/clock, resolved versioned source bindings, independent semantic and exposure oracles, exact-head executable harness and per-assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED. Synthetic invariant PASS is not human-learning efficacy; that additionally requires an ethically reviewed longitudinal comparison using delayed unaided retrieval, unseen transfer, missingness and subgroup/skill-balance checks.

## Required independent verification and non-goals

An independent harness must output exact curriculum and engine HEADs; executable command; resolved can-do/task/rubric/item-family/channel versions; reviewer-approved semantic novelty and comparability; trusted answer-bearing render and submission chronology; first unaided, seen-item delayed and unseen-transfer eligibility separately; pre-choice feasible alternatives and **actually delivered** method/dose; due/offered/rendered/completed/missing counts; matched-control observed-versus-expected outcomes; per-assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED; and reviewer signoff. A synthetic fixture without real bindings or an independent executor must report **UNSUPPORTED**, not PASS.

No learner tracking, live experiment, production rollout, cloud/Auth/Supabase/infrastructure change, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, CEFR level release or B2 Arc 6 policy change is authorized.

**Verdict:** a strong tutor must not confuse recognizing its own rehearsed answers with independently applying a language capability. Longitudinal instructional adaptation remains uncertain, reversible, skill-balanced and grounded in reviewed new-context evidence, not apparent novelty or short-term assisted correctness.
