## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Same numerator, different denominator.** A reviewed class survey records 3 of 10 students choosing a workshop; a second class records 3 of 100. Learner A says “Both classes had the same interest because three students chose it”; learner B independently tells a coordinator “The counts match, but the shares differ: three in ten versus three in a hundred.” Counterfactual: if the recipient asks only how many volunteers to contact, the equal count may answer that different question. If participation was optional or the response populations differ from enrollment, the oracle must identify the surveyed denominator and must not silently generalize to all students. A missing denominator licenses a clarification, not a guessed percentage.

**B — Absolute counts versus rates and exposure windows.** Two services report 12 incidents over 12,000 visits and 6 incidents over 1,000 visits in the same reviewed period. Learner A calls the first service riskier because 12 exceeds 6; learner B says “The first has more incidents in total, but fewer per thousand visits: 1 versus 6.” This does **not** prove a causal quality difference or equal case mix. Counterfactual: if one source counts only weekdays and the other includes weekends, or if visits overlap differently, the comparison is flagged INCOMPARABLE until reconciled. No real-world service recommendation is inferred from the synthetic example.

**C — Per-person, per-item and time-unit scope.** A reviewed notice says “Each participant may borrow two books per visit; visits are limited to once a week.” Learner A tells a group of four “You may borrow two books total for the week”; learner B independently explains “Each person may borrow up to two per visit, with one visit per week; four eligible participants could borrow up to eight in their own visits.” Counterfactual: “two books for the group” or “two per household” changes the denominator and thus the permitted count. “Once every two weeks” is not “twice a week”; “three times per day” is not “once every three days.” A permission is a ceiling, not proof that anyone actually borrowed books.

**D — Percentage points, relative change and baseline.** A reviewed survey shows an on-time rate falling from 20% to 15%. Learner A reports “It fell by 5%”; learner B independently says “It fell by five percentage points, which is a 25% decrease relative to the original 20%.” Counterfactual: if the original rate were 0%, the relative percent change is undefined; if the baseline is not supplied, the learner should request it rather than compute a relative change. Rounding to whole percentages may hide small underlying differences; the learner must not imply precision absent from the source. A different recipient may reasonably prefer the absolute point difference over the relative framing.

**E — Actually rendered multimodal channels and independent authorship.** Authoritative audio says “six per **one thousand visits**, **this month**”; ASR drops “thousand,” captions omit “this month,” a translated line changes “per visit” to “per person,” or an accessible chart precomputes and highlights the correct rate. Reconcile source with what the learner actually received; quarantine only the affected unit/interval/listening construct. Reading an accessible transcript may demonstrate reading, not unheard listening. A learner's first authored “Out of how many visits, and for what period?” at 10:00 remains independent if a queued model first renders at 10:01; an edited answer after seeing the model is ASSISTED. Screen-reader labels, autocomplete and calculator previews count as exposure when they reveal the answer. Replaying the same session-qualified event never creates another independent or delayed probe.

**F — Longitudinal method selection, reversibility and unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A first answers immediately after UNIT_AND_INTERVAL_ANNOTATION_THEN_FADE but later independently confuses per-day with per-week in a new recipient handoff. Comparable actually delivered DENOMINATOR_FIRST_REPHRASE trials precede a later unaided explanation of bicycles per household in a new community survey. Learner B improves after COUNT_VS_RATE_DIALOGUE but later confuses percentage points with relative change; method confidence decreases and balanced alternatives resume. If the annotation method always uses supplied denominators and simple integer rates while dialogue uses missing denominators, decimals, unfamiliar vocabulary or a different register, flag the confounds and infer **no winner**. Changing “10 students” to “20 students” in the same worksheet is not unseen transfer. Track eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing probes separately; missing is not wrong. Only repeated comparable delayed independent outcomes can justify a logged change in **actually delivered** instruction. A learner interested in music may receive rehearsal attendance examples more often, while transport, community services, work, health, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR objective and independently reviewed quantitative oracle.** A–F bind existing versioned descriptors/can-dos, recipient/purpose, source-world numerators/denominators/units/intervals/baselines, reviewed natural target-language realizations, accessible stimuli, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED, not invented CEFR certification.
2. **Part/whole denominator integrity.** A preserves 3/10 versus 3/100 and the surveyed population, distinguishes counts from shares, and asks for missing denominators instead of inferring a percentage.
3. **Comparable normalized rates, not causal claims.** B distinguishes 12 versus 6 absolute incidents from 1 versus 6 per 1,000 visits, checks comparable exposure windows and populations, and does not infer service quality or causality from rates alone.
4. **Per-unit and temporal scope.** C preserves each-person/per-visit/per-week boundaries, distinguishes a maximum allowance from actual usage, and rejects “twice per week” versus “once every two weeks” substitutions.
5. **Baseline and precision honesty.** D distinguishes five percentage points from a 25% relative fall, rejects relative change with zero or unknown baseline, preserves source precision and credits recipient-appropriate qualified paraphrases.
6. **Actually delivered accessible, chronological and idempotent evidence.** E distinguishes authoritative from rendered audio/ASR/captions/translations/charts/screen-reader/autocomplete/calculator, quarantines only affected constructs, orders first authorship against rendered help and deduplicates session-qualified events. Accessible reading does not certify unheard listening.
7. **Controlled, uncertain and reversible adaptation.** F compares feasible actually delivered methods with matched denominator completeness, arithmetic load, precision, interval, variety/register, modality, topic, prior help and follow-up. Sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence yields no winner and balanced exploration; later regression lowers confidence.
8. **Delayed first-unaided transfer and soft interest rotation.** Assisted copying, highlighted precomputed rates, same-session exercises and number-only worksheet swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, new source/recipient/domain/units, reviewed oracle, first-unaided recipient-correct communication and explicit missingness. Preserve all shared objectives, skill-family balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts.
9. **Honest support, efficacy and release boundary.** A documentation commit or green CI is not executable engine support, demonstrated longitudinal policy change or improved human retention. Require exact-head executable assertions against real objectives, measured actually delivered policy changes with delayed independent retention/transfer versus a balanced baseline, and later ethically reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims.
