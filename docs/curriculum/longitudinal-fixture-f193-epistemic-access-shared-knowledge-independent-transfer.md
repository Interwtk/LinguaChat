Not a shipped multi-party knowledge tracker, theory-of-mind diagnostic, universal conversational rule, new CEFR gate, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F78 concerns local common-ground confirmation; F101 concerns resuming a conversation after interruption; F102 concerns turn/addressee and acknowledgment force; F113 concerns audible speaker attribution; F126/F189 concern source reporting; F188 concerns clarification; F191 concerns answer relevance; F192 concerns cancellable inferences. **Distinct construct:** when an existing source-linked communicative task requires coordinating what different recipients have actually been told, the learner must distinguish **information available to a participant** from **information established as received, understood or agreed by that participant**, and choose a faithful next message without inventing another person's knowledge, ignorance, belief, intent or consent. Information access is a source/delivery question, not a personality or intelligence inference. English examples are illustrative only.

An independently reviewed **participant-access oracle** freezes each authorized message, author, addressed recipients, source version, public/private/group channel, send time, delivery evidence, participant-visible contents, omissions, attachments, quoted snippets and later corrections. Classify **SENT**, **DELIVERED/SHOWN**, **ACKNOWLEDGED**, **CONTENT_CONFIRMED**, **AGREED**, **NOT_SHOWN** and **UNKNOWN** separately. A sender's intention, a sent email, a platform delivery indicator, a read receipt and a participant's accurate paraphrase are different evidence. A missing receipt is not proof that a person never saw the information. Access to a group chat does not automatically establish that every member read a post; a forwarded summary does not imply its recipient saw an omitted attachment. Do not infer private thoughts, attention, memory, honesty or mental state from access logs.

A frozen **recipient-action oracle** records the task's real communicative goal, which facts may safely be treated as shared, which details must be newly supplied or confirmed, and whether a short recap, targeted question or conditional formulation suffices. A recipient may independently know the same fact through an authorized alternative source; where that is possible but unverified, accept calibrated uncertainty. Do not over-ask for confirmations when the source already contains explicit recipient confirmation. Accept natural variety-appropriate alternatives and accessible communication; do not impose one social norm.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — A private update is not public common ground.** At 09:00 Maya sends Theo a private message: “Friday's workshop moved to Room 3.” Nora is on the club committee but is not a recipient; the fixture provides no evidence Nora saw another copy. At 09:10 the learner must message Nora about Friday's workshop. Accept “The workshop is now planned for Room 3; have you seen the update?” or a clear new notice. Reject “As you already know, it moved to Room 3” as established shared knowledge. Also reject “Nora definitely doesn't know” because absence of recorded access does not establish her actual ignorance. If Nora later replies “Yes, Maya told me earlier,” update the ledger without calling the original cautious message wrong.

**B — A forward is not the whole original.** Theo forwards Maya's note to Nora: “The workshop is on Friday. Please see the attached room plan,” but the forwarded version visibly excludes the attachment. The learner needs to direct Nora to the accessible room entrance. Accept a message supplying the missing plan/directions or asking whether Nora has another copy. Reject “Nora has the map because she received the forward” and “Nora definitely has no map.” A later source-authorized attachment delivery changes what can be claimed. The rubric must not treat the original sender's access as the forward recipient's access.

**C — Delivery, acknowledgment and confirmed content differ.** A fictional group platform shows a delivered status for the room update. Nora replies “Thanks!”; this is evidence of an acknowledgment, not necessarily of agreement with a proposed schedule or proof she interpreted every detail. In a separate continuation Nora writes, “I saw the update: Room 3, Friday at 4.” This establishes content confirmation for those details. Accept a proportionate recap or next action in each branch; reject both “Nora approved the schedule” from “Thanks!” alone and repetitive demands to confirm Room 3 when the explicit source already confirms that exact content. Do not turn generic delivery telemetry into a reading-comprehension or consent oracle.

**D — Corrections reach only their actual recipients.** At 10:00 the public notice says “Room 3.” At 10:20 Maya sends a correction to Theo only: “The coordinator changed it to Room 5.” The learner must update Nora, who has access to the earlier notice but no verified access to the correction. Accept “Update: the room has changed from Room 3 to Room 5” and, if appropriate, a request to acknowledge the change. Reject “Nora knows the new room” or “The public notice was never Room 3.” A later public corrected notice, once actually shown to Nora, may establish broader access; a public post with no view evidence alone still does not prove Nora read it.

**E — Actual exposure, failed audio and replay cannot earn independence.** In a reading route at 09:01, the learner sees the source messages but no solution. At 09:02 a tutor-rendered example lists “Maya and Theo know; Nora hasn't been shown the update.” At 09:03 the learner copies that mapping into a message. Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED; do not overwrite a prior first unaided answer or award independent mastery. Replaying the same event at 09:04 cannot create another first attempt. In a distinct listening variant the audio fails and only captions render: no independent listening credit; evaluate the actually accessible reading route only if its rubric is valid. An unopened hint does not count as exposure. A genuinely new recipient/channel/source combination in a later distinct session without answer-bearing help is required for delayed independent transfer.

**F — Longitudinal method choice without topic or access leakage.** Learner A repeatedly assumes that every club member has seen every update. Across comparable sessions, WHO_SAW_WHAT_LEDGER, PUBLIC_PRIVATE_MESSAGE_CONTRAST and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated; later unseen first-unaided messages may cautiously favor a method only if source fidelity improves with matched recipient goal, message length, actual access evidence, help, channel, topic and delay. Learner B initially improves after access diagrams but later overcorrects by asserting “Nora knows nothing” whenever delivery is unknown; new independent errors reduce confidence and restore balanced sampling or calibrated-uncertainty practice. If one option used clearer explicit read confirmations, shorter messages, familiar gaming contexts, visible recipient maps or shorter follow-up delays, record confounding and **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up is unknown, not failed retention. Gaming interests can softly influence examples, but neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops and all required CEFR objectives and skills remain available.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos, independently reviewed access/recipient oracles and actual delivery evidence; preserve level-specific prerequisites, thresholds, rubrics, turn ceilings, required skills and exit gates. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Private versus shared.** A distinguishes a verified private recipient from an unverified third party; neither false shared knowledge nor false ignorance is rewarded. Later independently sourced confirmation can update the claim.
3. **Partial forwarding.** B treats forwarded text and omitted attachments separately; original-author access is not transferred to a new recipient, and missing access evidence is not proof of absence.
4. **Evidence-strength calibration.** C distinguishes sent/delivered, acknowledgment, content confirmation and agreement; avoids both false consent and needless clarification after explicit confirmation.
5. **Correction propagation.** D preserves the earlier notice, later correction, recipient set and actual visibility; an update is not presumed universally shared.
6. **Actual delivery and authorship.** E separates source stimulus, rendered answer-bearing help, unopened hints, failed audio, captions, first unaided authorship and replay; no phantom listening or assisted independent mastery.
7. **Matched, reversible adaptation.** F changes instructional hypotheses only when comparable actually delivered options lead to better later first-unaided recipient outcomes. Contradictory, sparse or confounded evidence reduces confidence; reasonable alternatives remain sampled.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and soft interests.** Require distinct real sessions, genuinely unseen recipient/channel/update combinations, unaided recipient-facing communication, explicit missing follow-up, receptive/productive balance and nonexclusive preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** This synthetic document and generic CI are not executable interaction QA, shipped epistemic tracking or proven human-learning gains. Require exact-head executable source/access/delivery/response/provenance checks, observed reversible policy decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer versus a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should teach the learner to adapt messages to what a recipient has verifiably received, preserve uncertainty about unobserved knowledge, and independently repair missing shared information in unfamiliar conversations. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
