# F62 — Receptive recognition must not be mistaken for independent productive transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped receptive-to-productive scheduler, verified CEFR coverage, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F28 guards unsupported cross-skill method generalization; F36 protects review opportunity cost; F47/F48 enforce source-specific and noncompensatory CEFR gates; F49 guards accessibility construct equivalence; F59 calibrates challenge; F61 tests blocked/interleaved ordering. F62 isolates **receptive-to-productive bridging**: recognizing the right meaning in listening/reading may support, but cannot substitute for, independently retrieving and using language in writing/speaking or spontaneous free production when the actual versioned objective requires those performances. The tutor may provisionally adjust the ratio, sequence, scaffold fading and spacing of receptive and productive activities based on delayed **independent** results. It must not treat a learner as permanently “receptive” or “productive,” infer skill from preferred modality, or allow easy recognition scores to replace required productive evidence.

## Controlled setup

Resolve an **actual versioned** CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, prerequisite graph, task, target meaning/form/function, required skill/modality, evaluator/rubric, permissible aids, independent mastery threshold, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, due reviews and exit gate. Predeclare which recognition and production tasks share a **supporting target**, and which assess **distinct constructs** with separate noncompensatory mastery gates. Recognition-to-production gains may be investigated without pooling incompatible scores into a single fabricated CEFR result. If speaking/listening capture or evaluation is unavailable, mark that construct `UNAVAILABLE` and do not claim text substitution proves it.

For comparable histories predeclare per-target **eligible, assigned, delivered, attempted and completed** opportunities; item difficulty, language load, target frequency, practice time when observable, feedback, explanation, cues, models, prior item exposure, delay and order. Distinguish recognition with answer options, cued completion, constrained sentence production and **free unaided retrieval**. Record when a displayed word bank, transcript, model answer or prior multiple-choice option reveals the target later demanded by an allegedly independent probe. A transfer task is unseen only if its answer-bearing realization has not been exposed, including through another modality. Do not invent a universal optimal receptive/productive ratio, latency cutoff or number of practice episodes.

Use pseudonymous deterministic synthetic histories, injected seed/clock, immutable session-qualified event IDs and distinct real sessions. Capture source/rubric versions, objective-specific skill gates, candidate practice sequences, selected versus **actually delivered** modality and support, first unaided semantic attempts, optional measured latency, recurring error types, retries, hints, models, word banks/transcripts, self-repair, independent free production, meaningfully delayed independent retrieval, unseen-context transfer, and eligible/offered/completed/missing follow-up probes. Keep interest weighting a separate soft context layer, rotating adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics. Never infer disability, intelligence, effort, personality or permanent learning style from speaking reluctance, speed or recognition/production gaps.

Predeclare `RECEPTIVE_RECOGNITION_ONLY`, `CUED_PRODUCTION`, `INDEPENDENT_FREE_PRODUCTION`, `BRIDGE_SELECTED`, `BRIDGE_DELIVERED`, `BRIDGE_ASSIGNED_NOT_DELIVERED`, `ANSWER_BEARING_EXPOSURE`, `ASSISTED_PRACTICE_ONLY`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_TRANSFER`, `SKILL_CONSTRUCT_MISMATCH`, `BRIDGE_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These are provenance states, not stable learner labels.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Recognition ceiling hides productive gap.** Learner A repeatedly chooses the correct meaning from multiple-choice reading prompts but cannot independently formulate the required request in an unseen conversation. Learner B has lower immediate recognition accuracy yet can produce an appropriate novel request unaided after mixed practice. The tutor must preserve separate receptive and productive evidence, avoid rewarding A with productive mastery, and provisionally offer a bridge from meaning recognition to faded-cue retrieval without lowering the common objective standard.
- **B — Productive bridge versus extra practice confound.** Two source-matched learners have comparable baseline receptive comprehension and prerequisites. One receives recognition followed by progressively less-cued completion and independent free production; the other receives additional recognition-only practice. The bridge appears superior on delayed free production, but one arm also receives more target exposures and more feedback. First mark the comparison `BRIDGE_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED`; only a matched-opportunity follow-up can support a provisional sequencing hypothesis. Do not infer causality from extra practice.
- **C — Cross-modal answer leakage masquerades as transfer.** A learner listens to an example with a transcript, sees a word bank in a reading item, and then types the same answer in an allegedly unseen writing probe. A second learner independently produces an equivalent communicative intent in a genuinely new neutral context with no answer-bearing cues. Preserve the first attempt and exposure chronology; the copied or recently revealed response is assisted/rehearsed, not unseen independent transfer.
- **D — Speaking unavailable, writing available, and accessibility valid.** A learner can independently write a source-valid response but microphone capture or the speech evaluator is unavailable; another uses an approved accessible presentation that does not reveal the answer; a third elects to pause a speaking task. Writing evidence must not certify a required speaking can-do, valid accommodations must not automatically invalidate evidence, and opt-out or technical unavailability must not be scored as linguistic failure. Maintain an honest outstanding skill gate and feasible balanced practice.
- **E — Reversible bridge with delayed reversal and interest rotation.** Early independent written probes favor a shorter recognition-to-production bridge for one learner, while another benefits from additional receptive contrast before free production. Later genuinely delayed unseen tasks reverse the first learner's apparent advantage. Update per-objective hypotheses and uncertainty, continue feasible alternative sampling, honor due reviews and weak skills, and retain neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar topics despite repeated high engagement with music-themed recognition.
- **F — Offline partial delivery, duplicates and rubric drift.** A productive prompt is assigned but never renders; a transcript appears before a retry but syncs after the response; the first unaided answer uploads twice; two sessions reuse a local activity ID; and a rubric revision changes what counts as acceptable pragmatic production. Reconstruct trusted event occurrence and **actual exposure** chronology using immutable session-qualified IDs, source/rubric versions and receipt-order permutations. Deduplicate first attempts, quarantine ambiguous exposure, and never manufacture comparability across incompatible rubric versions.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed source and separate skill gates.** For A–F emit actual versioned can-do, blueprint, prerequisite, task, evaluator/rubric, permissible support, receptive/productive construct mapping, independent mastery requirement, delayed and unseen transfer, due reviews and exit gate. Unsupported mappings are `UNSUPPORTED`; absent modality/evaluator is `UNAVAILABLE`. Never compensate missing productive evidence with recognition scores.
2. **Recognition is not unaided generation.** In A/C, distinguish answer-option recognition, cued completion, constrained production and free production. Preserve first unaided semantic quality and assistance chronology. A correct option selection, polished answer after a model or copied transcript is not proof of independent productive mastery.
3. **Selection is not delivery.** In B/F, record candidate and chosen bridges plus per-target eligible, assigned, rendered/heard, attempted and completed activities. A failed render, unplayed audio or unknown word-bank exposure cannot be counted as delivered or safely unaided.
4. **Compare sequencing without unequal dosage.** In B/E, match or explicitly report differences in prerequisite readiness, per-target opportunities, item difficulty, practice time when available, feedback, cues, exposure, task construct, topic and recency. Label unbalanced arms `BRIDGE_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED` rather than declaring one method superior.
5. **Independent delayed production is the outcome.** In A/B/E, require meaningful intervening material/time and source-valid independently completed unseen-context production when the objective requires it. Report eligible/offered/completed/missing delayed probe denominators. Immediate assisted correctness and calendar rollover alone do not establish durable learning.
6. **Cross-modal exposure is tracked.** In C/F, answer-bearing content heard, read, shown in captions, word banks or tutor hints must be linked to later probes across modalities. A different screen or modality does not make the same exposed answer unseen.
7. **Accessibility and missing modalities remain honest.** In D, source-permitted accessibility supports do not imply inability or assistance when they do not reveal the target; missing oral evaluation, learner pauses and unavailable capture remain unknown/unavailable rather than pass/fail. Preserve distinct CEFR skill requirements.
8. **Policy is reversible, balanced and replay-safe.** In E/F, show eligible options, selection, delivery, independent later outcomes, uncertainty and next reversible decision. Contradictory evidence can change the bridge; duplicate/offline event permutations cannot. Protect due reviews, required receptive/productive coverage and soft interest rotation without permanently classifying learners.
9. **No documentation-to-efficacy inflation.** For each history output source/rubric IDs, skill-gate ledger, candidate and delivered practice sequence, per-target opportunity/exposure counts, first/final attempts, cues, independent delayed and unseen outcomes with missingness, topic/skill coverage, provisional hypothesis/uncertainty and assertion-level `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. Green documentation CI does not establish executable adaptation, CEFR certification or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, immutable synthetic events with receipt-order permutations, source-resolved can-do/task/rubric versions, separate receptive and productive gates, comparable bridge opportunities or explicit confound labels, trusted actual delivery and cross-modal cue exposure, first-attempt semantic outcomes, independently completed meaningfully delayed unseen productive transfer with eligible/offered/completed/missing denominators, topic/skill balance, reversible policy decisions and assertion-level verdicts. Unknown exposure, unavailable oral/listening evaluation, missing follow-ups and unverified source equivalence stay explicitly unknown or unsupported.

Any future claim that an individualized receptive-to-productive bridge improves **durable independent production and transfer** requires separately reviewed longitudinal real-learner evidence against a credible balanced comparator, sufficient delayed unaided outcomes, checks for harm among sparse-data and accessibility-supported learners, and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor uses recognition as a possible stepping stone, but only credits independent communication when the learner can actually do it without the stepping stone.**
