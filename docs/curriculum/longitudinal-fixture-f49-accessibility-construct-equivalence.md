# F49 — Accessible pathways must preserve the language construct being assessed

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, released CEFR level, verified accessibility implementation, clinical inference or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F48 prevents graduation through unrelated strength. F49 isolates **construct-preserving access versus construct-changing assistance**: learners may need different ways to perceive instructions, navigate activities, enter responses, or receive feedback, but a change that supplies the language comprehension or production being evaluated cannot silently earn the same independent CEFR evidence. Accessibility is not a fixed “learning style”; an accommodation is not automatically a hint, a diagnosis, a failure or a waiver of a required objective.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real, versioned CEFR can-do and source rubric** before selecting a test. Record exactly what is measured: e.g. comprehension of **spoken** language, comprehension of **written** language, independent **written** production, or **spoken** production. Distinguish task directions, stimulus, response channel, linguistic content and scoring construct. Freeze the source blueprint, prerequisites, evaluator and required independent evidence/exit gates. Do not invent a universal modality substitution rule or passing threshold. When a source rubric or modality-specific accommodation policy is absent, report `UNSUPPORTED` and request curricular review; never assume that different skills are interchangeable.

Use a deterministic clock/seed, versioned accessibility-and-construct policy, immutable synthetic events across genuine sessions, comparable item difficulty/novelty and a balanced instructional comparator. Define a **construct map** for each task: required perceptual input, target language processing, target output, permitted nonlinguistic assistance, prohibited linguistic assistance and any validated alternative form. Mark the accommodation's **requested**, **approved**, **offered**, **actually delivered** and **used** states separately; failed delivery cannot be credited as access. Use a task-specific, not person-wide, equivalence decision: `EQUIVALENT`, `CONSTRUCT_CHANGED`, `UNVALIDATED`, `NOT_DELIVERED` or `UNAVAILABLE`. A learner's preference or disclosed condition does not prove equivalence or mastery.

Record stable learner pseudonym, level-qualified can-do, item/rubric/evaluator and construct-map versions, run/session/event/probe IDs, trusted occurrence/upload times, instruction and accommodation versions, delivery telemetry, device/input constraints when known, response provenance, first unaided attempt, model/hint/retry exposure, assistive-technology contribution, objective-specific quality, actual elapsed delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, eligibility/completion/missing follow-up, review debt and topic family. Separate **access needs**, **instructional-method hypotheses**, **construct-equivalence judgments**, **mastery evidence** and **level release**. Do not store diagnoses or sensitive health narratives in a curriculum ledger; minimize data and honor user agency and applicable privacy controls.

Predeclare `ACCESS_EQUIVALENT`, `CONSTRUCT_CHANGED`, `LINGUISTIC_ASSISTANCE`, `ACCESS_NOT_DELIVERED`, `ALTERNATIVE_UNVALIDATED`, `INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE_PENDING`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `REVIEW_DUE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Unknown is neither a fail nor a pass.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Motor-access support without language assistance.** A learner completes an independent writing can-do using a switch/alternative keyboard, enlarged controls and extra navigation time. The device selects keys but supplies no words, grammar, translation, autocomplete or rewriting. Under a source-validated construct map, score the learner's original independent text against the unchanged writing rubric; record access support without falsely marking a tutor hint. If the actual device behavior cannot be verified, withhold equivalence rather than assume it.
- **B — Captions change a listening construct.** A learner requests captions/transcript for a spoken-language listening assessment. Captions may be excellent *instructional* access, but visible target-language words could substitute reading for listening. Preserve useful accessible practice; do not award unaided listening evidence from the captioned attempt without an explicitly validated source-approved equivalent listening assessment. Offer a genuinely accessible alternative where available, or mark the listening gate unassessed/`UNAVAILABLE` without treating disability as failure.
- **C — Screen reader and text-to-speech boundaries.** A screen reader reads interface directions aloud during a written-reading task, leaving the target written passage untouched; this can be construct-preserving if source-validated. In a second attempt, text-to-speech reads the **target passage**, potentially replacing written decoding with listening. Distinguish these deliveries, source-specific reading demands and response provenance. Do not silently score the second as independent reading, nor prohibit benign navigation support.
- **D — Speech-to-text and language-generation contamination.** During a written-production task, dictated text is transcribed verbatim with verified no paraphrasing, grammar repair or predictive suggestions. The source construct map determines whether this preserves the intended writing evidence or changes required spelling/orthography. A later “smart rewrite” inserts fluent clauses, or a speaking task uses synthetic speech generated from typed text. Mark those contributions `LINGUISTIC_ASSISTANCE`/`CONSTRUCT_CHANGED` for the affected construct; no assisted answer becomes independent writing or spoken production by relabeling.
- **E — Equal objectives, different accessible routes.** Two learners share a CEFR can-do and unchanged evaluator. One needs visual enlargement and slower nonlinguistic navigation; the other needs keyboard-only access and an alternative explanation layout. Their instructional pacing, scaffold choice and interest-weighted contexts may diverge as uncertain hypotheses after comparable later independent outcomes. Both retain the same required objective, evidence standard, due reviews and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation. Do not force inaccessible exploration, permanently classify either learner or treat an accommodation preference as measured learning superiority.
- **F — Offline replay, missing delivery and changing accommodation policy.** A completed, construct-equivalent independent attempt is uploaded twice after a later assisted attempt; a requested caption option was never delivered; an offline event has an older rubric/construct-map version; and an accommodation flag is copied from another task. Deduplicate by stable event ID, use original task/construct and trusted chronology, preserve actual delivered-versus-requested support, quarantine unvalidated version migrations and do not transfer equivalence across skills. Two replay orders yield identical objective ledgers, review obligations and provisional teaching decisions.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-defined construct.** For A–F output exact source blueprint and level-qualified can-do, task stimulus/response demands, rubric/evaluator versions, source-mandated independent/transfer/review/exit gates and the task-specific construct map. If the source cannot justify a substitution, mark `UNSUPPORTED` rather than fabricate equivalence.
2. **Access is not automatically a hint.** In A, verified nonlinguistic motor/navigation support can preserve independence when the source validates the route. Score only the learner-generated language with the unchanged rubric; record actual delivery and uncertainty.
3. **Listening is not reading.** In B, captions/transcripts that reveal the target language may change the assessed listening construct. Allow accessible instruction, but do not convert captioned reading success into independent listening mastery or silently waive source-required listening evidence.
4. **Stimulus boundary matters.** In C, distinguish screen-reading of interface directions from audio rendering of the target reading passage. Evaluate equivalence against the actual source reading construct, not the tool's name or a blanket assistive-tech rule.
5. **Output provenance matters.** In D, verbatim transcription and automated language generation are not interchangeable; source-specific orthography/spoken-production demands control whether an alternative is equivalent. Assisted rewrites, generated voice, models and retries never masquerade as unaided target production.
6. **Equal standards with feasible accessible choice.** In E, adapt pace, explanation, response access, scaffolding and contexts without changing shared CEFR can-dos, rubrics, independent gates or due reviews. Sample only genuinely accessible implemented alternatives; keep learner agency and balanced skill/topic opportunities.
7. **Delivery, replay and policy-version integrity.** In F, requested-but-undelivered accommodations cannot prove accessible exposure; stable IDs, original objective, trusted chronology, completed-run provenance and frozen construct-map/rubric versions yield identical ledgers under duplicate/out-of-order replay. Quarantine unsupported migrations.
8. **No disability penalty or phantom success.** Across A–F distinguish unassessed, inaccessible, `UNAVAILABLE`, assisted and independently met evidence. Do not infer diagnosis, treat missing latency as poor ability, equate inaccessible performance with lack of mastery, or claim listening/speaking functionality not implemented. Expose unmet accessibility coverage and a nonpunitive route to further assessment.
9. **Conservative release and longitudinal claim boundary.** No level graduation when a source-required can-do lacks valid construct-equivalent independent evidence or a required delayed/transfer gate. A synthetic PASS proves a software invariant only; method benefit requires repeated comparable later unaided retention/transfer against a balanced baseline and separate ethically reviewed learner evidence.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, injected clock/seed, immutable events, source blueprint/rubric/evaluator and task-specific construct maps, requested/approved/offered/delivered/used access records, assistive technology's actual language contribution, first-attempt/hint/model/retry provenance, eligible/completed/missing delayed independent retrieval and unseen-context transfer, supported/unavailable modality coverage, both replay orders, reason codes and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. Include a source-approved accessible alternative or explicit `UNSUPPORTED` where equivalence is unvalidated. Never manufacture an accommodation, modality, learner diagnosis, success, causal effect or arbitrary threshold.

Any claim of durable benefit from accessible adaptive instruction requires separate reviewed longitudinal evidence and ethically reviewed human evaluation. **Access can change the route to showing a skill; it cannot quietly change which skill was shown.** This document does not implement accommodations, certify CEFR mastery or authorize opening levels.
