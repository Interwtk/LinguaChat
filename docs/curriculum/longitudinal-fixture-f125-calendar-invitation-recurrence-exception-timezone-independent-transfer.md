# F125 — Calendar invitations, recurrence exceptions, time-zone provenance and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run on a named exact engine HEAD against real versioned CEFR-linked tasks, independently validated multilingual calendar/invitation sources and accessible equivalents, and delayed first-unaided transfer. This does **not** ship a calendar connector, event editor, invitation sender, time-zone conversion engine, scheduling assistant, CEFR mastery, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 protect delivered-help chronology and tutor-origin mistakes; F49 protects accessibility; F50 keeps interests nonexclusive; F72 protects task goals and constraints; F82/F95 cover time references; F103 protects consequential confirmation; F104 isolates method from difficulty; F107 covers unreliable tutor feedback; F117 covers register/variety; F118 covers conditions, exceptions and negation; F119 covers clarification; F120 covers attributed certainty; F121 covers structured fields; F123 covers decision paths; F124 covers spatial route constraints. **Distinct construct:** can the learner independently **interpret, negotiate and accurately communicate a source-authorized event occurrence or invitation** by binding event identity, date, clock time, effective time zone, recurrence and exceptions, attendance status, and source version to the intended communicative action? Unlike an isolated time expression, this requires cross-field **occurrence-level binding** and distinguishes a proposed invitation from a confirmed meeting. It is not an arithmetic-speed test, a stable “calendar learner” type or a license to book real events. Source-authored tasks must remain CEFR- and age-appropriate; beginners can interpret explicit dates/times without an invented daylight-saving or recurrence-algorithm prerequisite.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Single occurrence versus recurring series.** A fictional language-club calendar v1 lists “Conversation practice: every Tuesday at 16:00, except Tuesday 13 October.” The authoritative occurrence set includes 6 October and 20 October, excludes 13 October. Learner A's first unaided message says “The club is cancelled every Tuesday”; learner B says “There is no session on the 13th, but the other Tuesdays still have sessions.” Counterfactual: change only the source-authored exception to “the whole October series is cancelled”; the correct message must change. Changing the color or ordering of occurrence tiles must not change the source occurrence set. If the exception label is cropped, affected recurrence evidence is UNSUPPORTED rather than a learner error.

**B — Proposed, accepted and confirmed are not interchangeable.** A fictional museum workshop invitation says “Suggested: Friday 9:00; waiting for organizer confirmation.” One invitee clicks “Accept” while the organizer has not confirmed the time. Learner A tells a friend “The workshop is definitely at nine”; learner B says “I accepted the proposed time, but it is not confirmed yet.” Counterfactual: a source-authoritative organizer update explicitly confirms 9:00; a direct confirmed statement becomes valid. If the task only asks the learner to report their own RSVP, “I accepted” is valid even without organizer confirmation. The evaluator must bind the actor and requested communicative purpose, not reward generic hedging.

**C — Source-qualified time zones and local-date rollover.** A fictional remote book-club invitation explicitly states “Thursday 22:30, Zone A (UTC+02:00)” and a **source-validated** Zone B rendering of “Thursday 17:30 (UTC-03:00).” Learner A reads 22:30 as the Zone B time; learner B reports 17:30 Zone B with the zone label. In a separate source-authored late-night case, the validated recipient display crosses into the **next calendar day**; the learner must preserve the recipient's date rather than mechanically copy the source date. Counterfactual: update the authoritative event time or offset and independently validate the resulting recipient display; expected answers follow the new source. When a named zone's daylight-saving transition creates a nonexistent or repeated local time, do not guess which instant was intended: ask a source-valid clarification and mark DST_GAP_OR_FOLD_UNRESOLVED. Do not demand manual UTC arithmetic from an A1 learner when the descriptor only requires reading an explicit validated display.

**D — Conflicting versions, translation and accessible render.** A fictional community-class event v1 says “Wednesday at 18:00”; v2, effective later, moves the **single occurrence** to Thursday at 18:00 while leaving future weekly sessions on Wednesday. Learner A responds to the v1 message before v2 is published: evaluate against v1. Learner B reads v2 and correctly distinguishes this week's Thursday occurrence from the unchanged series. On one phone the “this week only” label is cropped; a translated notification incorrectly says “every week”; a screen reader announces the old date after the new one without a version cue. Preserve source-effective and **actually delivered** representations, quarantine affected interpretation comparisons, and retain independently valid partial language evidence. Accessible text/audio must be independently validated for occurrence, version, date, time, zone, exception and status—not assumed equivalent because it is fluent.

**E — Assistance chronology and replay cannot manufacture mastery.** A learner independently writes “The Thursday class starts at six” at 10:00. A tutor's conversion card was queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01: it did not assist that first attempt. On a later retry, the learner copies the card: mark ASSISTED. An offline replay uploads the first attempt twice: count it once by immutable session-qualified event ID. Two calendar entries both called “Class A” are not identical if event/occurrence IDs differ. An RSVP status that changes after the answer cannot retroactively turn the earlier first attempt into an error. A copied correction cannot become delayed unaided retrieval even if a new calendar page is opened.

**F — Longitudinal reversible teaching and truly unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A performs well on immediate highlighted-calendar exercises but later independently misreports exception scope. After **actually delivered** OCCURRENCE_VERSUS_SERIES_CONTRAST on comparable CEFR tasks, A later sends a correct unaided message about an unseen recurring sports-club event with a different exception structure and recipient. Learner B instead improves after STATUS_AND_ACTOR_ROLEPLAY on independently comparable invitation tasks. Compare baseline proficiency, target language/variety, recurrence complexity, number of exceptions, explicit versus inferred zone, status ambiguity, topic familiarity, modality, accessibility, actual help exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If A only sees explicit one-exception calendars while B sees multi-zone, multi-exception invitations, flag CALENDAR_COMPLEXITY_METHOD_CONFOUNDED and infer **no method winner**. Recoloring the same calendar, renaming the club while preserving the exact answer path, or reusing the same occurrence is not unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable later outcomes may support different **actually delivered** next methods, with uncertainty; contradictory evidence lowers confidence and permits reversal. Preserve due reviews, receptive/productive balance, alternative exploration and nonexclusive topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and source linkage.** A–F resolve real versioned descriptor, blueprint/prerequisites, source-authorized event/occurrence IDs, recurrence/exception/status/time-zone provenance, target language/variety, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No invented scheduling expertise prerequisite.
2. **Event identity and recurrence binding.** A/D/E distinguish series, single occurrence, exception and cancellation scope. Cosmetic layout/notification changes cannot alter the authoritative occurrence set; source-effective edits can. A wrong-occurrence answer is not rescued by fluent grammar.
3. **Invitation status, actor and communicative intent.** B distinguishes proposed, invited, accepted, organizer-confirmed and unresolved states; valid utterances bind the correct actor and question. Do not equate attendee acceptance with organizer confirmation or reward hedging when certainty is source-supported.
4. **Time-zone and temporal validity.** C/D preserve date, clock time, effective zone/offset, source-validated local display, possible date rollover and source version. Missing zone or DST gap/fold produces bounded clarification/UNSUPPORTED, not an invented conversion. Beginner objectives do not silently become arithmetic exams.
5. **Actual first-unaided provenance and deduplication.** D/E record first authored response, source-effective version, retries, and tutor help **actually rendered before** the attempt. Queued/post-answer help is not retroactive assistance; copied models are assisted; offline replay does not double-count.
6. **Multilingual and accessible delivered-stimulus validity.** A/C/D validate exception wording, status labels, locale date order, accessible reading order, crop, translation and transcript against the authoritative source. Unreliable delivery quarantines affected construct evidence while preserving valid partial language performance.
7. **Delayed retention and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningful separated learning opportunities, source-authored new occurrence/exception/status/time-zone structures and recipients, and no answer-bearing help. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Renaming or recoloring the same event is insufficient.
8. **Reversible method selection and soft interests.** F records feasible, assigned and **actually delivered** alternatives, baseline/task comparability, complexity/topic confounds, uncertainty, exploration, contradictory outcomes, protected listening/reading/writing/speaking/free production, due reviews and exit gates. Interests shift example likelihood but never exclude adjacent, neutral or unfamiliar contexts or required objectives; no permanent “calendar learner” label.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/event/occurrence/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, source-effective and actually rendered accessible artifacts, first/assisted utterances, event/help ledger, counterfactuals, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Docs-only CI is not an executable calendar evaluator, reproducible product defect or evidence of improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked tasks; independently validated multilingual calendar/invitation sources and accessible equivalents; authoritative event/occurrence/recurrence/status/zone/version bindings; source-effective and actually rendered stimuli; first-unaided evidence; meaningful delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen calendar-to-communication transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded instructional alternatives; protected skill/review/exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and recurrence/exception/actor/zone/rollover/DST/version/translation/crop/replay counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Missing source authority, ambiguous zone, conflicting version, unreliable rendering, unvalidated accommodation or absent follow-up leaves the affected claim **UNSUPPORTED/unknown** while independently established partial language evidence remains.

A stronger claim that adaptive calendar instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual, multivariety and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparison, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps the learner communicate which event, which occurrence, whose status, and which local time is actually supported—not merely copy a highlighted calendar tile.**
