## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — A name versus an aim: boundaries not guaranteed by the signal.** A fictional organizer says **"We need a name"** while choosing a title for a new club. A different organizer says **"We need an aim"** while choosing the club's purpose. The acoustic sequences can overlap in ordinary connected speech. With the relevant preceding turns, a learner may independently identify the intended *title* versus *purpose*. If the preceding context is withheld or both interpretations remain plausible, **"Do you mean the club's name or its goal?"** is a valid first-unaided recipient-facing response. A forced choice based solely on an imagined phonetic boundary fails the oracle. Do not treat correct written spelling from a transcript as listening evidence.

**B — Want to versus want a: reduction changes the next action.** In a fictional library conversation **"I want to book a study room"** may be reduced in speech to **"I wanna book a study room"**, a request about reserving a room. **"I want a book about study skills"** is a request for a physical book. The learner's first-unaided response must address the *reservation* versus *book* as supported by the actual utterance and conversation; **"Are you trying to reserve a room or find a book?"** is acceptable when the delivered signal/context is insufficient. A written exercise that simply labels *wanna* is diagnostic practice, not independent oral transfer. Do not penalize an appropriate regional full form.

**C — Got a versus got to: object possession versus obligation.** A fictional volunteer says **"I've got a badge"** (possession) or **"I've got to check the badges"** (an action they need to perform), with natural reductions in actual speech. A listener should not infer an obligation from the first utterance or possession of a badge from the second. A context-preserving paraphrase or targeted clarification passes. When the recording is noisy enough to obscure the relevant material, quarantine the item instead of awarding false certainty. Match lexical burden, speaker, rate and audio quality when comparing teaching methods.

**D — She's as she is versus she has: contraction meaning from syntax and context.** In a fictional study group **"She's waiting outside"** expands to *she is waiting*, while **"She's been waiting outside"** expands to *she has been waiting*. The learner independently relays the relevant state and aspect without forcing a single expansion of *'s* in all contexts. A different speaker or new continuation is required for genuinely unseen transfer. A text-only grammar identification task may support reading evidence but cannot stand in for unheard speech.

**E — Delivered signal, caption drift, replay and help chronology.** The reviewed original audio says **"I've got to check the list"**, but an erroneous caption says **"I've got a checklist"** and an alternate TTS rendering blurs the boundary. Preserve the actual audio/caption versions, quarantine meaning-changing drift and do not mark a learner wrong for following what was actually exposed. At 09:02 the learner, before any transcript appears, independently asks **"Do you have a checklist, or do you need to check the list?"**. At 09:03 a boundary hint is rendered; at 09:04 the learner repeats its exact wording. Preserve the 09:02 FIRST_UNAIDED clarification, mark 09:04 ASSISTED and deduplicate replayed event IDs. A selected but never rendered hint does not retroactively contaminate the earlier answer. Slowed playback and replays must be logged, not silently presented as unassisted first exposure. An accessible transcript is valuable access, but not proof of independent listening to the unheard original.

**F — Outcome-responsive method exploration, reversal and new-voice transfer.** Learner A immediately segments practiced audio after reading written boundary markers but fails delayed first-unaided comprehension with a new voice and new utterances. After matched **actually delivered** SHORT_CHUNK_TO_CONNECTED_SPEECH trials, later independent new-speaker comprehension and clarification improve in neutral and unfamiliar topics. Cautiously raise that method's probability while retaining balanced listening, full-form contrasts and exploration. Learner B initially benefits from chunks but later overinterprets an acoustically ambiguous *a name/an aim* probe; reduce confidence, review the evidence and reopen contextual prediction and clarification methods. If chunk trials use studio recordings and the comparison method uses noisy recordings, **no method winner** is inferable. Replaying the same clip with a new picture, changing only music to games, or reading the transcript is **not** unseen auditory transfer. Missing later probes remain missing, not failures or gains.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing objective and reviewed audio oracle.** A–F must bind an existing versioned CEFR listening/communication can-do, reviewed source-world/acoustic ambiguity oracle, actual accessible audio and context, first-unaided rubric, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Acoustically plausible boundary ambiguity.** A accepts a context-supported interpretation or a targeted clarification when *a name/an aim* is not reliably distinguishable; it never grades invented sound distinctions or assumes one universal accent.
3. **Reduction and recipient-action meaning.** B distinguishes booking a room from seeking a book using actual context/signal; accepts natural reductions and full forms; does not equate written recognition of *wanna* with independent listening.
4. **Possession versus obligation.** C preserves *got a* versus *got to* meanings, validates only signal-supported claims, and quarantines unusable or misleading audio rather than assigning learner errors.
5. **Contraction expansion and variation.** D uses syntax/context to interpret *she's waiting* versus *she's been waiting*; accepts reviewed language-variety/register equivalents and does not turn a single English contraction rule into a cross-language CEFR gate.
6. **Actual delivery, help chronology and accessibility.** E audits original and rendered audio/TTS/ASR/captions/translations, preserves first authorship before hints, logs replay/slow playback and answer exposure, deduplicates stable events, and separates accessible reading from listening constructs.
7. **Controlled uncertain reversible instruction.** F compares feasible **actually delivered** methods on matched objective, speaker, accent, rate, signal quality, ambiguity, help, topic, prior exposure, spacing and follow-up opportunity. Sparse, confounded or contradictory evidence yields no winner; later regression reduces confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent listening and soft interests.** A model-assisted transcript match, same-clip replay, near-identical phrase with only a topic swap or a same-session repair cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, **new reviewed recording/voice or materially new boundary pattern and recipient information gap**, first-unaided meaning/action or justified clarification, and explicit follow-up missingness. Preserve shared CEFR gates, skill balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Documentation CI is not executable acoustic QA, a shipped adaptive tutor, or evidence of improved human retention. Require exact-head executable assertions against actual objectives and delivered assets, demonstrated longitudinal outcome-driven instructional choices, and independently reviewed later unaided listening transfer versus a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design/probe histories, not observed learner outcomes or executable QA. Preserve genuine acoustic ambiguity, context, actual delivery, accessible construct labels, reversible instructional adaptation and UNSUPPORTED gates until independently reviewed implementation and longitudinal evidence exist.
