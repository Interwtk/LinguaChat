# F112 — TTS-rendering channel errors, listening-evidence attribution and independent unseen transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD using real versioned CEFR-linked listening tasks, actual authorized audible stimuli, player/render telemetry, valid response evidence and longitudinal first-unaided outcomes. This is **not** a shipped TTS quality gate, listening evaluator, reproduced production defect, CEFR listening mastery or proof of human learning gains. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 guards genuinely unseen transfer; F37 distinguishes assigned versus delivered help; F38 tutor-origin errors; F49 accessibility construct equivalence; F56 feedback timing; F62 receptive/productive boundaries; F104 confound-aware method choice; F107 unreliable feedback provenance; F109 code-switching; F110 paralinguistic cues; F111 ASR channel noise and speaking-evidence attribution. **Distinct construct:** when a learner listens to tutor-generated speech, can LinguaChat distinguish a failure to comprehend **valid, actually heard target-language audio** from a synthetic-voice, rendering, playback, device or transcript error, without awarding listening mastery from reading, imitation or assisted replay? A source text or TTS request is not proof of what was acoustically delivered.

## Source, channel and independence contract

Resolve the **real versioned** CEFR descriptor/can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, listening communicative purpose, interlocutor and target variety/register, authorized source utterance, semantic contrasts (including numbers, names, negation and timing), task difficulty, rubric/evaluator, supported audio and accessible route, allowed playback rate/repeats, independent threshold, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer, due reviews and exit gate. Specify whether the construct is gist, detail, inference, spoken interaction, prosodic meaning or recognition of a phonological contrast. Never silently substitute written comprehension, spoken production, ASR transcription or recognition of an already displayed answer for the required listening construct. When audible source evidence or a valid source-approved alternative is unavailable, mark the listening claim **UNSUPPORTED/UNAVAILABLE**, not learner failure.

Keep a privacy-minimized, immutable **audible-stimulus provenance** trace: source text/SSML and version; source-authorized language, variety and pronunciation; TTS engine, voice, synthesis configuration, dictionary/phoneme overrides and audio artifact/version; source-to-waveform verification/adjudication (if available) with uncertainty; requested versus generated versus buffered versus **actually played/audible** segments; playback start/end, interruption, clipping, silence, overlap, buffering, rate, repeats, device/channel issues and captions/transcripts actually displayed; learner first response and subsequent retries; answer-bearing hints/models/feedback delivered before each attempt; recipient/context, independent semantic quality, delayed/unseen probe eligibility/offering/completion/missingness; chosen teaching method, feasible alternatives, exposure dose, task difficulty, topic familiarity and prior item exposure. Telemetry can show a player advanced, **not** certify what a person heard: distinguish observed delivery from inferred audibility and use authorized adjudication when critical.

Task-local labels may include SOURCE_VALID_AUDIO, SOURCE_WAVEFORM_MISMATCH, TTS_MISPRONUNCIATION, TTS_OMISSION, TTS_INSERTION, WRONG_LANGUAGE_OR_VOICE, PROSODY_CHANGED_MEANING, AUDIO_NOT_GENERATED, PLAYBACK_NOT_DELIVERED, CLIPPED_OR_OVERLAPPED, DEVICE_AUDIBILITY_UNKNOWN, VALID_SLOW_PLAYBACK, REPLAY_PERMITTED, REPLAY_AS_SCAFFOLD, CAPTION_EXPOSURE, FIRST_UNAIDED_LISTENING, ASSISTED_LISTENING, READING_ONLY_EVIDENCE, LISTENING_CONSTRUCT_UNSUPPORTED, LEARNER_ORIGIN_COMPREHENSION_ERROR, CHANNEL_ORIGIN_ERROR, DELAYED_UNAIDED_RETRIEVAL, UNSEEN_UNAIDED_TRANSFER, FOLLOWUP_MISSING, REPLAY_DEDUPED, VERSION_CONFLICT_QUARANTINED, BALANCED_DEFAULT and NOT_APPLICABLE. These describe **task events**, never stable auditory/visual learner types, accents, disability, intelligence or effort.

Feasible reversible instructional candidates include BALANCED_DEFAULT, MEANING_FIRST_LISTENING, DETAIL_CONTRAST_WITH_VALID_AUDIO, SHORT_CHUNKS_WITH_FADING, LISTEN_THEN_INDEPENDENT_RESPONSE, SOURCE_VALID_RATE_VARIATION, INTERLOCUTOR_VARIATION, VALID_REPLAY_WITH_HELP_ACCOUNTING, AUDIO_CHANNEL_TROUBLESHOOTING and SOURCE_VALID_OTHER. Repairing a broken speaker, wrong TTS voice or corrupted audio is **measurement repair**, not evidence that a pedagogy improved learning. Adapt modality mix, explanation, scaffolding, pacing, spacing, activity ordering and examples only without reducing CEFR objectives, independent evidence, protected skills or due reviews. Topic interests remain a **separate soft sampler** that rotates preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Source sentence versus actually synthesized sentence.** A source-authored listening detail task requires understanding “The train leaves at **fifteen** past six.” The authorized source text and rubric are correct, but one synthetic voice audibly says “fifty” because of a documented normalization fault. A learner responds “fifty” consistently with the faulty audible stimulus. A second learner hears verified correct audio and answers “fifty” incorrectly. A third receives only a successful synthesis-request log with no recoverable audible artifact. Do not score the first learner's channel-induced mismatch as deficient listening; evaluate the second against the valid stimulus; mark the third listening verdict UNSUPPORTED. A transcript containing “fifteen” cannot override the acoustic evidence or award mastery.

**B — Negation, prosody and language variety.** A source-authored task contrasts “You **can** enter” with “You **can't** enter” and requires an authorized intelligible variety. A voice drops the negative segment; another renders the intended words but shifts prosodic emphasis so a source-authorized pragmatic inference becomes ambiguous; a third uses a task-valid variety unfamiliar to the learner but verified intelligible to the intended recipient. Keep phonetic, semantic and pragmatic constructs separate. Do not assume TTS punctuation expresses the intended prosody or penalize an unfamiliar but valid variety as incorrect. If source-valid adjudication cannot resolve the contrast, the relevant detail/inference claim is UNSUPPORTED.

**C — Requested playback, delivered playback and transcript leakage.** One learner requests audio, but the player fails before the target phrase; a transcript displays the entire answer and the learner selects it. Another hears valid audio and answers correctly before any transcript is rendered; a later transcript-assisted repeat must not retroactively contaminate the first response. A third opens an answer-bearing caption before the initial response, then listens to the same clip. Separate request, generation, render, audibility uncertainty, transcript exposure and first-response chronology. The first and third histories cannot receive first-unaided listening credit solely for correct reading-based answers; the second may supply valid listening evidence if its source rubric and delivery are established.

**D — Slow speed, replay and construct-specific accommodation.** In a source-authored untimed listening-comprehension task, slower playback is explicitly permitted without revealing words; a learner independently extracts meaning. A different task explicitly assesses real-time turn uptake and limits replay, so repeated slowed playback changes the construct. A third learner uses an approved accessibility route that preserves the listening target, while a fourth hears text-to-speech of the **written target** during a reading assessment. Respect task-specific permissions and source-validated equivalence. Never call all replay cheating, never assume unlimited replay preserves a timed construct, and never convert target-passage TTS into independent reading evidence.

**E — Method gains versus audio quality, exposure and topic confounds.** Across matched fictional sessions, learner A initially answers more music-themed items correctly with slow segmented TTS, but those items have cleaner audio and repeated target exposure; delayed unaided comprehension with a new neutral speaker is worse. After matched audio quality, task difficulty, practice dose, prior exposure and follow-up, independent normal-speed contrast practice yields better delayed unseen comprehension for A, while learner B shows the opposite supported pattern. A third learner's device changes mid-comparison and the follow-up is missing. Treat early effects as confounded, keep capability-local uncertain reversible hypotheses, sample feasible alternatives, retain required reading/writing/speaking/free-production coverage, and rotate preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics. Never optimize player completion, replay counts or assisted immediate accuracy as durable learning.

**F — Offline caching, audio version drift and duplicate play receipts.** Two real sessions reuse the same local clip ID; a true retry packet is duplicated; an offline valid first response uploads after a later answer-bearing transcript; the cached audio was generated with voice version V1 while a regenerated URL now serves V2 with a changed number; a player reports “completed” even though the output device was muted. Use session-qualified IDs, trusted occurrence/render chronology, audio artifact hashes and source/voice/rubric versions to deduplicate only true replays. Do not reconstruct V1's waveform from V2, infer audibility from completion alone, or let upload order rewrite first-unaided status. Quarantine incompatible versions or unresolved audible delivery; preserve allowed minimal provenance and never fabricate missing media.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid listening construct and modality.** A–F emit actual versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, source utterance, listening construct, permitted variety/rate/replay, rubric/evaluator, independent threshold, delayed/unseen transfer, reviews and exit gate. If the audio modality, source-valid route or adjudication is absent, return UNSUPPORTED/UNAVAILABLE, not a substitute reading score.
2. **Source-to-waveform fidelity and error attribution.** A/B distinguish source text/SSML, generated waveform, source-authorized pronunciation/prosody, delivered segment and learner response. Misread numbers, dropped negation, language-routing errors and meaning-changing prosody are channel faults unless trustworthy evidence attributes a learner-origin comprehension error; unresolved cases stay unknown.
3. **Requested is not delivered or heard.** C/F distinguish synthesis request, file existence, buffering, player state, audible segment and user audibility uncertainty. Neither a success callback nor a transcript proves valid listening input. Technical silence or clipping is not scored as learner failure.
4. **First-unaided and answer exposure chronology.** C/D/F retain exact first learner response and actual audio, transcript, caption, hint, model, replay and feedback exposure. Reading an answer-bearing transcript cannot silently earn listening mastery. Later assistance does not retroactively alter a genuinely earlier independent attempt.
5. **Task-specific replay, rate and accessibility.** B/D enforce source-authorized replay/speed and valid language variety; a permitted slower presentation may preserve one listening construct but not a real-time timing construct. Approved construct-equivalent access is not automatically a hint; reading, target-passage TTS and unrelated skill substitutions require their own source-valid evidence.
6. **Instructional selection versus channel repair.** A/E/F log candidate and actually delivered methods, support dose, item difficulty, source voice/channel, topic familiarity, exposure, comparison uncertainty and fallback. A voice/model/device fix is not a learning effect; contradictory delayed evidence can reverse a capability-local hypothesis without fixed learner types.
7. **Delayed independent retention, unseen transfer and skill coverage.** A–F use source-authored novel clips, meanings, speakers and contexts with meaningful intervening material/time and verified independent listening responses; prevent repeated/captioned clip memorization from being called unseen transfer. Emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators, protect due reviews and all required CEFR receptive/productive gates.
8. **Soft interests, replay and version invariance.** E/F rotate preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics without changing objectives/thresholds. Session-qualified deduplication, delayed upload, voice regeneration, rubric changes, player receipts and differing audio artifacts cannot manufacture listening evidence or alter historical assistance status; irreconcilable cases are quarantined.
9. **Assertion-level reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F output source/CEFR/rubric/evaluator and audio artifact/voice versions, fidelity adjudication, actual delivery/exposure chronology, channel-versus-learner attribution, uncertainty, independent and delayed/unseen outcomes, missingness denominators, method/topic controls and replay/version permutations with **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. A synthetic CI PASS is not proof of shipped TTS quality, CEFR listening mastery, human learning gains or a production defect.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; deterministic seed/clock; real versioned CEFR-linked listening tasks and approved source utterances; verifiable acoustic artifacts and source-to-waveform fidelity checks (with authorized independent adjudication when required); source-specific speed/replay/variety rules; actual playback/caption/hint chronology and audibility limitations; first-unaided responses versus reading/assisted responses; meaningfully delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen speaker/context transfer; comparable or explicitly noncomparable method/topic/audio conditions; protected skill/review/exit gates; follow-up denominators; and offline/replay/version permutations with assertion-level verdicts. Without valid acoustic input, reliable exposure provenance or independent follow-up, the affected claims remain UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

A stronger claim that listening-aware adaptation **improves durable independent comprehension** requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator or ethically reviewed study, source-valid variety/accessibility adjudication, audio-channel and missingness analysis, subgroup-harm checks and appropriate privacy safeguards. **A strong tutor must not blame a learner for words the synthetic voice changed, nor mistake reading the transcript for understanding speech.**

**Boundary:** curricular acceptance documentation only. No Pre-A1 edits, A1–C2 release, engine, Auth, cloud, infrastructure, production, Supabase, paid Claude, EvoLabs or auto-merge changes. Integration/watchdog alone aggregates owner actions.
