## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — “The left one” depends on viewpoint.** In a source-approved instruction scene, two identical parcels are on opposite sides of a table. A courier standing opposite the learner says “Take the parcel on my left.” The learner's screen-left parcel is on the courier's right. Learner A selects screen-left by default; learner B independently identifies the courier's frame and selects the courier-left parcel, then gives a recipient-valid confirmation. In a matched source world the courier says “on your left,” reversing the valid choice. In a third source world no viewpoint is established: asking “Your left or mine?” is source-valid; a forced selection cannot earn independent certainty. Hold object appearance and task burden constant.

**B — Stable object identity despite motion and occlusion.** A source-authored short sequence shows a green-labeled crate moving behind a divider and emerging on the opposite side while another similar crate stays still. The speaker says “Put that crate next to the door” with a source-valid initial pointer. Learner A binds “that” to the **current position** of the stationary lookalike; learner B follows the originally indicated object's identity through the authorized sequence and chooses the correct destination. A controlled counterfactual swaps the movement trajectories; the correct object follows source identity, not color, nearest position or last frame. If the crucial frame is missing or identity cannot be adjudicated, the correct evaluation is UNSUPPORTED/clarification, not an invented tracking success.

**C — Misleading overlays and delayed exposure.** A source-valid floor plan identifies exit B; a machine-generated highlight erroneously circles exit C. Learner A follows the highlight despite a clear source-authored legend; learner B independently uses the source legend and gives the valid route. A third learner sees a tutor-created correct circle **before** answering and cannot claim first-unaided visual grounding merely because the answer is correct. A fourth answers correctly *before* the overlay is actually rendered; a delayed network receipt must not retroactively mark that answer assisted. A separate source-approved map revision can legitimately move the exit; freeze source-effective times instead of judging historical answers against the latest map.

**D — Accessibility and different constructs.** A learner cannot use the source's small unlabeled visual arrows; an authorized structured text scene says “From the entrance, the pharmacy is the second door on the right,” preserving an explicitly validated **reading/mediation** goal. That performance cannot silently certify a visual-deixis or video comprehension goal. A source-authored tactile/spoken spatial description may count for the original task **only if the descriptor, source and evaluator prevalidate construct equivalence**. When no accessible equivalent exists, mark that construct UNSUPPORTED rather than penalizing the learner. Language variety, visual disability, device size or bandwidth is never a learner deficit. A source-valid clarification remains possible when even the accessible description lacks a unique referent.

**E — Method selection must survive unseen scenes.** Across matched fictional sessions, one learner has high immediate assisted accuracy after arrows and color highlights; another learns through VIEWPOINT_SWAP_WITH_VERBAL_CHECK with lower immediate coached accuracy. Later, on a genuinely unseen scene with new objects, neutral context, changed viewpoint and no hints, the latter method yields better first-unaided grounding and recipient action for one learner, while another learner shows the reverse. Compare or disclose baseline, object count/similarity, motion/occlusion, perspective complexity, language burden, source quality, exposure, topic familiarity, accessibility and missing follow-up. The tutor updates a **reversible, uncertain** method hypothesis only when comparable delayed independent outcomes support it; continue sampling alternatives and preserving required receptive/productive skill balance.

**F — Offline replay, frame drift and versioned reference.** Two actual offline sessions reuse a local object label `box-2` but have different session-qualified object IDs. One response is retried twice on upload; the learner's first answer predates an answer-bearing overlay, though the overlay log arrives first. A cached video shows object A left of B while a regenerated URL silently reverses the frame order; an authorized later floor-plan correction changes the target door. Deduplicate true event replays by immutable session/event identity; preserve trusted occurrence and render chronology, original media/frame/object ledger and source-effective times. Never reconstruct the historical stimulus from a mutable URL, promote a later assisted retry to independent, treat a different session's `box-2` as the same object, or relabel an earlier correct response as wrong because the source was subsequently revised. Quarantine irreconcilable versions and record unknown follow-up as missing, not failure.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Applicability and versioned source.** A–F resolve real CEFR descriptor/can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored deictic task, scene/object ledger, viewpoint, valid variety, rubric/evaluator, accessible route, independence threshold, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No source-required visual/spatial reference obligation means NOT_APPLICABLE; missing authorized scene truth means UNSUPPORTED, not a fabricated universal CEFR requirement.
2. **Referent uniqueness and perspective.** A/D distinguish speaker-left, learner-left, map-left and unknown frames; unique reference from underdetermination. A legitimate source-valid clarification can pass; visually salient or nearest-object guesses cannot replace explicit source authority.
3. **Object identity and scene time.** B/F preserve source object identity through motion, occlusion, frame swaps and screen position changes. Score the correct source-time referent and recipient destination; missing decisive frames or inconsistent scene ledgers are unknown/UNSUPPORTED rather than learner-origin errors.
4. **Recipient consequence.** A/B/C/D require the action licensed by the source-authorized reference: select, place, guide, defer or clarify. A fluent description with the wrong object, location, temporal state or safety-critical destination cannot pass recipient-valid transfer; do not demand actions absent from the frozen task.
5. **Source truth versus generated cues.** C/F distinguish original approved visual/linguistic evidence from auto-highlights, visual-model boxes, overlays, regenerated media and later corrections. A model's confidence cannot override source truth; render chronology decides assistance, not assignment or packet arrival.
6. **Accessibility, consent and construct fidelity.** B/C/D/F require permitted stimuli, minimal retention, no biometric/sensitive inference, source-validated equivalent routes and explicit construct labels. Reading a verbal scene may assess reading/mediation but cannot silently count as a required visual/video construct; unavailable modalities are not learner failure.
7. **First unaided, delayed and unseen evidence.** A–F record first learner-authored independent grounding and recipient action versus actual hints/retries; later meaningfully separated sessions with new source-authored objects, viewpoints and contexts, plus due-review coverage. Repeated identical images, copied labels or highlighted targets do not count as unseen transfer. Emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
8. **Reversible adaptation and invariance.** E/F record feasible, assigned and actually delivered methods; baseline, difficulty, visual quality, modality/accessibility, exposure, topic and attrition confounds; uncertainty, alternative sampling and changed next choice. Deduplicated retries, delayed events and scene-version changes must not fabricate independent evidence or alter historic labels. Maintain soft preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation and all CEFR obligations.
9. **Assertion-level reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic clock/seed, exact engine HEAD, source/CEFR/rubric/evaluator hashes, authorized scene/object/frame provenance, viewpoint/ambiguity adjudication, actual cue exposure, first/assisted responses, recipient action, delayed/unseen denominators, version/replay permutations and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion**. Documentation or synthetic CI alone cannot establish shipped vision, learner mastery, real-world learning gains or a reproduced production defect.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; deterministic seed/clock; real versioned CEFR-linked spatial-reference tasks; authorized source-validated scenes and object/coordinate/frame ledger; consent and construct-valid accessible alternatives; independently adjudicated ambiguity, occlusion and source-effective versions; actual media/overlay render chronology; first-unaided grounding and recipient decisions; meaningful delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen spatial transfer; comparable or explicitly noncomparable methods, contexts and media; protected skill/review/exit requirements; follow-up denominators; and event/replay/version permutations with per-assertion verdicts. If scene authority, chronology, modality access, source truth or independent follow-up is missing, affected claims remain UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

Any stronger claim that scene-grounded adaptation **improves durable independent communication** requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence with a credible balanced comparator or ethically reviewed study, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, measurement-error/attrition analysis, privacy and subgroup-harm checks. **A strong tutor teaches the learner to identify what “this,” “there” and “left” actually refer to—and to clarify when the scene does not justify certainty.**
