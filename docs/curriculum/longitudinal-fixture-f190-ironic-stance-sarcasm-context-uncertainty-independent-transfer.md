# F190 — Irony, sarcastic stance, context-supported interpretation and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable exact-head checks bind independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific stance oracles, actually delivered context/prosody, first-authorship provenance and genuinely delayed first-unaided unseen transfer to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a shipped sarcasm detector, emotion reader, universal cultural convention, new CEFR gate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F73 concerns indirect pragmatic intent; F96 concerns conventional figurative expressions/idioms; F171 concerns prosodic question force; F176 concerns degraded audio; F186 concerns register; F189 concerns reporting another speaker's stance. **Distinct construct:** where a source-linked task genuinely requires it, the learner distinguishes an utterance's *literal positive/negative wording* from a context-supported ironic or sarcastic **evaluative stance**, communicates that stance without inventing an emotion or personal motive, and knows when evidence is insufficient to choose a nonliteral reading. Irony is not automatically hostility; teasing is not automatically consent; a negative event does not prove the speaker was sarcastic. English examples are illustrative, not universal cues or a requirement at every CEFR level.

A frozen, independently reviewed **context-and-stance oracle** records the exact utterance, preceding and subsequent turns actually available, speaker/addressee roles, event outcome, supported literal reading, supported ironic/nonliteral readings, explicit cues (e.g., contradiction between an announced success and an observed failure), and **all defensible interpretations**. Distinguish ironic praise, sincere praise, playful teasing, frustration and ambiguous understatement only where evidence supports that distinction. A task may allow a cautious interpretation or a targeted clarification instead of forcing one hidden speaker intention. A bad event by itself is not an irony oracle. Never infer cultural identity, sarcasm propensity, mental state or consent from the learner or speaker.

A frozen **channel-and-prosody oracle** records whether the learner actually heard original audio, saw a transcript, read punctuation/emoji, or received captioned/accessibility alternatives. Tone, pitch, timing and stress are available only when actually delivered and independently reviewed; punctuation, emoji and capitalization are weak and variety-dependent. If a transcript omits decisive audio cues, accept its legitimate ambiguity or mark ACCESS_DIFFERENT_CONSTRUCT/UNSUPPORTED rather than punishing a literal interpretation. Do not credit independent listening from failed audio or infer a sarcastic voice from plain text. A source-provided explicit label is part of the task stimulus only if predeclared; a tutor-provided answer label is assistance when actually shown.

A frozen **semantic-and-recipient oracle** accepts multiple natural, locally reviewed paraphrases that preserve the speaker's supported stance and uncertainty without inventing intent, anger, contempt, relationship, agreement or a commitment. “That sounds ironic given what happened” can be valid when context supports it; “Maya hates Theo” cannot be inferred from one remark. Source-appropriate clarification is valid when the reading is underdetermined. Do not equate accent, humor style, neurotype or directness with ability.

A **first-authorship and longitudinal ledger** retains pseudonymous learner, fixed can-do/task/context/stance/prosody/rubric versions, immutable session-qualified event IDs and replay deduplication, exact actually rendered turns/audio/captions/hints/models/feedback with chronology, first eligible unaided interpretation or response, supported alternatives, later assisted revision, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity, recurring error, optional reliable hesitation, and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes. Preserve meaningful elapsed time, genuinely distinct sessions and independently reviewed unseen cue/stance combinations. Unopened hints are not exposure; visible model interpretations are answer-bearing help. A corrected assisted response never overwrites first-independent evidence.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Sincere and ironic praise require context.** In a fictional club, a working projector is successfully repaired; Maya says, “Great, it works again!” A source-reviewed sincere interpretation is valid. In a distinct scripted scenario, the projector breaks again immediately before a presentation and Maya says, “Great, it broke again.” If the independently reviewed co-text supports ironic negative evaluation, a learner can paraphrase that Maya is *not pleased about the breakdown*. Reject a learner who treats both “Great” tokens as equally positive, but also reject “Maya hates the technician” as EMOTION_INVENTED/RELATIONSHIP_INVENTED. An unseen task must vary the event, words and recipient, not just replace “great” with “wonderful.”

**B — Text-only ambiguity cannot manufacture sarcasm.** A message reads “Well, that was helpful” with no prior exchange or accessible prosody. Both sincere and ironic readings remain plausible; “I'm not sure whether they mean it sincerely” or a source-valid clarification is acceptable. If a later independently reviewed turn says the volunteer's instructions solved the problem, sincere praise gains support; if the instructions caused confusion and the speaker explicitly contrasts that failure, an ironic reading may gain support. Never assume an exclamation mark, emoji or unfortunate event proves sarcasm.

**C — Prosody must be delivered, not imagined.** A scripted audio clip includes independently reviewed prosodic cues supporting an ironic reading of “Fantastic timing” after a late arrival. Learner A actually hears it and responds appropriately. Learner B receives only a transcript after audio playback fails, with no decisive context. B's cautious interpretation is not a listening failure; score the actually delivered reading/interaction route or mark the stance unresolved. A caption that faithfully includes the source's independently validated stance label can support a predeclared accessible reading task, but does not create phantom audio proficiency.

**D — Playful teasing is not hostility or permission.** In a source-validated exchange between fictional classmates, one says “Nice of you to show up early!” to a friend who arrives late; the friend replies playfully and the task supports a light teasing interpretation. The learner can acknowledge the lateness and respond without claiming the speaker is angry, that the friendship is damaged or that the remark grants permission to miss future meetings. In a second scenario with an unknown relationship and no response, a cautious neutral reply or clarification may be the only supported outcome. Do not use one culture's humor conventions as a universal key.

**E — Answer exposure and event replay cannot earn independence.** At 09:01 the tutor renders an explicit card: “The speaker is being sarcastic; they dislike the delay.” At 09:02 the learner repeats the interpretation. Mark ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED; preserve any earlier unaided response. Replaying the same event at 09:03 does not create another independent first attempt. A later genuinely distinct session with a new reviewed stance scenario, no answer-bearing model and an independent recipient-facing paraphrase may earn evidence; a synonym swap, crossed midnight or rehearsed script cannot.

**F — Reversible methods, matched comparisons and nonexclusive interests.** Learner A persistently treats ironic positive words as sincere despite reviewed contradiction cues. Across comparable real sessions, CONTEXT_CUE_MAPPING, SINCERE_IRONIC_MINIMAL_PAIRS and BALANCED_DEFAULT are **actually rendered** and alternated with matched cue strength, variety, help, accessibility and delays. Later independent unseen paraphrases may cautiously favor a method only if they improve faithful stance interpretation without overcalling sarcasm. Learner B initially benefits from context contrasts but begins labeling *every* praise as sarcastic; contradictory evidence lowers confidence and shifts toward UNCERTAINTY_AND_CLARIFICATION_PRACTICE or balanced sampling. If one option used clearer audio, friendlier speakers, favorite gaming topics, extra hints or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up cannot prove retention. Gaming interests may raise example frequency but must not exclude neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops or required modalities.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed language/variety-specific context, stance, semantic and delivery oracles; preserve prerequisites, turn ceilings, rubrics, independent thresholds and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Sincere versus ironic evaluation.** A separates context-supported sincere and ironic readings without treating a positive word as sufficient evidence of praise or inventing emotion, hostility or relationships.
3. **Underdetermination and clarification.** B accepts multiple defensible readings or a targeted clarification when source context cannot resolve stance; do not reward confident mind-reading.
4. **Actual channel and accessibility.** C distinguishes heard/reviewed prosody, text-only ambiguity, failed audio and legitimate accessible alternatives; never relabel reading as independent listening/speaking.
5. **Social and cultural restraint.** D recognizes source-supported teasing without universalizing politeness, presuming consent or inferring stable speaker/learner traits.
6. **First authorship and exposure.** E preserves first unaided output, rendered answer-bearing help, later assisted copying and replay deduplication; neither exposure nor same-script repetition earns independent mastery.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F updates method hypotheses only from comparable actually delivered options and later independent stance-faithful performance; contradictory, sparse or confounded evidence lowers confidence and exploration continues.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and soft interests.** Require distinct real sessions, independently reviewed new context/wording/cue combinations, first-unaided recipient-facing responses, explicit missing follow-up and original receptive/productive balance. Interest weighting is nonexclusive and cannot skip required CEFR objectives.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable interaction QA, shipped sarcasm comprehension or demonstrated human learning gains. Require exact-head executable stimulus/context/prosody/provenance checks, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should help a learner distinguish sincere and ironic evaluations **when evidence supports that distinction**, preserve uncertainty when it does not, and transfer the skill independently to unfamiliar exchanges. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
