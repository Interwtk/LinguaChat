An independently reviewed **quantifier-and-scope oracle** freezes the exact source utterance, language/variety, speaker, addressee, timestamp, channel, visible context, referent set, grammatical quantifier scope, relevant unit, exactness/rounding convention if specified, and source-licensed inference. Distinguish EXACT_COUNT, LOWER_BOUND, UPPER_BOUND, APPROXIMATE_COUNT, RANGE_WITH_SOURCE_DEFINED_ENDPOINTS, PARTIAL_GROUP, EXPLICIT_NOT_ALL, UNIVERSAL_GROUP, PER_MEMBER_LIMIT, GLOBAL_LIMIT, UNKNOWN_DENOMINATOR and UNKNOWN_QUANTITY. “At least twelve volunteers signed up” licenses twelve or more, not exactly twelve; “up to twenty seats” gives a ceiling, not a guarantee that twenty remain. “Some volunteers replied” does **not** by itself entail “not all” in every target-language/variety and context; only an explicit “some but not all” or other source evidence warrants that exclusion. “About thirty” is approximate, and its tolerance cannot be invented as a universal fixed percentage. Ambiguous endpoints in natural-language “between ten and fifteen” must follow reviewed local convention or be represented cautiously; do not impose a mathematical endpoint rule that the source did not provide.

A separate independently reviewed **group, status and unit oracle** freezes who belongs to the reference group, the relevant denominator when actually known, what was invited/registered/confirmed/attended, whether counts are per person, per group or total, and whether limits apply to capacity, permission or actual usage. “Eight of twenty invited people confirmed” does not establish that twelve declined, or that only eight will attend. “Each club may bring two guests” does not mean two guests total across all clubs. “Two tickets per participant” is a per-participant limit, not an invitation to calculate a grand total without a known number of participants. Keep count units and referents explicit; do not turn people into seats, reservations into attendance, or estimates into contractual commitments. All examples use fictional low-stakes school clubs, libraries, community workshops or book exchanges, not regulated, medical, financial, legal, safety-critical or private personal data.

An independently reviewed **recipient-action oracle** fixes the communicative goal: accurately summarize a count, ask one necessary question about a missing denominator, distinguish a cap from confirmed availability, request a source-justified update, or report uncertainty without inventing precision. Accept concise natural alternatives appropriate to the language and variety. Do not require calculations, excessive hedging or repeated clarifications when the source already answers the question.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Lower bound, exact count and approximation.** A fictional library organizer writes, “At least twelve volunteers have signed up; about thirty visitors may come.” The learner must update Maya. Accept “Twelve or more volunteers have signed up, and the visitor estimate is around thirty.” Reject “Exactly twelve volunteers signed up,” “Thirty visitors confirmed,” or a fabricated narrow numerical interval. In a separate source continuation, the organizer supplies a verified exact total of fourteen volunteers; only then may a later report use fourteen, without retroactively marking the earlier lower-bound summary incorrect.

**B — A ceiling is not capacity already available or a guaranteed commitment.** A community workshop note says, “The room holds up to twenty people, but we have not checked how many seats are still free.” Accept “The room's maximum is twenty; current availability is unknown.” Reject “Twenty seats are available” or “All twenty registrations are confirmed.” A later source-visible message “Only six seats remain” supports six remaining seats at that time, not a permanent capacity change. The learner should ask about remaining availability only when the recipient actually needs it.

**C — Group denominator and status cannot be silently rewritten.** A student club invited twenty people; eight confirmed, five explicitly declined and the rest have no recorded response. Accept “Eight of twenty invited people have confirmed; five declined; the remaining responses are unknown.” Reject “Twelve declined,” “Only eight will attend,” or “Everyone else is unavailable.” A later “some volunteers replied” with no denominator must not be interpreted as “some but not all” unless source evidence explicitly establishes that restriction. An explicit “some, but not all, replied” does establish non-universality.

**D — Per-person and global limits require their source-defined reference group.** A fictional book exchange says, “Each participating club may bring up to two guests.” Accept “The limit is two guests per participating club.” Reject “Only two guests may attend the entire exchange,” “Every club is bringing two guests,” or a grand total invented without knowing the number of participating clubs. A second source says “We can admit ten guests in total”; this is a separate global limit, not proof that any club has used its allowance. If the two limits jointly matter, report both without inventing an allocation algorithm.

**E — Answer-bearing help, inaccessible audio and replay are not independent learning.** At 09:01 the learner independently reads “At least twelve” and responds “Exactly twelve”: retain the first-attempt error. At 09:02 the tutor renders a full worked answer contrasting lower bound and exact count. At 09:03 the learner copies “twelve or more”: record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED, not independent mastery. Replaying the same source at 09:04 cannot create a second first attempt. In a listening variant, audio fails while captions render: no listening credit; evaluate a valid reading route only if bound. An unopened optional hint is not exposure. A later transfer probe must use a genuinely different session, unseen source/recipient and changed semantic structure, not merely “at least thirteen” in the same script.

**F — Longitudinal method selection, counterevidence and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly mistakes “up to” for a guarantee. Across matched real sessions, EXACT_VS_BOUND_CONTRAST, GROUP_DENOMINATOR_RECAP and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated. Tentatively favor an option only if later first-unaided unseen messages better preserve capacity, actual availability and per-person scope at comparable language burden, numeracy demand, topic, help, channel and delay. Learner B initially improves with a number table but later overcorrects by treating an explicit exact count as uncertain; new independent errors lower confidence and restore balanced sampling. If the apparent winner had smaller numbers, a displayed answer key, familiar gaming-event examples or shorter delays, mark confounding and **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up is unknown, not failed retention. A music interest may softly weight concert examples; adjacent clubs, neutral libraries, unfamiliar workshops and all required CEFR objectives/modalities remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR boundaries and appropriate numeracy.** A–F bind only existing applicable versioned can-dos and independently reviewed target-language quantity/group/recipient oracles with actual source delivery. Preserve level-specific burden, prerequisites, turn ceilings, rubrics, thresholds, skill coverage and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Lower bound and approximation.** A preserves minimum versus exact and approximate versus confirmed quantities; no fabricated numerical tolerance, exactness or attendance claim earns credit.
3. **Ceiling versus actual availability.** B distinguishes a capacity cap, currently free seats and confirmed reservations; later evidence changes only the source-licensed status and time window.
4. **Denominator and response status.** C preserves invited/confirmed/declined/unanswered groups and unknown denominators; “some” is not automatically “some but not all” without explicit evidence or reviewed context.
5. **Per-member and global scope.** D retains each-versus-total quantifier scope, units, source-defined limits and unknown participating-group counts; no invented allocation or guarantee.
6. **Actual delivery, first authorship and accessibility.** E records first unaided errors, rendered worked answers, assisted copying, unopened hints, failed audio, captions and replay without phantom listening or duplicate independent credit.
7. **Matched reversible adaptation.** F updates uncertain method hypotheses only from comparable actually delivered alternatives and stronger later first-unaided transfer; sparse, contradictory or numeracy/help/topic/channel/delay-confounded evidence lowers confidence and preserves exploration.
