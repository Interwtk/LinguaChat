# F191 — Question relevance, partial answers, unwarranted inferences and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable exact-head checks bind independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific question–answer oracles, actual stimulus/help/accessibility delivery, first-authorship provenance and genuinely delayed first-unaided unseen transfer to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a shipped conversational-relevance evaluator, intention detector, universal conversational norm, new CEFR gate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F180 covers quantifier scope; F182 covers indirect requests; F188 covers clarification and collaborative repair; F189 covers source attribution; F190 covers irony. **Distinct construct:** for a source-linked communicative task, the learner identifies *what question is actually under discussion*, whether a reply answers it fully, answers it only partly, provides relevant background without settling it, or leaves it unanswered; the learner then communicates only warranted conclusions or requests the missing detail. A fluent, topically related reply is not necessarily an answer. Conversational relevance does not justify inventing a decision, promise, booking, completed action or speaker motive. English examples are illustrative, not universal pragmatic rules.

A frozen independently reviewed **question-under-discussion oracle** records exact question and accessible prior context, requested information slots (e.g., whether a room is confirmed, which room, by when), relevant presuppositions, possible ambiguity, source-provided answer conditions and legitimate reformulations. A frozen **answer-status oracle** separately records the reply's asserted propositions, source, temporal and modal scope, which requested slots are actually filled, which remain unknown, and whether the reply is a direct answer, partial answer, relevant nonanswer, explicit refusal, request for clarification or context-supported indirect answer. Do not treat a plausible implicature as a confirmed fact. Multiple defensible interpretations or a targeted clarification are valid when the context is insufficient; do not penalize directness, cultural variety or neurotype.

A frozen **recipient-communication oracle** accepts multiple independently reviewed natural target-language/variety-specific formulations that preserve what is known, what is pending and what the recipient still needs. A learner may accurately report "The coordinator was contacted, but the booking is not confirmed in this exchange" rather than pretend to know the actual booking status. Distinguish **not established** from **established false**: "We have not been told it is booked" does not entail "It was not booked." Do not infer evasiveness, dishonesty, politeness, social hierarchy or personal intention from a nonanswer unless independently reviewed context directly supports it. An answer can be contextually indirect and still complete if its entailment is independently justified; never impose a mechanical keyword-match rule.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Relevant background is not a booking answer.** In a fictional club, Maya asks, "Has the library confirmed the rehearsal room?" Theo replies, "I emailed the coordinator this morning." The only established fact is that Theo sent an email; confirmation remains unknown. Accept "Theo emailed, but we don't know from this exchange whether the room is confirmed" or "Did they reply?" Reject "The room is confirmed" (UNKNOWN_AS_CONFIRMED) and "The room wasn't booked" (UNKNOWN_AS_FALSE). Do not label Theo evasive or dishonest. In a distinct version, Theo says, "Yes, the coordinator confirmed Room 2 in writing"; this directly fills the requested slot, and unnecessary clarification must not be rewarded.

**B — A partial answer must retain the missing part.** A volunteer asks, "Which room is available on Friday, and from what time?" The posted reply reads, "Room 3 is available after lunch; the exact starting time has not been posted." A valid recipient-facing answer identifies Room 3 and the approximate restriction while explicitly leaving the clock time unresolved. Reject "Room 3 is available all Friday" or an invented 1:00 p.m. start. A targeted "What time after lunch?" is appropriate; asking "Which room?" again is unnecessary. If the source itself defines "after lunch" with an exact start in context, use that actual definition rather than pretending the time is unknown.

**C — Named examples do not complete an exhaustive question.** The club asks, "Have all the volunteers replied?" The organizer says, "Maya and Theo have replied; I haven't checked the others yet." Accept "At least Maya and Theo replied; whether everyone replied is unknown." Reject "Everyone replied" and "Only Maya and Theo replied." The issue is the *unfilled yes/no slot*, not a new arithmetic or universal quantifier gate. If the organizer later explicitly reports that all replies are in, update the answer prospectively; do not rewrite the earlier incomplete message.

**D — Preference and progress do not settle a group decision.** The team asks, "Can we announce Tuesday as the workshop date?" Nora replies, "Tuesday works for me." Her availability is established; team approval is not. In another exchange, "The designer finished the poster layout" is relevant progress but does not directly answer "Are the printed posters ready?" The learner should communicate the narrower fact and request confirmation of the actual decision or finished output when needed. Reject invented group consensus, completed printing or a promise. An independently reviewed context with delegated authority or explicit completion may make a similar indirect reply sufficient; the oracle must recognize it rather than hard-code every indirect answer as a nonanswer.

**E — Visible answer, inaccessible channel and replay do not earn independence.** At 09:01 the learner sees the question "Is Room 3 confirmed?" and an accessible transcript of "I asked yesterday"; original audio playback failed. At 09:02 a tutor card explicitly states "This does not confirm the booking." At 09:03 the learner copies "Booking status unknown." Record the actual reading route, ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED; preserve any earlier first-unaided attempt and award no independent listening. Replaying the same event at 09:04 cannot create another first attempt. An unopened hint is not exposure. Only a distinct later session with a genuinely new reviewed question, reply and recipient-facing answer, no answer-bearing help and meaningful delay can support independent transfer.

**F — Reversible method choice, confounds and topic diversity.** Learner A repeatedly treats relevant background as complete answers. Across comparable real sessions, QUESTION_SLOT_MAPPING, ANSWER_VS_BACKGROUND_CONTRAST and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually rendered and alternated with matched answer explicitness, language variety, help and delay. Later independent unseen recipient replies may cautiously favor a method only when they preserve missing slots without over-requesting clarification. Learner B initially benefits from slot mapping but later marks direct confirmations as "unknown" and asks unnecessary questions; contradictory outcomes lower confidence and return the tutor to balanced sampling or a direct/indirect contrast. If one option used easier direct answers, familiar gaming topics, clearer audio, more hints or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up cannot establish retention. Gaming interests may raise example probability but cannot exclude neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops or required objectives/modalities.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific question, answer-status, recipient and delivery oracles; preserve prerequisites, turn ceilings, rubrics, thresholds and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Relevant versus responsive.** A distinguishes an email sent from booking confirmation, preserves the unknown and accepts a targeted follow-up without inventing negative or positive status; a direct confirmation does not require unnecessary clarification.
3. **Partial answer and temporal precision.** B preserves the answered room slot and missing exact time, does not fabricate a clock time or expand a limited availability window, and uses actually supplied contextual definitions when present.
4. **Unanswered exhaustive question.** C distinguishes named known replies from evidence that everyone or only those named replied, and preserves chronological corrections without retroactive source alteration.
5. **Decision versus preference or progress.** D rejects fabricated group approval, commitments or completed output while allowing independently justified context-supported indirect answers; never classify by surface keywords alone.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E separates failed audio, accessible text, displayed answer-bearing help, unopened hints, assisted copying and replay deduplication; preserve the original first-independent attempt and do not invent modality credit.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F updates method hypotheses only from comparable actually delivered options and later independent answer-faithful communication. Sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence reduces confidence; reasonable alternatives remain sampled.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Require genuinely distinct sessions, independently reviewed new questions/replies/recipients, first-unaided recipient-facing responses, explicit missing follow-up and original receptive/productive balance. Interests adjust context probabilities only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain reachable.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable interaction QA, shipped adaptive tutoring or demonstrated human-learning gains. Require exact-head executable stimulus/answer/provenance checks, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should help a learner answer the question actually asked, distinguish partial information from settled facts and independently request missing details without unnecessary over-clarification. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
