## Fixed CEFR objectives and independently reviewed discourse oracles

Bind only **existing versioned** CEFR interaction, mediation, listening, reading, writing or free-production can-dos that actually require recognizing or producing contrast, concession, counterpoint or respectful disagreement. Preserve can-do IDs, prerequisites, target language/variety, accessibility, rubrics, thresholds, first-independent and delayed/unseen evidence, receptive/productive balance, and mastery/exit gates. If the objective does not require this discourse function, mark NOT_APPLICABLE. Do not upgrade a recognition-only B2 objective to open retrieval under this document. Use fictional, low-stakes library, workshop, poster, rehearsal and study-group situations; no political persuasion, high-stakes medical/legal/financial advice, or scoring a learner's personal beliefs.

A frozen, independently reviewed **claim-and-discourse oracle** records each speaker, explicitly supplied claim and evidence, attributed uncertainty, accepted common ground, opposing point, proposed qualification, temporal and causal constraints, and the communicative task (identify contrast, acknowledge, qualify, correct, or respond). Distinguish **concession** (grant a point without abandoning the whole position), **contrast** (two differing propositions), **correction** (replace an incorrect assertion), and **counterargument** (a reasoned response). Mark a connective such as *but*, *although*, *however*, *even though* or *on the other hand* by its contextual function, not keyword presence alone. Do not require the learner to agree with a contested claim to earn language evidence.

A **semantic-fidelity and evidence oracle** accepts multiple natural formulations preserving what each speaker actually said, including partial agreement and an explicit “we do not know” when warranted. Reject strawman rewrites, exaggerated universal claims, unsupported causal leaps, invented measurements, stronger commitments and reversed concession scope. Separate linguistic coherence, factual fidelity to the provided fictional scenario and social appropriateness from the **persuasiveness or ideology** of a learner's position. A reason can be linguistically coherent while its factual support remains unknown; mark those dimensions separately. Independent reviewers approve permissible target-language/variety alternatives and accessibility-equivalent routes; accent, dialect and lack of a preferred connective are not automatic errors.

An **actual-delivery oracle** records hashes and versions of exactly displayed/heard source turns, audio/text, captions, speaker roles, argument cards, example rebuttals, feedback, translations, scaffolds and hints before each attempt. An offered but unopened example is not exposure. An answer-bearing sample visible before response makes the attempt assisted; failed audio cannot count as independent listening. An accessible text alternative measures the reading/mediation route actually used, never relabeled as listening or speaking.

A **first-authorship and longitudinal ledger** records pseudonymous learner, fixed can-do/oracle/rubric versions, stable session-qualified event IDs, replay deduplication, first eligible unaided response and error category, actually delivered method/help, supported/unsupported claim boundaries, later assisted repair, due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes, real elapsed time, distinct-session provenance and unseen-context identity. Optional latency/hesitation is weak evidence only. An assisted correction cannot overwrite the first unaided result or manufacture mastery.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Concede without surrendering the whole position.** Fictional workshop: “Digital notes are easier to share, but printed maps help visitors without phones.” A learner reports, “They admit digital notes are easy to share, but still think some printed maps are useful.” Accept CONCESSION_VALID and OPPONENT_POINT_PRESERVED. Reject “They now agree that printed maps are useless” or “Digital notes are never helpful.” Alternative natural concessions are acceptable without requiring *although* or *however*.

**B — Counterpoint must preserve the other person's claim.** A fictional organizer says, “The evening session is quieter, but some volunteers cannot attend after six.” The learner replies, “I understand that evening is quieter. However, we should also consider volunteers who cannot stay that late.” Accept a faithful counterpoint. Reject “You said no volunteers can attend evenings” as STRAWMAN_DRIFT, and reject “The evening session is canceled” as an invented decision. The language task does not choose the schedule or grade which option the learner personally prefers.

**C — Contrast is not proof of cause or contradiction.** The fictional record says, “The poster was printed on Tuesday. The invitations were emailed on Wednesday. The poster used blue ink; the invitations used green.” Accept “The poster and invitations used different colors” as contrast. Reject “The poster caused the invitations to be late” because no lateness or cause is supplied. Reject a mandatory rule that *but* always expresses disagreement; “The poster was blue, but the invitations were green” can be a simple contextual contrast. If a prompt asks why they differ, “We are not told” is justified.

**D — Source uncertainty, qualification and legitimate disagreement.** A fictional classmate says, “I heard that the library might have a larger room; nobody has confirmed it.” The learner responds, “A larger room could help, but we should confirm availability before planning around it.” Accept source uncertainty and a qualified counterpoint. Reject “The library guaranteed a larger room” or “The classmate lied” because neither is supported. A learner may respectfully disagree with the suggested plan and still pass if the given claim, uncertainty and language function are preserved. Do not score agreement with the tutor.

**E — Visible model, failed listening and replayed events.** At 10:01 the task's source audio fails before playback; independent listening is unavailable. At 10:02 an accessible text version appears alongside an answer-bearing “Example reply: I see why digital notes help, but printed copies are useful too.” At 10:03 the learner repeats that model. Count the actually rendered text, ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED for the reading/production route, not independent listening or free production. A hidden example never opened does not count as delivered. Replaying the same stable event at 10:04 cannot create another first attempt. A later real session with a genuinely new speaker/claim pair, no exposed model and independent response can supply new evidence; same-day relaunch or a synonym swap is not sufficient delay or unseen transfer.

**F — Reversible method selection and nonexclusive interests.** Learner A repeatedly overstates opposing claims in first-unaided replies. Across comparable real sessions, actually delivered TWO_SPEAKER_CLAIM_MAPPING, QUALIFY_WITHOUT_STRAWMAN and BALANCED_DEFAULT alternate with matched support. Later independent unseen replies may cautiously favor a method associated with better faithful concessions, but alternatives remain sampled. Learner B initially benefits from connective cards, then uses *however* mechanically even where no contrast exists; lower confidence, reverse the preference or sample CONNECTIVE_FUNCTION_NOTICING and role reversal. If one method had easier claims, more familiar music topics, an answer-bearing example, extra help, different speaker stance or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up, copied models and assisted retries cannot prove retention. Music interests can increase example frequency but must not exclude neutral library, unfamiliar workshop or other required topics and modalities.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing objectives and independent oracles.** A–F bind only applicable versioned CEFR can-dos and independently reviewed target-language/variety discourse, semantic and delivery oracles. No changed B2 Arc 6 recognition/open-retrieval contract, thresholds, prerequisites or release status; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Faithful concession.** A preserves both the acknowledged advantage and the remaining reservation, permits reviewed equivalent forms, and rejects an exaggerated or reversed stance.
3. **Counterpoint without strawman.** B retains the original speaker's actual constraint, distinguishes a response from an unmade decision, and does not grade the learner's personal position.
4. **Discourse relation versus factual inference.** C recognizes legitimate contrast, rejects unsupported cause or lateness, and does not award correctness merely for including a connective.
5. **Uncertainty and evidence boundary.** D preserves attributed possibility, accepts justified qualification or respectful disagreement, and rejects invented confirmations, dishonesty claims or proof.
6. **Actual delivery and independent provenance.** E scores only actually rendered/heard material, excludes failed listening and answer-exposed replies from independent credit, retains first authorship and deduplicates stable events.
7. **Longitudinal, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F can change method probabilities from comparable actually delivered choices and later independent outcomes; contradictory, sparse or confounded evidence cannot identify a causal winner, and alternative sampling continues.
8. **Unseen delayed transfer and balanced soft interests.** Require genuinely distinct real sessions and independently reviewed new claims, speakers, contexts and communicative functions, first-unaided transfer, explicit missing follow-up and original receptive/productive balance. Interest categories change probabilities only; neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar contexts remain reachable.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic histories and generic CI are not executable discourse QA, a shipped adaptive policy or proof of human learning gains. Before such claims require exact-head executable stimulus/provenance assertions, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes or A1–C2 enablement.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor can teach concession and disagreement while preserving evidence boundaries, allowing legitimate alternatives and adapting cautiously from delayed independent outcomes.
