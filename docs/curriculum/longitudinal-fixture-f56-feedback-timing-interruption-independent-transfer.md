# F56 — Feedback timing must not substitute for independent conversational transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped adaptive feedback policy, CEFR certification, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F54 distinguishes learner-initiated communicative repair from supplied repair. F56 distinguishes **when and how the tutor intervenes** from the learner's later ability to communicate independently. Immediate correction, end-of-turn feedback, post-task reflection, brief recasts, elicitation and temporary non-intervention are candidate instructional choices, not fixed learner types. A fluent-looking dialogue after continuous tutor prompting is not independent mastery; equally, withholding essential accessibility support or source-required safety/clarification is not a valid experiment.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a real versioned CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, task, modality, evaluator/rubric and permitted interaction/support before scoring. Freeze communicative intent, language burden, target difficulty, legitimate interlocutor moves, independent performance requirements, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, review debt and level-exit gates. Feedback timing may change *instruction*, not the assessed construct or the required standard. If the task requires a live oral exchange but speech recognition or spoken assessment is unavailable, record `UNAVAILABLE`; do not substitute typed dialogue or claim oral evidence.

Use deterministic pseudonymous synthetic learners, injected clock/seed, stable session-qualified event IDs and immutable event streams across genuinely distinct sessions. Record assigned **and actually delivered** feedback policy; error onset; first learner-authored attempt; tutor/interlocutor role; feedback type, content, timing and delivery; whether it disclosed a target answer; learner uptake and self-repair; retries; optional latency/hesitation only where reliably observed; subsequent independent attempts; delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer. Keep conversation completion, communicative adequacy, accuracy, learner agency and assisted versus independent production separate. Mark missing audio, delivery chronology and follow-up explicitly. Do not infer anxiety, disability, cognitive ability or a permanent “needs interruption” style from pauses or mistakes. Minimize personal transcripts and raw audio.

Predeclare `FEEDBACK_IMMEDIATE_DELIVERED`, `FEEDBACK_DEFERRED_DELIVERED`, `FEEDBACK_WITHHELD_BY_POLICY`, `FEEDBACK_UNDELIVERED`, `SOURCE_REQUIRED_CLARIFICATION`, `LEARNER_SELF_REPAIR`, `TUTOR_SUPPLIED_REPAIR`, `INDEPENDENT_TRANSFER`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. An assigned correction that was never shown is not an intervention.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Same final answer, different interruption provenance.** Learner A independently repairs an incorrect target-language request after noticing the listener's confusion. Learner B produces the same correct final request only after the tutor interrupts and supplies the missing phrase. Both finish the task. Preserve A's learner-initiated repair, B's coached final response, original first attempts and legitimate interlocutor clarification. Never collapse equal final text into equal independent evidence.
- **B — Immediate correction improves practice but weakens later retrieval.** In one learner's repeated source-matched practice, immediate form-focused interruption yields high assisted accuracy. End-of-turn feedback produces messier immediate dialogue but stronger later unaided use of the same construction in an unseen context. Compare genuinely delayed probes with intervening material, equivalent task difficulty and assistance-free scoring. The apparent immediate winner must not be permanently preferred or used to certify durable mastery.
- **C — Deferred feedback is not always better.** For a different learner and objective, delayed post-task comments leave a recurring meaning-changing error unnoticed across turns, while a brief non-answer-revealing prompt leads to independent repair and better later unaided transfer. Update the provisional policy toward timely prompting with explicit uncertainty. Distinguish tutor prompts from ordinary source-valid interlocutor questions and retain exploration of other options.
- **D — Legitimate interruption, accessibility and unavailable modality.** A source-valid interaction includes an interlocutor's clarification request that is necessary to complete the information gap; another task requires a supported caption or accommodation; a third requires oral evidence but the speech evaluator is unavailable. Do not penalize legitimate clarification as answer leakage, withhold a required accommodation for experimental purity, or award oral mastery from a text-only transcript. Where equivalence is not source-validated, return `UNSUPPORTED` or `UNAVAILABLE`.
- **E — Interest and topic familiarity masquerade as feedback benefit.** A learner receives deferred feedback during familiar music examples and immediate feedback during unfamiliar administrative examples. Early outcomes favor deferral, but counterbalanced neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar contexts reverse the difference. Keep topic family, linguistic load, method assignment, delivered feedback, order and evaluator version distinct. Rotate contexts without turning interests into a hard topic filter or changing the can-do or independent standard.
- **F — Offline interruption event arrives late.** An offline learner records an independent first attempt, receives a tutor correction before revising, and later syncs the correction event after the revised answer. Another session reuses the local turn ID; a duplicate submission arrives; the task rubric has changed between sessions. Replay receipt orders using trusted occurrence/**delivery** chronology, session-qualified IDs and immutable rubric versions. Preserve the pre-correction first attempt, classify the revised answer as assisted when delivery is established, deduplicate, and quarantine disputed independence or unsupported cross-rubric comparisons. Unknown delivery is not proof of either assistance or independence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-defined construct preserved.** For A–F report exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task, modality, evaluator/rubric, permissible interlocutor moves and support, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, transfer and exit obligations. Missing source rules or unsupported modality yield `UNSUPPORTED`/`UNAVAILABLE`, never invented credit.
2. **Delivery, not assignment, determines exposure.** Log selected versus actually delivered feedback timing/content, its relation to the first attempt and subsequent revisions, and whether a model phrase was revealed. Undelivered or chronology-unknown feedback cannot be treated as proven exposure.
3. **Independent self-repair differs from supplied repair.** In A, identical final answers produce distinct evidence provenance; record first attempts, learner-initiated repair, tutor cue and legitimate partner replies without erasing semantic quality or wrongly certifying coached production.
4. **Delayed independent outcomes outrank assisted fluency.** In B and C, compare source-matched later unaided retrieval and unseen transfer, not immediate correctness or conversational smoothness alone. A missing follow-up is `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, not a failure or success.
5. **Reversible adaptation with uncertainty.** Report available alternatives, selection rationale, uncertainty, exploration, counterbalanced task order and outcome-driven changes. Opposite histories must support different *provisional* feedback choices without fixed learner labels or irreversible classification.
6. **No harm from experimental non-intervention.** In D, respect source-required clarification, learner accessibility, legitimate mediation and modality boundaries. Do not withhold necessary support, misclassify interlocutor replies as tutor hints or fabricate oral mastery from text.
7. **Instruction and interest are orthogonal.** In E, counterbalance topic familiarity, context difficulty and activity order; retain adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts and all CEFR objectives. A topic-only effect must not be reported as feedback-method superiority.
8. **Deterministic replay and rubric fences.** In F, permute offline receipt order and duplicate delivery, preserving exactly-once evidence, actual cue chronology, session-qualified identity and original rubric versions. Quarantine uncertain exposure and mismatched rubrics rather than silently promoting credit.
9. **Design-only release boundary.** Produce an auditable fixture ledger for each history: observed first attempts, feedback delivery, independent versus assisted outcome, later probe eligibility/completion, uncertainty, method choice and unchanged CEFR gates. A document passing review is not an executable test, shipped adaptive tutoring or proof of human-learning gains; require exact-engine executable longitudinal tests and an ethically reviewed learner pilot before any efficacy claim.
