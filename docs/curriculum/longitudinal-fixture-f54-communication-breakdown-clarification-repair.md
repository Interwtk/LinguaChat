# F54 — Communication breakdown, clarification and independent repair

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped dialogue-repair evaluator, CEFR certification or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F53 separates context-appropriate register from legitimate language variation. F54 separates **recognizing a communicative breakdown, seeking relevant clarification, and independently restoring shared meaning** from merely repeating a supplied answer or obtaining a superficially successful final exchange. In an authentic interaction task, an interlocutor's ordinary response to a learner-authored clarification request may be part of the *assessed interactional construct*, not automatically a tutor hint. Conversely, a tutor-provided repair script, answer-revealing transcript or partner-supplied target phrase can contaminate independent evidence. Apply the actual versioned CEFR task/rubric; do not invent a universal repair score or penalize every request for repetition.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a real versioned CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, task/item, modality, evaluator and rubric **before** awarding credit. Freeze the source-defined communicative goal, information gap, audience, interaction channel, language burden, acceptable clarification and confirmation moves, legitimate interlocutor behavior, support boundaries, independence requirements, unseen-context transfer, delayed retrieval, review debt and exit gates. Where the source requires spoken comprehension or production, a written transcript, translation, scripted response or text-only surrogate does **not** silently certify that modality. Where the source allows normal clarification within a dialogue, do not count the interlocutor's ordinary answer as forbidden coaching. Missing source rules, unavailable modality or disputed pragmatic equivalence must yield `UNSUPPORTED`, not invented thresholds.

Use deterministic synthetic pseudonymous learners, injected clock/seed and immutable event streams across genuinely separate learning sessions. Record objective/blueprint/rubric/evaluator versions; session, task, breakdown, turn, cue and event IDs; speaker role; actual learner-authored first attempt; what each interlocutor said; intended versus learner-understood meaning; learner's detected mismatch; clarification request; received answer; learner's subsequent reformulation, confirmation and final communicative outcome. Keep **repair initiative**, **relevance of question**, **comprehension of clarification**, **independent formulation**, **task completion** and **language accuracy** distinct. Record tutor/peer models, hints, captions/transcripts, translations, audio replay/slowing and delivery chronology. Treat optional hesitation/latency as noisy diagnostic evidence, never an automatic mastery or disability proxy. Do not infer a learner's identity, impairment or competence from accent, silence or a slow response. Preserve missingness explicitly and minimize personal audio/transcript data.

Predeclare `BREAKDOWN_DETECTED`, `CLARIFICATION_LEARNER_INITIATED`, `SOURCE_VALID_INTERLOCUTOR_REPLY`, `REPAIR_INDEPENDENT`, `REPAIR_ASSISTED`, `REPAIR_UNRESOLVED`, `MEANING_CONFIRMED`, `MODALITY_CONTAMINATED`, `INDEPENDENT_PROBE_PENDING`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. An agreeable final "yes" or the partner's corrected sentence is not proof that the learner understood and repaired the problem independently.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Spontaneous clarification versus supplied prompt.** In the same source-defined information-gap task, two learners encounter an ambiguous meeting time. A independently notices the conflict and asks a specific question that resolves it. B gives a vague acknowledgement until the tutor suggests the exact clarification question; B repeats it successfully. Preserve A's spontaneous initiative and B's assisted repair as different evidence even if both conversations end with the correct meeting time. An authorized interlocutor's ordinary answer to A's question is not automatically a hint.
- **B — Polite repetition without recovered meaning.** A learner asks the partner to repeat an unfamiliar direction and receives a legitimate rephrasing. The learner repeats the words back but then selects the wrong destination. Another learner asks which landmark the partner means, integrates the response and accurately confirms the route. Separate a source-valid clarification attempt from actual comprehension and communicative resolution. Do not infer failure from a single nonresponsive turn when task state or modality is missing; preserve uncertainty.
- **C — Repair under genuine interaction versus answer leakage.** Learner A encounters an unexpected disagreement in a source-valid interaction task, independently paraphrases the other speaker's concern and negotiates a workable solution. Learner B receives the partner's ready-made target phrase and a tutor-provided model, then repeats the solution. Distinguish legitimate interlocutor contributions and negotiation from coached language production. A's interaction is not disqualified solely because a partner participated; B's copied resolution cannot be promoted to unaided production.
- **D — Immediate repair, delayed breakdown in a new context.** After several scaffolded practice exchanges, a learner successfully repairs a familiar restaurant-order misunderstanding with a displayed model. On a later genuinely separate session, a new unassisted appointment-rescheduling exchange produces an unresolved misunderstanding. Record the assisted practice benefit without claiming durable independent repair. Keep due review, unseen-context transfer and source-defined exit evidence pending; a calendar-day change alone does not prove meaningful delay.
- **E — Different instructional options, reversible evidence.** Two learners work toward the same versioned can-do, rubric and independent repair standard. A shows stronger later unaided clarification and confirmation after concise contrastive examples; B initially improves more after short role-play with progressively faded prompts. Counterbalance interlocutor, topic familiarity, order, register and difficulty; sample both methods. Later independent probes reverse B's apparent advantage, reducing confidence and changing the provisional instructional choice. Never label either learner as permanently auditory, verbal, visual or role-play-oriented. Rotate adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics independently of instructional choice.
- **F — Late cue, reused turn ID and offline replay.** A learner asks a clarification question offline; a tutor hint is delivered *before* the final reformulation but its event arrives after the answer. A partner's ordinary reply is logged after an unrelated transcript toggle; the final submission is duplicated; a new session reuses the same local turn ID; an older rubric and newer rubric disagree about whether the partner's reply is permitted. Replay both receipt orders using trusted occurrence/delivery chronology, stable session-qualified IDs and immutable rubric versions. Preserve the original unaided first question, classify the final reformulation using delivered-cue provenance, deduplicate, quarantine unsupported rubric migration and converge on the same evidence/review ledger. Unknown cue chronology fails closed for disputed independent credit.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Construct and source first.** For A–F emit exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task/item, modality, evaluator/rubric, source-defined breakdown/clarification/repair criteria, legitimate interlocutor replies, support permissions, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, transfer and exit obligations. Missing criteria or unsupported modality yield `UNSUPPORTED` rather than guessed credit.
2. **Repair initiative and assisted imitation differ.** In A, preserve the learner's first mismatch detection, self-authored targeted question and the actual timing/content of any tutor cue. Identical successful final conversations must not erase independent-versus-assisted provenance. Ordinary source-permitted answers to a learner's question remain distinct from instructional coaching.
3. **Question quality is not communicative resolution.** In B, report whether the clarification question was relevant, whether the reply was understood, whether meaning was confirmed and whether the final action achieved the task. A polite request or echoed sentence alone cannot prove comprehension; ambiguous observations remain unknown, not automatic failure.
4. **Source-valid dialogue is not automatically assisted.** In C, credit learner-authored negotiation and repair only where the interactional rubric supports it; classify partner-supplied scripts, target phrases, tutor models and copied production separately. Keep original turns and final outcome without awarding an unobserved solo-production skill.
5. **Delayed independence is a separate gate.** In D, report elapsed interval, genuinely distinct session/context, eligible/completed/missing unaided probes and independent unseen transfer. An immediate assisted repair cannot settle later retention or required review/exit gates, and a missing follow-up is not a negative score.
6. **Adapt instruction from longitudinal outcomes, not fixed types.** In E, log available/selected/delivered explanation, role-play and scaffold options, uncertainty, first-attempt quality, retries, hint dependence and later independent outcomes. Counterbalance topic and interlocutor effects, explore alternatives, lower confidence or reverse decisions when contradicted, and preserve balanced receptive/productive CEFR coverage.
7. **Interests and accessibility remain separate layers.** Across A–F, soft interests may affect example probability but never exclude neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar settings, override explicit topic boundaries, suppress required can-dos or change evaluation burden. Audio replay, captions, transcript, slower playback and accessible response modes must be logged as delivered affordances; do not equate access support with coaching or claim unsupported listening/speaking mastery.
8. **Replay preserves chronology and independence.** In F, duplicate, stale, out-of-order and session-local events converge across both arrival orders on first/final quality, cue exposure, authorized partner replies, repair status, independent evidence, due review and provisional policy. Keep rubric versions immutable; no retroactive promotion of hinted answers, no invented cue-delivery order and no unreviewed migration.
9. **Report limits without release inflation.** For each history output source/rubric IDs, detected breakdown, learner-authored question, partner response, first/final meaning, communication result, hint/transcript chronology, assigned/delivered instructional options and uncertainty, skill/topic rotation, delayed independent outcomes and missingness, with per-assertion `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. Passing documentation checks or CI is not a shipped repair engine, validated CEFR mastery or proof of durable learning gains.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable test command, injected clock/seed, immutable synthetic event streams in both receipt orders, versioned CEFR blueprint/task/evaluator/rubric, source-defined interlocutor allowances, learner-authored first and final responses, intended versus recovered meaning, cue delivery and transcript provenance, valid interaction versus coaching classification, per-learner method alternatives and uncertainty, independently completed delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer with eligible/completed/missing denominators, unchanged review/exit obligations and verdict per assertion. If the source rubric, modality, repair outcome, cue chronology or authorized partner behavior is unknown, expose the limitation rather than manufacturing a successful independent repair.

Any claim that dialogue-repair instruction improves **durable independent** communication requires separately reviewed longitudinal evidence with comparable tasks and ethically reviewed real-learner evaluation. **A strong tutor teaches learners to notice misunderstandings and restore meaning, not merely to repeat the tutor's correction.**
