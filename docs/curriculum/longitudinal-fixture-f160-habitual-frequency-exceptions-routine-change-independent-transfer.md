## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Usually versus always, one event versus habit.** A fictional learner receives: **“I usually take the bus to class, but on rainy Mondays I sometimes walk.”** A handoff saying **“She always takes the bus, including rainy Mondays”** fails; **“She usually takes the bus but sometimes walks on rainy Mondays”** preserves the qualified habit. Counterfactual: an independently reviewed new source says **“I took the bus yesterday.”** That establishes one reported event, not a daily routine. If a later statement says **“I always take the bus now,”** the oracle changes only from the new source's effective time; it does not rewrite earlier exceptions.

**B — Numeric frequency and alternating anchors.** A fictional club note says **“The reading group meets twice a week, on Tuesday and Thursday.”** Reporting **“once every two weeks”** fails. Counterfactual: **“We meet every two weeks on Thursday”** is a different cadence; **“every other Thursday”** requires a starting Thursday or other anchor to identify the next meeting. When the anchor is absent, **“Which Thursday is the first one?”** is a valid recipient-oriented clarification. Do not use an unprovided calendar date or time zone to invent the next meeting. A group meeting pattern is not proof that each member attends.

**C — Negated frequency and exception scope.** A fictional volunteer says **“I don't always work Saturdays; I work only the first Saturday of each month.”** A learner who says **“She never works Saturdays”** fails; a recipient-correct summary preserves the limited recurring exception. Counterfactual: **“I rarely work Saturdays”** does not mean zero Saturdays; **“I never work Saturdays”** does. Another source says **“I work every Saturday except holidays.”** The holiday exception belongs to that speaker's source; do not generalize it to other volunteers or silently convert it into a guaranteed public schedule.

**D — Past habits, current changes and reported versus verified.** A fictional student says on 10 October **“I used to study at the library every evening, but since September I mostly study at home.”** Reporting **“They study at the library every evening now”** fails. A faithful handoff distinguishes former and current typical routines and does not invent an exact present frequency from **“mostly.”** Counterfactual: a new message says **“Starting next Monday I plan to return to the library on weekdays.”** That is a future plan, not proof the new habit has already started. A checked attendance log may support specific observed visits; an unverified personal report must retain attribution.

**E — Accessible delivery, actual help and event chronology.** An original fictional audio message says **“I do not always go on Fridays; I usually go every other Friday.”** ASR omits **“not”**; captions omit **“every other”**; a translation turns the habitual **“usually”** into an unconditional calendar commitment; screen-reader ordering drops the exception. Quarantine construct-changing delivered-channel evidence rather than penalizing the learner for corrupted input. At 10:00 a learner independently asks **“Which Friday does the alternating pattern start?”**; an answer-bearing model actually renders at 10:01. The 10:00 clarification remains FIRST_UNAIDED, while a copied explanation at 10:02 is ASSISTED. A queued-but-unrendered model does not contaminate earlier authorship. Replaying the same stable session-qualified event cannot manufacture a new independent attempt or review day. Corrected accessible text can support reading but not certify unheard listening. A later source update does not rewrite the earlier source oracle.

**F — Longitudinal adaptation, reversal and nonexclusive interests.** Fictional learner A performs well immediately after FREQUENCY_CONTINUUM_WITH_UNCERTAINTY but later tells a new recipient **“never”** when an unfamiliar speaker said **“rarely.”** Several matched, actually delivered HABIT_VS_EVENT_CONTRAST opportunities precede better delayed first-unaided reports across changed speakers, routines and exception structures; repeated comparable outcomes may cautiously favor contrast while retaining exploration. Fictional learner B initially benefits from PAST_PRESENT_ROUTINE_TIMELINE but later treats **“I plan to start”** as an established present habit; confidence falls, review becomes due and alternatives resume. If contrast receives a short explicit habit while timeline receives three exceptions and a complex tense shift, or only one method includes a completed answer model, infer **no winner**. Swapping “gaming” for “music” in the identical template is not unseen transfer. Missing delayed follow-up is MISSING, not failure. Neutral/unfamiliar contexts and shared CEFR objectives remain protected.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR objective and independently reviewed routine oracle.** A–F resolve a versioned real can-do, prerequisites, source/recipient/information gap, reviewed habit/frequency/exception oracle, target language/variety/register, accessible delivered task, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; do not impose advanced aspect or calendar arithmetic on beginners.
2. **Typicality is not an unconditional event guarantee.** A distinguishes “usually” from “always,” an observed single occurrence from a habit, and a source-reported pattern from an independently verified attendance record. The evaluator cannot infer tomorrow's event solely from a habitual statement.
3. **Numeric cadence and anchors are preserved.** B distinguishes twice weekly, every two weeks and every other weekday. Where identifying the next date requires a missing alternating anchor, credit a targeted clarification rather than a fabricated calendar result.
4. **Negation and exception scope remain intact.** C never promotes “not always” to “never,” “rarely” to “never,” or “every Saturday except holidays” to an exception-free rule. Source- and speaker-specific exceptions cannot silently migrate to another routine.
5. **Temporal change, intention and provenance remain distinct.** D distinguishes past-only habit, current qualified habit and future plan; applies revisions from their effective source times without retroactive rewriting; preserves attribution and unknown attendance rather than inventing observed history.
6. **Actually delivered accessible evidence and authorship chronology.** E reconciles original and rendered ASR/captions/translations/screen-reader cadence, negation and exception structure, quarantines meaning-changing corruption, preserves first authorship before actual help, deduplicates event replay and distinguishes access-equivalent from different constructs. Text cannot certify unheard listening.
7. **Controlled, uncertain and reversible instructional adaptation.** F compares feasible actually delivered methods under matched objective, frequency expression, exception/aspect load, modality, language, topic, help, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, selectively observed, contradictory or confounded outcomes yield no winner; delayed regression lowers confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed first-unaided unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Hinted timelines, copied frequency scales, immediate repetition and topic-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, new speaker/recipient/context **and changed frequency, exception or temporal-change structure**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided actionable handoff and explicit follow-up missingness. Preserve shared objectives, protected receptive/productive coverage and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Green documentation CI is not a shipped habituality evaluator, an outcome-responsive tutor or proof of improved human retention. Require exact-head deterministic executable assertions against real objectives, logged delivered instructional changes, later independent transfer against a balanced baseline and separately reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs work, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are fictional design/probe cases, not observed learner histories or executable QA. Preserve the difference between typicality, frequency, exceptions, changed routines and confirmed events; keep explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and learner-evaluation evidence exist.
