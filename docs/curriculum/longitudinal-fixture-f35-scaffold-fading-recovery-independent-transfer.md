# F35 — Scaffold fading and recovery must lead to independent transfer, not assisted-success inflation

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named, exact engine HEAD. This is not a reproduced defect, shipped adaptive-tutor capability, causal estimate or evidence of improved learner outcomes. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F03 prevents a single assisted repair from counting as mastery, F25 protects skill coverage, F26 protects delayed-retrieval provenance, F33 guards against exposed answers and F34 against answer-bearing bilingual cues. F35 isolates the **longitudinal support trajectory**: a good tutor should increase support when useful, fade it when justified, restore it after setbacks, and still obtain independently valid delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer. Neither permanently high scaffolding nor premature unsupported independence is itself evidence of a better teaching method.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do** and an actually supported skill, including its real rubric, prerequisite state, assessment demand and mastery/exit gates. Compare eligible instructional policies A (`adaptive_fade_recover`) and B (`fixed_high_support`) or a fixed balanced baseline, with the same required objective, calibrated independent anchor probes, feasible modalities, review obligations and topic-context coverage. The comparison must not change difficulty, probe novelty, reward or graduation requirements to make either policy look better.

Use a deterministic seed and clock with an append-only synthetic event ledger. Record stable learner, objective/version, skill, session/learning-day, teaching exposure, activity, attempt, event, probe and item-family IDs; event occurrence and upload times; candidate instructional options and actual assignment; **scaffold type, intensity, timing and visibility** (e.g. explanation, hint, sentence starter, model answer, translation, corrective feedback); whether support was requested, automatically shown, available or actually observed; first-attempt quality, retries, error type, repair and completion; assistance/answer exposure across sessions; actual delayed intervals, intervening practice, independent production, verified unseen transfer, assessment/rubric versions, context family, review debt, modality availability and missing provenance. Separate *support used to learn* from *answer-bearing support accessible during assessment*. Do not assume absence of logged support proves no support was visible.

Declare a versioned `scaffold_transition_policy` with explicit, auditable conditions for `MAINTAIN`, `FADE`, `RESTORE`, `ALTERNATIVE` and `BALANCED_FALLBACK`. Track support dose and confidence per objective/skill, not permanent learner categories. Do not invent a universal fade schedule, threshold, learning-style label or optimal support level. A transition may be provisional; only independently valid later outcomes can justify durable-retention claims.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Assisted-success trap.** With B, a learner completes many activities correctly after viewing sentence starters and model answers. First attempts are weak and the later target-language-only unfamiliar probe fails. High assisted completion is diagnostic, not independent mastery or proof that fixed high support teaches better.
- **B — Premature fade and responsible recovery.** With A, two strong immediate recognition turns trigger an early removal of all support for a required free-production task. The learner struggles independently; a later scaffolded repair succeeds. Preserve the failed independent observation, restore appropriate instructional support and schedule a new clean probe. Neither the repair nor recognition success fills the productive-skill gate; do not permanently classify the learner as needing high support.
- **C — Successful gradual fade.** Across genuinely distinct sessions, A moves from worked examples to partial prompts and then no answer-bearing cues. After an actual delay and intervening material, the learner independently produces a correct first response to a calibrated unfamiliar context. Retain this valid retrieval/transfer evidence, with the full support trajectory and uncertainty; do not infer a causal advantage from one learner.
- **D — Accessibility versus answer-bearing support.** A learner uses larger text or a nonsemantic navigation aid while completing a reading can-do; another uses subtitles or an answer-revealing transcript during a listening-comprehension probe. Classify by the *specific construct and rubric*: accessibility that preserves the measured skill need not invalidate evidence, while an answer-bearing cue that changes the listening task cannot be silently treated as unaided listening. If availability or visibility is unknown, mark the affected claim uncertain.
- **E — Method reversal and unequal support dose.** Early immediate scores favor B, whose prompts expose more of the answer; later comparable clean delayed independent and unseen probes favor A, with contradictory valid observations and unequal exposure opportunities. Report support dose, comparable denominators, uncertainty and potential selection/confounding; revise a provisional objective/skill-specific hypothesis only when supported. Continue alternative sampling and balanced receptive/productive coverage.
- **F — Offline replay and hidden support.** A hint was viewed offline before an assessment but uploaded after it; the hint event is duplicated; one probe has no reliable scaffold-visibility provenance; another records a later instructional hint only after a completed independent probe. Deduplicate by stable IDs, order by actual occurrence when trusted, preserve causal chronology and quarantine ambiguous timing. Late-arriving earlier support must not be laundered into an independent pass; later support must not retroactively contaminate a verified earlier probe.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Explicit support trajectory.** Per policy expose eligible/offered/exposed/completed counts, first-attempt independent outcomes, support type/dose/availability/visibility, fade/restore decisions with reasons, independently assessed follow-ups, actual delays, unseen transfer and unknown provenance. Missing observations are neither success nor failure.
2. **No assisted-success laundering.** In A, a high rate of correct scaffolded completions cannot establish independent mastery, delayed retrieval, unseen transfer or B superiority. Preserve useful diagnostic/error-repair information separately.
3. **Recover without lowering standards.** In B, independent difficulty may trigger appropriately logged instructional support restoration and a new unaided probe opportunity; assisted repair never erases the failed attempt, substitutes for free production or weakens the CEFR rubric.
4. **Valid fade evidence survives.** In C, earlier instructional support does not invalidate a later verified, first-attempt unaided, calibrated unfamiliar probe after a meaningful delay. Report actual intervals, exposure lineage, sample size, support trajectory and uncertainty; do not automatically declare a winning policy.
5. **Accommodation is construct-sensitive.** In D, separate access aids from answer-bearing assessment help using the existing can-do and evaluator. Never invent unavailable oral/listening evidence or universally mark all accommodations as assistance; uncertainty remains explicit when cue visibility is unknown.
6. **Outcome-based reversible adaptation.** In E, compare matched delayed independent anchors rather than raw scaffolded accuracy, preserve opportunity and support-dose confounders, lower confidence on contradictory evidence, permit hypothesis reversal and continue eligible alternative sampling without permanent learner labels.
7. **Replay and visibility invariance.** In F, stable-ID deduplication and trusted occurrence-time ordering produce the same support classifications, independent denominators and decisions regardless of upload order. Ambiguous or untrusted visibility/time stays `UNKNOWN`, not silently independent.
8. **Shared curriculum and soft interests remain intact.** Every history preserves versioned CEFR can-do, rubric, mastery threshold, overdue review debt and required receptive/productive coverage. Interest weights may vary examples but cannot determine fade eligibility, exclude neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar contexts, or hide required objectives and supported modalities.
9. **Conservative release gate.** When support provenance, calibrated independent anchors, meaningful delay, item novelty or comparable follow-up is insufficient, mark method efficacy `UNSUPPORTED`/`NOT_COMPARABLE` and use the shared balanced curriculum with normal scaffolding. Never claim durable independent improvement solely from an adaptive fade animation or assisted correctness.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, deterministic seed/clock, versioned policies, complete synthetic event ledger, existing CEFR objective and supported skill, candidate/assigned options, support dose and visibility provenance, first-attempt/error/repair events, actual delay and intervening material, independently scored unseen probes, calibrated item/rubric versions, eligibility/exposure/follow-up denominators, coverage/review debt, uncertainty and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Include fixed-support and balanced-baseline comparisons on genuinely equivalent independent assessments, not just completed assisted exercises.

If the engine cannot reconstruct support availability and first-attempt independence, report **UNSUPPORTED** rather than assume a clean fade. Deterministic PASS establishes a software invariant only. Claiming that adaptive instruction improves durable independent learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal learner evidence versus a balanced baseline, comparable opportunities, verified delayed retrieval and unfamiliar transfer, uncertainty calibration and unchanged CEFR/skill coverage.
