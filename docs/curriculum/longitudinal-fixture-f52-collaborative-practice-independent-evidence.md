# F52 — Collaborative practice is not automatically independent CEFR evidence

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped collaboration feature, CEFR certification, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F51 separates spontaneous from prompted repair. F52 separates **what a learner actually contributes in social practice** from peer-authored, jointly edited, copied or coached output. Collaborative learning can be valuable; an interactional CEFR can-do may legitimately require other speakers. The error is to treat an impressive *group artifact* as proof of each participant's unaided individual production, or conversely to reject valid independently produced interactional turns merely because another person was present. Respect the exact source-defined construct, not a universal rule that all collaboration is either independent or assisted.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a real versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task, item and rubric/evaluator before scoring. Explicitly distinguish (a) independent individual production, (b) source-authorized interactive competence where interlocutor responses are part of the task, (c) coached production, (d) shared/group artifacts, and (e) later unaided retrieval and unseen transfer. Freeze whether the source rubric permits interlocutor clarification, negotiation, repair, co-planning, peer feedback or joint drafting, and which *individual* actions demonstrate the assessed construct. **Never invent universal collaboration penalties, participation quotas, mastery thresholds, or rules that every turn in dialogue is assisted.** If source scoring or contributor provenance is missing, preserve observations and mark the disputed credit **UNSUPPORTED**.

Use synthetic pseudonymous learners, a deterministic clock/seed and immutable event histories across multiple sessions. Record source-qualified objective and rubric versions; task modality, construct and context; stable learner, group, session, turn, artifact, revision and event IDs; participant roles; per-span/turn authorship; actual delivered peer/tutor cues; shared-document edit history; independent first observation; final individual utterance; group product; assistance exposure; option selected; skill/topic coverage; later independent retrieval/transfer and review debt. Record provenance of transcription, translation, predictive text and pasted material where available. Preserve missingness explicitly and avoid unnecessary identifiable peer data or voice recordings. Do not infer fixed learning styles or causal method superiority from participation counts.

Predeclare PARTICIPANT_AUTHORED_TURN, SOURCE_VALID_INTERACTION, PEER_CUED_PRODUCTION, COAUTHORED_ARTIFACT, COPIED_ORIGIN, CONTRIBUTOR_UNKNOWN, INTERLOCUTOR_EXCHANGE_ALLOWED, INDEPENDENT_PROBE_PENDING, REVIEW_DUE, EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED, RUBRIC_MISMATCH, UNAVAILABLE and UNSUPPORTED. An unknown author or an unknown delivered cue never becomes independent credit by default.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Group success, uneven contributions.** Three learners complete an apparently excellent shared proposal. Learner A authors a substantive explanation, B contributes a short acknowledgement and C contributes no independently attributable language. The group product is useful practice, but cannot give A, B and C identical individual productive mastery. Preserve each person's actual turns and any source-valid interactional evidence without awarding missing solo evidence.
- **B — Same final paragraph, different authorship.** A learner independently writes a paragraph; another submits an identical paragraph after a peer rewrites key clauses in a shared document; a third pastes the group's final answer. The visible final text is identical, but the independent evidence differs. Keep the initial drafts, span-level revisions and cue exposure; unknown edit provenance is not a positive independence finding.
- **C — Authentic interaction versus scripted coaching.** In a source-valid CEFR interaction task, learner A independently asks follow-up questions, handles unexpected disagreement and clarifies meaning with a peer. Learner B reads a peer-supplied script while the partner supplies target phrases. Do not penalize A simply for receiving ordinary interlocutor replies that the rubric permits; do not promote B's coached repetition to independent interactional control. If the task's construct or permitted interlocutor support is undefined, report UNSUPPORTED.
- **D — Collaboration now, delayed individual transfer later.** A learner participates actively in joint planning and produces an excellent team presentation. On a later novel, unaided individual task, the learner cannot reproduce the relevant target structure. Preserve collaborative progress and engagement separately from durable independent mastery; keep source-required review and exit obligations pending.
- **E — Opposite learners, changing instructional hypotheses.** With the same can-do, source rubric, difficulty and comparable topic mix, learner A later transfers independently after paired discussion followed by solo retrieval; learner B transfers after individual worked examples followed by paired discussion. Counterbalance activity order, peer proficiency and context familiarity; sample both methods, track actual participation and independently completed follow-ups. Later contradictory outcomes lower confidence and can reverse the provisional ordering. Never classify learners permanently as social or solitary types.
- **F — Offline edits, late peer hints and replay.** A shared response is drafted offline; a peer suggestion is delivered before an edit but its log arrives afterward; an individual turn is uploaded twice; another session reuses a local artifact ID. Replay events in both receipt orders using trusted occurrence/delivery chronology, participant- and session-qualified IDs, stable revision provenance and source versions. Deduplicate and quarantine uncertain authorship/cue exposure. The final evidence ledger, pending reviews and policy hypotheses must match.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Construct and rubric first.** For A–F report exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task/item, evaluator and source rules for individual production, legitimate interaction, permitted peer response, within-task assistance, delayed retrieval and exit. Missing rules yield UNSUPPORTED rather than invented scoring.
2. **Individual evidence is not group output.** In A, group quality never creates equal per-learner independent evidence. Attribute each eligible utterance or authored span to its actual learner; absent participation is unknown/not demonstrated, not automatically a failed language ability.
3. **Authorship and assistance survive polishing.** In B, retain first observations, delivered peer edits, copy/paste provenance, final response and the group artifact as distinct records. Neither identical final text nor learner ownership of the submit button establishes independent authorship.
4. **Interaction is not automatically dependence.** In C, allow independently generated turn-taking, clarification, negotiation and repair when the *source-defined interactional construct* requires an interlocutor; distinguish normal task responses from target-language coaching or supplied scripts. Unknown boundaries fail closed for disputed credit.
5. **Collaborative achievement is not delayed mastery.** In D, preserve practice benefit while requiring later eligible unaided retrieval and unseen transfer for claims that depend on them. Report eligible/completed/missing probes and actual delay, not just a next-day calendar label; preserve review debt and graduation gates.
6. **Instructional adaptation remains uncertain and causal claims limited.** In E, log assigned and delivered method, actual participation, available alternatives, confidence and comparable later independent outcomes. Separate peer/topic/order effects; retain exploration, balanced skill coverage and unfamiliar contexts; reverse hypotheses when justified.
7. **Topic interests and boundaries stay separate.** Across A–F, learner interests may softly weight conversation themes, never exclude neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar topics, override explicit topic boundaries, change can-do difficulty, or skip required objectives. A group preference is not a learning-style label.
8. **Replay is idempotent and privacy-aware.** In F, duplicate and out-of-order events yield identical per-learner evidence, provenance, cue classification and review obligations. Distinguish participant identity from session-local labels; unknown authorship, stale rubrics and absent cue-delivery proof cannot be laundered into independent credit. Minimize peer personal data.
9. **Report limits without release inflation.** For every scenario emit participant-authored/peer-cued/coauthored/unknown observations, source-valid interaction turns, first/final quality, assigned/delivered instructional options, topic and skill coverage, later independent outcomes, missingness and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. A documentation fixture or exact-head QA PASS is not shipped collaboration support, validated CEFR mastery or evidence of better learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F provide the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, injected clock/seed, immutable event streams in two arrival orders, rubric/blueprint versions, group and per-learner trace, peer/tutor cue delivery chronology, authored spans and edits, valid interaction versus coaching classification, policy alternatives with uncertainty, eligible/completed/missing independent delayed probes, unchanged review/exit gates and per-assertion verdicts. When authorship, source interaction allowances, modality support or later independent follow-up cannot be established, say UNAVAILABLE/UNSUPPORTED rather than force PASS.

Any claim that paired or collaborative practice improves durable learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal evidence, balanced comparators and ethically reviewed real-learner evaluation with genuinely unaided retention/transfer. **The tutor may use collaboration as a teaching method without confusing the group's answer with an individual's independently demonstrated competence.**
