**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F198 integrates claims; F200 distinguishes conditional plans; F201 handles source attribution; F202 preserves qualifiers in summaries; F203 chooses clarification; F204 tracks multi-speaker references. **Distinct construct:** can the learner interpret and communicate *when* something is said to happen by anchoring relative expressions to the actual speaker/message time, preserving deadline versus event-time scope, sequencing dependencies and resisting unjustified certainty? This is language-mediated temporal interpretation and repair, not automatic adjudication of whether a real event happened.

## Fixed CEFR objective, reviewed temporal oracles and delivery evidence

Freeze can-do ID/version, prerequisites, target language and variety, source, task/rubric/oracle versions, accessible modality, help policy, meaningful-turn ceiling where applicable, thresholds, first-unaided and delayed/unseen standards, receptive/productive balance and exit gates. No new graduation gate. Preserve unresolved B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix disagreement; B2 stays unavailable.

An independently reviewed **temporal-language oracle** freezes each speaker turn, utterance timestamp and location/zone **only when explicitly supplied**, actual message delivery order, quoted versus reported speech, event-time claims, date/time precision, temporal expression, antecedent event, inclusivity or boundary where justified, and any later correction. It distinguishes event date, message date, deadline, tentative plan, condition, duration, sequence and genuinely unknown information. Relative terms such as “tomorrow,” “yesterday,” “next Friday” and “tonight” require the appropriate utterance anchor; “next Friday” can be dialect/context-sensitive, so accept a targeted date clarification instead of silently imposing one interpretation. Never invent a timezone, date, confirmation, receipt, approval, clock precision or real-world occurrence. Review equivalents in the actual target language/variety rather than translating English temporal rules mechanically.

A separate **communicative-response oracle** scores what the learner needs to do: identify the time reference, relay an old message to a new listener, distinguish “by” from “on,” sequence two tasks, or ask one targeted question when an unknown date/zone materially blocks coordination. Accept natural paraphrases and pragmatically suitable reformulations, not one exact phrase. Reject unnecessary clarification when the given timestamp and relevant reference are sufficient. A later correction changes the learner's evidence only if it was actually delivered before their response. A planned deadline is not proof of completion; “after approval” is not proof approval occurred.

Duplicate replay, a midnight boundary, renamed scripts and assisted copying cannot create independent or delayed mastery.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Anchor yesterday to the original speaker, not the relay date.** A message explicitly timestamped Tuesday 6 October 2026 says, “I sent the draft yesterday.” On Thursday 8 October, the learner relays it. Accept “The sender said on Tuesday that they sent the draft on Monday 5 October,” or “They reported sending it on Monday.” Reject “They sent it yesterday [Wednesday]” as a Thursday-centered rewrite and reject asserting independently that delivery/receipt was verified. The oracle freezes the original timestamp, who authored the claim and what the recipient actually saw. A model that rewrites the date before the learner responds is answer exposure.

**B — A deadline is not an appointment or proof of completion.** A coordinator says, “Please send the poster by Friday 9 October; the review meeting is on Monday 12 October.” Asked when the poster is due and when the meeting happens, accept “No later than Friday; the meeting is Monday.” Reject “The poster must be sent on Friday,” “The review is on Friday,” or “It has already been sent.” “By Friday” permits an earlier submission; it does not imply a specific hour. If the task needs a precise cutoff hour, ask a targeted question rather than inventing 17:00 or midnight.

**C — Preserve before/after dependencies and distinguish plan from occurrence.** A volunteer says, “First check the room number. After the coordinator confirms it, print the flyers. If the room is not confirmed, wait.” The learner summarizes the sequence. Accept “Check the room, get confirmation, then print; otherwise wait.” Reject “Print before asking,” “The room is already confirmed,” or “The flyers were printed.” If a later message actually says “Room 4 is confirmed,” a subsequent response may advance the plan, but cannot infer the flyers have already been printed.

**D — Time-zone information is a communicative precondition, not a guessed conversion.** A collaborator says, “Let's meet Friday at 9,” without a timezone, and the task explicitly involves people in different zones. Accept “9 in which time zone?” or “Could you confirm the timezone for Friday at 9?” Reject confidently translating the time to the learner's local clock. In a separately frozen variant, the speaker supplies an unambiguous date, clock time and zone and the learner is only asked to relay those given facts; then unnecessary questions should not be rewarded. Do not perform zone arithmetic unless independently reviewed zone/date and daylight-saving metadata and an appropriate can-do are bound.

**E — First authorship, correction delivery, modality and replay.** At 10:01 the learner receives B and independently writes, “The poster is due Monday.” Record FIRST_UNAIDED_RESPONSE and DEADLINE_SCOPE_MISREAD. At 10:02 a rendered contrast explains “by Friday” versus “on Monday”; at 10:03 the learner copies the corrected dates. Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED_REPAIR, not independent mastery. At 10:04 a replay with the same stable event identity is deduplicated. An unopened hint is not exposure. If the audio fails but the transcript renders, no listening credit; an independently bound reading route may count. A later unseen probe must genuinely change deadline/event phrasing and communicative setting, not simply replace “poster” with “flyer.” If a correction is scheduled but not delivered, it cannot retroactively fix the earlier response.

**F — Reversible teaching choices, delayed contradiction and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly anchors quoted “tomorrow” to the current session rather than the original speaker. Across comparable completed sessions, ORIGINAL_MESSAGE_ANCHOR, QUOTED_VS_REPORTED_TIME_CONTRAST and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and sampled. Tentatively favor a method only if later first-unaided unseen relay and delayed retrieval improve at matched turn count, timestamp visibility, linguistic difficulty, help, modality, topic and delay. Learner B improves on assisted timeline sorting but fails a delayed independent deadline-versus-meeting task: lower confidence, restore exploration and avoid a mastery claim. A simpler calendar, familiar gaming event, extra answer hint, explicit date absent in a comparison, or shorter delay is a confound, not proof of method superiority. Missing follow-up is unknown, not failure or success. Gaming examples may be more frequent but must coexist with adjacent sports, neutral library and unfamiliar community contexts; no required skill or objective disappears.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR standard and language-specific validity.** A–F bind applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed temporal and response oracles, preserving level burden, accessibility, help policy, skill balance, thresholds and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Relative expressions retain the source anchor.** A resolves “yesterday” relative to Tuesday's original utterance when relayed Thursday; it neither silently reanchors to the relay date nor upgrades a reported sending claim into verified receipt.
3. **Deadline scope differs from event time.** B keeps “by Friday” distinct from “on Monday,” permits earlier submission and does not invent a deadline hour or completed action.
4. **Sequence and conditions do not become completed facts.** C preserves checking, confirmation and printing order, and treats a delivered confirmation separately from an inferred print completion.
5. **Missing time-zone information prompts targeted clarification.** D requests a zone only when materially needed, never guesses conversion, and avoids unnecessary questions where explicit information already suffices.
6. **Actual delivery, first authorship and channel integrity.** E distinguishes unaided error, rendered answer-bearing explanation, assisted copying, unopened hints, undelivered correction, failed audio, possible reading route and replay deduplication.
7. **Reversible matched instructional adaptation.** F compares genuinely delivered alternatives against later unaided temporal performance; timestamp visibility, complexity, help, topic, channel, register and delay confounds lower confidence and prevent causal-winner claims.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer with nonexclusive interests.** Require separate genuine sessions, changed temporal constructions and contexts, first-unaided output, explicit missing follow-up, no script leakage, balanced receptive/productive opportunities and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation.
9. **Honest evidence and protected boundaries.** Synthetic histories and generic CI are not executable temporal-dialogue QA, shipped personalization or evidence of improved human retention. Require exact-head executable delivery/provenance/response checks, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent outcomes against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design histories, not observed learner behavior or executable tests. They specify temporal anchoring, deadline scope, event sequencing and justified uncertainty in communicative exchanges. Remain UNSUPPORTED until independently implemented and verified.
