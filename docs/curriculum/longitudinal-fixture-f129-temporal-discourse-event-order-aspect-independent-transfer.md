## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Mention order is not event order.** A fictional English-language note says, “Mina sent the report **after** Leo checked the figures.” The first clause mentions sending first, but checking happened before sending. Learner A repeats clause order as chronology; learner B independently retells “Leo checked the figures, then Mina sent the report.” Counterfactual: replace **after** with **before**, holding events and people constant; expected order reverses. A mere swap of fictional names must not change the temporal oracle. Do not mistake general reading fluency or a working-memory error for a tense-specific deficit without separate evidence.

**B — Deadline versus continuing duration.** Fictional English instructions state, “Send your question **by Thursday**; the help desk is open **until Thursday**.” The first sets a latest permitted submission time (subject to the source's unspecified hour); the second describes continued availability ending on Thursday, without guaranteeing an exact closing hour. Learner A interprets both as “start on Thursday”; learner B explains the distinct obligations and asks for the missing hour when needed. Counterfactual: change “by Thursday” to “on Thursday” in an independently validated source; a Thursday-only reading becomes plausible, not the original deadline rule. English prepositions are illustrative, not language-universal equivalents. Do not fabricate exact cutoff times.

**C — Completed versus in-progress event.** In an independently validated fictional English conversation, “When Sam arrived, Jo **was writing** the letter” places writing in progress at arrival; “When Sam arrived, Jo **had written** the letter” places completion before arrival. Learner A treats both as proof that Jo finished after Sam arrived; learner B preserves the different event relations. Counterfactual: change only the validated aspectual form and update the accepted event model. Other target languages may encode the distinction lexically, contextually or through different morphology; validate the actual source language and level prerequisites instead of projecting English past progressive/perfect labels onto them.

**D — Reference-time shift and habituality.** A fictional message written on Monday says, “I **usually** take the bus, but **yesterday** I walked.” Its report time is Monday; the single walking event occurred Sunday relative to that anchored report time, while bus travel is habitual and does not assert a Monday bus ride. Learner A reads “yesterday” relative to the day they open the message and invents a Monday bus trip; learner B explains the anchored Sunday event and habitual pattern. Counterfactual: change the source's authenticated report date to Tuesday, keeping wording fixed; the anchored day changes to Monday. If the message timestamp is unavailable or conflicting, accept a precise clarification instead of assigning a fabricated absolute date. Do not treat one usual action as evidence it occurred on a specific day.

**E — Accessible delivery, version fidelity, chronology and replay.** An independently validated target-language audio story says an event occurred **before** another; a caption mistakenly says **after**, an automatic translation omits “already,” and a visual timeline is read by a screen reader in the reverse sequence without relational labels. Freeze authoritative and **actually rendered** stimuli; mark affected event-order/aspect evidence UNSUPPORTED while retaining separately valid language observations. A learner's accurate independent retelling at 10:00 stays FIRST_UNAIDED if a tutor timeline queued at 09:59 first **renders** at 10:01; a later retelling copied from it is ASSISTED. Offline replay of the same immutable event ID does not generate another first attempt; a genuinely new session-qualified event can. Never attribute a delivery defect to weak learning.

**F — Longitudinal method choice and unfamiliar recipient transfer.** Fictional learner A immediately copies a tutor's event diagram but later misorders events in unaided retelling. After **actually delivered** CONTRASTIVE_BEFORE_AFTER practice on comparable CEFR tasks, A independently explains an unseen fictional museum-visit sequence to a new classmate after a meaningful delay. Learner B instead benefits from SHORT_STORY_RETELLING on comparable tasks and later independently recounts a different fictional travel disruption. Compare baseline proficiency, target language/variety, event count, clause embedding, temporal-connective complexity, reference-time anchors, duration/aspect load, sentence length, modalities, accessibility, topic familiarity, help exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If one method always receives two explicit events and the other six implicit events, mark METHOD_EVENT_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Changing names while retaining the same event graph, connective pattern and answer path is not genuinely unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may justify **actually different next instruction**, with reversible uncertainty; contrary evidence reduces confidence. Preserve exploration, protected skill balance, due reviews and nonexclusive topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and event-source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, independently validated source/event/relation IDs, language/variety, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Advanced aspect is never silently imposed on beginner objectives.
2. **Event versus mention order.** A/C bind event chronology to authoritative temporal markers and aspect, not clause presentation order. Changing only a validated connective or aspectual construction changes the expected relation; cosmetic character substitutions do not.
3. **Deadline, duration and temporal scope.** B distinguishes “by” from “until,” avoids invented hours and keeps source-supported deadline and availability claims separate. Counterfactual wording changes must update the oracle rather than reuse a memorized date answer.
4. **Reference-time, habituality and uncertainty.** C/D distinguish event time, report time, habitual versus single occurrence and completed versus ongoing states. Missing/conflicting source anchors remain unknown, and warranted clarification is creditable.
5. **First-unaided evidence, actual help chronology and replay.** E freezes first authored response, retries and answer-bearing help **actually rendered before** it. Queued, failed or post-answer models do not retroactively contaminate an independent attempt; copied answers are assisted; duplicate event replay is idempotent.
6. **Language-specific validity and accessible delivery.** B/C/E validate target-language temporal distinctions and approved varieties independently, including audio, captions, translations, diagram labels, screen-reader order and cropping. Broken delivery quarantines only affected constructs; do not force English morphology onto other languages.
7. **Delayed retention and genuinely unseen communicative transfer.** A–F require meaningful separate learning opportunities, changed event graphs and temporal relations, new recipients and first-unaided explanation/production without answer-bearing help. Publish eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; cosmetic retellings do not count as unseen.
8. **Reversible adaptation, confounds and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** methods, event and memory-load comparability, topic familiarity, uncertainty, exploration and contradictory outcomes. Preserve receptive/productive skills, due reviews, CEFR gates and neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar topic rotation. No permanent learning-style label.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/event/relation/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, reference-time anchors, authoritative and delivered accessible artifacts, learner/help/event timeline, validated temporal oracle, counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently validated target-language temporal constructions and accessible alternatives; frozen event relations and reference-time anchors; first-unaided responses and actual help chronology; delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen recipient transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skills, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and connective/aspect/deadline/reference-time/translation/caption/crop/screen-reader/version/replay counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. A stronger claim that adaptive temporal-discourse instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners independently reconstruct and communicate event relations, not just copy a timeline.**
