# F28 — Instructional-method success in one skill is not automatic evidence for another

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until run against a named engine HEAD. This document does not report a reproduced defect, shipped support, or improved learner outcomes. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F23 separates topic familiarity from method effects; F24 tests assignment bias; F25 protects required skill coverage; F27 handles noisy latency. F28 isolates **cross-skill extrapolation**: success after one instructional option for one language skill or can-do must not be promoted to a universal learner "style" or substituted for independent evidence in another skill.

## Controlled setup

Select two versioned, currently supported CEFR can-dos (or two supported skill-specific evidence routes for one shared objective) with explicitly recorded skill families and real independent evaluators. For example, compare independent reading comprehension with supported written production; use listening or speaking only if that modality and its evaluator genuinely exist. Hold objective versions, prerequisites, difficulty, rubrics, required coverage, evaluation opportunities and eligible instructional options fixed within each comparison. Do not pretend that unlike skill assessments are exchangeable.

Use a deterministic balanced-policy baseline and a candidate adaptive policy with the same required objectives, access constraints and fixed policy seed. Maintain an append-only synthetic ledger containing learner/objective/version, skill family, activity/run/event IDs, session and learning-day provenance, instruction choice and exposure, topic/context family, first attempt, retries, hint/model/scaffold exposure, error category, independent evaluation result, actual delay/intervening material, unseen-context transfer, assignment/offer/completion, missingness and confidence. Track evidence *per skill and objective*; optional related-skill evidence may inform a **weak, revisable prior** only when its relationship is explicit and validated. Never silently count cross-skill observations as target-skill mastery.

## Synthetic histories

- **A — Reading gain, writing non-transfer.** Learner L improves later independent reading after concise explanations, but repeated independent writing probes show persistent grammatical errors after the same option. A global "concise learner" label would select the wrong method for writing. The writing hypothesis must remain distinct and responsive to its own evidence.
- **B — Opposite within-learner responses.** For the same learner and CEFR band, worked examples lead to better delayed independent written production, while short contrastive explanations lead to better delayed listening comprehension (only if listening is implemented). Both patterns recur across distinct sessions and neutral/unfamiliar contexts. The tutor may choose different methods by supported skill without changing standards.
- **C — Sparse target-skill observations.** Many successful independent reading probes exist, but the learner has only one assisted speaking attempt or no supported speaking evaluator. The policy cannot infer speaking mastery, confident speaking-method superiority or completed speaking coverage from reading outcomes. Keep the target hypothesis uncertain and use a balanced supported fallback.
- **D — Same skill, new can-do and context.** A method performs well on familiar vocabulary recognition, then is offered for a harder independent reading-inference can-do. Without comparable later evidence on the new can-do, transfer of the *instructional preference* is a hypothesis, not proven effectiveness. Distinguish new objective demands from topic novelty and from the learner's interest.
- **E — Reversal after genuine target-skill evidence.** A tentative writing-method choice based on related-skill data is contradicted by multiple later independent writing and unseen-context transfer probes. The policy reduces confidence, revises the writing choice and continues exploring alternatives. It does not overwrite valid reading history or permanently assign a learner type.
- **F — Cross-skill exposure and opportunity bias.** One method receives frequent easy reading assignments with short follow-up, while another receives fewer difficult production tasks and longer delays; missing production outcomes are common. A pooled success rate favors the first method. Compare outcomes within eligible skill/objective/difficulty and actual review opportunity before inferring any cross-skill advantage.

## Acceptance assertions

1. **Scoped attribution.** Every instructional outcome is attributed to its verified objective, skill family, instruction exposure and evaluator. Reading success cannot increment independent writing, listening or speaking mastery, delayed retrieval or transfer counts.
2. **No fixed learner type.** Do not assign a permanent modality preference or universal teaching style from A–F. A learner may have different, changing instructional hypotheses across can-dos and skill families.
3. **Target-skill evidence priority.** In A, B and E, comparable later independent target-skill retention and unseen transfer outrank a contrary pooled or related-skill estimate. Assisted correctness is diagnostic, never independent target-skill proof.
4. **Conservative related-skill borrowing.** In C and D, any explicit cross-skill prior is low-confidence, reversible, separately reported and never sufficient to satisfy a CEFR evidence gate. Where no validated relationship exists, use the balanced default.
5. **Skill and objective coverage preserved.** Required productive/receptive can-dos and due reviews stay eligible. The policy cannot repeatedly substitute an easier strong skill for a weak required one; unsupported modalities remain UNAVAILABLE, not passed.
6. **Comparable opportunities.** In F, report assignments, offers, completed independent probes, missing outcomes, actual delay, difficulty and context by skill/objective/method. Do not infer superiority from pooled or completed-only success rates with unequal exposure.
7. **Interest-method separation.** Familiar or favorite topics may increase engagement but cannot prove a method generalizes across skills. Retain adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts with equivalent linguistic demand and evaluator meaning.
8. **Deterministic, reversible policy.** With the same validated ledger, policy version, objective graph, supported candidates and seed, produce the same scoped estimates, uncertainty, method decisions, exploration choices and coverage debt. Replays do not duplicate evidence.
9. **Safe fallback and honest claims.** If target-skill evidence is sparse, incomparable, assisted or missing, report uncertainty and sample balanced supported options. Never relax shared CEFR standards or claim human-learning improvement from this design fixture.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the exact tested HEAD, executable command, synthetic ledger/event IDs, objective and skill-family mappings, fixed seed, exposure and assistance provenance, target-versus-related evidence counts, per-option opportunity/missingness, delayed independent and unseen-context outcomes, estimated uncertainty, selected instruction, protected coverage, and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Include at least one counterexample where pooled success suggests the *opposite* of valid target-skill evidence, plus a sparse-data fallback and a later reversal. Test both assignment invariance and replay/idempotence.

An executable deterministic PASS would establish contract behavior only. Evidence that adaptation **improves durable independent learning** requires a separately reviewed longitudinal learner evaluation against a balanced baseline, with comparable delayed probes, genuine unseen transfer, calibrated uncertainty and no loss of required CEFR skill coverage. Do not describe adaptive scaffolding alone as completion of the tutor vision.
