## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Near completion versus completed with a small margin.** A fictional club volunteer says **“I almost finished setting up the chairs.”** A learner who tells the organizer **“The chairs are ready”** fails: the speaker has not asserted completion. **“The volunteer says the setup is nearly finished; please confirm what remains”** preserves the reported state. Counterfactual: the source changes to **“I barely finished before the guests arrived.”** Reporting **“They failed to finish”** now fails; the source asserts completion with little time margin. A later confirmed completion update applies from its own timestamp and cannot rewrite the earlier almost-finished statement.

**B — Enough and too much relative to a purpose.** A fictional group needs **eight seats** and a reviewed room note says **“There are exactly eight usable seats for this meeting.”** **“There are enough seats for eight attendees”** is justified for the stated purpose; **“The room is large enough for any group”** is not. Counterfactual: **nine attendees** are expected; the original eight seats are **not enough** without additional arrangements. Another fictional note says **“This box is too large for the shelf”**: that is a fit constraint for that shelf, not a universal claim that the box is too large for every use. If the usable-seat count is uncertain, credit a targeted clarification rather than inventing a capacity.

**C — Negation and degree scope.** A fictional book-club member says **“The new room isn't very quiet, but it's not too noisy for our discussion.”** A learner who reports **“The room is completely silent”** or **“It's unusably noisy”** fails. The reviewed report preserves both qualified judgments and their task-relative purpose. Counterfactual: **“The room is not quiet enough for recording”** concerns a different purpose and cannot be collapsed into the earlier discussion judgment. **“Not very expensive”** does not necessarily mean cheap; **“hardly ever”** is near-zero frequency in a reviewed variety but must not be scored as mathematically zero. Language/variety review handles natural scalar and polarity readings rather than imposing a universal numeric degree.

**D — Approximate quantities and explicit thresholds.** A fictional supply message says **“We have almost ten markers.”** A learner cannot claim exactly ten or guarantee an order needing ten; the exact count is unknown from the utterance alone. Counterfactual: an independently reviewed inventory says **“We have nine markers”**, establishing nine in that source-world. Another reviewed requirement says **“At least ten markers are needed.”** Nine does not meet that threshold; ten meets it. If a different source says **“Just over ten”** without an exact count, preserve the directional approximation without inventing an exact value. Do not convert colloquial “almost” into a mathematically specified distance from a threshold.

**E — Accessible delivery and actual help chronology.** An original fictional audio message says **“I'm not quite ready; I have almost finished the posters.”** ASR drops **“not”**; captions omit **“almost”**; translation changes **“not quite”** to a positive readiness assertion; screen-reader order detaches the negation from **“ready.”** Quarantine construct-changing delivered-channel evidence rather than penalizing a learner for corrupted input. At 10:00 the learner independently asks **“Are the posters actually finished, or nearly finished?”**; a tutor's answer-bearing model renders at 10:01. The 10:00 clarification remains FIRST_UNAIDED; a copied report at 10:02 is ASSISTED. Queued-but-unrendered help does not contaminate earlier authorship. Stable event replay cannot manufacture a new independent attempt or learning day. Corrected accessible text may support reading but cannot certify unheard listening. A later source update cannot rewrite the original oracle.

**F — Outcome-responsive method reversal and nonexclusive interests.** Fictional learner A answers immediate ALMOST_VS_BARELY_MINIMAL_PAIRS correctly with support, but in later unaided unfamiliar handoffs repeatedly treats **“almost complete”** as complete. Several matched, actually delivered COMPLETION_STATE_TIMELINE opportunities precede stronger delayed first-unaided reports with new recipients, different threshold purposes and different degree constructions; only repeated comparable evidence may cautiously favor the timeline while retaining alternatives. Fictional learner B initially benefits from ENOUGH_FOR_PURPOSE_READBACK but later independently generalizes **“enough for this group”** to **“enough for everyone”**; confidence falls, review becomes due and exploration resumes. If timeline tasks have explicit numeric thresholds while readback tasks use vague judgments, or one method includes a completed answer model, infer **no winner**. Swapping gaming for music in an otherwise identical sentence is not unseen transfer. Missing delayed follow-up is MISSING, not failure; unfamiliar and neutral topics remain eligible.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR objective and reviewed degree oracle.** A–F require a versioned existing can-do, prerequisites, source/recipient/information gap, reviewed graded-state/threshold oracle, target language/variety/register, accessible actually delivered stimulus, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; no invented advanced scalar semantics requirement for beginners.
2. **Near-miss and completion truth conditions.** A distinguishes “almost finished” from “barely finished” and from confirmed completion; preserve original speaker attribution and update time. Neither almost-completion nor a plan to finish establishes a completed event.
3. **Purpose-bound sufficiency and excess.** B preserves the stated recipient, task and threshold for “enough,” “not enough” and “too.” Adequacy for one purpose cannot silently become universal capacity, suitability or safety.
4. **Modifier, polarity and degree scope.** C distinguishes “not very,” “not quite,” “hardly,” “too” and “not enough” without inferring opposite extremes. Accept independently reviewed language/variety-specific paraphrases; do not impose a universal intensity percentage.
5. **Approximation versus explicit bound.** D preserves unknown exact quantities in “almost”/“just over,” while applying an explicitly given inclusive “at least” threshold to a reviewed exact count. Do not invent numerical precision or treat colloquial approximation as a formal interval.
6. **Actually delivered access and first-authorship chronology.** E reconciles original versus rendered ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader negation and degree, quarantines construct-changing corruption, preserves first authorship before actual help, deduplicates stable events and distinguishes access-equivalent from different constructs. Text cannot certify unheard listening.
7. **Controlled, uncertain, reversible instructional adaptation.** F compares feasible actually delivered methods under matched objective, threshold explicitness, modifier and negation burden, source completeness, modality, language, topic, help, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, selectively observed, contradictory or confounded outcomes yield no winner; delayed regression lowers confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed first-unaided unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Copied degree scales, hinted thresholds, immediate repetitions and topic-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, new speaker/recipient/context **and changed modifier, purpose, threshold or completion-state structure**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided actionable handoff and explicit follow-up missingness. Preserve shared objectives, supported receptive/productive balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Green documentation CI is not a shipped degree evaluator, an outcome-responsive tutor or proof of improved human retention. Require exact-head deterministic executable assertions against real objectives, logged delivered instructional changes, later independent transfer versus a balanced baseline and separately reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs work, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are fictional design/probe cases, not observed learner histories or executable QA. Preserve near-completion versus completion, task-bound adequacy, graded polarity and source-bounded uncertainty; maintain explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and learner-evaluation evidence exist.
