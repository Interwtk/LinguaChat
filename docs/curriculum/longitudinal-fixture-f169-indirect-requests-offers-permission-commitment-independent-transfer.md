# F169 — Indirect requests, offers, permission and commitment boundaries in independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until exact-head executable assertions bind independently reviewed pragmatic/source-world oracles to existing versioned CEFR communicative can-dos, actual accessible delivery, and later first-unaided genuinely unseen recipient-facing transfer. This is **not** a shipped speech-act evaluator, a universal politeness rule, a new CEFR exit condition, proof of outcome-responsive instructional adaptation, or evidence of improved human retention. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F159 covers attributed speech and epistemic stance; F162 reference clarification; F164 contrast/correction; F165 presupposition and loaded questions; F168 spoken numeric identity. **Distinct construct:** can the learner independently infer or clarify a speaker's **communicative action**—a context-supported indirect request, a genuine information question, an offer, a permission request, a suggestion or a commitment—without treating politeness formulas, ability statements, tentative plans or acknowledgements as automatic consent or completed actions? Repeating polite expressions or identifying their grammar does not establish recipient-actionable pragmatic understanding.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — "Could you open the window?" versus ability information.** In a fictional study room, a coordinator points to a closed window while the other person is beside it and says **"Could you open the window, please?"** The context-supported indirect request is answered with an actionable **"Sure, I'll open it"** (or an appropriate refusal), not merely **"Yes, I am able to."** Counterfactual: a language tutor asks **"Could you open a window if the handle were stiff?"** as a genuine ability question; answering that ability question is appropriate. Without relevant context, do not force the request interpretation. The same string need not always have the same speech act.

**B — Offer, acceptance and task completion are three events.** A fictional volunteer says **"I can bring extra chairs if you need them."** This is an offer, not proof the other person accepted and not evidence the chairs arrived. The recipient independently replies **"Yes, please bring two"** to accept, or **"Thanks, we have enough"** to decline; a later source-world event separately establishes delivery. Counterfactual: **"Thanks, I heard you"** acknowledges hearing but does not unambiguously accept. Do not upgrade a tentative offer to a fulfilled commitment without the relevant response and subsequent evidence.

**C — Asking permission is not receiving it.** A learner asks the fictional librarian **"May I use this desk?"** The librarian replies **"I need to check whether it's reserved."** The learner appropriately waits or asks for confirmation; the reply does **not** grant permission. In a matched counterfactual the librarian says **"Yes, you may use it now"** and permission is established for that desk and time, not for unrelated rooms. A respectful indirect alternative in the target language is valid if the independently reviewed register oracle supports it. No real-world authorization decision is inferred.

**D — Suggestion versus confirmed plan, and register variation.** In a fictional club meeting **"We could meet on Thursday"** is a proposal; **"Thursday works for me"** signals one person's availability but may not settle the whole group's schedule. **"Agreed, let's meet Thursday at four"** after all relevant parties accept can establish the reviewed group plan. The learner independently relays **"Thursday has been suggested; we're waiting for confirmation"** before agreement and **"The group agreed to Thursday at four"** afterward. An equally appropriate informal or formal formulation should pass when it preserves the action and relationship; do not grade politeness by rigid lexical templates.

**E — Actual delivery, negation drift, chronology and accessibility.** A reviewed original fictional audio reply says **"You don't need to bring the projector"** but TTS drops **"don't"** and the caption incorrectly shows **"You need to bring the projector."** Quarantine the construct-changing variants; do not mark the learner wrong for responding to what was actually rendered, or claim independent listening from a transcript. At 09:02, before a hint is actually displayed, the learner independently asks **"Do you mean I should bring it or not?"**; at 09:03 a suggested answer appears; at 09:04 the learner copies it. Preserve the 09:02 FIRST_UNAIDED clarification, mark 09:04 ASSISTED, and deduplicate replayed event IDs. A selected-but-not-rendered hint cannot retroactively contaminate 09:02. A screen-reader alternative can provide accessible reading evidence but not unheard speech evidence.

**F — Outcome-responsive exploration, reversal and unseen recipient transfer.** Learner A immediately recognizes indirect requests after a worked phrase chart, but fails delayed first-unaided replies to **new speakers and different request actions**. Across matched **actually delivered** TWO_TURN_RECIPIENT_ROLEPLAY sessions, later independent transfer improves in neutral and unfamiliar contexts; cautiously increase roleplay probability while retaining chart, clarification and register exploration. Learner B initially benefits from roleplay but later treats a new permission-check reply as approval; lower confidence, bring review forward and reopen alternatives. If roleplay uses clear explicit requests while chart trials use ambiguous multi-party conversations, infer **no winner**. Changing only the club from music to games while reusing the same dialogue is a topic swap, **not** unseen pragmatic transfer. Missing delayed probes are MISSING, not failures or evidence of gain.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR objective and reviewed pragmatic oracle.** A–F bind existing versioned communicative can-dos, a real recipient information gap, independently reviewed speaker/context/action oracle, target language/variety/register, accessible actual delivery, unaided rubric, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Indirect request versus information question.** A uses the actual conversational context to distinguish a request from a literal ability inquiry, accepts actionable natural responses or appropriate refusal, and does not assert one universal reading of an ambiguous question.
3. **Offer, acceptance and fulfillment boundaries.** B distinguishes an offer, explicit acceptance/refusal and subsequent task completion; acknowledgement alone cannot fabricate acceptance or a completed action.
4. **Permission versus unresolved authorization.** C distinguishes asking permission from receiving it, respects the actual scope and timing of any explicit grant, and accepts clarification or waiting when the answer remains pending.
5. **Tentative suggestion, agreement and register.** D preserves who proposed, who accepted and whether the shared plan is actually confirmed; allow independently reviewed natural formal/informal and cross-variety equivalents without stereotype-based penalties.
6. **Actual delivery, chronology, authorship and accessibility.** E audits original versus rendered text/audio/TTS/ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader output, quarantines action-changing negation or pragmatic drift, preserves pre-help first authorship, records answer exposure and deduplicates stable events. Reading, listening and free production are different evidence constructs.
7. **Controlled uncertain reversible method selection.** F compares feasible **actually delivered** methods with matched pragmatic ambiguity, turn count, register, language/variety, modality, help, signal quality, topic, prior exposure, spacing and later opportunity. Sparse, confounded or contradictory evidence yields no winner; later regression lowers confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Same-session repairs, copied politeness scripts, model-assisted replies and topic-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, **new speech act or pragmatic ambiguity and changed speaker/recipient action**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided correct actionable response or justified clarification, and explicit follow-up missingness. Preserve shared CEFR gates, modality balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Documentation CI is not executable pragmatic QA, a shipped adaptive tutor or evidence of improved human learning. Require exact-head executable assertions against real objectives and demonstrated longitudinal outcome-driven instructional changes, then independently reviewed later unaided transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design/probe histories, not observed learner outcomes or executable QA. Preserve contextual intent, explicit offer/permission/commitment boundaries, genuine ambiguity, accessibility, reversible adaptation and UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and independently reviewed longitudinal evidence exist.
