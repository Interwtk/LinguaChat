## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Left/right depends on who is facing which way.** Fictional grid: a corridor runs east–west; learner's addressee stands at the center facing **east**; a library doorway is on the addressee's **left (north)**. Independently validated English instruction: “Facing east, turn left into the library.” Learner A uses the map viewer's left while looking at a rotated display and selects the south doorway; learner B explicitly anchors left to the addressee's orientation and identifies the north doorway. Counterfactual: the addressee now faces **west** at the same point; left becomes south, and the original library-left instruction no longer denotes the north doorway. A fixed screen-side heuristic must fail the counterfactual. A separate source that never establishes facing direction must be marked FRAME_AMBIGUOUS rather than guessed from conventional north-up display.

**B — Landmark relations versus ordered motion.** Fictional map graph: Start S connects east to junction J; J connects north to museum M and south to café C; museum M connects east to destination D. The source says, “From S, go to J, pass the museum on your way to D, and do not stop at the café.” A learner who names all landmarks but walks J→C→J→M→D violates the prohibition; a learner who follows S→J→M→D preserves the specified route. Counterfactual: replace “pass the museum” with “stop at the museum before D”; the required-stop oracle changes, even if the geometric path is unchanged. If the source does not distinguish passing a landmark from entering it, the evaluator must use the independently reviewed intended meaning and not fabricate a stop. Validate `past`, `through`, `toward` and `at` per language and context rather than treating them as interchangeable.

**C — Closed edge and accessible route constraint.** Fictional station: Platform P connects to exit E by stairs; P connects to lift L and L connects to E by a longer corridor. A source explicitly says, “The stairs are closed. Use the lift and follow the corridor to the exit.” Learner A repeats a memorized shorter stair route; learner B independently explains P→L→E and the closure. Counterfactual: the lift is marked unavailable too, with no validated alternative; the correct communicative action is to state that the provided map offers **no supported route** and request updated directions, not invent an accessible passage. Accessibility is a **source/task constraint**, never an inference about the learner's health or mobility. No synthetic route is a real emergency instruction.

**D — Multilingual spatial relations and viewpoint shifts.** In independently reviewed fictional English and Spanish tasks, “to the left of the museum” / “a la izquierda del museo” may require context to establish whose viewpoint or map convention governs the relation; “turn left” / “gira a la izquierda” requires a moving addressee's orientation. A learner correctly translates individual words but silently switches from the walker frame to a map-viewer frame; another preserves the authorized frame and communicates the intended relation. Counterfactual: the source changes from a walker instruction to a north-up map legend with an explicit westward direction; re-resolve the licensed target-language construction and graph oracle. Language-specific absolute, relative and intrinsic frames must be independently reviewed, including varieties that do not encode these contrasts like English. Code-switched or ambiguous stimuli without a validated oracle are UNSUPPORTED for the affected construct.

**E — Actual delivery, accessibility, assistance chronology and replay.** Authoritative fictional directions say “turn **right** after the bridge,” but the delivered caption says “left”; a map image is rotated 180° while the north arrow is clipped; an alt-text description omits the required lift; a screen reader announces the answer-bearing highlighted path before the task; or TTS/ASR corrupts the word `north`. Freeze both authoritative and **actually delivered** channels. Contradictory or inaccessible directional evidence is UNSUPPORTED for the affected construct, not a learner failure. A first independently authored route at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED if a hint was queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01; a later route copied from the displayed overlay is ASSISTED. Duplicate session-qualified event replay cannot create another first attempt or a delayed retrieval. An accessible equivalent must preserve the same orientation, graph constraints and route-choice evidence or explicitly record non-equivalence.

**F — Reversible methods, balanced skills and genuinely unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A correctly follows an arrow-highlighted route immediately but fails delayed first-unaided directions after a viewpoint switch. After **actually delivered** LEARNER_VERBALIZES_FRAME and CONTRASTIVE_VIEWPOINT_PAIRS on comparable routes with fading help, A later independently instructs a new recipient in a **new** map graph and detects a closed path. Fictional learner B instead improves delayed spoken route communication after LANDMARK_FIRST_DESCRIPTION with comparable branching and language load. Freeze baseline, real CEFR descriptor, graph size, turn/landmark counts, frame switches, constraints, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity, help exposure, review interval and offered follow-up. If A receives a two-edge straight corridor and B receives a six-edge route with two frame switches, mark METHOD_ROUTE_LENGTH_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_VIEWPOINT_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Renaming streets on an identical route graph or rotating a trained screenshot with the same answer path is not genuinely unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may justify an **observed change in later delivered instruction**, with uncertainty, continued sampling and reversal after contrary evidence. Interest-based café, music or travel examples may be more probable but cannot exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar contexts or skip protected CEFR skills.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and spatial-source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, reviewed language/variety-specific spatial constructions, source-effective map graph, orientation, speaker/addressee frame, task/rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Complex route planning is not silently assigned to beginners.
2. **Reference-frame fidelity.** A/D distinguish walker-relative, viewer-relative, intrinsic and cardinal frames where independently licensed; reversing facing direction or changing the source's frame changes the oracle. Missing orientation remains ambiguous rather than silently north-up.
3. **Route order, landmarks and communicative meaning.** B verifies traversable edges, ordered actions, required stops, prohibitions and distinction between passing and stopping. A route with all named landmarks but wrong order or forbidden detours is not fully correct.
4. **Closures, accessibility and unsupported routes.** C rejects explicitly closed edges, preserves source-stated accessibility constraints and rewards specific requests for updated directions when no validated route remains. The evaluator must not hallucinate a path or infer the learner's abilities.
5. **Multilingual validity and warranted uncertainty.** A–D use independently reviewed language/variety/register-specific spatial oracles, accept licensed alternatives and preserve ambiguity. Word-for-word translations, unvalidated code-switching and map conventions cannot override the source's authorized frame.
6. **Actual delivery, accessibility, authorship, help chronology and replay.** E checks authoritative versus delivered captions, audio, maps, north arrows, alt text, TTS/ASR and screen-reader order. Answer-bearing help **actually exposed before** a response contaminates independence; queued-but-not-rendered help does not. Replays are idempotent and defective channels quarantine affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen communicative transfer.** A–F require distinct real learning opportunities, changed graph topology or constraints, new speaker/viewpoint/recipient and first-unaided interpretation, targeted clarification or free production. Cosmetic street-name changes are not unseen transfer. Preserve eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
8. **Reversible adaptation, matched difficulty and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** instruction, matched route complexity/frame switches/landmarks/language load, uncertainty, alternative sampling, contrary evidence and **observed change in subsequent instruction**. Interests remain a probabilistic context bias; protected skills, CEFR gates, reviews and neutral/unfamiliar contexts remain.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/graph/frame/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, actually delivered artifacts, first/help/event chronology, reviewed spatial-language oracle, orientation/closure/translation/caption/map-rotation/accessibility/replay/source-version counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot establish a shipped route-language evaluator, reproducible runtime defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific spatial semantics and alternatives; source-effective fictional graph and frame; accessible authoritative and actually delivered stimuli; independently attributable first responses and actual answer-bearing help chronology; delayed first-unaided retrieval and genuinely unseen topology/viewpoint/constraint transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skill coverage, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and frame/route/closure/accessibility/translation/caption/alt-text/map-rotation/replay/source-version counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Unvalidated spatial expressions, absent orientation, contradictory delivered channels, inaccessible route constraints and missing follow-ups remain **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive spatial-language instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor teaches learners to anchor directions in the correct viewpoint, preserve constraints and ask when a route is genuinely unknown—then demonstrate that skill independently in new situations.**
