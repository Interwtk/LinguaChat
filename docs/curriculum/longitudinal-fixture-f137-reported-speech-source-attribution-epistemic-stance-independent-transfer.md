## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Direct quotation versus accurate indirect report.** At fictional 09:00, Maya says to Arun: **“I might arrive after lunch.”** At 10:00 Arun reports Maya's words to Bea. Learner A says “Maya promised she would arrive after lunch”; learner B says “Maya said she **might** arrive after lunch.” The latter preserves the speaker and uncertainty without asserting an arrival. Counterfactual: source changes to “I **will** arrive after lunch”; a stronger report may now be licensed, but “promised” still requires a speech-act oracle, not merely future tense. A report that removes quotation marks but preserves the source's proposition can be an acceptable paraphrase; invented words enclosed in direct quotation marks are not. If the delivered transcript lacks the modal and no reliable accessible equivalent supplies it, certainty grading is **UNSUPPORTED**.

**B — Nested hearsay and source ownership.** Fictional sources: Leo says **“Nina told me the workshop may close on Friday.”** Nina's original statement is unavailable. Learner A reports “Nina confirmed the workshop closes Friday”; learner B reports “According to Leo, Nina **may have said** the workshop could close on Friday; Nina's words have not been checked.” The source licenses only a report of Leo's report, not direct evidence from Nina. Counterfactual: add an authenticated fictional direct statement from Nina, “The workshop will stay open Friday.” The correct summary now notes a **conflict** between Leo's earlier second-hand account and Nina's direct statement; it cannot silently erase one source or treat Leo as having quoted Nina verbatim. No inference about which person is dishonest is licensed.

**C — Pronoun, deictic time and reporting-time shift.** Fictional dialogue, explicitly timestamped Monday 15:00: Sam tells Jo, **“I sent your notes yesterday; I'll check them tomorrow.”** On Wednesday, Jo reports the exchange to Kai. Independently reviewed English interpretation: Sam says they sent **Jo's** notes on **Sunday** and intended to check them on **Tuesday**; the source does not confirm that the Tuesday check occurred. Learner A reports that Jo sent Sam's notes on Tuesday and already checked them Wednesday; learner B preserves speaker, owner, anchored dates and intention versus completed event. Counterfactual: move the original utterance to **Tuesday** while keeping the words; the anchored dates shift to Monday/Wednesday. If timezone or original date is omitted, use source-relative wording or targeted clarification, not invented absolute dates. Language-specific backshift is accepted only when independently validated and semantically faithful; mechanical English tense-backshift is not a universal cross-language rule.

**D — Negation, denial and qualified evidence.** Fictional notice says, **“The organizer did not confirm that the lecture was canceled.”** Another source says, **“The organizer confirmed that the lecture was not canceled.”** These are **not equivalent**: the first denies a confirmation event; the second asserts a confirmation of non-cancellation. Learner A collapses both to “The organizer canceled the lecture”; learner B independently distinguishes the two and says cancellation status is unknown from the first alone. Counterfactual: replace the first with “The organizer denied that the lecture was canceled”; the source now reports a denial, but that is still an attributed claim rather than external proof. A translated double negative or ASR deletion of “not” without a validated source oracle is UNSUPPORTED for the affected polarity judgment.

**E — Delivered accessibility, source conflict, authorship chronology and replay.** An authoritative fictional recording contains “**might not** open”; the delivered caption omits “not”; TTS pronounces “might” as “must”; a screen reader reads speaker B's line before speaker A's; a cropped screenshot hides quotation marks; or an automatic summary converts “according to Leo” into an unqualified assertion. Freeze authoritative and **actually delivered** channels. A learner cannot be penalized for a distinction unavailable in the delivered accessible stimulus; quarantine the affected construct as **UNSUPPORTED**. A first learner-authored summary at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED if a tutor model was queued at 09:59 but **first rendered at 10:01**; a later copied answer is ASSISTED. Duplicate immutable session-qualified event replay cannot create a new first attempt, distinct session or delayed retrieval. Equivalent accessible channels must preserve speakers, modality, polarity, temporal anchoring and quotation boundaries or declare non-equivalence.

**F — Reversible methods, matched source difficulty and unseen-audience transfer.** Fictional learner A immediately paraphrases a highlighted transcript correctly but later upgrades hearsay into certainty without help. After **actually delivered** SOURCE_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_TABLE and EPISTEMIC_STRENGTH_SORT on matched two-speaker sources with fading prompts, A later independently briefs an unfamiliar recipient about a **new** conflicting report and qualifies the uncertainty. Fictional learner B instead improves delayed spoken reporting after SPEAKER_TURN_MAPPING and AUDIO_REPORT_TO_WRITTEN_SUMMARY with comparable source depth and linguistic demands. Freeze baseline, CEFR descriptor, speakers, report depth, modal/negation count, utterance length, time shifts, source reliability, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity, help exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If A receives one direct short quote and B receives four nested contradictory reports with pronoun shifts, mark METHOD_REPORT_DEPTH_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_LANGUAGE_LOAD_CONFOUNDED and infer **no method winner**. Changing only character names or recording the same transcript with a new voice is **not** genuinely unseen transfer; require a new source structure, conflict, stance relation or audience purpose demanding fresh attribution. Track eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may justify an **observed change in subsequent delivered instruction**, with calibrated uncertainty, alternative sampling and reversal after contrary evidence. Interests in music, gaming or travel can influence example probability but must not exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar contexts.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR, reviewed language and source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, independently reviewed language/variety/register reporting expressions, source-effective utterance versions, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Nested hearsay is not assigned before prerequisites.
2. **Speaker, quotation and reporting fidelity.** A/B preserve original speaker, reporter, direct quotation versus paraphrase, nested attribution and permitted speech-act strength. Counterfactual source wording changes only licensed reporting claims; unverified direct quotes or speaker merges fail.
3. **Epistemic strength and source-versus-fact separation.** A/B/D distinguish might/will/confirmed/denied/unknown, second-hand claims and independently established facts. An uncertain report cannot become a verified event or an unqualified promise.
4. **Pronoun, time and negation scope.** C/D anchor speaker/addressee pronouns and event/report dates, preserve intention versus completion and distinguish “not confirmed canceled” from “confirmed not canceled.” Time and polarity counterfactuals update only their affected meanings.
5. **Conflicting sources and warranted clarification.** B/D retain source conflicts without declaring a liar or silently selecting a convenient claim. Missing original utterances, timestamps, context or unresolved referents support specific clarification or qualified reporting, not invented facts.
6. **Actual delivery, accessibility, help chronology and replay.** E compares authoritative with actually delivered audio/captions/ASR/TTS/translation/speaker labels/reading order and quote boundaries. Answer-bearing help **exposed before** first authorship contaminates independence; queued but unrendered help does not. Duplicate event replay is idempotent; defective channels quarantine affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen communicative transfer.** A–F require distinct real learning opportunities, independent first attempts and changed source structure, epistemic relation or audience purpose. Cosmetic names or a new voice reading the same source do not count. Preserve eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
8. **Reversible adaptation, comparable difficulty and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** instruction, matched speaker/depth/modal/negation/time burden, uncertainty, alternative sampling, contrary evidence and **observed change in later instruction**. Interests are a probabilistic context bias; CEFR objectives, protected skills, due reviews and neutral/unfamiliar contexts remain mandatory.
9. **Exact-head reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/utterance/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, delivered accessible artifacts, source/speaker/time/help/event chronology, reviewed target-language oracle, nested-hearsay/modal/negation/pronoun/date/translation/caption/replay/source-version counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot prove a shipped reporting evaluator, reproducible runtime defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently reviewed target-language/variety/register reporting oracles; versioned fictional utterances with speaker, reporter, original/report times and epistemic strength; accessible authoritative and **actually delivered** sources; first-unaided authorship and actual answer-bearing help chronology; delayed first-unaided retrieval and genuinely unseen source/audience transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skills, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and per-assertion verdicts for source attribution, quote/paraphrase, nested hearsay, modal strength, pronoun, date, negation, conflict, accessibility, translation, replay and source-version counterfactuals. Missing original statements, unreliable transcripts, contradictory delivered channels and absent follow-ups remain **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptation **improves durable independent reporting** requires separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners say who said what, how certain it was, what remains unknown and how to report it independently to someone new.**
