# F68 — Metacognitive calibration and strategic help must lead to independent transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced production defect, implemented confidence estimator, validated metacognitive intervention, CEFR certification, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 protects unseen transfer, F35 scaffold fading, F37 assigned-versus-delivered treatment, F50 soft-interest breadth, F51 spontaneous repair, F56 feedback timing, F60 explanation selection, F62 independent production, F65 practice dose and F67 original example generation. **F68 isolates metacognitive calibration and help-seeking:** a learner's confidence, willingness to answer or frequency of requesting help must not be mistaken for independent mastery. The tutor may teach learners to check their own understanding, identify uncertainty and request useful non-answer-bearing support, but must measure whether those choices eventually improve delayed independent language use.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real, versioned** CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, task and communicative function, modality, evaluator/rubric, required semantic/pragmatic criteria, permitted assistance, independent evidence, meaningful delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer, due reviews and level exit gate. Freeze all source-defined demands before comparing instructional options. Do not invent a universal confidence cutoff, a mandatory self-rating, a requirement to answer without any clarification when the CEFR task permits ordinary interactional repair, or a text proxy for unavailable speaking/listening assessment.

Predeclare feasible, reversible candidates `DIRECT_PRACTICE`, `OPTIONAL_PRE_RESPONSE_PREDICTION`, `POST_RESPONSE_EVIDENCE_CHECK`, `GUIDED_ERROR_DIAGNOSIS`, `STRATEGIC_HINT_CHOICE` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. The instructional decision concerns **how to help the learner judge and regulate their learning**, not whether a learner belongs to a confident, anxious, visual or auditory type. The learner may skip optional self-ratings without losing access, progress or credit for otherwise valid independent evidence. Avoid making confidence questions so frequent that they change the construct or burden of the language task.

For each comparable learner/objective history, retain frozen source/rubric versions; available, assigned and actually rendered instructional option; first unaided semantic response; optional pre-answer prediction or confidence and its exact question/time; whether confidence was explicitly supplied, skipped or unavailable; correctness, meaning and pragmatic validity; learner-requested clarification versus tutor-initiated cue; precise content and **delivery** chronology of hints, models and answer-bearing feedback; learner-initiated error checking, retries and revisions; opportunity/exposure/difficulty/topic/modality/accessibility; independent delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer; and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Use session-qualified immutable event IDs, content hashes and trusted event occurrence/delivery order, not upload order. Keep optional self-report ephemeral or minimally scoped; do not infer personality, disability, mental health, motivation or private attributes from hesitation, confidence or help requests.

Observation labels: `CONFIDENCE_REPORTED`, `CONFIDENCE_SKIPPED`, `CONFIDENCE_NOT_OBSERVED`, `OVERCONFIDENT_IMMEDIATE_ONLY`, `UNDERCONFIDENT_INDEPENDENT_SUCCESS`, `SELF_CHECK_INITIATED`, `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, `NON_ANSWER_BEARING_HELP`, `ANSWER_BEARING_CUE_DELIVERED`, `ASSISTED_CORRECTION`, `INDEPENDENT_FIRST_ATTEMPT`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_TRANSFER`, `TREATMENT_NOT_RENDERED`, `FOLLOWUP_MISSING`, `TOPIC_METHOD_CONFOUNDED`, `ACCESSIBILITY_CONFOUNDED`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These describe observations, never permanent learner identities.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Confident recognition is not productive mastery.** Learner A rates confidence highly after recognizing a correct sentence and finishes several assisted multiple-choice items. At a later independent, source-equivalent free-production probe with unfamiliar meaning, A fails the communicative function. Learner B expresses uncertainty during practice yet independently produces and later transfers the target function. Do not use A's confidence or B's self-doubt to override the actual independent outcomes; preserve the distinct receptive and productive evidence gates.
- **B — Useful self-checking versus coached correctness.** Learner A identifies an ambiguous referent in their own first answer, asks for the task condition to be repeated without target-language wording and independently repairs meaning. Learner B requests a model, receives the full target sentence and repeats it flawlessly. Mark the model-assisted revision as supported, not independent. If the frozen can-do permits clarification as part of authentic interaction, score A against that exact rubric; do not categorically penalize legitimate clarification or equate it with an answer-bearing hint.
- **C — Low confidence must not trigger endless scaffolding.** A learner repeatedly reports low confidence but has several valid, unaided first attempts and delayed transfers. A tutor that keeps displaying examples before every attempt would remove opportunities to prove mastery. Another learner reports high confidence yet repeatedly omits required meaning in unseen tasks. Compare reversible support choices using later independent results, not the self-rating, latency or number of requests alone; optional self-rating refusal is not an error.
- **D — Metacognitive instruction can help one objective and hurt another.** Matched source-equivalent histories initially suggest brief post-response evidence checks improve later independent conditional requests, while the same learner's routine introductions improve more under direct practice. Later delayed evidence contradicts the conditional-request advantage. Show uncertainty, balanced exploration, objective-specific choices and an actual reversal. Do not promote one reflective routine into a permanent learner style.
- **E — Familiar topics and accessible response paths distort calibration.** Confidence prompts appear with favorite music examples, while direct practice uses unfamiliar civic situations; another learner uses an accessible input method with different interaction time. Equalize or explicitly disclose context familiarity, language burden, accessibility, prompt frequency and exposure before attributing outcomes to metacognitive coaching. Keep adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics and all required modalities/skills reachable. Do not score unavailable voice timing as evidence of hesitation or low confidence.
- **F — Offline chronology, missing probes and self-report replay.** An optional confidence question is assigned but never renders; a tutor answer is delivered before a revision but uploads later; two sessions reuse the same local attempt ID; a self-rating is resubmitted offline; a delayed probe is offered but not completed; and the evaluator version changes before a follow-up. Reconstruct session-qualified immutable event order, rendered content and cue exposure, deduplicate replay and separate missing from failed follow-up. Both upload-order permutations must yield the same independent-evidence ledger and reversible next choice; unresolved chronology or rubric mismatch fails closed.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Frozen construct and noncompensatory evidence.** For A–F emit actual versioned can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, source task, modality, evaluator, semantic/pragmatic demands, allowed clarification and help, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, unseen transfer, reviews and exit gate. Confidence or help-seeking cannot waive or create independent mastery; unresolved source mappings remain `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Optional, well-timed and non-diagnostic self-report.** In A/C/E/F distinguish confidence supplied before the answer, after feedback, skipped, unrendered and unknown. A rating is not independent performance, personality, disability, motivation or a learning-style label. Skipping must not suppress normal progress, accessibility or independent assessment.
3. **First-attempt and support provenance.** In A/B/C/F separate first unaided semantic performance from retries, clarifications, tutor hints, models, feedback and copied corrections. Determine answer-bearing exposure from actual delivery chronology, not upload order or the tutor's intended treatment.
4. **Source-valid strategic clarification.** In B distinguish asking for a permissible task-condition repeat, checking meaning independently, receiving a non-answer-bearing process cue and receiving the actual answer. Apply the frozen interactional rubric; neither punish authentic repair automatically nor award independent target-form credit after a model supplies it.
5. **Delayed independent calibration outcome.** In A/C/D/F compare predictions and interventions against source-equivalent delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer with meaningful intervening practice/time. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators and the direction of calibration error; immediate confidence shifts, assisted success and incomplete follow-ups are not durable learning gains.
6. **Comparable options and equitable access.** In D/E compare or disclose objective, exposure, difficulty, topic familiarity, available modalities, accessibility, task burden and feedback opportunities. A confidence survey must not create unequal practice dose or prevent required receptive/productive coverage; unavailable modalities stay `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`.
7. **Rendered-treatment and replay-safe reconstruction.** In B/F trace candidate, assigned, rendered, attempted and completed interventions, rendered cue/content hashes, session-qualified IDs and trusted event order. Deduplicate offline replays; alternate receipt orders must yield identical independence labels and next-policy choices. Unverifiable event order and changed rubric versions cannot silently produce a PASS.
8. **Reversible method adaptation with protected breadth.** In C/D/E/F emit feasible alternatives, selected and delivered choice, objective-specific later outcomes, uncertainty, contradictions, exploratory sampling and next reversible choice. Maintain due reviews, independent production, modality balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts. Interest signals affect context probabilities, never mastery or method efficacy directly.
9. **Assertion-level verdicts without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/rubric IDs, optional-rating status/timing, first/final semantic outcomes, help and cue chronology, rendered-treatment status, delayed/unseen results, follow-up denominators, confounds, skill/topic/review debt, confidence in the *policy hypothesis*, replay equivalence and per-assertion `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. A documentation QA pass is not a passing executable tutor test or evidence of improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command; deterministic seeded multi-session histories and controlled clock; actual source-resolved CEFR tasks/rubrics; optional and accurately timestamped self-report events; first unaided semantic scoring; authentic clarification and tutor cue delivery traces; rendered intervention evidence; source-equivalent independent delayed and unseen probes; follow-up denominators; matched instructional opportunities or declared confounds; protected reviews/skills/topic coverage; replay-order permutations; reversible policy traces; and assertion-level verdicts. If the tutor lacks reliable self-report, evaluator, source mapping, rendered events or follow-up, mark the corresponding assertion `UNSUPPORTED` or unknown rather than inventing evidence.

A claim that metacognitive coaching **improves durable independent retention or transfer** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, sufficient independent delayed/unseen outcomes, privacy and accessibility review, subgroup harm checks and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners recognize what they can do without help; it never treats feeling sure, asking for help or refusing a confidence question as a substitute for doing it.**
