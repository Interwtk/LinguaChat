# F89 — Contrastive focus, corrective emphasis, alternative selection and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. This is not a shipped focus/prosody evaluator, a CEFR award, a reproduced production defect, proof of real-learner gains or a requirement that every language marks focus in the same way. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 distinguish assigned from actually rendered instruction and answer-bearing tutor cues; F47/F48 preserve noncompensatory CEFR evidence; F49 protects accessibility; F50 keeps interests soft; F53 validates variety/register; F54 tests repair; F71 tracks discourse referents; F73 pragmatic inference; F74 attribution; F79 shared understanding; F81 negation scope; F82 time; F86 evidence; F87 comparison. **Distinct construct:** independently identifying and communicating **which part of a proposition is being contrasted, corrected or selected among alternatives**, and producing a response that repairs the intended interlocutor misunderstanding. Correct words with misplaced focus can lead to the wrong person, item, time or action being selected. A generic correct/incorrect sentence classification or a focus-free transcription cannot establish this outcome. Apply only when a **real versioned CEFR descriptor/can-do and source-authored communicative task** license the construct; otherwise `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Right proposition, wrong corrected person.** Source-authored exchange: “Did **Mara** send the invitation?” / “No, **Lina** sent it.” The actual sender is Lina. Learner A says “Mara sent the *invitation*, not the reminder,” preserving an object contrast but failing the requested agent correction; learner B independently identifies the agent contrast and informs the addressee that Lina, not Mara, sent it. A later exchange contrasts *what* Lina sent rather than *who* sent it. Both must shift focus without changing source facts. Typography here describes the synthetic contrast; it is not a required target-language marking strategy.
- **B — Same words, different alternative set.** Source-authored dialogue contrasts “I ordered **tea**, not coffee” with “**I** ordered tea; my colleague ordered coffee.” Learner A selects the beverage correction for both; learner B independently distinguishes item contrast from agent contrast and chooses the appropriate recipient action. A language with optional or absent stress marking may realize the distinction through context, particles, word order or an explicit reformulation. Where the source does not disambiguate, clarification is correct; invented certainty fails.
- **C — Negation, corrective focus and partial agreement.** Source says “We didn't cancel the **morning** workshop; we moved it to the afternoon,” with an independently verified new schedule. Learner A reports that the workshop was canceled or that *nothing* changed; learner B preserves the continued event, time contrast and corrective intent. Later, “not only Tuesday” must not be paraphrased as “not Tuesday.” Test scope and real addressee understanding, not isolated negation-token recognition.
- **D — Repair under channel and accessibility constraints.** A recorded instruction distinguishes the **blue** folder from the **green** one using contrastive prominence; a caption omits the prominence and does not otherwise disambiguate the referent. Learner A guesses; learner B asks which folder is intended when using captions, then independently completes the selection once accessible source context is supplied. A text-first alternative uses an explicit validated lexical correction. Missing acoustic information cannot be scored as a failed text-only comprehension or as proof of oral proficiency; unavailable listening/speaking remains unproven.
- **E — Immediate coached success versus delayed independent reversal.** In matched sessions, `CONTRASTIVE_PAIR_RECAST` produces more immediately correct but model-dependent corrections than `ALTERNATIVE_SET_DIALOGUE`. On later separate sessions with novel speakers, source facts and contrast targets, the dialogue condition yields stronger first-try unaided corrections and successful addressee selections. Compare or disclose baseline, task difficulty, modality, register, variety, feedback, practice dose, topic familiarity and accessibility. Decrease confidence in the immediate winner, retain alternative sampling, and record the **actually rendered** next method rather than declaring a permanent “dialogue learner.”
- **F — Interest bias, missing probe and reordered tutor cue.** A learner repairs focus in familiar gaming chat but misdirects a neutral community schedule and an unfamiliar workplace handoff. An audio-only prompt is inaccessible; a delayed probe is eligible and offered but never rendered. Two genuine sessions reuse an offline local event ID; an answer-bearing highlighted alternative was rendered before the learner response but uploaded later, while one offline record has a conflicting rubric version. Deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, quarantine unresolved versions, reconstruct trusted render order and preserve the same assistance labels, review debt, context rotation and reversible next-method rationale under receipt-order permutations. Written performance never certifies oral/listening evidence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid CEFR applicability.** A–F require a real versioned can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/varieties, source-authored alternatives and intended contrast, task outcome, validated rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, threshold, required skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing or ambiguous source focus returns `UNSUPPORTED`/`NOT_APPLICABLE`, never invented proficiency.
2. **Proposition and focus are separately scored.** A/B/C preserve actual facts while correctly identifying the focused agent, item or time. A true sentence that corrects the wrong dimension fails the task-specific communicative assertion; a correct focus with false source facts also fails.
3. **Alternative-set and scope fidelity.** B/C distinguish what is contrasted, what is retained and what is negated. Reject unlicensed alternatives, canceled-versus-rescheduled collapse, and “not only”/“not” confusion. Validate natural target-language and variety-specific strategies instead of literal English emphasis.
4. **Interlocutor outcome and repair.** A/C/D require source-defined recipient selection, revised instruction or explicit appropriate clarification when focus is genuinely underdetermined. Do not reward confident guessing or polite fluency that produces the wrong action; a later source correction is versioned rather than retroactive proof.
5. **First independent move and actual cue exposure.** D/F use the first learner-authored contrast and trusted render chronology. A tutor-provided corrected alternative, highlighted focus or model reply rendered beforehand marks that move assisted; selected-but-never-rendered instruction does not. Keep legitimate task prompts separate from coaching leakage.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer.** C/E/F require separate real sessions, new speakers/contexts/alternative sets, first unaided focus interpretation or production and observable source-defined outcome. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Same-session rephrasing, answer repetition, missing probes and assisted repairs cannot establish durable mastery.
7. **Comparable methods, accessibility and soft interests.** D/E/F match or disclose source ambiguity, difficulty, variety/register, modality, feedback, dose, accessibility and topic familiarity. Preserve CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation. Interests adjust probabilities, never exclude required contexts or skills.
8. **Reversible longitudinal decisions and replay invariance.** E/F record feasible options, selected/assigned/**actually rendered** instruction, later independent outcomes, uncertainty, confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicting data use `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified deduplication, frozen versions and trusted render chronology produce receipt-order-invariant assistance, focus interpretation, review debt and next choice; unresolved conflicts are quarantined.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** A–F emit exact source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen propositions and alternatives, first/final learner-authored moves, rendered cues, recipient outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is neither executable evaluator PASS nor evidence of human learning gains.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic clock/seed, multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked tasks, validated language/variety-specific focus rubrics, versioned propositions and alternative sets, trusted cue render chronology, first unaided contrast/correction, recipient outcomes, completed delayed genuinely unseen transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skill and review obligations, receipt-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, reliable contrast signal, accessible channel, completed independent follow-up or trustworthy chronology remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

Any claim that adaptation **improves durable independent corrective communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen tasks, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners correct exactly the misunderstanding that matters—not merely say a fluent sentence with the right vocabulary.**
