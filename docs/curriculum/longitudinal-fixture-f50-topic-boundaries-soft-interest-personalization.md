# F50 — Topic boundaries constrain examples, not CEFR learning obligations

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped boundary-preference feature, implemented safety system, verified CEFR assessment, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F49 distinguishes accessibility support from a change in the language construct. F50 isolates **learner topic boundaries versus soft interest weights**: a learner can decline a topic, change their mind or stop a conversation without losing access to the same required language objective. The tutor should offer a genuinely equivalent, acceptable context rather than pressure the learner, silently resurrect excluded content, or pretend that skipping a topic waives CEFR evidence. Explicit boundaries are not inferred learning styles, diagnoses or proof that an instructional method works.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a real, versioned source CEFR can-do and blueprint before constructing each test. Freeze communicative intent, prerequisites, rubric/evaluator, independent production/reception requirements, delayed retrieval, transfer, review debt and exit gates. Predeclare a set of source-valid topic realizations with comparable language load, task novelty, response burden and evaluation difficulty; when no equivalently assessable safe realization exists, report `UNSUPPORTED`/a pending objective and request curricular review, not a fabricated pass or coerced exposure.

Use an injected clock and seed, immutable synthetic learner events across genuinely separate sessions, a balanced topic sampler and independent instructional-option policy. Separate **explicitly stated topic boundary**, **current conversation consent**, **interest evidence**, **instructional-method hypothesis**, **objective mastery**, **topic availability** and **retention outcome**. A boundary applies to content selection, not automatically to the linguistic construct; a content-safety restriction may override an interest boost. Distinguish learner-declared exclusions from speculative inferences based on silence, response speed, performance, inferred identity or emotion. Do not store inferred diagnoses, trauma narratives or sensitive identity labels in the curriculum ledger.

Record pseudonymous learner, level-qualified can-do and rubric versions, topic-family and realization IDs, consent/boundary event with scope and trusted timestamp, offered/selected/delivered context, neutral fallback, exposure/skip reason, source-valid difficulty and evaluation mapping, activity/run/event IDs, first independent attempt, model/hint/retry exposure, later independent retrieval and unseen transfer, eligibility/completion/missing follow-up, exploration opportunity, review debt, and policy/version lineage. Minimize sensitive boundary text: a scoped category or opaque exclusion identifier should be sufficient for replay; preserve user agency to revise or clear it under applicable privacy controls.

Predeclare `TOPIC_DECLINED`, `BOUNDARY_ACTIVE`, `BOUNDARY_REVOKED`, `SAFE_EQUIVALENT_SUBSTITUTION`, `NEUTRAL_FALLBACK`, `CONTEXT_NOT_EQUIVALENT`, `OBJECTIVE_PENDING`, `TOPIC_EXPOSURE_UNKNOWN`, `INTEREST_SOFT_WEIGHT`, `CONTEXT_ROTATION_DUE`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Missing evidence is neither a positive preference nor an independent pass.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Explicit topic decline, unchanged language objective.** During practice for a source-required can-do about making and justifying a choice, a learner explicitly declines a proposed family-related scenario. The tutor acknowledges the boundary without requesting an explanation and offers an equivalent neutral scenario (e.g. choosing between community activities), preserving the same required communicative moves, linguistic burden, evaluator and future independent probe. The declined example never reappears through a synonym, adjacent prompt, review or automated recommendation while its boundary remains active. Declining the scenario does not count as failure, mastery or a reason to omit the can-do.
- **B — Enthusiastic interest without an exclusive filter.** A learner repeatedly chooses music and shows engagement, while the tutor has a source-valid set of music, adjacent arts, neutral daily-life and unfamiliar civic contexts for the same objective. Music receives a soft probability boost, but multi-session sampling still offers the other eligible contexts and independently tests transfer outside music. A high short-term score on familiar vocabulary does not prove the music context or a teaching method improved durable learning; no universal topic quota is invented.
- **C — A boundary changes, and the latest explicit choice wins.** A learner initially excludes sports, later explicitly opts back into sports examples for a limited conversation, then declines a specific competitive-sports scenario. Respect scope, timestamp and specificity: the narrow opt-in does not erase the later narrow opt-out, nor does it grant perpetual global consent. Reopening a prior session, resuming offline work or replaying an old recommendation must not override the newer applicable boundary. Do not silently treat an inferred interest as permission.
- **D — Topic familiarity is confounded with teaching method.** A tutor tries a concise explanation on a familiar game context and a worked example on an unfamiliar transport context. The learner answers the first faster, then needs a hint on the second. These unequal contexts cannot establish that concise explanation is superior. Compare source-equivalent difficulty, rotate or counterbalance contexts and explanation methods, and wait for repeated later independent retrieval and unseen-context transfer before updating a method hypothesis. Preference for the topic is not evidence of method benefit.
- **E — No equivalent acceptable topic currently available.** A required role-play objective is mapped only to an excluded scenario in the current catalog; an alternative would remove essential negotiation or production demands. Do not repackage the excluded scenario, secretly change the rubric, award phantom evidence, or force participation. Offer a nonpunitive pause and request an authored, source-valid neutral alternative. Keep the required objective and any due independent/review gate visibly pending; `UNAVAILABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED` is not learner failure.
- **F — Concurrent updates, offline replay and boundary leakage.** An old interest event and cached excluded-topic prompt arrive after a newer boundary; a boundary update is uploaded twice; an out-of-order revocation is scoped only to one conversation; a recommendation was selected before the boundary but not delivered until afterward; and a stale rubric version is present. Deduplicate stable event IDs, apply trusted chronological/scope semantics, revalidate **at actual delivery**, quarantine unsupported policy/rubric migrations and preserve the unchanged objective ledger. Both replay orders yield identical allowed contexts, recorded exposures, review debt and independent-evidence eligibility. A previously delivered excluded topic is not retroactively erased from provenance, but cannot authorize future delivery.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source and construct are fixed.** For A–F output exact source CEFR blueprint, level-qualified can-do, prerequisites, evaluator/rubric versions, source-required independent/review/transfer/exit gates, and the mapping showing why each offered substitute preserves communicative intent, difficulty and response demands. Unsupported equivalence is `UNSUPPORTED`, not a guessed pass.
2. **Explicit boundaries govern delivery.** In A, an applicable learner-declared decline prevents delivery or resurfacing of that topic through recommendations, hints, reviews, synonyms or related examples within the stated scope. The learner need not disclose a reason. An unrequested boundary cannot be invented from latency, a wrong answer or a sensitive inferred attribute.
3. **Same objective, respectful alternative.** In A, the offered and delivered neutral substitution retains the unchanged CEFR objective, evaluator, evidence standard and genuine later independent transfer. The refusal itself is neither evidence of low ability nor grounds to waive the objective.
4. **Interest remains probabilistic.** In B, demonstrated/onboarding interests can increase eligible topic selection without becoming a hard exclusive filter. Audit adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts across multiple sessions and preserve required skill-family opportunities; no fixed universal selection percentage is asserted.
5. **Scope, revision and agency.** In C, later explicit scoped opt-in/opt-out choices supersede only the relevant earlier choices; opt-in is not indefinite global permission. Respect a learner's choice to pause or choose a neutral example, without coercion, punishment or permanent profiling.
6. **Do not confuse topic and method effects.** In D, unequal familiarity/difficulty, hints and immediate success cannot certify an instructional method. Keep topic and teaching-option assignments independently traceable, sample comparable alternatives, retain uncertainty and use repeated later unaided retrieval/novel transfer before attributing method benefit.
7. **Unavailable substitution does not create mastery.** In E, if no source-equivalent acceptable context exists, do not pressure the learner, replace a required language construct, hide review debt or declare graduation. Expose `OBJECTIVE_PENDING` with `UNAVAILABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED` and an authored-alternative requirement.
8. **Deterministic replay and delivery-time protection.** In F, duplicate/out-of-order events, cached prompts and offline resume must preserve latest applicable scope, stable provenance, source versions and identical objective ledgers in both replay orders. Revalidate boundary at delivery, not just at recommendation; quarantine stale or ambiguous versions instead of leaking excluded content.
9. **Evidence and release honesty.** Across A–F report allowed/declined/offered/delivered topic families, balanced context coverage, accessible feasible alternatives, source-qualified independent first attempts, assistance, eligible/completed/missing delayed probes, unseen transfer and due reviews. A synthetic PASS only verifies an executable invariant on the exact engine HEAD; it does not prove user safety, longitudinal learning improvement, implemented feature coverage or permission to open CEFR levels.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, injected clock/seed, immutable events and both replay orders, source CEFR blueprint and rubric/evaluator versions, boundary scopes and effective timestamps without sensitive narratives, proposed-versus-actually-delivered contexts, objective-equivalence matrix, interest weights and nonexclusive sampling trace, explanation/scaffold choices, independent and assisted first-attempt provenance, delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer with eligible/completed/missing denominators, reason codes and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion.

Any claim that respecting topic boundaries while adapting instruction improves durable learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal independent retention/transfer against a balanced comparator and ethically reviewed learner evaluation. **The learner controls unacceptable contexts; the curriculum retains the shared language goals.** Documentation alone neither implements a boundary-aware sampler nor certifies CEFR mastery.
