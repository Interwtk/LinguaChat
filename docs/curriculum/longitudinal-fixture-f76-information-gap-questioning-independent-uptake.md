# F76 — Strategic information-gap questioning, answer uptake and independent task transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a shipped question-quality evaluator, autonomous dialogue agent, CEFR certification, reproduced production defect or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 guards genuinely unseen transfer; F38 preserves tutor-origin attribution; F47/F48 preserve source-defined noncompensatory CEFR evidence; F49 protects accessibility construct equivalence; F50 protects topic breadth; F53 preserves register and legitimate variation; F54 tests repair after an actual communication breakdown; F58 protects learner initiative; F62 distinguishes recognition from independent production; F66 varies interlocutors; F68 tests calibrated uncertainty; F69 prioritizes feedback; F72 tracks communicative goals and constraints; F73 tests implicit intent; F74 protects source fidelity; F75 tests reasoned argument. **F76 isolates learner-initiated information seeking and subsequent use of the answer:** producing a grammatically polished question, waiting for a tutor prompt, or obtaining an answer does not establish that the learner independently identified the information gap, asked a context-appropriate question and used the reply to advance the source-defined communicative goal. Unlike F54, an information gap may exist *before* any misunderstanding or breakdown. Apply this construct **only** when a real versioned CEFR-linked task actually requires inquiry, clarification, information exchange or a decision informed by a reply. Never add compulsory questions, a fixed question count or an advanced interview burden to unrelated can-dos.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real, versioned** CEFR can-do/descriptor, curriculum blueprint, prerequisites, task and source packet, communicative goal, known and missing information, what the learner and interlocutor can each legitimately know, information-access permissions, source-defined required and optional questions, acceptable question formulations and varieties, audience/role relationship, expected register, response protocol, actual interaction channel and supported modalities, evaluator/rubric, assistance rules, independent evidence threshold, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer, due reviews and level exit gate. Freeze the information distribution and task difficulty before comparing methods. A source-defined task may call for a single simple question, a multi-step inquiry or no question at all; do not infer a universal interrogative grammar or number of turns. Mark ambiguous or nonadjudicable requirements **UNSUPPORTED**, rather than inventing a hidden correct question.

Predeclare reversible instructional candidates `GAP_MAP_THEN_ASK`, `CONTRASTIVE_QUESTION_MODELS`, `QUESTION_AND_UPTAKE_ROLEPLAY`, `LISTEN_OR_READ_REPLY_THEN_ACT`, `OPEN_UNSEEN_INFORMATION_EXCHANGE` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. They may vary explanations, activity order, supported listening/reading/writing/speaking mix, scaffolding and its fade, pacing, review spacing and examples while keeping fixed the source-defined can-do, information burden, interlocutor rules and independent mastery standard. A tutor's suggested question, prefilled form, translation, answer-bearing hint, recommended decision or model follow-up is **assistance** for the affected target. An ordinary interlocutor's source-permitted answer to a genuinely learner-authored question is **task information**, not automatically tutoring; a response that coaches the next question or decision must be separately tagged. Preserve reasonable alternative-method sampling; never classify the learner as a permanent “inquisitive,” “visual” or “auditory” type.

Use deterministic pseudonymous multi-session histories with injected seed/clock and immutable session-qualified event IDs. Record objective, blueprint, source, rubric and evaluator versions; information-state snapshots and source truth/acceptable uncertainty; learner/interlocutor knowledge boundaries; goal, role, channel, language variety, topic familiarity and accessibility; **first learner-authored** question, whether it targets a consequential gap, clarity/register and semantic sufficiency; interlocutor's exact reply, source-supported facts and ambiguity; learner's acknowledgment, follow-up, answer integration and independent final outcome; selected/assigned/**actually rendered** instruction; exact tutor suggestions, models, translations, captions and other cue-delivery chronology; retries and scaffold dependence; delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen interlocutor/context/answer-pattern transfer; and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Do not infer hesitation from asynchronous message timestamps, listening from a text transcript, private knowledge from a profile, or question quality from punctuation alone.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Elegant question, wrong information gap.** In a source-defined task of choosing a suitable appointment, a learner asks a polished question about the venue's decor while availability and accessibility remain unknown; another uses a simple but intelligible source-valid question about the relevant appointment constraint. Both receive ordinary interlocutor answers. Score question grammaticality/register separately from relevance to the required gap, information gained and independent task completion. Do not punish a valid simpler form or reward sophisticated but immaterial syntax. If decor is genuinely required by the frozen task, it is not automatically irrelevant.
- **B — Asking is not using the answer.** A learner independently asks whether a meeting starts before a fixed deadline, receives a clear “after the deadline,” then selects it anyway. Another asks an equally adequate question, incorporates the reply and chooses a source-valid alternative. A third receives an ambiguous answer and asks a permissible targeted follow-up before deciding. Keep inquiry, reply comprehension, justified uncertainty, follow-up and decision outcome distinct. An information-gathering task with no required decision must not be graded against an invented downstream choice.
- **C — Tutor prompts contaminate apparent initiative.** Before the first question, the tutor highlights the missing detail and displays “Ask what time it closes”; the learner repeats the question and completes the task after an answer-bearing decision hint. Another learner initially fails to ask, practices with scaffolds, then independently identifies a *new* information gap and uses an ordinary interlocutor reply in a meaningfully delayed source-equivalent task. Preserve exact rendered question and decision cues, first attempt, coached revision and later unaided outcome; tutor-elicited questions cannot prove independent gap identification.
- **D — Method advantage reverses under delayed unseen transfer.** Matched early independent probes suggest `GAP_MAP_THEN_ASK` helps learner A more than `QUESTION_AND_UPTAKE_ROLEPLAY`, while B shows the reverse. Later delayed probes with a new interlocutor and different but source-equivalent information distribution reverse A's apparent advantage. Match or disclose number and consequentiality of unknowns, answer ambiguity, response length, role power, topic familiarity, language burden, modality, practice dose, feedback exposure and evaluator versions. Reduce confidence, sample alternatives and log an **actually rendered** changed next instruction, not merely an assigned policy choice.
- **E — Favorite-topic fluency and unsupported channels.** A learner repeatedly asks accurate questions in favorite-game contexts but omits essential details in neutral and unfamiliar service encounters. A text-only simulation returns typed replies while an oral comprehension/speaking route is unavailable; a source-permitted accessibility accommodation displays equivalent task information without revealing the correct question. Rotate favorite, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts, protect due reviews and required skill families, and separate accessibility from answer-bearing coaching. Written success must not silently certify listening or speaking.
- **F — Reordered replies, coaching leakage and missing follow-up.** Two offline sessions reuse a local turn ID; a tutor's suggested question is displayed before a revision but uploaded after it; an interlocutor reply is edited while retaining its cached response ID; a caption leaks the answer to one route; a delayed probe is offered but never completed; and a rubric revision changes whether the final decision is required. Reconstruct from immutable session-qualified IDs, source/question/reply hashes, trusted display chronology and frozen rubric versions. Deduplicate replay, quarantine irreconcilable edits and coaching leakage, keep missing follow-up unknown, and require identical independent/assisted labels, answer-uptake verdicts and next-choice traces across receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-resolved inquiry burden.** For A–F emit the real versioned CEFR-linked can-do, blueprint, task, goal, information distribution, permitted question/answer variants, role/channel, rubric, assistance, independent standard, delayed/unseen probes, reviews and exit gate. Missing source validation or an invented compulsory question/decision yields `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Question form is not question utility.** In A/B emit first independent question, language clarity, source-defined register, targeted information gap, semantic sufficiency and task relevance separately. Surface polish or interrogative punctuation cannot compensate for a required missing information move; valid paraphrases, direct/indirect questions and language varieties remain eligible.
3. **Answer uptake and uncertainty are separate gates.** In B/F report exact source-supported reply, learner interpretation, permissible follow-up, information-state update and source-required final outcome. A question alone does not establish comprehension or use; a source-valid clarifying follow-up is not an error, and unsupported or ambiguous replies do not justify fabricated certainty.
4. **Task information versus tutoring.** In B/C/F distinguish ordinary interlocutor facts supplied under the frozen task protocol from tutor-provided question templates, translations, answer-bearing prompts and coached decisions. Track actual rendering and first learner-authored attempt; a reply containing unauthorized coaching is contaminated rather than silently counted as independent.
5. **Independent delayed unseen inquiry is decisive.** In C/D/F require meaningfully separate sessions, intervening time/practice and genuinely new source-equivalent information gaps, interlocutors or reply patterns. Emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Same-template repeats, immediate polished questions and absent follow-ups do not prove durable independent inquiry and answer uptake.
6. **Role, language and channel remain source-qualified.** In A/B/E preserve source-defined relationship, question directness, register, permissible alternatives, modality and accessibility. Do not demand an English-centric question form, impose stereotypes about social roles or convert written exchanges into oral proficiency. Missing audio or unvalidated response equivalence is `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED`.
7. **Comparable instruction and topic breadth.** In D/E match or disclose gap complexity, answer ambiguity, prior knowledge, topic familiarity, role/interlocutor, task dose, cue exposure, access and opportunity. Preserve CEFR objectives, noncompensatory skill evidence, due reviews and favorite/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation; interest weights never filter required contexts or lower mastery thresholds.
8. **Reversible uncertainty and replay invariance.** In C/D/E/F emit feasible candidates, selected/assigned/actually rendered instruction, independent delayed outcomes, uncertainty, contradictory evidence, exploration and next choice. Sparse/confounded evidence falls back to `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. In F, event and content hashes, trusted delivery chronology, deduplication, version handling and arrival-order permutations must preserve question independence, answer uptake, follow-up missingness, review debt and next-choice rationale; unresolved mismatch fails closed.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/rubric IDs, information-state and role snapshots, first/final independent/assisted questions, reply provenance, learner uptake and source-required outcome, rendered cues, delayed/unseen denominators, method/topic/access confounds, skill/review debt, replay equivalence, policy uncertainty and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is not a shipped dialogue evaluator, CEFR certification or a human-learning study.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command; deterministic multi-session seed/clock; real source-resolved CEFR inquiry tasks and language-aware validated rubrics; frozen consequential information gaps, roles, permissible questions and reply truth; learner-authored first question and actual reply uptake; trusted tutor/interlocutor cue provenance; meaningfully delayed unseen independent information exchange with follow-up denominators; comparable instruction/context/access exposure; required skill and review protection; replay-order permutations; reversible next-instruction traces; and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, response adjudication, actual oral modality, trusted chronology, question/reply versions or follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown, never invented competence.

A claim that instruction **improves durable independent information-seeking communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, independently completed delayed unseen exchanges, multilingual evaluator validation and human adjudication, privacy/accessibility and subgroup-harm review, and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners notice what they need to know, ask for it and use the reply themselves—not simply repeat a supplied question.**
