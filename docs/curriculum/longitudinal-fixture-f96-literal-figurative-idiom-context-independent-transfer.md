## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Same words, different context.** In an English-language task with source-validated readings, an outdoor instruction describes physically breaking ice on a frozen surface; a separate meeting invitation says an activity should "break the ice" among strangers. Learner A interprets both as physically smashing ice. Learner B independently paraphrases the second as helping people become comfortable speaking, but keeps the first literal. A later genuinely unseen target-language pair changes both surface expression and domain; no score is awarded for merely spotting the familiar phrase. If the target language lacks this expression, author equivalent **new source-valid target-language tasks**, not an English calque.
- **B — Figurative reading without invented consequences.** A source-authored colleague says "We're on the same page about the plan" after agreeing on priorities; a separate document discussion literally refers to two readers viewing the same page. Learner A reports that the colleague signed a contract and the document was finalized, although neither is stated. Learner B conveys shared understanding while preserving that formal approval remains unknown, and identifies the literal reading in the document context. An unseen handoff tests a different conventional expression with the same distinction between communicated meaning and unsupported extra facts.
- **C — Target-language variety and translation trap.** A bilingual learner encounters a verified conventional expression in one supported regional variety, then a formally similar expression in another variety for which the intended reading is different or not validated. Learner A uses a word-for-word first-language gloss as if it were a universal idiom. Learner B checks source context, paraphrases an accepted meaning where documented, and asks a bounded clarification where the variety/meaning is unresolved. Score source-validated target-language comprehension, not the learner's native-language translation speed or presumed cultural membership.
- **D — Legitimate ambiguity, register and accessibility.** An accessible audio exchange uses a familiar expression with a tone that supports a figurative reading, but a transcript alone leaves two plausible readings; a second source provides an explicit literal action. Learner A guesses a figurative meaning for both and receives an apparently fluent score. Learner B asks the interlocutor a source-permitted clarification for the ambiguous transcript and correctly responds to the literal request. If the decisive prosody is missing, captioned audio is inaccurate, or the expression is unsuitable for the specified register, mark the evidence confounded or accept the predeclared alternatives rather than punishing the learner.
- **E — Immediate memorization loses to delayed independent inference.** Across matched early sessions, LITERAL_FIGURATIVE_PAIRING produces fast correct choices after visible answer-bearing examples; CONTEXT_CONTRAST_INFERENCE yields slower but independent paraphrases. On separate later sessions with genuinely unseen expressions, changed topic/variety-validated context and a real recipient decision, contextual inference produces stronger first unaided fidelity and fewer invented implications for this learner. Match or disclose baseline proficiency, idiom frequency, source difficulty, cultural familiarity, practice dose, feedback, modality/accessibility, register and topic. Prefer delayed unseen independent outcomes over assisted recognition, lower confidence when evidence conflicts, retain exploration and record the **actually rendered** next method; never label a permanent "idiom learner".
- **F — Interest overfit, late cue and event conflict.** A learner succeeds with conventional expressions in favorite gaming conversations but struggles with a neutral workplace update and unfamiliar public-service dialogue. A tutor-selected explanatory gloss was **rendered** before the first response but uploaded after it; another gloss was selected but never rendered. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; a source correction changes the expression's context; a later rubric version recognizes an additional legitimate regional reading; an eligible delayed probe was offered but never completed. Reconstruct trusted render chronology, deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, quarantine unresolved version conflicts and preserve unknown follow-up, review debt, soft-interest rotation and reversible method rationale across receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real source and CEFR applicability.** For A–F emit the real versioned descriptor/can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/variety, source-authored communicative task, intended recipient, expression/context and validated readings, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, threshold, required skills, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Unsupported idiom/variety or missing source mapping is UNSUPPORTED/NOT_APPLICABLE, never fabricated proficiency.
2. **Literal versus conventional meaning.** A/B/D distinguish source-supported literal and figurative readings for the same or related wording, with context and register. A correct phrase label without a faithful recipient-facing paraphrase is insufficient; literal use must not be wrongly treated as idiomatic.
3. **Fidelity without invented implications.** B/C/D assess whether the learner conveys the intended meaning, uncertainty and recipient action without inventing agreement, commitment, approval, intent or completed events not in the source. Accept meaning-equivalent validated paraphrases rather than requiring exact wording or a single English gloss.
4. **Variety, ambiguity and appropriate clarification.** C/D/F honor region/variety-validated conventional readings and genuinely ambiguous contexts. Source-permitted clarification can be more accurate than an unsupported guess; cultural familiarity, prosody or translation traps must not be converted into a false deficit.
5. **First independent move and actually rendered cues.** A/D/F score the first learner-authored recipient-facing interpretation/clarification against trusted render chronology. A tutor-provided meaning, worked paraphrase or answer-bearing gloss rendered before it contaminates the affected move; selected-but-unrendered cues do not. Distinguish legitimate source-authored stimuli from interlocutor coaching leaks.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer.** A–F require distinct real sessions, new expressions/context contrasts, first unaided responses and observable recipient outcomes. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Repeated phrases, copied glosses, assisted repairs and uncompleted probes do not establish durable mastery.
7. **Comparable instruction, accessibility and soft interests.** D/E/F match or disclose idiom familiarity/frequency, target variety, context load, source ambiguity, register, practice dose, modality, accessibility, feedback and preferred versus unfamiliar topic exposure. Preserve shared CEFR goals, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and broad topic rotation; do not punish missing audio/prosody or unsupported cultural knowledge.
8. **Reversible adaptation and event-order invariance.** E/F log feasible options, assigned/selected/**actually rendered** instruction, later independent outcomes, uncertainty, confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicted evidence falls back to BALANCED_DEFAULT. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen versions and trusted render chronology must yield identical assistance labels, verdicts, review debt and next method across receipt permutations; quarantine conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit, no efficacy inflation.** A–F emit exact source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, validated reading/context/variety, first/final learner utterances, rendered cues, recipient outcomes, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Documentation CI is not executable evaluator PASS, a CEFR award or evidence that adaptation improved real learners' retention.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, real CEFR-linked source-authored figurative-language tasks, validated target-language/variety readings, frozen contexts and rubrics, trusted cue render chronology, first unaided recipient-facing moves, observable recipient outcomes, completed delayed genuinely unseen transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skills/reviews, receipt-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source validity, accessible channel, accepted reading, trusted chronology or independent follow-up remains UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

Any claim that adaptation **improves durable independent interpretation and use of figurative language** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners understand what an expression means here, communicate it faithfully, and recognize when clarification is better than guessing.**
