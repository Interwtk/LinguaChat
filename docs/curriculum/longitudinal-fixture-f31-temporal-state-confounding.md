# F31 — Temporal learner-state confounding cannot masquerade as instructional-method efficacy

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named engine HEAD. This is not a reproduced defect, implemented adaptive-tutor capability, causal finding, or evidence of improved learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F24 guards against policy selection bias, F27 against latency observation bias, F29 against overlapping instructional carryover, and F30 against assessment-calibration drift. F31 addresses a distinct threat: **time-varying learner state and secular learning trends**. A teaching option may be disproportionately offered when a learner is newer, tired, interrupted, recently practised, returning after a break, or already improving for reasons unrelated to that option. Later independent success or failure cannot automatically be attributed to whichever method happened to coincide with that period.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to one existing versioned CEFR can-do, one supported skill and genuinely independent assessment routes. Define two eligible instructional options, `concise_explanation` (A) and `worked_example` (B). Hold objective, prerequisites, task demand, modality availability, scoring instrument/rubric version, mastery threshold, review obligations and required skill coverage constant. Use matched independent anchor probes and unfamiliar-context transfer where actually supported. Keep topic-interest sampling a separate soft layer.

Inject a deterministic clock and seed. Use an append-only synthetic ledger containing learner/objective/skill; event, run and session IDs; **event occurrence versus upload time**; option eligibility, assignment/offer/completion and assignment probability **only when recorded**; instructional exposure IDs/order; assessment instrument/item/rubric versions; first attempt and assistance provenance; independent retrieval and unseen transfer; actual delay and intervening practice; pre-exposure baseline; recent practice load and elapsed time since prior study when observed; interruption/return-to-session flags and known fatigue or accessibility context **only when explicitly and appropriately observed**; missingness/reason codes; and policy version. Do not infer fatigue from a slow answer, fabricate a baseline or derive a causal treatment effect from timestamps alone.

Predeclare a versioned *temporal-comparability policy*: distinguish observed within-window comparable evidence from between-period comparisons confounded by ability growth, practice opportunity, session state or missing provenance. If state or baseline data are absent, preserve independently valid can-do observations but mark **method efficacy** `CONFOUNDED`, `NOT_COMPARABLE` or `UNSUPPORTED` as appropriate. No universal washout duration, fatigue score, clock-time threshold or adjustment weight is assumed.

## Synthetic histories

- **A — Late-method illusion.** A is mostly offered during early novice sessions; B appears after several weeks of general practice and successful independent retrieval. Raw B scores exceed A. Without comparable baselines or time-balanced allocation, the policy must not conclude B is superior; report time/practice imbalance and uncertainty.
- **B — Rested versus interrupted sessions.** A is completed in uninterrupted sessions; B is assigned during sessions with observed interruptions and reduced opportunity to finish. B has fewer completed probes. Distinguish offered, completed and assessed opportunities; an interrupted or missing follow-up is not a failed independent attempt. Do not infer a stable learner trait from temporary state.
- **C — Practice-driven improvement without method change.** A and B exposure frequencies remain fixed, while repeated independent practice, intervening review and task familiarity increase over time. A later improvement must not be credited to the method more common in the final period. Keep independent can-do evidence valid while classifying method attribution as temporally confounded.
- **D — Time-balanced anchored comparison.** Use repeated, eligible A-only and B-only occasions interleaved across comparable periods, with matched pre-exposure baseline, assessment calibration, opportunity, assistance status and documented intervening practice. Include genuinely delayed independent anchor probes and unseen-context transfer. These observations may support a **provisional** uncertainty-aware comparison, while unmatched periods stay excluded. Do not manufacture randomization or claim causal identification if assignment was not actually controlled.
- **E — Changing benefit and reversal.** Early comparable independent delayed outcomes favor A for the target skill; later comparable observations after a documented learner-state change favor B, with a contradictory outlier in each period. The hypothesis may change over time and confidence must respond to contradictory evidence. Neither an early winner nor a temporary slump may create a permanent learner-type label or erase required review/skill coverage.
- **F — Offline replay and unknown state.** Duplicate an assessment event, reverse upload order, include one event with unreliable occurrence time and another with missing interruption/baseline metadata. Valid unique independent observations survive; unknown state is not automatically rested, tired, success or failure. Replaying the same ledger in different arrival orders must yield the same eligible comparisons, exclusions and uncertainty classification.

## Acceptance assertions

1. **Chronology and state provenance.** Every method-comparison observation exposes actual event occurrence time, session/exposure linkage, known practice history and observed versus missing state fields. Upload order, local-day boundaries and unmeasured fatigue must not be used as substitutes.
2. **No secular-trend winner.** In A and C, a method concentrated later in a learner's general improvement cannot win merely from higher raw scores. Report per-option baseline/time/practice distributions, comparable versus confounded counts and uncertainty.
3. **Interrupted opportunity is not failure.** In B, distinguish eligible, offered, completed and independently assessed occasions. Missing or interrupted follow-up is neither a method failure nor a retention success; do not turn latency or accessibility conditions into unsupported ability judgments.
4. **Comparable evidence stays separate.** In D, expose anchored matched-period independent attempts, actual retrieval delays, unseen transfer, sample sizes, assistance, missingness and confidence separately from noncomparable observations. A matched comparison is provisional, not automatically causal.
5. **Revisable, skill-specific hypotheses.** In E, changing comparable delayed outcomes can revise method selection probabilities and confidence for the same objective/skill. Preserve exploration of eligible alternatives; never extrapolate one skill's apparent winner to all skills or label a fixed learning style.
6. **Idempotent event-time replay.** In F, stable event IDs deduplicate results; occurrence chronology and declared missingness rules determine eligibility. Unreliable timestamps or baseline/state gaps remain explicit exclusions, and replay order cannot change method-effect decisions.
7. **Durable independent outcomes and coverage.** Immediate assisted repair, recognition speed or repeated known prompts cannot outrank later unaided retrieval and unseen transfer. No comparison may lower CEFR can-do/mastery standards, erase overdue review, skip difficult production or count unavailable modalities as complete.
8. **Interest independence.** Changing onboarding or demonstrated-interest weights alone may change example contexts, but cannot alter temporal-comparability classifications, objective requirements, neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar rotation or method-effect claims.
9. **Conservative uncertainty-aware fallback.** If balanced comparable delayed evidence is insufficient, report `UNSUPPORTED` or `CONFOUNDED`, use the shared balanced curriculum and continue eligible alternative sampling. Never invent propensity weights, unobserved counterfactuals, causal certainty or a permanent preference.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F report the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, seed/injected clock, policy version, objective and supported skill, synthetic ledger, option eligibility/offer/completion, assessment calibration, baseline and practice opportunity, observed state versus missingness, first-attempt/assistance provenance, actual delay, independent retrieval and unseen transfer, matched versus excluded counts, time-period stratification, confidence/reason codes, outstanding reviews and coverage debt, and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Include an early-versus-late imbalance, an interrupted-opportunity case, time-balanced anchored controls, a reversal, and an offline idempotent replay.

If the engine cannot record relevant state/provenance or establish temporal comparability, report **UNSUPPORTED** instead of inferring a teaching-method winner. Even a deterministic PASS would establish only a software invariant. Any claim that adaptation improves durable independent learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal learner evidence against a balanced baseline with equivalent assessment opportunity, calibrated uncertainty, independent delayed retrieval, unseen transfer and unchanged CEFR coverage.
