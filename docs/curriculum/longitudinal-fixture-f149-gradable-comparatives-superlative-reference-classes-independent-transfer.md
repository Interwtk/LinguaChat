## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Relative comparison does not entail an absolute property.** A reviewed clinic equipment sheet lists a shelf at 170 cm and a cart at 165 cm, with no definition of “tall.” Learner A says “The shelf is taller than the cart, so both are tall”; learner B independently says “The shelf is 5 cm taller; whether either is tall depends on the standard.” Counterfactual: a reviewed safety rule explicitly defines tall as at least 168 cm, licensing “the shelf meets the tall threshold, the cart does not.” A source giving only an ordinal ranking without measurements licenses the ordering, **not** a fabricated 5 cm difference. “A is not taller than B” does not by itself prove B is strictly taller; equality remains possible.

**B — Superlatives require a bounded comparison class and tie handling.** A reviewed volunteer roster shows Ana 8 hours, Bo 8 hours and Chen 6 hours this week. Learner A reports “Ana was the only volunteer who worked the most hours in the organization”; learner B independently reports “Ana and Bo tied for the most hours **among these three listed volunteers this week**.” Counterfactual: a complete organization-wide record showing Ana alone at 9 hours could license the stronger organization-wide unique claim for that stated period. If a fourth volunteer's hours are missing, no global or four-person winner may be asserted. An incomplete or changing reference set requires a bounded claim or clarification, not guessed membership.

**C — Scalar direction, units and strictness in a recipient decision.** A reviewed repair schedule offers slots with waits of 12 and 18 minutes; the recipient asks which has the **shorter wait**, not which appointment is “better” overall. Learner A selects 18 because the number is larger and then says “the second is better”; learner B independently reports that 12 minutes is 6 minutes shorter, without inferring service quality. Counterfactual: a recipient who prioritizes later arrival over wait time might reasonably choose a different slot; “shorter wait” still has the same direction. In a separate constraint, “under 20 minutes” excludes exactly 20, while “20 minutes or less” includes it. Do not collapse strict and nonstrict bounds, convert units silently, or treat a source estimate as exact.

**D — Comparative ellipsis, ambiguity and language-specific expression.** In reviewed English dialogue, “Maya likes tea more than Lee” can mean Maya likes tea more than Lee likes tea, or Maya likes tea more than she likes Lee; without context, learner A insists on one reading, learner B asks “More than Lee likes tea, or more than she likes Lee?” Counterfactual: an explicit prior question “Who likes tea more?” can resolve the intended subject comparison. A separate Spanish/Japanese task requires independently reviewed natural constructions for the same comparison and audience, not English morphological inversion or forced -er/-est forms. A contextually natural target-language paraphrase receives credit even if its word order differs.

**E — Delivered channels, accessible evidence, authorship and replay.** Authoritative audio says “**No more than** fifteen seats are available,” while ASR drops “no,” captions change “at most” to “more than,” translation reverses “least” and “most,” or a bar chart is pre-sorted and highlights the winner before the learner's response. Reconcile the source against **actually rendered** material; quarantine only the affected scalar/comparison construct when adequate evidence was absent. A reviewed accessible text may demonstrate reading comprehension, not unheard listening. At 10:00 the learner independently asks “Does the list include the missing team?”; a hint queued at 09:59 but first rendered at 10:01 cannot retroactively assist that question. A correction at 10:02 after exposure is ASSISTED. Autocomplete adding “the only” or a chart tooltip revealing the winner is assistance. Replayed session-qualified IDs cannot manufacture new independent attempts, sessions or delayed success.

**F — Longitudinal instructional comparison, confounds and independent transfer.** Fictional learner A succeeds immediately with REFERENCE_SET_BOUNDARY_MARK_THEN_FADE but later overgeneralizes a superlative to an unlisted population. Comparable actually delivered TIES_AND_MISSING_CANDIDATES_DIALOGUE trials precede a genuinely new unaided handoff comparing staff shifts for a different recipient, set, property and purpose. Learner B improves delayed first-unaided scalar-direction explanations after COMPARISON_DIRECTION_AND_UNIT_CHECK_THEN_FADE, then regresses on unfamiliar units; confidence must fall and alternatives resume. If one method receives complete three-item tables with familiar adjectives and the other incomplete five-item tables with ambiguous ties or more help, mark METHOD_SET_COMPLETENESS_CONFOUNDED and/or METHOD_LEXICAL_FREQUENCY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Changing only names or numbers in the same ranking frame is not unseen transfer. Log eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing separately; missing is not failure. Only repeated comparable later first-unaided outcomes justify a logged change in **actually delivered** instruction. A learner interested in gaming may compare game durations more often, but neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar healthcare, transport, workplace and community comparisons, plus every required CEFR objective, remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real shared CEFR-linked objective and reviewed oracle.** A–F bind existing versioned objectives, prerequisites, target language/variety/register, communicative recipient, reviewed scalar/source-world oracle, accessible stimuli, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. No invented comparison-specific CEFR descriptor.
2. **Relative versus absolute and supported differences.** A licenses reviewed strict comparison and numerical differences only when measured; relative “taller” does not establish an absolute “tall” threshold, and negated strict comparison does not entail strict reverse ordering.
3. **Superlative domain, ties and missing members.** B bounds claims to the reviewed complete comparison class and time, preserves ties, and refuses uniqueness/global maximality when the domain is incomplete or unverified.
4. **Scale orientation, units and strictness.** C preserves whether smaller or larger is desirable for the actual recipient purpose, correct units and conversions, strict versus nonstrict limits, and the distinction between measured/estimated values and invented precision.
5. **Ambiguous ellipsis and independently reviewed natural variants.** D credits clarification for genuinely unresolved comparative readings and equivalent natural target-language expressions, without projecting English inflection or syntax into other languages.
6. **Actually rendered accessible and idempotent evidence.** E compares authoritative with rendered ASR/TTS/captions/translation/visual/screen-reader/autocomplete, quarantines only unsupported constructs, orders learner authorship against **rendered** help, and deduplicates session-qualified events. Reading evidence does not certify unheard listening.
7. **Confound-aware uncertain reversible adaptation.** F compares feasible accessible delivered methods under matched class completeness, tie frequency, scale direction, lexical burden, variety, register, modality, topic, prior help, interval and follow-up. Sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence yields no method winner and balanced fallback.
8. **Delayed first-unaided transfer and balanced coverage.** Assisted highlighted rankings, model-copy edits, same-session drills and number/name-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require distinct eligible later sessions, genuinely new property/reference class/recipient/purpose, reviewed oracle, first-unaided recipient-correct production and explicit missingness. Preserve listening, reading, writing, speaking, free production and neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar topic rotation.
9. **Evidence honesty and release boundary.** Document/CI success does not demonstrate executable engine support, delivered outcome-driven method changes or improved human retention. Require exact-head executable assertions tied to real objectives, longitudinal independent delayed/transfer comparison against a balanced baseline and later ethically reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims.
