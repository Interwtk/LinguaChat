# F86 — Epistemic possibility, certainty, inference and evidence-limited claims in independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a shipped epistemic-language evaluator, fact-checker, CEFR certification, reproduced production defect, high-stakes decision aid or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F31 guards transient learner-state confounds; F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 distinguish assigned from actually rendered teaching and tutor-origin answer cues; F47/F48 protect noncompensatory mastery; F49 accessibility equivalence; F50 broad topic rotation; F53 accepted language varieties and register; F54 clarification and repair; F73 pragmatic inference; F74 source attribution; F80 authoritative updates; F81 negation/quantifier scope; F82 temporal reference; F83 conditional commitments; F85 **deontic** permission/obligation/prohibition. **F86 isolates a distinct CEFR-linked communicative construct:** independently understanding and expressing **what is known, possible, probable, inferred, reported, uncertain or contradicted**, with appropriately limited claims in a source-defined communicative exchange. “You must submit it” (obligation) and “It must be the correct file” (inference) are not interchangeable. Do not turn this into a universal truth detector, numeric probability test, mind-reading task or prescriptive cultural rule for expressing confidence. Apply only where a **real versioned CEFR descriptor/can-do and task** require communicating epistemic stance; otherwise `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Freeze task-licensed source statements, speaker/addressee, information access, observation versus report versus inference, claim content, polarity and scope, source provenance and effective version, explicit counterevidence, unresolved contradictions and permitted requests for evidence or clarification. Use task-scoped annotation labels `OBSERVED`, `REPORTED`, `INFERRED`, `POSSIBLE`, `SUPPORTED_LIKELY`, `SOURCE_CONFIRMED`, `CONTRADICTED` and `UNKNOWN` **only when justified by the frozen source/rubric**. These are not learner-facing permanent categories, objective truth labels or a universal ordered numeric confidence scale. “Could,” “might,” “must,” “apparently,” “I think” and their equivalents vary by target language, context, variety and register; validate intended meaning rather than imposing English grammar.

A tutor's correct inference, highlighted uncertainty marker, scripted hedged reply or answer-bearing source summary contaminates the affected first learner move **only when actually rendered before it**. Source-permitted interlocutor clarification or new evidence is part of the task; an unlicensed interlocutor supplying the answer is coaching. Interests influence example probability only; rotate preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts. Never label a learner permanently “cautious,” “confident,” “auditory” or “visual.”

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Possible is not certain.** A source-authored planning conversation says a guest *might* arrive after lunch; arrival is not confirmed. Learner A announces the guest *will* arrive, treating a possibility as a fact. Learner B independently conveys the uncertainty in an accepted target-language expression and asks a permitted follow-up if a firm plan is required. A later actual arrival cannot retroactively turn A's unsupported earlier certainty into accurate evidence calibration.
- **B — Observation, report and inference differ.** A task supplies a direct observation (“the lights are on”), a peer's unverified report (“the room is occupied”) and a tentative inference (“someone may be inside”). Learner A converts the inference into “I saw someone”; learner B independently distinguishes what was observed, what another person said and what remains inferred. F74 source attribution alone does not prove calibrated epistemic force; an accurate attribution paired with unwarranted certainty still fails the construct.
- **C — New counterevidence changes a responsible claim.** A source-defined exchange initially supports “the meeting is probably in room A,” then a trusted task update establishes that room A is unavailable while the replacement room is **not yet specified**. Learner A continues confidently naming A or invents room B; learner B independently retracts the old claim, communicates what is known and asks for the missing location. Do not treat an authoritative correction as license to invent unspecified details. A tutor-displayed corrected message before the first retraction marks that retraction assisted.
- **D — Deontic versus epistemic ‘must’.** In validated target-language task pairs, “participants must wear badges” is a rule, while “the badge must be in the drawer” expresses an inference from source-provided clues. Learner A treats the inference as an instruction or the instruction as a certainty claim; learner B independently communicates each task-intended meaning, including permitted ambiguity/clarification. Avoid imposing a one-to-one English modal mapping where the target language expresses these forces differently. This distinguishes F86 from F85 without weakening either objective.
- **E — Immediate confidence reverses after delayed transfer.** On matched immediate exchanges, `STANCE_CONTRAST` yields more fluent first attempts than `EVIDENCE_TO_CLAIM_DIALOGUE`. After meaningful separation, genuinely new speakers, information gaps, counterevidence and accepted target-language markers yield stronger unaided communicative outcomes following dialogue practice. Match or disclose task difficulty, evidence availability, register, modality, practice dose, feedback, language variety, topic familiarity and accessibility. Lower method confidence, sample alternatives and record the **actually rendered** next choice; never assign a fixed “dialogue learner” label.
- **F — Favorite topics, offline replay and unknown follow-up.** A learner expresses appropriate uncertainty in a familiar gaming rumor but overclaims certainty in a neutral travel update and an unfamiliar community notice. An image-only notice is inaccessible, so the response is not evidence of deficient language skill. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; a tutor's answer-bearing certainty cue was rendered before the learner's response but uploaded later; source and rubric versions conflict; a delayed unseen probe was offered but never completed. Deduplicate only genuine session-qualified replays, quarantine unresolved versions, preserve missingness and show identical assistance labels, claim interpretations, review debt and next-method rationale under receipt-order permutations. Text evidence cannot establish listening or speaking mastery.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid construct before scoring.** For A–F require a real versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, source-authored task/outcome, target language/varieties, validated epistemic readings, evaluator/rubric, assistance policy, independent threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing mapping, unvalidated language reading or invented external facts yields `UNSUPPORTED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, not fabricated proficiency.
2. **Epistemic force, polarity and scope.** In A/D/E distinguish possibility, qualified inference, source-established fact, explicit uncertainty and contradiction according to task-validated meanings. Reject unjustified “might”→“will,” “probably”→“certainly” and negation/scope changes even when grammatically fluent. Do not require numeric confidence or assume every language has the same hedge inventory.
3. **Evidence access and source fidelity.** In B/C/F distinguish directly observed facts, reports and inferences; preserve speaker/source provenance, version and what each participant could know. A learner cannot claim firsthand observation of something merely reported. Unresolved evidence remains unresolved; source claims are not automatically objective truth.
4. **Counterevidence, repair and legitimate clarification.** In A/C/D independently update or retract claims when task-authorized new evidence changes what can responsibly be said; accept source-permitted requests for missing information. Do not invent a new answer, assume a universal authority hierarchy or punish an appropriate “I don't know yet.”
5. **First independent move and actually rendered cues.** In C/F anchor the first learner-authored epistemic claim or revision to trusted tutor/interlocutor render chronology. An answer-bearing confidence marker, model reply or correction rendered beforehand marks the affected move assisted; assigned-but-unseen cues do not. Distinguish legitimate task-supplied evidence from out-of-protocol answer leakage.
6. **Delayed, genuinely unseen independent communication.** In C/E/F require meaningful session separation, new source claims/speakers/evidence conflicts and first unaided target-language moves with observed source-defined communicative outcomes. Emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Same-session paraphrase, memorized hedges, assisted repeats and uncompleted follow-ups do not prove durable independent transfer.
7. **Comparable adaptation, skill protection and broad topics.** In E/F match or disclose evidence complexity, register, modality, practice dose, feedback, variety, topic familiarity and accessibility. Preserve the same CEFR objective, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation. Interest weights are never exclusion rules.
8. **Reversible instruction hypotheses and deterministic replay.** In C/E/F emit feasible alternatives, selected/assigned/actually rendered instruction, later independent outcomes, confidence/confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse, conflicted or inaccessible evidence uses `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified deduplication, frozen source/rubric versions and trusted render chronology must produce receipt-order-invariant assistance, epistemic interpretation, review debt and next choice; quarantine unresolved conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, validated claim/evidence/stance facts, first/final learner-authored moves, actual cues, observed outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay-equivalence evidence and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI does not prove a shipped evaluator, calibrated real-world truth judgments, CEFR certification or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked epistemic tasks, validated multilingual/variety-aware rubrics, frozen claim/source/evidence/version facts, trusted rendered-cue provenance, first unaided epistemic communication, observed source-defined outcomes, completed delayed unseen transfer with denominators, comparable instruction/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skill/review obligations, replay-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, validated meaning, reliable evidence, accessible modality or completed follow-up stays `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

A claim that adaptive instruction **improves durable independent expression of knowledge and uncertainty** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen interactions, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners say what the evidence supports—and what it does not—rather than merely sound certain.**
