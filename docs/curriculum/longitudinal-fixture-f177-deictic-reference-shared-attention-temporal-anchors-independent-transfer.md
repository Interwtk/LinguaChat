## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Demonstrative without shared attention.** A fictional volunteer says, “Put **that** box next to **this** one.” Two identical boxes are in frame, but the independently reviewed delivered audio contains no visible pointing and no prior shared-attention anchor. Neither demonstrative is uniquely recoverable. The learner asks “Which box do you mean by ‘that’?” before moving anything; this is calibrated communication. The hidden source-world intention (box B beside box A) is **not** proof that a confident guess was independently justified. In a separately reviewed version, a visible synchronized gesture uniquely identifies both boxes; the learner may then act unaided. Do not score the audio-only version as a gesture-recognition failure.

**B — Speaker-relative here and there.** On a fictional club call, Noor is at the library desk and Ivo is at the meeting room. Noor says, “Leave the folder **here**, not **there**.” The source-world binds “here” to Noor's desk only if the listener has actually received the speakers' location mapping. A learner who maps “here” to their own location or to the most recent scene fails a resolvable perspective case; if the locations were never delivered, “Where are you calling from?” is appropriate. A map appearing **after** the first response can support a later assisted correction, not retroactive first-unaided mastery.

**C — The other one and discourse candidates.** In a fictional classroom, two projectors (P1 and P2) are discussed. A speaker says “P1 is broken. Use **the other one**,” with exactly two projectors in the independently reviewed shared discourse. P2 is recoverable. In a variant, P1, P2 and P3 are available and no pair is established; “the other one” is underdetermined. A learner who requests “Do you mean P2 or P3?” should not be forced to guess. A later highlighted P2 is answer exposure; the label is not evidence of independent reference resolution.

**D — Relative dates anchored to utterance time.** A fictional organizer records on **Monday 26 October 2026, 10:00 America/Santiago**, “The practice is **tomorrow**.” The intended date is **Tuesday 27 October 2026**, regardless of when the recording is replayed. A learner who hears it on Wednesday 28 October must not schedule the practice for Thursday 29 October. The time anchor must have been actually available to the learner (e.g., a verified dated message header) to grade unaided resolution. In a separate undated recording, asking “When was this recorded?” is correct; an inferred server receipt time or guessed time zone is not a valid source. Relative-day calculations must use the reviewed local calendar, not a fabricated universal UTC day.

**E — Chronology, accessibility and first authorship.** At 09:02 the learner hears “Bring that one here” in a video where the hand is occluded, and first asks “Which one, and where is ‘here’?” Preserve FIRST_UNAIDED_INTERACTION and TARGETED_REFERENCE_CLARIFICATION. At 09:03 a clearly rendered annotated scene, accessible description and answer-bearing highlight become available; at 09:04 the learner identifies box B and the desk. That response is ASSISTED and ANSWER_EXPOSURE, not an overwrite of 09:02. A help control only offered but never opened is not exposure. Deduplicate a retried 09:04 event by stable session-qualified ID. If video playback failed, mark delivery unusable rather than penalizing reference comprehension. A text-first accessibility route is legitimate but evaluated under the actually delivered construct.

**F — Reversible longitudinal adaptation and confounding.** Learner A correctly recognizes isolated demonstratives but repeatedly moves the wrong object in independent multi-turn scenes. Across matched sessions, actually delivered EXPLICIT_SPEAKER_PERSPECTIVE_MAPPING and SHARED_ATTENTION_CHECK_AND_CLARIFY are alternated with BALANCED_DEFAULT. Better later first-unaided recipient actions and targeted clarification on **new scenes, speakers, neutral/unfamiliar topics and independently reviewed time anchors** cautiously raise the supported method's probability while retaining uncertainty and alternative sampling. Learner B initially performs well after GESTURE_PLUS_LANGUAGE_THEN_FADE, but later fails an unseen no-gesture scene by guessing; lower confidence and explore DISCOURSE_ANTECEDENT_TRACKING. If one method received more visible gestures, answer-bearing labels, easier referent sets, clearer location maps, known dates, favourite topics or more follow-up, mark **no causal winner**. Reusing the same scene with different labels, a calendar boundary, an assisted re-answer or missing follow-up cannot establish durable unseen transfer.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR and independently reviewed oracles.** A–F bind relevant existing versioned can-dos, source-world reference and temporal oracles, actual-delivery provenance, unchanged rubric and due/delayed/unseen probes; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED. No new level gates.
2. **Shared attention is observable, not presumed.** A distinguishes a genuinely visible synchronized gesture from an absent/occluded one; targeted clarification is valid where the referent is unrecoverable. Hidden author intent cannot grade audio-only guessing.
3. **Speaker-relative perspective.** B resolves “here/there” from actually available speaker locations, not the learner's default vantage; missing perspective evidence warrants clarification, not forced-confidence scoring.
4. **Discourse antecedents and ambiguity.** C resolves the uniquely recoverable two-item “other one,” withholds a single answer in the three-candidate variant and rejects highlighted-answer leakage as unaided success.
5. **Verified relative-time anchoring.** D binds “tomorrow” to the original verified utterance date and reviewed local calendar, not replay time; undated messages and unknown zones remain uncertain, with targeted clarification accepted.
6. **First authorship, actual help and accessibility.** E preserves the earliest independent attempt, only counts actually rendered help/labels/gesture descriptions as exposure, deduplicates retries, handles failed delivery honestly and reports accessible routes under their real construct.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F updates instructional probabilities only from comparable **actually delivered** methods and eligible later independent outcomes; confounds, sparse data and regressions prevent a causal-winner claim and trigger alternative sampling.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and interest diversity.** Require a meaningfully later distinct session, new independently reviewed referents/speakers/perspectives/time anchors, first-unaided recipient-facing action or clarification and explicit missing follow-up. Preserve modality balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics without changing CEFR standards.
9. **Evidence and protected-scope boundary.** Generic CI and synthetic prose cannot prove executable deictic QA, shipped adaptation or human retention. Require exact-head executable source/delivery assertions, longitudinal outcome-driven instruction changes and independently reviewed delayed unaided transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, A1–C2 enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable QA. Preserve actual shared attention, perspective, utterance-time anchors, justified clarification and reversible instruction; keep UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independently verified learning evidence exist.
