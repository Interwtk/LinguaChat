# F29 — Overlapping teaching exposures cannot be attributed to the last method by default

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until an executable check runs against a named engine HEAD. This is not a reproduced defect, implemented tutor capability, causal finding, or evidence of improved learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F15 prevents an assessment probe from masquerading as a teaching exposure; F24 tests selection bias; F28 prevents cross-skill extrapolation. F29 tests a distinct longitudinal problem: **instructional carryover**. When a learner receives more than one teaching option before a later independent assessment, that outcome may reflect several exposures, intervening practice or their interaction. A simplistic “credit the most recent option” rule can manufacture a method preference.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to one existing, versioned CEFR can-do and one genuinely supported independent evaluator. Use two eligible instructional options, `concise_explanation` (A) and `worked_example` (B), with unchanged target skill, objective version, prerequisite, task demand, rubric, mastery gate, modality availability and due-review requirements. Preserve neutral and unfamiliar assessment contexts with comparable difficulty. Keep interest-based context sampling separate from method selection.

Use an injected clock, stable deterministic seed, and an append-only synthetic event ledger with learner/objective/skill, event/run/session IDs, learning-day provenance, policy version, **instructional exposure ID and type**, exposure start/end and order, active teaching versus independent assessment, context family, option assignment probability when actually logged, retries, scaffold/model/hint exposure, first attempt, intervening practice, probe linkage, real delay, independent outcome, unseen transfer, missingness and reason codes. Distinguish an assigned or displayed option from a completed instructional exposure. Do not invent exposure durations, causal shares or counterfactual outcomes.

Define a documented, versioned *attribution eligibility policy* before running the fixture. It may conservatively mark mixed-exposure outcomes **AMBIGUOUS / UNATTRIBUTED**; it must not silently choose a universal washout interval, assume that the last exposure caused the result, or treat ambiguous evidence as failure. Retain valid independent can-do evidence separately even when method attribution is uncertain.

## Synthetic histories

- **A — Last-touch illusion.** The learner completes A instruction and meaningful A practice, then briefly views B before a genuinely delayed independent probe succeeds. A last-touch estimator credits B alone. The acceptance policy must instead report overlapping A/B provenance and uncertainty; a displayed but incomplete B cannot automatically inherit the successful outcome.
- **B — Reversed order control.** Repeat A with the same objective, total exposure, supported task demand and delayed independent probe, but reverse A/B order. A change in the *name of the credited winner* solely because the last option changed is unsupported. Record any genuine order effect as a separate hypothesis, not proof of B or A superiority.
- **C — Clean separated controls.** Provide comparable A-only and B-only teaching occasions, each followed by genuinely delayed independent retrieval and unseen-context transfer, with balanced assignment and documented intervening practice. Valid clean observations may inform provisional option estimates; do not mix them indistinguishably with A+B occasions.
- **D — Mixed exposure with contradictory transfer.** A+B produces fast correct recognition and assisted repair, but later independent free production fails in an unseen context. A-only occasions produce slower immediate practice but stronger later independent transfer. Prioritize comparable independent outcomes; do not credit A+B's assisted recognition as durable mastery or declare the last option superior.
- **E — Carryover across sessions.** A occurs in session s1, B in s2 and an independent probe in s3. The sessions and learning days differ, but the teaching history still overlaps within the declared attribution window. Session/day boundaries alone cannot erase A's possible contribution. If the window or provenance is insufficient, mark attribution uncertain rather than fabricate washout.
- **F — Missing and duplicated exposures.** Offline replay duplicates a B exposure event; an A exposure has unknown completion; an independent probe arrives before its instruction log. Replays and upload order must not multiply B's weight or backfill unknown A as completed. Preserve the independently observed probe and flag attribution provenance as incomplete.

## Acceptance assertions

1. **Separate outcome from attribution.** An independently verified can-do attempt remains usable for its existing evidence route, while a mixed or uncertain teaching history cannot be promoted to a clean option-specific efficacy observation. Never lower CEFR thresholds.
2. **No automatic last-touch winner.** In A and B, the policy cannot claim the most recently displayed or completed option caused the later result solely from ordering. Record all eligible exposure IDs and a documented attribution decision/reason.
3. **Clean evidence is distinguishable.** In C, option estimates must expose clean single-option versus mixed/ambiguous exposure counts, actual delay, independent outcomes, context and uncertainty. Do not invent causal weights for mixed histories.
4. **Later independent outcomes dominate.** In D, immediate assisted correctness, recognition speed or copying cannot replace delayed unaided production and unseen transfer. Preserve required productive/receptive skill coverage and outstanding reviews.
5. **Session boundaries are not washout.** In E, an artificial session/day change alone cannot certify that earlier teaching ceased to matter. If no validated attribution horizon exists, report **UNSUPPORTED** rather than assume a universal one.
6. **Idempotent chronology.** In F, deduplicate exposure/probe IDs; distinguish occurrence time from upload time and quarantine conflicting or unreliable ordering. Replaying the same ledger in different upload orders yields the same accepted evidence and uncertainty classification.
7. **Assignment and opportunity visibility.** Report which methods were eligible, assigned, offered, actually completed and independently assessed, including missing follow-ups. A method with fewer clean probes is not automatically inferior; missing outcomes are neither successes nor failures.
8. **Interest independence.** Changing favorite-topic weights while preserving instructional exposures and valid outcomes may change example contexts, but cannot by itself change method-effect estimates or CEFR evidence requirements. Neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar contexts remain eligible.
9. **Reversible, uncertainty-aware fallback.** With insufficient clean comparisons, use the shared balanced policy and continue eligible exploration; later comparable delayed independent evidence can revise a provisional hypothesis. Never create a permanent “learning style” label.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish exact tested HEAD, executable command, policy version/seed/clock, objective and skill mapping, synthetic ledger with exposure/probe IDs, assignment/offer/completion status, ordered teaching history, intervening practice, actual delay, assistance and first-attempt provenance, delayed independent and unseen-context results, clean-versus-mixed counts, attribution reason codes, uncertainty, coverage debt, and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Include an order-reversal control, a genuine A-only/B-only comparison, and an offline replay/idempotence control. If the engine cannot distinguish completed teaching exposures from assessment probes or lacks attribution provenance, report **UNSUPPORTED**; do not assert a successful adaptation.

Even deterministic PASS would establish only a software invariant. The stronger claim that adaptive teaching improves durable independent learning requires a separately reviewed longitudinal learner comparison with a balanced baseline, comparable follow-up opportunity, calibrated uncertainty, independent delayed retrieval, unseen transfer and unchanged CEFR coverage.
