## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Indirect request versus literal ability question.** In a validated fictional English cafe dialogue, a diner asks a server, “**Could you bring some water, please?**” The contextual action is a request for water, not merely an exam about physical ability. Learner A replies only “Yes, I can” and does nothing; learner B acknowledges the request or appropriately responds that water is unavailable. Counterfactual: a language teacher explicitly asks a student “**Could you bring water if you had a tray?**” during an ability exercise; the intended act may be an ability question, not a real service request. Changing context while holding modal words similar changes the oracle. A literal answer may be valid in a genuinely ability-testing context. Validate other target languages independently.

**B — Offer is not acceptance or consent.** Fictional classmate Nia says “**I can share my notes if you'd like**.” This is an offer conditional on the recipient's choice; it does not mean the recipient has agreed, the notes have been sent or that Nia may share someone else's private notes. Learner A announces “You already sent me the notes” or treats silence as acceptance; learner B independently accepts, declines or asks when they could receive them. Counterfactual: source-authored reply “**Yes, please send them**” establishes acceptance of that offer, not acceptance of unrelated actions. The evaluator must not equate pragmatic appropriateness with authorizing real-world data disclosure; no external action is executed.

**C — Softened refusal remains a boundary.** A fictional invitation receives “**Thanks for inviting me, but I can't come on Friday**.” Learner A interprets thanks as acceptance and continues pressuring the speaker; learner B recognizes a refusal for Friday and replies without coercion. Counterfactual: “**Thanks, I can come on Friday**” removes the negation and changes the act; “**I can't come on Friday, but Saturday might work**” rejects Friday and suggests an uncertain alternative, not a firm Saturday commitment. Avoid presuming a culture-specific indirect refusal where the actual source does not warrant it; explicit refusal takes priority over speculative politeness cues.

**D — Permission, obligation and uncertain hints.** In a validated fictional classroom exchange, “**May I leave early?**” asks for permission; “**You must leave early**” imposes an obligation in the authored scenario; “**You may leave early**” grants permission without requiring departure. Learner A treats all as instructions to leave; learner B distinguishes who has agency and whether an action is optional. Counterfactual: a fictional speaker says “**It's getting cold in here**” with no additional context. This could be an observation or a hint; an independent targeted clarification such as “Would you like me to close the window?” is acceptable. Do not mark a guessed request to close the window as certain or presume a gesture, hierarchy or intent absent from the source. For languages where equivalent modality differs, validate authentic alternatives and level prerequisites.

**E — Delivered prosody, accessibility, help chronology and replay.** An independently validated spoken dialogue uses a contrastive intonation and preceding turn that make a question an offer, but the caption loses the preceding turn, an automatic translation turns it into a command, and the screen-reader order presents the answer before the offer. Freeze authoritative and **actually rendered** channels; quarantine affected pragmatic-act evidence as UNSUPPORTED while retaining separately valid language observations. A learner's accurate unaided interpretation at 10:00 stays FIRST_UNAIDED if a tutor intent label queued at 09:59 first **renders** at 10:01; later copying is ASSISTED. Offline replay of the same immutable event ID does not create a new first attempt; a genuinely new session-qualified attempt can. Never infer low pragmatics proficiency from a defective channel.

**F — Longitudinal reversible method choice and unfamiliar-recipient transfer.** Fictional learner A initially repeats memorized polite forms but later misinterprets offers and refusals in first-unaided conversations. After **actually delivered** LITERAL_VS_ACTION_CONTRAST on comparable CEFR tasks, A independently responds to an unseen fictional library request and later recognizes a refusal from a different recipient. Learner B instead shows stronger delayed first-unaided transfer after DIALOGUE_NEXT_TURN_PRACTICE with comparable difficulty. Compare baseline proficiency, language/variety, register, dialogue length, number of turns, indirectness, roles, social distance only if source-authored, modality, accessible delivery, topic familiarity, hint exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If one method receives direct transparent requests and the other ambiguous multi-turn refusals, mark METHOD_INDIRECTNESS_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_ROLE_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Renaming people while preserving the same dialogue-act sequence, cue and answer path is not unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups: missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed unaided results may justify **actually different next instruction**, reversibly and with uncertainty; contrary evidence lowers confidence. Keep alternative sampling, skill balance, due reviews and nonexclusive topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR, source and pragmatic-act linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, independently validated utterance/turn/act IDs, language/variety/register, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No advanced cross-cultural inference is silently imposed on beginner objectives.
2. **Literal form versus contextual action.** A/D bind pragmatic acts to independently warranted context, not question marks or modal keywords alone. The validated ability-question counterfactual changes the expected response; cosmetic name changes do not.
3. **Offers, acceptance and permission boundaries.** B/D distinguish offering, recipient acceptance, authorization, permission and obligation. Silence is not automatically agreement; no real-world action or personal data transfer is triggered by an inferred speech act.
4. **Refusal, conditionality and agency.** C/D preserve explicit refusal and conditional availability, distinguish tentative suggestions from commitments, and avoid rewarding pressure or an invented obligation. Negation changes the oracle.
5. **Warranted clarification and uncertainty.** D distinguishes underspecified hints from clear requests; a targeted clarification earns credit where the source genuinely leaves intent uncertain. Unjustified certainty and indiscriminate hedging do not.
6. **Language/variety validity, accessible delivery, chronology and replay.** A–E validate pragmatic interpretations across approved varieties/registers and delivered audio, captions, translation, screen-reader order and turn boundaries. Only answer-bearing help **actually rendered before** the first response contaminates independence; duplicate event replay is idempotent; broken delivery quarantines affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen communicative transfer.** A–F require meaningful separate learning opportunities, different dialogue-act structures and recipients, first-unaided responses without answer-bearing help and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Surface paraphrase or character renaming alone does not qualify as unseen.
8. **Reversible adaptation, confounds and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** methods, comparable indirectness/role complexity, uncertainty, alternative sampling and contrary outcomes. Interests affect example probability only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts, CEFR objectives, protected skills and due reviews remain available.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/turn/act/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, source-effective and delivered artifacts, first/help/event timeline, validated pragmatic oracle and counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot establish a shipped evaluator, reproducible runtime defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently validated language/variety/register-specific dialogue acts and accepted alternatives; authoritative and actually delivered accessible stimuli; first-unaided responses and actual help chronology; delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen recipient transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skills, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and context/negation/conditionality/permission/translation/caption/prosody/turn-order/version/replay counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Unvalidated interpretations, inaccessible stimuli, missing context or absent follow-up leave affected claims **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not invented learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive pragmatic instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners understand what another person is actually asking or declining—and respond independently while respecting boundaries—not merely recite polite formulas.**
