**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD with real versioned CEFR-linked source tasks, adjudicated cue provenance, supported modalities and longitudinal first-unaided outcomes. This is **not** shipped video/audio interpretation, emotion recognition, a reproducible production defect, CEFR mastery or proof of human learning gains. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 distinguish assigned from actually rendered help and tutor-origin errors; F49 protects accessible construct equivalence; F53 language variety/register; F73 implicit pragmatic intent; F77/F102 turn-taking and recipient alignment; F78/F79 common ground; F86 evidential certainty; F96 literal versus figurative meaning; F103 critical-detail readback; F104 confound-aware method selection; F107 unreliable feedback provenance; F108 within-turn disfluency; F109 language boundaries. **Distinct construct:** when a source-authored communicative task includes punctuation, emoji/reactions, intonation, timing or visible gestures, can a learner use **only source-warranted cues** to interpret intent, commitment and speaker/recipient scope while preserving ambiguity? Can the tutor distinguish cue-supported comprehension from guesses, stereotype-based affect inference, or inaccessible/absent media? This is not a mandate to infer feelings, personality, deception or intent from faces, voices, accents or response speed.

## Source, cue and instructional-evidence contract

Resolve the real versioned CEFR descriptor/can-do, level blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored purpose, source wording, intended communicative outcome, cue inventory and cue reliability, speaker/addressee/reaction target, language/variety/register, relevant cultural conventions *only when validated for this task*, evaluator/rubric, accessible modality or validated construct-equivalent alternative, first-unaided threshold, help policy, protected skills, due reviews, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer and exit gate. A task must explicitly establish whether the assessed construct is lexical meaning, listening prosody, interpreting text punctuation, gesture-supported mediation, pragmatic clarification or another defined ability. **Do not retrofit a multimodal requirement onto a text-only can-do.** Without source-valid cue semantics, return UNKNOWN/UNSUPPORTED or NOT_APPLICABLE rather than guessing what a person 'really meant'.

Record an immutable, session-qualified **cue-to-utterance provenance trace**: source/task/CEFR/rubric/evaluator versions; exact original words; available and actually rendered text/audio/video/reaction surfaces; cue time span and alignment to speaker, message and addressee; original emoji/punctuation and platform reaction semantics; media consent/availability and accessibility route; caption/ASR/transcript hypotheses and confidence; first learner-authored unaided interpretation/response; retries; hints, examples, paraphrases or cue annotations actually rendered and their times; independent recipient outcome; follow-up eligibility/offering/completion/missingness; method, scaffold dose, topic familiarity, difficulty, prior exposure and review state. Separate **observed signal**, **source-adjudicated meaning**, **learner inference** and **tutor suggestion**. An automatic transcript, emoji classifier, prosody model, gesture detector or latency heuristic is not ground truth.

Task-local event labels may include SOURCE_VALID_CUE, CUE_AMBIGUOUS, CUE_ABSENT, CUE_CONFLICT, CUE_SOURCE_UNTRUSTWORTHY, REACTION_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_ONLY, EXPLICIT_COMMITMENT, RECIPIENT_SCOPE_RESOLVED, RECIPIENT_SCOPE_UNRESOLVED, PROSODIC_CONTRAST_EVIDENCED, PUNCTUATION_CONTRAST_EVIDENCED, GESTURE_MEANING_SOURCE_VALID, CLARIFICATION_WARRANTED, OVERINTERPRETATION, ACCESS_ROUTE_EQUIVALENT, ACCESS_ROUTE_DIFFERENT_CONSTRUCT, FIRST_UNAIDED, ANSWER_BEARING_CUE_EXPLANATION_RENDERED, ASSISTED, DELAYED_UNAIDED_RETRIEVAL, UNSEEN_UNAIDED_TRANSFER, FOLLOWUP_MISSING, REPLAY_DEDUPED, VERSION_CONFLICT_QUARANTINED, BALANCED_DEFAULT, NOT_APPLICABLE and UNSUPPORTED. Labels apply to **task events**, not stable learner traits, emotional states, disabilities, nationalities or learning styles.

Feasible reversible instructional candidates include BALANCED_DEFAULT, CUE_VS_WORDING_CONTRAST, EXPLICIT_COMMITMENT_CHECK, INTONATION_WITH_SOURCE_VALID_AUDIO, PUNCTUATION_CONTEXT_COMPARISON, REACTION_SCOPE_ROLEPLAY, CLARIFICATION_WITHOUT_MIND_READING, ACCESSIBLE_SOURCE_EQUIVALENT and SOURCE_VALID_OTHER, only if implemented and supported. An answer-bearing caption, intonation label, gesture explanation or tutor paraphrase is rendered assistance when it discloses the answer. Vary methods, modality mix, scaffolding, pacing, ordering, repetition and review spacing from **later independent retention and transfer**, with uncertainty and continued safe alternatives. Interest topics remain a separate soft layer, rotating preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts without changing the can-do, cue demands or mastery threshold.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Intonation versus lexical meaning, without emotion diagnosis.** In a source-authored listening task, two consented, adjudicated recordings use the same words, “That is finished,” with source-defined prosodic contours that distinguish a statement from a genuine question. One learner recognizes the distinction from the audio unaided; another reads an automatically inserted question mark in a transcript and answers correctly only after the mark supplies the distinction. The latter has reading/transcript evidence, not independent prosody-listening evidence. In a third recording the contour is inconclusive: the correct source-grounded response is to request clarification, not label the speaker angry, sarcastic, dishonest or uncooperative. When trustworthy audio is absent, the listening verdict is UNSUPPORTED, not learner failure.

**B — Emoji and reaction scope versus explicit agreement.** In a fictional group scheduling exchange, Maya proposes Friday, Luis adds a thumbs-up reaction to *Maya's message*, and Amina reacts to a different message containing only a shared file. The task's source-validated platform semantics define a thumbs-up as acknowledgement, not a binding acceptance. A learner who reports “Everyone confirmed Friday” overgeneralizes; one who says “Luis saw the proposal; we still need confirmation” preserves commitment scope. A separate source-authored exchange contains an explicit “Yes, Friday at 3 works for me” from Luis, which may evidence his own agreement but not Amina's. Do not infer unanimous consent from reaction counts or icons alone.

**C — Gestures, convention limits and accessible alternatives.** A source-authored in-person roleplay includes a clearly adjudicated gesture pointing to the left-hand counter while the speaker says “over there”; an accessible route describes the same source-valid directional referent where that route is construct-equivalent for the objective. A second clip shows an unfamiliar hand sign with no source-validated convention, and a third has occluded hands. Only the first supports a specific spatial interpretation. The latter cases warrant clarification or UNKNOWN, not a cultural stereotype. If the descriptor specifically requires visual gesture recognition, a text route cannot silently certify that visual construct; offer a validated alternate objective/evidence route if authorized, otherwise mark the visual evidence UNSUPPORTED.

**D — Conflicting cues and respectful clarification.** A speaker writes “Sure.” but appends a source-authored conditional clause, “if the room is available”; another interlocutor says “Fine” with an ambiguous pause; a message includes an apparently enthusiastic emoji while explicitly saying “I cannot attend.” The learner must preserve the *explicit condition or refusal* rather than overriding it with a guessed emotional reading. When the task intentionally leaves pragmatic intent unresolved, asking “Should I treat this as confirmed?” is a valid communicative strategy. A tutor must not reward confident mind-reading or penalize legitimate uncertainty.

**E — Longitudinal method reversal and interest separation.** Across matched fictional sessions, CUE_VS_WORDING_CONTRAST produces high immediate assisted correctness on a learner's favorite gaming-chat examples, but delayed first-unaided interpretation of unfamiliar service messages incorrectly treats emoji reactions as commitments. EXPLICIT_COMMITMENT_CHECK looks harder initially yet yields stronger independent unseen transfer for that learner. A second learner shows the reverse pattern on comparable source-defined tasks. Control source objective, difficulty, language variety, media accessibility, prior cue exposure, topic familiarity, actual rendered help, practice dose and follow-up missingness. Revise a **capability-local uncertain method hypothesis**, sample feasible alternatives, preserve required receptive/productive practice and rotate adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts; do not label either learner a “visual” or “auditory” type.

**F — Media versioning, cue chronology and replay.** A consented source clip is revised to correct a mislabeled speaker; the original caption places “?” before the learner's first attempt but the video was unavailable until afterward; a later tutor cue annotation is uploaded before an earlier offline learner attempt; two genuine sessions share a local event ID while a third packet is a true replay. Use source-effective versions and session-qualified event identities, reconstruct trusted cue/render times, deduplicate only genuine replays and quarantine irreconcilable media/rubric chronology. A caption actually displayed before a response can contaminate unaided prosody evidence even if its upload is later; an assigned but unseen cue annotation cannot. Receipt order must not change first-unaided status, commitment verdicts, due-review debt or next-method rationale.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid objective and cue boundaries.** A–F require a real versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, task-authored communicative purpose, cue meaning and scope, source/rubric/evaluator, modality/access policy, independence threshold, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing cue norms or untrustworthy media are UNSUPPORTED; irrelevant cues are NOT_APPLICABLE. No universal emotion, emoji or gesture dictionary may substitute for source adjudication.
2. **Separate words, signals and warranted inference.** A/C/D independently retain lexical content, observed cue, source-authorized interpretation and learner response. Contradictory or weak cues cannot override explicit source facts, negations, conditions or refusals; ambiguous intent is not a hidden definitive answer. Asking for clarification may be the source-valid independent outcome.
3. **Speaker, recipient, reaction and commitment scope.** B/D/F associate each reaction or gesture with the correct message, person, time and recipient. Acknowledgement, receipt, politeness, tentative possibility and explicit acceptance remain distinct; one participant's signal never becomes a whole group's commitment.
4. **Actual cue and assistance chronology.** A/C/F record what the learner actually heard, saw or read before each attempt, including captions, punctuation, tutor explanations, retries and cue render times. An answer-bearing rendered annotation contaminates later independent evidence; an assigned but unrendered annotation does not. Offline receipt order and replay do not rewrite the learner's original exposure.
5. **Access, culture and privacy boundaries.** A/C/F use only consented task-relevant media and source-validated, variety-aware interpretations; no facial emotion, accent, disability, nationality, deception or personality inference. A validated construct-equivalent alternative may satisfy its authorized objective; a different-construct text substitute cannot silently certify listening or visual recognition. Missing media/access yields UNSUPPORTED, not failure.
6. **Reversible instruction with uncertainty.** D/E/F log feasible alternatives, selected/assigned/actually delivered method, support dose, task/topic/access controls, delayed first-unaided outcomes, uncertainty, safe fallback and alternative sampling. Immediate cue-assisted success cannot establish a fixed method winner; conflicting delayed evidence can reverse the recommendation.
7. **Delayed independent and unseen transfer, protected skills.** A–F require source-authored fresh cue realizations, genuinely different recipients/contexts where appropriate, meaningful intervening time/material, first-unaided comprehension and production where required, and source-defined communicative outcomes. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Preserve CEFR prerequisites, listening/reading/writing/speaking/free-production obligations, due reviews and exit gates.
8. **Interest separation and version/replay invariance.** E/F rotate preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts without changing the cue construct, objective or rubric. Effective source/media/caption versions apply only at authorized times. Session-qualified replay deduplication, reordered network receipt and irreconcilable-version quarantine preserve source evidence and reversible method rationale.
9. **Assertion-level reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit task/source/CEFR/rubric/evaluator hashes, cue/speaker/recipient alignment, consented media and accessibility provenance, uncertainty, exact learner-first attempt and rendered help chronology, commitment/interpretation verdicts, delayed/unseen denominators, skill/review coverage, candidate-method rationale, replay permutations and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Synthetic or documentation CI PASS is not proof of shipped multimodal ability, real learner gains, CEFR mastery or a production defect.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; deterministic seed/clock; real versioned CEFR-linked source tasks; independently adjudicated cue meanings and speaker/recipient scope; consent-respecting trustworthy media or source-validated accessible alternatives; actual delivered cue and scaffold chronology; separate first-unaided and assisted attempts; source-defined recipient outcomes; delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer; comparable or explicitly noncomparable method/topic/access conditions; protected skills/reviews/exit requirements; follow-up denominators; replay/version permutations; and assertion-level verdicts. Without source-valid cues, trustworthy modality/render chronology or independent follow-up, the relevant claims remain UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

A stronger claim that cue-sensitive instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator or ethically reviewed study, validated multilingual/variety and accessibility adjudication, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis, consent/privacy safeguards and monitoring for overconfident cue interpretations. **A strong tutor teaches learners to use communicative signals without inventing commitments or claiming to read minds.**
