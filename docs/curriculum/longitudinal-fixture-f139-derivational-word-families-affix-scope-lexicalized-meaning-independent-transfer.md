## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Transparent derivation and word-class shift.** In a fictional community-class notice, **“The teacher will explain the plan clearly”**, a learner must distinguish **teach** (verb), **teacher** (person noun) and **clearly** (adverb related to **clear**). Learner A assumes that every word sharing a root can occupy the same grammatical position; learner B independently explains the role and produces a natural message: **“The teacher explained it clearly.”** Counterfactual: replace **teacher** with **teaching**; the meaning and syntactic role change, and the oracle must not award person-noun evidence merely for recognizing the shared stem. A word-family diagram or highlighted suffix shown before the response makes that response assisted. The goal is communicative interpretation/production, not isolated labeling of parts of speech.

**B — Affix scope and non-equivalent negatives.** A fictional library sign says **“These bags are reusable, but the damaged bag is unusable.”** In the reviewed English context, **reusable** means capable of being used again; **unusable** means not usable. **Not reusable** does **not** necessarily mean **unusable**: a bag might work for a single use but not repeated use. Learner A treats all visible negative or repeat prefixes as interchangeable; learner B independently paraphrases the difference to another volunteer. Counterfactual: replace **unusable** with **not reusable**; the oracle must no longer infer that the bag cannot be used even once. Counterfactual 2: substitute **unhelpful** for **harmful** in a separate low-stakes description; not being helpful does not by itself establish causing harm. Judge the actual reviewed context and target-language conventions, not universal English prefix rules.

**C — Lexicalization and false decomposition.** A fictional book-review sentence says **“Her advice was invaluable.”** In the reviewed English sense, **invaluable** means extremely valuable, not worthless. Another sentence uses **“I understand the instructions”**; **understand** cannot be interpreted as physically standing under something. Learner A applies a naive negative **in-** rule to *invaluable* or splits *understand* into literal spatial parts; learner B checks contextual and attested senses and communicates the intended meaning independently. Counterfactual: substitute **valueless** for **invaluable**; the intended evaluation reverses. A valid learner may say that a form is unfamiliar and request context; guessing a compositional meaning with unwarranted confidence is not independent lexical mastery. Etymological history is not a required explanation.

**D — Restricted productivity, naturalness and lexical-family traps.** A fictional club update uses **“The committee made a decision after the explanation.”** The reviewed English families include **decide → decision**, **explain → explanation** and **arrive → arrival**. Learner A extends a superficially similar **-tion** pattern to produce *arrivation* in standard contemporary English; learner B independently uses **arrival** in a new message and knows that a plausible-looking affix combination is not automatically attested or idiomatic. Counterfactual: present an explicitly fictional coined word in a creative-writing activity; the evaluator may accept the coinage **for that stated creative purpose**, but cannot silently count it as conventional formal usage. Separate morphology from spelling, register and vocabulary frequency; do not punish independently reviewed regional or specialized variants merely for differing from a default variety.

**E — Actually delivered lexical cues, multilingual boundaries and replay.** The authoritative target-language sentence contains **“invaluable”** but an accessible caption substitutes **“valueless”**; an ASR transcript substitutes another word; a highlighted prefix suggests the wrong boundary; a translation invents a one-to-one English affix correspondence; or autocorrect replaces the learner's authored form. Preserve the **actual delivered** sentence, word boundary, pronunciation and assistance chronology. A first response authored at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED if a word-family hint was queued at 09:59 but **first rendered** at 10:01; a revised answer after the hint is ASSISTED. If a caption or audio substitution destroys the target contrast, quarantine only that affected judgment as **UNSUPPORTED**, not learner failure. A Spanish, French, Arabic, Japanese or other target-language variant needs its **own reviewed lexical oracle**; do not transplant the English segmentation or assume the same affixes and word classes. Duplicate immutable event replay cannot create extra first attempts or delayed evidence.

**F — Longitudinal adaptation, confounding and unseen lexical transfer.** Fictional learner A gets immediate answers right after **MORPHEME_BOUNDARY_AND_MEANING** but repeatedly misreads lexicalized words on delayed unaided tasks. After comparable, **actually delivered** **CONTRAST_TRANSPARENT_VS_LEXICALIZED** and **CONTEXT_FIRST_LEXICAL_INFERENCE** activities, A later independently explains a **new** derived expression in a message to an unfamiliar recipient. Fictional learner B instead improves delayed independent production after **WORD_CLASS_CONTRAST** and **LEARNER_GENERATED_CONTEXT_SENTENCE**. Match baseline vocabulary, descriptor, affix familiarity, transparency, frequency, word class, contextual clues, modality, time interval and follow-up opportunity; otherwise flag METHOD_FREQUENCY_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_TRANSPARENCY_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_CONTEXT_CLUE_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Merely changing a topic while reusing the same lexical item, sentence template and sense is **not** unseen transfer; require an unexposed word family or new semantic/communicative demand with a reviewed oracle. Track eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided results may support an **observed change in later actually delivered instruction**, with uncertainty, continued alternative sampling and reversal after contrary evidence. Interests in games, music or travel may increase related example probability but must not exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar topics or any required CEFR objective.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and target-language linkage.** A–F resolve a versioned communicative can-do/blueprint, validated lexical and skill prerequisites, target-language/variety/register oracle, task/rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, due reviews, delayed and unseen probes and exit gate. No invented CEFR derivational descriptor or cross-language English-rule shortcut.
2. **Transparent derivation and grammatical function.** A distinguishes stem relationships from derived part-of-speech/function, and derivation from inflection. A shared root or correct suffix name without independent communicative use is insufficient for mastery.
3. **Affix attachment and semantic scope.** B preserves **reusable / unusable / not reusable** contrasts and contextually warranted meaning. Counterfactuals update only licensed claims; “not helpful” does not silently become “harmful.”
4. **Lexicalized senses and false segmentation.** C requires attested/contextually appropriate meanings for **invaluable** and **understand**. Surface resemblance or etymological speculation cannot override reviewed current usage; uncertain interpretations may elicit appropriate clarification.
5. **Productivity, attestation and register.** D separates a theoretically parseable coinage from conventional lexical usage, preserves reviewed variants and rejects unsupported generalization from **decision / explanation** to *arrivation* as a standard form.
6. **Accessible delivery, language boundaries and first-unaided authorship.** E compares authoritative with **actually delivered** text/audio/captions/ASR/translation/highlights/autocorrect; invalid lexical contrasts are quarantined. Only help actually exposed before authorship marks an attempt assisted; replay is idempotent. Target-language analyses require independent review.
7. **Matched instructional alternatives and calibrated uncertainty.** F compares only feasible and delivered methods with matched frequency, transparency, lexical familiarity, morphological depth, context informativeness, objective, modality and follow-up. Confounds, sparse data or contradictory evidence require no method winner and a balanced fallback; preserve bounded exploration.
8. **Durable independent transfer and coverage.** Delayed first-unaided retrieval and genuinely new lexical-family/recipient demands govern adaptation; immediate hinted success and same-item repetition do not. Preserve protected receptive/productive modalities, due reviews, common mastery gates, nonexclusive interest rotation and explicit missing-follow-up accounting.
9. **Honest release boundary.** A passing synthetic fixture, document, QA workflow or scaffold toggle does **not** establish that a tutor changed instruction in response to outcomes or improved retention. Require exact-engine executable assertions, observable changed **delivered** decisions, longitudinal independent outcomes against a balanced baseline and later ethically reviewed learner evidence before any effectiveness claim.
