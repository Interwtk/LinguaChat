# F226 — Multi-turn interaction, pragmatic repair and independent communicative transfer

**Status: DESIGN-ONLY / UNSUPPORTED.** Synthetic longitudinal curriculum acceptance fixture, not an implemented conversation assessor, speaking/listening capability, CEFR certification, evidence of human-learning gains or permission to experiment with learners. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable. No Auth, cloud, infrastructure, production, Supabase, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge or B2 Arc 6 policy mutation.

**Governing contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F224 addresses answer-bearing item exposure; F225 addresses challenge/difficulty drift. **Distinct failure under test:** a tutor can reward isolated sentence accuracy, model-led dialogues or transcript-mediated responses as though they establish independently sustained, context-appropriate interaction. Real communicative transfer requires tracking what the learner contributes across turns, including initiation, contingent responses, clarification and repair, without assuming that every hesitation or request for clarification is a failure.

## Construct binding and immutable interaction ledger

Bind every scenario to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do** and independently reviewed meaning/function rubric, communicative setting, interlocutor role, prerequisite demands, skill/channel requirements, acceptable language varieties, accessibility accommodations and independent exit evidence. These scenarios do **not** invent new CEFR objectives or universal pass scores. Oral, audio and real-time dialogue evidence remain **UNSUPPORTED** wherever the product lacks independently verified capture/evaluation. Text simulations may test textual interaction only; they cannot certify oral/listening ability.

For each interaction freeze before choice: pseudonymous learner and can-do/rubric versions; candidate methods and feasibility/accessibility; interlocutor policy/model version; topic interest weight; prompt and dialogue family; communicative intent and pragmatic burden; difficulty and item-lineage review; planned turns; actual delivered prompts and model/answer-bearing hints; timestamped learner turns and corrections; independent versus scaffolded status **per turn**; interlocutor leading/cueing; learner-initiated clarification and successful contingent repair; independent unseen and meaningfully delayed follow-ups; latency provenance/uncertainty; due/offered/rendered/completed/missing counts; and stable session/attempt/turn/event IDs with occurrence and arrival separately.

A dialogue's apparent fluency is not a sufficient mastery metric. Review whether the learner independently communicates the required meaning, adjusts to an unanticipated but valid response, and recovers from misunderstanding. A model's perfect replies, a scripted branch, or an answer supplied before the learner turn must not inflate the learner's contribution. Valid requests such as “Could you repeat that?” may be positive communicative evidence when the rubric requires clarification; they are not automatically incompetence. Treat missing, inaccessible or unobserved turns as UNKNOWN, not errors or successes.

## Six deterministic synthetic histories and matched controls

**A — Isolated correctness is not sustained interaction.** On three distinct sessions, A0–A2 are unassisted isolated sentence responses meeting 9/10, 10/10 and 10/10 reviewed form demands. A3 is a due, unrendered multi-turn task for the *same existing objective* where the interlocutor changes an appointment time and the learner must respond contingently. Unsafe policy certifies full interaction because isolated grammar is strong. **Expected:** retain the valid isolated evidence, but interaction/repair remains UNMEASURED and cannot be inferred from form accuracy. **Matched control:** render an independently reviewed, accessible, unseen contingent exchange; only actual learner turns meeting the rubric can update interaction evidence, not phantom oral skill.

**B — Interlocutor cueing creates apparent fluency.** B0 records a frozen pre-choice choice between worked-example dialogue P and retrieval-first dialogue Q. B1–B3 show three correct learner replies, but the tutor first supplies each target phrase or asks a leading yes/no question. B4 is an independent unseen response due but not rendered. Unsafe policy counts all B1–B3 as independent multi-turn mastery and permanently favors P. **Expected:** record legitimate scaffolded practice and tutor cue exposure, not independent exit; maintain method uncertainty. **Matched control:** independently reviewed, non-leading, semantically equivalent dialogue with no answer-bearing prompt before the learner response and a meaningful delayed transfer opportunity.

**C — Clarification is a communicative strategy, not an automatic failure.** C0 is an unfamiliar but reviewed accessible exchange. C1 learner asks for repetition without receiving the answer. C2 interlocutor repeats the same meaning without simplifying the objective. C3 learner gives a correct context-sensitive answer and C4 later performs an unseen independent repair. Unsafe policy penalizes C1 as failure or treats the repeated turn as answer-bearing scaffolding by default. **Expected:** independently review whether clarification itself meets the can-do, distinguish neutral repetition from target-answer revelation, and retain independent contribution where genuinely unassisted. **Matched control:** same rubric and burden with a genuine answer-bearing model revealed at C2; C3 must then be marked assisted, while C4 is independently adjudicated.

**D — Speech recognition, delays and accessibility are not language deficits.** D0 is an oral probe offered on an unsupported/unverified audio path. D1 contains an ASR transcript with a missing negation, D2 an observed long pause during a device/network interruption, and D3 a text-only correct answer. Unsafe policy scores the ASR transcript as a learner semantic error, infers weak fluency from elapsed time and certifies speaking from D3. **Expected:** mark oral/listening evidence UNSUPPORTED without a reviewed capture and transcription oracle; latency contaminated by device/network conditions is not mastery evidence; text response may count only toward its valid construct; retain accessible alternatives and oral skill debt. **Matched control:** a supported, consented and independently reviewed audio capture/transcription with channel-matched semantic rubric, accessible timing and a separate unseen oral probe. No synthetic audio claim is allowed.

**E — Favourite topic masks pragmatic demand.** E0–E2 are correct scripted travel dialogues where the interlocutor always accepts a request immediately. E3–E4 are neutral/unfamiliar dialogues requiring a polite negotiation after refusal; outcomes are missing because the tutor never offers them. Unsafe policy equates the easy travel scripts with negotiation, filters non-travel topics and concludes that the travel context is a superior teaching method. **Expected:** separate topic affinity from pragmatic burden, mark unrendered negotiation as UNMEASURED, preserve adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation and the shared exit standard. **Matched control:** reviewer-approved interest and neutral contexts with equivalent refusal, register, turn contingencies, item novelty and channel; collect delayed independent transfer before any cautious method inference. If equivalence is unavailable, return NOT_COMPARABLE.

**F — Late turn revisions, replay and incomplete dialogues.** F0 freezes a choice among two accessible methods with low confidence and a three-turn unseen exchange due. F1 learner independently completes turn 1 (attempt F-T1); F2 replays F-T1 under the same ID; F3 is a model-led turn 2 with answer exposure; F4 records a due turn 3 never rendered; F5 arrives late with independently reviewed evidence that the interlocutor's original turn 1 was mistranscribed, preserving original and revised rubric/channel versions; F6 is a genuinely delivered unseen neutral exchange with outcome pending. Unsafe policy counts F-T1 twice, counts F3 as independent, treats F4 as a failure, retroactively rewrites F0 and declares the dialogue complete. **Expected:** deduplicate by stable identity, preserve occurrence versus arrival and immutable original choice, keep both review versions and a NOT_COMPARABLE bridge until adjudicated, count actual delivered turns only, retain pending/skill debt and revise *future* hypotheses without backdating. **Matched control:** render a valid independently reviewed turn 3 with trusted answer isolation; its result may update later mastery, never historical F0.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed objectives and gates:** every case preserves the existing reviewed CEFR can-do, meaning/function and pragmatic rubric, prerequisites, accessibility and independent exit requirements; no Pre-A1 edit or A1–C2 release.
2. **Construct-specific evidence:** isolated form, scripted dialogue, text interaction, listening, speaking and contingent repair are distinct; no cross-channel or cross-construct certification without independent validation.
3. **Learner contribution versus tutor contribution:** leading prompts, supplied phrases, transcript exposure and model-led dialogue are provenance-tagged per turn; assisted success cannot become unassisted mastery.
4. **Clarification and repair adjudication:** a legitimate request to repeat or clarify is not automatically a failure; neutral repetition versus answer-bearing support is reviewed against the fixed communicative objective.
5. **Contingency and delayed transfer:** full interaction evidence requires actually rendered, independently reviewed contingent turns and meaningful unseen/delayed follow-ups; due but unrendered dialogue is neither pass nor fail.
6. **Channel/accessibility honesty:** ASR errors, device delay and unsupported audio cannot be scored as learner deficits or oral proficiency; accessible alternatives preserve construct where possible and retain skill debt otherwise.
7. **Interest and challenge separation:** favourite topics remain soft weights, not exclusions; compare only matched pragmatic burden, item novelty, channel, difficulty and missingness, preserving unfamiliar and neutral contexts.
8. **Chronology and reversible choice:** stable turn/attempt IDs, trusted occurrence/arrival, review revisions, pre-choice alternatives, actual delivered dialogue and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators survive duplicates and late events without rewriting prior choices.
9. **Executable and efficacy boundary:** an independent exact-head harness must bind real reviewed CEFR objectives, semantic/pragmatic and audio/accessibility oracles, transcript/render chronology, matched controls and per-assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED. Synthetic checks alone do not prove an implemented tutor or human-learning gains; later efficacy requires ethical longitudinal independent transfer evaluation and subgroup/missingness analysis.

## Independent verification and non-goals

A future harness must report exact curriculum/engine HEADs, executable command, reviewed source can-do/rubric and pragmatic equivalence, valid channel and audio/transcription support, independent reviewer signoff, turn-by-turn learner versus tutor contributions, hint/render chronology, stable deduplicated events, pre-choice and delivered policy, independent first attempts, meaningful delayed unseen interaction, due/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators, matched-control results and each case/assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED. Without those bindings, **F226 remains UNSUPPORTED**.

No real learner monitoring, live experimentation, new audio capability, Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase changes, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, CEFR level release or B2 Arc 6 policy change is authorized.

**Verdict:** a strong tutor should teach and support interactive repair, but must measure what the learner can independently communicate across real contingent turns, not how fluent the tutor makes a scripted exchange look.
