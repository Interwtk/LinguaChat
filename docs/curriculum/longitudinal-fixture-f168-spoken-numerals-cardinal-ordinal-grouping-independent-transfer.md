## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Thirteen is not thirty.** A fictional club has exactly **13** chairs in Room A and **30** in Room B. The reviewed, clearly audible English source says **“Bring thirteen chairs to Room A.”** The learner independently tells the recipient **“Thirteen chairs for Room A”** and selects the 13-chair plan. “Thirty” changes the recipient's action and fails when the sound distinction was genuinely available. Counterfactual: background noise masks the critical syllable; **“Did you say thirteen or thirty?”** is the correct targeted clarification, not an unsupported guess. A visible transcript showing 13 before response invalidates a claim of first-unaided listening.

**B — Cardinal count versus ordinal place.** In a fictional classroom queue, **“There are five students”** gives a count; **“Please call the fifth student”** identifies the student in position five, not all five. A learner independently calls the fifth student in the reviewed queue and explains the distinction naturally without needing to name grammatical categories. Counterfactual: the queue order was never supplied and two arrangements remain plausible; **“Who is fifth in this queue?”** is valid clarification. A language/variety may encode ordinals differently; use its reviewed realization, not an English suffix-only scoring rule.

**C — Numeric label versus numerical value and leading zero.** A fictional library uses exact printed shelf labels **“04”** and **“4”** for two different bins; the source-world says to take the book from shelf **“zero four.”** An actionable readback **“Shelf zero four, with the leading zero”** selects the correct bin. In a separate task counting books, **“four books”** and a displayed **“04”** can represent the same quantity if the reviewed counting task permits that representation. Do not generalize an opaque identifier's exact-string rule to all counts. If the source is visually blurred or the audio could mean either bin, ask which label rather than inventing certainty.

**D — Spoken grouping, place value and unresolved role.** A fictional event has table **115** and table **150**; the speaker clearly says **“table one-one-five”** with reviewed digit-by-digit intent, and the learner relays table 115. In a different fictional exchange **“Meet me at one fifteen”** lacks the surrounding context needed to determine whether this means 1:15 or an opaque meeting-point label 115; **“Do you mean the time or the meeting-point number?”** is a correct targeted question. The word sequence alone does not justify silently selecting a time. A reviewer must distinguish language interpretation from doing arithmetic or guessing local time conventions.

**E — Rendered signal, first authorship, accessibility and event replay.** At 09:00 a reviewed original audio says **“forty”**; TTS incorrectly renders **“fourteen,”** an ASR caption shows **“40,”** and a screen reader exposes **“forty”** before a purported listening response. Quarantine construct-changing audio/caption variants rather than marking the learner wrong for a number never heard or granting unaided listening credit from a visible answer. At 09:02 the learner, before any actually rendered tutor cue, independently asks **“Was that fourteen or forty?”**; at 09:03 a highlighted answer-bearing comparison appears; at 09:04 the learner says **“forty.”** Preserve the 09:02 FIRST_UNAIDED clarification and label 09:04 ASSISTED. A comparison selected but never rendered does not contaminate 09:02. Deduplicate replayed event IDs; do not treat accessible reading as unheard audio evidence.

**F — Outcome-responsive method exploration, regression and unseen transfer.** Learner A repeats teen/ty pairs accurately while a visual chart is present but fails delayed first-unaided recipient-facing spoken number selection. Across multiple matched **actually delivered** NUMBER_TO_RECIPIENT_ROLEPLAY opportunities, later unaided accuracy improves on **new numbers, speakers and recipient actions** in neutral and unfamiliar contexts; cautiously increase roleplay probability while continuing chart, minimal-pair and clarification exploration. Learner B initially benefits from roleplay but later independently confuses **sixteen/sixty** on a genuinely new task: lower confidence, bring review forward and reopen alternatives. If minimal-pair tasks use clear three-syllable examples while roleplay tasks use degraded audio, unfamiliar accents and longer number strings, infer **no method winner**. Reusing “thirteen” with a music-club story instead of a game-club story is a topic swap, **not** unseen numeral transfer. Missing delayed probes remain MISSING, not failures or evidence of gain.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing objective and reviewed numerical oracle.** A–F bind a versioned existing CEFR communicative can-do, prerequisites, genuine recipient information gap, language/variety, reviewed number/role/label oracle, actual accessible source, independent rubric and threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Spoken number-word contrast.** A distinguishes 13 from 30 only when the actually rendered source makes the contrast recoverable; under real ambiguity it accepts a targeted teen/ty clarification and rejects an invented confident number.
3. **Cardinal versus ordinal recipient action.** B preserves a count of five versus the fifth person's identity and accepts natural language-variety equivalents; missing order is clarified, not guessed.
4. **Quantity versus opaque-label identity.** C preserves task-specific leading zeros and grouping for exact labels without imposing identifier rules on ordinary mathematical counts. Equivalent formats require explicit reviewed source-world support.
5. **Grouping, role ambiguity and practical readback.** D preserves a reviewed spoken digit sequence and distinguishes time from label interpretation only with adequate context; otherwise it accepts concise recipient-actionable clarification. Do not infer a unique role from bare “one fifteen.”
6. **Actual delivery, chronology, authorship and accessibility.** E audits source versus actually rendered audio/TTS/ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader output; quarantines number-changing drift, protects pre-help first authorship, records answer exposure and deduplicates stable events. Reading, listening and free spoken production are not interchangeable evidence.
7. **Controlled uncertain reversible method selection.** F compares feasible **actually delivered** methods with matched number length, phonetic confusability, role, language/variety, modality, help, source quality, topic, prior exposure, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, contradictory, selective or confounded evidence yields no winner; later regression reduces confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Assisted repeats, same-session practice, copied digits and topic-only swaps do not earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, **new number/contrast and changed recipient task or role**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided correct actionable response or justified clarification, and explicit missingness. Preserve shared CEFR gates, modality balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Documentation CI is not executable numeral QA, shipped adaptive tutoring or proof of improved human learning. Require exact-head executable assertions against real objectives and demonstrated longitudinal outcome-driven instructional changes, then independently reviewed later unaided transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design/probe histories, not observed learner outcomes or executable QA. Preserve task-specific numeric meaning, ambiguity, actual accessibility, reversible adaptation and explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and independently reviewed longitudinal evidence exist.
