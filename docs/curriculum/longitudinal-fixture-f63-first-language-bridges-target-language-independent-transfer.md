# F63 — First-language bridges must support, not replace, independent target-language transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped multilingual adaptation, proof of CEFR coverage, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F49 protects construct-preserving accessibility; F53 protects context-appropriate register/variety; F57 separates confidence from competence; F60 tests explanation depth; F62 separates recognition from independent production. F63 isolates **strategic first-language (L1) or other known-language bridges**: concise translations, contrastive explanations and permitted multilingual rehearsal can be useful *instructional* options, but their apparent short-term benefit cannot certify independent **target-language (L2)** performance. No permanent "translation learner" type, monolingual-only ideology, assumption that the learner has exactly one L1, or prohibition on ordinary multilingual communication is justified.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real versioned** CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisite, task, intended communicative meaning/function, target language and acceptable varieties, source rubric/evaluator, permitted code-switching/translation/mediation, independent evidence standard, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, review obligations and exit gate. Some source tasks may explicitly assess **mediation across languages** or permit multilingual interaction: score those against their actual source-defined construct. For a task requiring independent target-language production, a translation displayed by the tutor, a bilingual word bank or a model that supplies the answer is instructional assistance, not unaided production. Never invent a universal "L2-only" graduation rule.

Predeclare comparable, feasible candidate methods: `TARGET_LANGUAGE_EXPLANATION`, `BRIEF_KNOWN_LANGUAGE_GLOSS`, `CONTRASTIVE_EXPLANATION`, `BILINGUAL_REHEARSAL`, `FADED_BRIDGE` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. A learner may know several languages with different proficiencies, literacy and familiarity; explicit preferences and voluntarily provided language information are **soft access/context signals**, not fixed learning-style assignments or evidence of benefit. Unknown proficiency stays unknown. Do not infer nationality, ethnicity, migration status, intelligence, disability or language ability from interface locale, accent, name or latency. If an actually supported bridge language or reliable translation is unavailable, use a safe target-language/neutral fallback and mark the bridge `UNAVAILABLE`; do not fabricate translations.

For comparable histories record target language and versioned source, candidate/selected/actually delivered instructional option, known-language choice and its provenance, objective-specific first unaided semantic attempt, optional observed latency, retries, hints, glosses, word banks, translated target phrases, models, error types (including false friends and pragmatic transfer), self-repair, original response language(s), source-permitted mediation, exposure chronology, independent delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer. Record eligible/assigned/offered/rendered/attempted/completed and missing follow-ups, per-target practice exposure and feedback, item difficulty, topic/context, session-qualified immutable event IDs, trusted occurrence versus upload times, rubric/translation versions, and review debt. Separate **linguistic accuracy**, **pragmatic adequacy**, **construct validity**, **assistance provenance**, **method hypothesis** and **interest context**. Predeclare `BRIDGE_SELECTED`, `BRIDGE_DELIVERED`, `BRIDGE_NOT_DELIVERED`, `ANSWER_BEARING_TRANSLATION`, `ASSISTED_TARGET_PRODUCTION`, `SOURCE_VALID_MEDIATION`, `INDEPENDENT_L2_PRODUCTION`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_TRANSFER`, `METHOD_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED`, `TRANSLATION_UNVERIFIED`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These are event/task states, not learner identities.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Helpful gloss, unsupported mastery claim.** Learner A initially misunderstands a request in the target language, sees a brief known-language gloss and then produces the exact revealed target phrase correctly. Learner B receives a target-language paraphrase and independently formulates a semantically equivalent new request without a revealed answer. Record A's supported improvement as instruction, not independent mastery; score B only if its first attempt and task construct truly qualify. Later unseen, unaided target-language probes—not immediate polished replies—decide whether the gloss hypothesis gains support.
- **B — Contrastive explanation versus dosage confound.** Two learners share a versioned objective and comparable baseline false-friend errors. A contrastive known-language explanation seems to reduce errors relative to target-language examples, but the contrastive arm received more examples, corrective feedback and target exposure. Mark `METHOD_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED`. Only matched opportunities, feedback, novelty and later independent target-language outcomes can support a provisional method preference; do not mistake extra practice for a language-bridge effect.
- **C — Multilingual communication versus target-language assessment.** A learner code-switches naturally to repair a real conversational misunderstanding; the interaction succeeds. A source-defined mediation task may legitimately credit that repair, while a separate source-defined target-language production task still requires its own independent evidence. A second learner uses an approved source-permitted multilingual mediation route without a tutor supplying the answer. Preserve the two distinct task constructs and do not universally punish code-switching or silently award L2 production mastery.
- **D — Several known languages, ambiguous gloss and accessibility.** A learner uses two known languages with uneven literacy; the interface language does not reliably identify the most helpful explanation language. A proposed gloss is idiomatic in one variety but misleading in another. Treat the unverified translation as `TRANSLATION_UNVERIFIED`, avoid answer-bearing delivery, retain an accessible target-language or verified neutral alternative, and keep uncertainty high. Neither translation preference nor slower text entry proves linguistic inability or a fixed teaching type.
- **E — Reversal, balanced language skills and topic rotation.** Early matched independent probes suggest short contrastive explanations improve delayed target-language writing for one learner, while target-language examples help another. Later independently completed unseen speaking/writing tasks reverse the first learner's apparent advantage. Revise the objective-specific method hypothesis, continue feasible alternative sampling and protect due reviews and required listening, reading, speaking and writing opportunities. High engagement with travel-themed bilingual examples must not exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar contexts.
- **F — Offline answer leakage, replay and rubric drift.** A bilingual gloss is assigned but fails to render; a translated model is displayed offline before an allegedly independent response but syncs later; one first attempt uploads twice; two sessions reuse a local activity ID; and a rubric revision changes whether multilingual mediation is allowed. Reconstruct trusted actual exposure and source-specific task chronology using session-qualified IDs and frozen rubric/translation versions. Deduplicate, quarantine ambiguous answer exposure and unsupported version migration, and produce the same evidence ledger and next provisional decision under either upload order.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-defined language construct.** For A–F output real level-qualified can-do, blueprint, prerequisite, target language/acceptable varieties, task, evaluator/rubric version, source-permitted multilingual mediation, independent threshold, delayed/transfer gates, reviews and exit gate. Missing mappings are `UNSUPPORTED`; unavailable modalities or bridge languages are `UNAVAILABLE`. Do not replace required L2 production with unrelated translation or recognition evidence.
2. **A revealed translation is assistance, not independence.** In A/F distinguish first unaided target-language meaning, subsequent gloss/model exposure, supported revision and genuinely new unaided production. A copied or paraphrased revealed answer cannot become independent simply because it is typed rather than selected.
3. **Selection is not delivery.** In D/F log eligible, selected, offered, rendered/heard, actually seen/used, attempted and completed bridge events. Unknown or failed rendering is not evidence that an explanation was received; delayed upload must not conceal answer-bearing exposure.
4. **Method comparisons control opportunity and translation quality.** In B/D/E report differences in prerequisite readiness, known-language access, translation validity, item novelty/difficulty, practice dosage, feedback, cue exposure, target-language load, topic and timing. Mark unmatched comparisons `METHOD_COMPARISON_CONFOUNDED` or `TRANSLATION_UNVERIFIED` rather than declaring a superior pedagogy.
5. **Task-specific code-switching and mediation.** In C, apply the actual source construct: communicatively valid multilingual mediation may earn mediation evidence when authorized, but cannot be silently recast as unaided target-language speaking/writing. Avoid blanket "no L1" penalties and blanket "all multilingual success counts" substitutions.
6. **Durable unaided target-language outcomes.** In A/B/E require meaningfully delayed, source-valid, independently completed target-language retrieval and genuinely unseen-context transfer for the affected objective. Record eligible/offered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Same-day reopens, calendar rollover, immediate assisted correctness and repeated revealed phrases are insufficient.
7. **Multiple languages, learner agency and access.** In D/E treat volunteered language preferences as revisable, non-diagnostic signals. Do not infer language proficiency from locale, identity, accent, speed or reluctance. Sample only verified, available and accessible methods; retain balanced skills and learner agency without weakening common CEFR standards.
8. **Reversible, separate and replay-safe policy.** In E/F emit alternatives, chosen and delivered instruction, uncertainty, independent later outcomes and the next reversible decision. Contradictory evidence may reverse a method choice; offline reordering/duplicates cannot change it. Keep interest-topic weights separate, with adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar rotation and protected due reviews.
9. **No documentation-to-efficacy inflation.** For each history report exact source/rubric/translation IDs, task-construct and skill-gate ledgers, selected versus delivered bridges, opportunity/exposure counts, first/final attempts, cue chronology, delayed/transfer outcomes and missingness, topic/skill coverage, hypothesis/uncertainty, replay-order comparison and assertion-level `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. Green documentation CI proves neither executable multilingual adaptation nor CEFR certification nor improved human retention.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, injected seed/clock, immutable session-qualified synthetic events and upload permutations, real source-resolved can-do/task/rubric versions, verified bridge-language and translation provenance, delivered-versus-selected evidence, answer-bearing exposure chronology, comparable opportunities or explicit confounds, source-valid first unaided outcomes, meaningful delayed retrieval and unseen independent target-language transfer with eligible/offered/completed/missing denominators, construct-specific multilingual mediation gates, topic/skill balance, reversible decisions and per-assertion verdicts. Unknown exposure, unsupported translations, unvalidated source equivalence, unavailable modalities and missing follow-ups remain unknown or unsupported.

Any claim that a first-language bridge improves **durable independent target-language retention and transfer** requires separately reviewed longitudinal real-learner evidence against a credible balanced comparator, sufficient delayed unaided outcomes, checks for harm among sparse-data, multilingual and accessibility-supported learners, and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor may use a language the learner knows to explain a concept, but it must still establish which language skill the learner can perform independently.**
