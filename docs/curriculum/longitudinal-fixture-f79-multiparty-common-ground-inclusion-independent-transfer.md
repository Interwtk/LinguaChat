# F79 — Multiparty common ground, audience inclusion and independent collaborative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a shipped group-interaction evaluator, CEFR certification, reproduced production defect or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 protect actual instruction delivery and tutor-origin attribution; F47/F48 preserve noncompensatory source-defined mastery; F49 preserves accessibility equivalence; F50 protects topic breadth; F53 respects legitimate register/variety; F54 addresses breakdown repair; F58 protects learner initiative; F66 varies interlocutors; F71 guards referent coherence; F72 tracks goals and constraints; F74 protects mediation fidelity; F77 addresses turn management; F78 tests *bilateral* common-ground alignment. **F79 isolates source-required multiparty coordination:** independently tracking what different participants know, making a necessary contribution understandable to its intended audience, checking or repairing a material mismatch that affects the group's communicative goal, and responding to valid contributions without the tutor scripting the coordination. Bilateral agreement between two speakers is not proof that a third participant shares the plan. Conversely, equal airtime, forced round-robin turns, universal invitations to speak, or a particular culture's group etiquette are **not** invented CEFR requirements. Apply only to a real, versioned CEFR-linked collaborative-interaction/mediation can-do and task that genuinely require these moves.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real, versioned** CEFR descriptor/can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, source-defined group task and communicative goal, participant roles/relationships, language varieties, source-required information and interaction moves, allowed norms and accommodations, channel, evaluator/rubric versions, assistance limits, independent threshold, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer, due reviews and level exit gate. Freeze a participant-by-proposition knowledge matrix and each scripted participant's private instructions **before** comparing methods; the learner must not receive omniscient access. Mark source-relevant propositions `SHARED_BY_ALL`, `SHARED_BY_SUBSET`, `PARTICIPANT_ONLY`, `APPARENT_GROUP_CONSENSUS`, `MATERIAL_GROUP_MISALIGNMENT`, `REPAIRED_GROUP_ALIGNMENT`, `AMBIGUOUS` or `UNOBSERVABLE`. These are adjudicated **scripted task-state** labels, not mind-reading of real participants. For an individual-only task, return `NOT_APPLICABLE` rather than manufacture group evidence.

Predeclare reversible feasible instruction candidates `AUDIENCE_KNOWLEDGE_CONTRAST`, `MULTIPARTY_PERSPECTIVE_ROLEPLAY`, `GROUP_SUMMARY_AND_CHECK_PRACTICE`, `CONTRIBUTION_INTEGRATION_PRACTICE`, `OPEN_UNSEEN_GROUP_TASK` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. They may vary implemented/evaluable listening, reading, writing, speaking and free-production mix, explanation, activity order, scaffold, feedback, pacing, examples and review spacing **without** changing the source-defined objective, participant-information burden, independent standard or skill requirements. A tutor's highlighted missing participant, prewritten group recap, suggested question, annotated participant-knowledge matrix or model inclusion phrase is **assistance** for the affected move. A scripted participant's ordinary source-permitted objection, clarification or contribution is task interaction; a participant unexpectedly disclosing the exact target answer is a coaching leak. Keep feasible alternative sampling, balanced abilities and favorite/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation; never assign permanent “group learner,” “shy,” “auditory” or similar types.

Use deterministic pseudonymous multi-session histories with injected seed/clock and immutable session-qualified event IDs. Capture source/can-do/task, group roster/roles and language varieties, frozen per-participant knowledge and source-valid alternative readings, actual channel, first learner-authored audience-directed contribution/check/recap/repair, addressed participants, who actually received and understood which source-required proposition in the frozen simulation, and source-defined group outcome. Log selected/assigned/**actually rendered** method, tutor cues/models and trusted display chronology, interlocutor replies and script deviations, first-attempt quality, retries/scaffold dependence, optional calibrated timing only when reliable, topic familiarity, group size/composition, accessibility and language-ability confounds, independent delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen group/context/information-asymmetry transfer, and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Do not infer every participant's understanding from one “okay,” count messages as inclusion, treat asynchronous response lag as hesitation, or infer spoken group management from typed simulations.

Evidence labels: `SOURCE_VALID_GROUP_TASK`, `SUBSET_AGREEMENT_ONLY`, `GROUP_CONSENSUS_UNVERIFIED`, `MATERIAL_PARTICIPANT_EXCLUDED_FROM_REQUIRED_INFORMATION`, `AUDIENCE_ADAPTATION_INDEPENDENT`, `GROUP_KNOWLEDGE_CHECK_INDEPENDENT`, `VALID_GROUP_SUMMARY`, `GROUP_MISALIGNMENT_REPAIRED_INDEPENDENTLY`, `SOURCE_VALID_ROLE_ASYMMETRY`, `FIRST_INDEPENDENT_GROUP_MOVE`, `TUTOR_GROUP_CUE_EXPOSED`, `ASSISTED_GROUP_MOVE`, `PARTICIPANT_TASK_REPLY`, `PARTICIPANT_COACHING_LEAK`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_GROUP_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_GROUP_TRANSFER`, `TOPIC_METHOD_CONFOUNDED`, `GROUP_COMPOSITION_CONFOUNDED`, `ACCESSIBILITY_CONFOUNDED`, `FOLLOWUP_MISSING`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `TASK_STATE_VERSION_MISMATCH`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. ## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Two agree; the third cannot act.** In a source-defined three-person coordination task, A and B agree that “the revised time” is 15:30; C joined late and still has 14:30. Learner A closes after B says “agreed,” and C follows the old plan. Learner B independently recaps the time to all source-required recipients, notices C's mismatch, clarifies and completes the task. A source-valid variant assigns C no responsibility for this decision; it must not impose an irrelevant inclusion move. Score actual source-defined audience coverage and outcome, not number of invitations or politeness.
- **B — A tutor supplied the perfect group recap.** Tutor renders “Tell C the venue changed, then ask both participants to confirm their roles.” Learner A copies it and the group succeeds **with assistance**. In a meaningfully later session with new participants and different unseen information asymmetry, learner B independently discovers a missing role assignment, asks a source-valid group question and completes the goal. A merely selected but unrendered model cannot contaminate first independent evidence; an answer-bearing cue actually displayed before the move must.
- **C — Legitimate unequal roles and language varieties.** A facilitator and two participants have unequal speaking responsibilities under a source-validated task. Learner A mechanically insists on identical speaking time and misses a material disagreement; learner B accepts valid role asymmetry, paraphrases a participant's unfamiliar but source-valid expression for the relevant audience and checks the actual decision. Another culturally legitimate indirect contribution achieves the same source-defined construct. Do not impose equal turn counts, English-centric phrases or one group's politeness convention.
- **D — Apparent method advantage reverses.** Matched early independent group probes favor `GROUP_SUMMARY_AND_CHECK_PRACTICE` for learner A and `MULTIPARTY_PERSPECTIVE_ROLEPLAY` for learner B. On delayed unseen group tasks with different membership, source-equivalent knowledge gaps and equivalent communicative burden, A's advantage reverses. Match or disclose group size, role distribution, language variety, interlocutor cooperativeness, task difficulty, topic familiarity, practice dose, modality/accessibility, feedback and rubric versions. Reduce confidence, sample alternatives and record the **actually rendered** revised instruction.
- **E — Favorite contexts and access do not define ability.** A learner coordinates a favorite-game team smoothly but omits a required recipient in neutral community-event and unfamiliar service-planning tasks. A text-access user completes a source-validated equivalent collaborative construct but has no recorded audio/prosody evidence; another caption names the participant missing information and thus constitutes assistance. Rotate favorite, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts, keep due reviews and receptive/productive obligations, and never infer disability or network delay as group incompetence.
- **F — Offline replay, changed roster and missing participant reply.** Two offline group sessions reuse a local message ID; a participant's private brief changes under the same run ID; a tutor's missing-participant highlight was displayed before a learner recap but uploaded later; one participant's reply never arrives; and a rubric revision changes who must receive the decision. Use session-qualified immutable IDs, frozen roster/task-state/rubric hashes and trusted cue delivery chronology. Deduplicate true replays, quarantine conflicting versions, preserve missing participant and follow-up data as unknown, and require identical independent labels, group/review debt and next-choice rationale across receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real source-defined multiparty construct.** For A–F emit versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, source-defined group goal, participant roster/roles, knowledge matrix, required audience and moves, allowable norms, channel, rubric, assistance rules, independent threshold, delayed/unseen probes, due reviews and exit gate. Missing source mapping or invented equal-airtime/inclusion duties yields `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Subset consensus is not group completion.** In A/C distinguish bilateral agreement, information received by each source-required participant, actual knowledge mismatch, source-valid role asymmetry and independent goal completion. An unaddressed material participant cannot be credited as aligned because someone else said “yes.”
3. **Audience-aware contribution, not participation counts.** In A/C/E credit source-appropriate independently initiated recaps, checks, paraphrases and integration of contributions only when they advance the actual task. Message count, universal round-robin invitations and formulaic “everyone agrees?” do not replace verified source-required information transfer; legitimate concise or indirect alternatives remain eligible.
4. **Tutor versus participant provenance.** In B/F preserve first learner-authored move, exact answer-bearing tutor cues and trusted render chronology. Rendered group-knowledge hints make affected moves assisted; merely assigned unseen cues do not. Frozen participant dialogue remains normal task interaction unless it leaks a tutor-like solution outside protocol.
5. **Variety, modality and access boundaries.** In C/E validate legitimate role/variety-specific practices and source-equivalent accommodations. Text-only evidence cannot establish unobserved audio or speech; a caption revealing the missing recipient is assistance. Optional latency counts only when directly captured and reliable.
6. **Independent delayed unseen group transfer.** In B/D/F require meaningfully separated sessions, intervening time/practice, genuinely new participant constellation and information gap, first unaided group move and completed source-defined group outcome. Rehearsed scripts, immediate replay, tutor-supplied recaps and absent participant replies cannot establish durable independent group competence; emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
7. **Comparable method effects and interest breadth.** In D/E match or disclose group size/roles, information asymmetry, language variety, interlocutor behavior, topic familiarity, practice dose, feedback, channel and accessibility. Preserve CEFR goals, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and favorite/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation; interests never become topic filters.
8. **Reversible adaptation and replay invariance.** In B/D/E/F emit feasible options, selected/assigned/actually rendered instruction, later independent outcomes, confidence/contradictions, exploration and next choice. Sparse or confounded data falls back to `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Validate roster/task/rubric hashes, deduplicate stable events and reconstruct trusted cue chronology; receipt-order permutations must produce identical independence, missingness, group/skill/review debt and rationale. Irreconcilable provenance fails closed.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen per-participant knowledge and valid alternatives, first/final learner moves, actual rendered assistance, audience coverage, group outcome, independent delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay equivalence, policy uncertainty and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI does not prove a shipped group evaluator, CEFR certification or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock and multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR group tasks with validated multilingual/variety-aware rubrics, frozen participant knowledge and role expectations, captured actual cue delivery and first learner-authored group moves, independently completed delayed unseen group/context/information-gap probes with denominators, comparable instruction/topic/group/access exposure, protected skill/review obligations, replay-order permutations, reversible next-instruction traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, group-state adjudication, delivery provenance, equivalent modality evidence or follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown, never invented proficiency.

Any claim that adaptive instruction **improves durable independent multiparty collaboration** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, meaningfully delayed unseen group exchanges, multilingual/variety and accessibility evaluator validation, human adjudication, privacy and subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps a learner notice who needs which information to achieve a shared goal—without dictating every turn or manufacturing group consensus.**
