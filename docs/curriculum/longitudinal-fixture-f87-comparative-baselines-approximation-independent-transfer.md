## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Direction and baseline are not interchangeable.** A source-authored exchange says route Blue takes 18 minutes and route Green takes 24 minutes, with the addressee asking which is *quicker than Green*. Learner A says Green is six minutes quicker than Blue despite producing fluent comparative grammar; learner B independently states that Blue is six minutes quicker than Green. A later request switches the reference to Blue: both must preserve direction relative to the newly named baseline. Source-normalized durations avoid turning this into a mental-arithmetic exam.
- **B — Degree, equality and negation scope.** A task licenses three descriptions: the second package is *slightly heavier* than the first; the third is *about as heavy as* the first; and the fourth is *not as heavy as* the second. Learner A collapses all into “heavier,” or interprets “not much heavier” as “lighter”; learner B independently preserves comparison direction, approximate equality and the task-validated scope of negation. Accepted varieties and natural alternatives are rubric-validated, not judged by literal English word order.
- **C — Approximate does not mean exact.** A source marks a meeting duration as “around 40 minutes,” and an unrelated departure deadline as “exactly 4:00.” Learner A promises a precise 40-minute ending or treats the exact deadline as approximate; learner B independently distinguishes estimated duration from firm time. If the source gives no justified tolerance, do not invent a numeric interval or assume “around” licenses any value. A later source revision must not retroactively make an earlier exact claim appropriate.
- **D — Changing reference frames and missing comparators.** A source compares a desk with a doorway width, then a different desk with a shelf depth; the addressee switches from *wider than the doorway* to *shallower than the shelf*. Learner A keeps the previous reference or compares width with depth; learner B independently tracks the dimension and the new baseline. When the shelf depth is absent, an appropriate clarification is accepted rather than a guessed comparison. This is relative referent management, not F84's left/right perspective.
- **E — Short-term fluency reverses under delayed unseen transfer.** Matched immediate tasks make `BASELINE_CONTRAST` appear better than `COMPARE_AND_JUSTIFY_DIALOGUE` on fluent comparative utterances. At a later genuinely separate session, novel speakers and new baseline switches produce stronger independent, source-accurate communication following dialogue practice. Match or disclose task complexity, source access, practice dose, feedback, register, variety, modality, topic familiarity and accessibility. Lower method certainty, explore feasible alternatives, and log the **actually rendered** next instruction rather than creating a permanent “dialogue learner” label.
- **F — Topic familiarity, inaccessible chart and offline replay.** A learner compares gaming device weights correctly but reverses a neutral commute comparison and avoids an unfamiliar community-schedule comparison. A color-only chart is inaccessible; an unanswered probe is merely offered, not completed. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; an answer-bearing tutor comparison cue was rendered before the learner's response but uploaded afterward; source/rubric versions conflict. Deduplicate only genuine session-qualified replays, quarantine unresolved versions, preserve missingness and show identical assistance, comparison interpretation, review debt and next-method rationale across receipt-order permutations. Written evidence cannot certify listening or speaking mastery.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid CEFR construct.** A–F require a real versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, task/outcome, target language/varieties, validated comparative readings, source baselines/dimensions, evaluator/rubric, assistance policy, threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing mappings, ambiguous comparisons or unvalidated target-language forms yield `UNSUPPORTED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, never invented proficiency.
2. **Direction, referent and dimension fidelity.** In A/B/D preserve which item is compared with which baseline, the direction and the licensed dimension/unit. Reject reversing “quicker than,” switching width to depth, or changing a comparator even when the resulting sentence is grammatical.
3. **Degree, equality, precision and scope.** In B/C distinguish “slightly,” “much,” approximate equality, “not as,” “not much,” “around” and “exactly” according to task-validated semantics. Preserve negation scope. Do not invent numeric tolerances, one-to-one English marker mappings or claims unsupported by the source.
4. **Baseline switching, missing information and repair.** In A/D recognize addressee-driven baseline changes; independently repair a miscomparison when authorized source facts change; accept clarification if a required reference or measurement is unavailable. Do not substitute guesses or tutor-provided answers for learner evidence.
5. **First independent move and rendered-cue provenance.** In D/F anchor the first learner-authored comparison to trusted render chronology. An answer-bearing comparison, model or highlighted baseline rendered beforehand makes the affected move assisted; an assigned-but-never-rendered cue does not. Keep legitimate task measurements distinct from interlocutor coaching leaks.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent communication.** In C/E/F require meaningful separation across real learning sessions, new source items/dimensions or baseline switches, first unaided target-language comparisons and observed source-defined communicative outcomes. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Same-session rephrases, assisted repetition, memorized examples and incomplete follow-ups cannot prove durable transfer.
7. **Comparable adaptation and noncompensatory skill protection.** In E/F match or disclose difficulty, measurement access, register, variety, modality, dose, feedback, topic familiarity and accessibility. Preserve shared CEFR objective, receptive/productive evidence, due reviews, and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation; interest weights never become exclusion rules.
8. **Reversible hypotheses and receipt-order invariance.** In E/F emit feasible methods, assigned/selected/actually rendered instruction, later independent outcomes, confidence/confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse or conflicted data use `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen versions and trusted render chronology must produce receipt-order-invariant assistance labels, interpretation, review debt and method choice; quarantine unresolved conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen comparative facts and references, first/final learner-authored moves, actual rendered cues, observed outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay-equivalence evidence and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is not an executable evaluator PASS, a CEFR award or evidence of human-learning improvement.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked comparative tasks, validated target-language/variety-aware rubrics, frozen baselines/dimensions/units/precision and versions, trusted rendered-cue chronology, first unaided comparative communication, source-defined outcomes, completed delayed genuinely unseen transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skills/review obligations, replay-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, comparator, validated meaning, accessible modality, reliable evidence or completed follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

A claim that adaptive instruction **improves durable independent comparative communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen exchanges, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor teaches learners to communicate what is being compared, to what, by how much and with what precision—without hiding behind fluent but misleading language.**
