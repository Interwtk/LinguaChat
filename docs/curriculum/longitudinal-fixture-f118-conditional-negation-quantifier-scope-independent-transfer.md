# F118 — Conditional, negation and quantifier scope in independent communicative transfer

This does **not** ship a semantic parser, cross-language logic evaluator, safety-critical decision engine, CEFR mastery or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 protects genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 distinguish actually rendered help from assigned help and tutor-origin errors; F49 protects accessible construct validity; F50 keeps interests nonexclusive; F63 covers first-language bridges; F68 addresses metacognitive calibration; F104 method confounds; F107 unreliable feedback; F109 code-switching; F110 multimodal cue ambiguity; F112 TTS noise; F113 speaker attribution; F117 variety and audience fit. **Distinct construct:** when a source-authored communicative task uses negation, quantifiers, conditional language, exceptions or temporal restrictions, can the learner recover the **correct scope, commitment and permitted action** and express them independently in a new context, without a tutor silently substituting a logically stronger/weaker claim or treating ambiguity as certainty? Do not assume formal truth-table semantics for every natural-language construction: language, dialect, context, modality, conversational implicature and source-approved readings matter.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Negation scope changes the claim.** In a source-authored message, "Not every package arrived" means that at least one did not arrive, **not** that no package arrived. Learner A independently selects "none arrived" after a tutor has repeatedly shown simplified all-or-none examples; learner B correctly distinguishes "not all" from "none". Score the source-validated communicative consequence separately from grammaticality and tutor exposure. A counterfactual changes only the source stimulus to "No packages arrived": the correct interpretation must change while the objective and level stay fixed. For target languages with different negation constructions, require source-validated equivalent readings rather than literal English mapping. If context makes the intended scope genuinely ambiguous, clarification remains valid.

**B — Quantity bounds are not interchangeable.** A source-valid task asks for "at least three forms" and a separate task asks for "no more than three forms". The learner's first-unaided plan to submit four satisfies the first and violates the second; exactly three satisfies both. An evaluator that awards both because the learner identified the number "three" fails. In a controlled counterfactual, replace "at least" with "exactly" while holding topic, quantity and difficulty comparable. Keep source-authorized numeric representation, units and accessibility route fixed; audio confusion between numbers is a channel issue (F112), not proof of missing quantifier knowledge. Do not treat completion of an external transaction as evidence unless the source task explicitly and safely validates the communicative construct.

**C — Necessary conditions do not promise outcomes.** The source-authored instruction "You can enter only if your name is on the list" makes being listed **necessary**, not a guarantee of entry. Learner A infers "I'm listed, so entry is guaranteed"; learner B says "I still need to check the other requirements." A parallel source-valid case "If the alarm sounds, leave the room" requires recognizing the stated trigger without assuming the converse ("If it does not sound, it is always safe"). Score appropriate interpretation, clarification and action choice against source-authored conditions; never turn these synthetic examples into real-world safety or access advice. If the task source supplies a biconditional or an additional sufficient condition, the interpretation can change accordingly.

**D — Exceptions, temporal boundaries and uncertain status.** A learner reads "Do not submit the form until the reviewer approves it" with approval currently **unknown**. First-unaided submission is not supported; requesting confirmation or waiting is source-valid. A source-authored later message confirms approval, making submission permitted under that version. A different instruction says "Submit by Friday unless an extension is approved": an unconfirmed extension does not automatically cancel the original deadline. Validate local time/date and inclusive/exclusive deadline conventions from the task source rather than imposing universal interpretations. In a spoken variant, TTS drops "not"; log AUDIO_TEXT_CONFLICT and mark affected listening inference UNSUPPORTED instead of marking the learner wrong.

**E — Method adaptation requires later independent semantic transfer.** Fictional learner A performs well on assisted negation drills but repeatedly over-strengthens statements in delayed unseen requests. After actually delivered ACTION_CONSEQUENCE_ROLEPLAY, A improves on first-unaided transfer to new recipients and topics. Learner B shows the reverse pattern and improves after SCOPE_CONTRAST_MINIMAL_PAIRS. Compare CEFR objective, baseline, task complexity, language/variety, stimulus scope, modality/access route, topic familiarity, assistance rendered, review interval and attrition. Only with comparable longitudinal evidence may the next actually delivered method differ by learner with uncertainty bounds; continue sampling alternatives and preserving all skills and contexts. If roleplay always tests easy "not all" cases and minimal pairs always test harder "only if" cases, flag SCOPE_METHOD_CONFOUNDED instead of a winner. Later contradictory outcomes must lower confidence and allow reversal.

**F — Source version, speaker and event-order integrity.** An offline learner answers "You may send two copies" before a source revision changes the authorized quantity to "at most one". The original first attempt is scored against its original effective source/rubric, never retroactively penalized. Another session reuses a local task ID for a different speaker and recipient, and one event is uploaded twice. Deduplicate by session-qualified immutable event IDs, preserve source-effective version and speaker attribution (F113), and quarantine irreconcilable conflicts. A tutor hint appears in the queue before the answer but is actually rendered **after** it; the first answer remains unaided. A new delayed probe must be independently authored and genuinely unseen; a recycled minimal pair with changed names is not transfer. Missing follow-up remains missing, not success or failure.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real source/CEFR linkage and level validity.** A–F resolve versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, source task, language-specific scope readings, rubric/evaluator, assistance and accommodation policy, independent threshold, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Do not manufacture advanced logic requirements or target-language equivalents.
2. **Scope-sensitive meaning adjudication.** A/B distinguish wide/narrow negation and exact/minimum/maximum quantity; preserve action-relevant meaning and critical bounds. Equivalent source-validated expressions pass; unvalidated readings remain READING_UNVALIDATED/UNSUPPORTED, not automatically wrong.
3. **Conditional direction and uncertainty.** C/D distinguish necessary versus sufficient conditions, exception applicability and true/false/unknown antecedents. No converse, guarantee or permission is inferred without source evidence. Source-valid clarification and pending decisions receive their own adjudication.
4. **Temporal and speaker/source fidelity.** D/F use source-effective deadlines, revisions, authorized local time conventions and speaker attribution. A later version or another speaker cannot rewrite earlier independent evidence or silently authorize a new action.
5. **Actual exposure and first-attempt chronology.** A/E/F record first unaided meaning/action, hint/model/translation actually rendered before response, retries, later rewrites and answer-bearing exposure. Queued or post-answer help cannot retroactively contaminate an independent first attempt.
6. **Construct-valid modality and uncertainty.** B/D distinguish TTS/ASR, audio-text conflicts, numerical rendering, accessibility accommodations and source-approved language varieties from learner semantic errors. Missing reliable stimulus or equivalence yields bounded UNSUPPORTED, not fabricated proficiency loss.
7. **Delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require separated sessions and new source-authored speakers, situations, scope constructions or action contexts without answer-bearing hints. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; copied phrasing and cosmetic topic swaps cannot pass unseen transfer.
8. **Reversible adaptive selection with topic diversity.** E/F log feasible, assigned and actually delivered methods, comparable baseline/objective/scope difficulty, language, modality/access, exposure, uncertainty, topic and missingness. Preserve alternative sampling, protected receptive/productive skills, due reviews and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts. No "learning style" labels or interest-exclusive topic filters.
9. **Exact-head reproducibility and bounded conclusions.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, validated readings, first and assisted answers, exposure/event ledger, delayed/unseen denominators, confound/replay/version permutations and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation CI success alone is not an executable semantic evaluator, a reproducible product defect or evidence of real-learner gains.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD, reproducible command and deterministic clock; real versioned CEFR-linked tasks; language-specific independently validated scope interpretations; source-effective constraints and speaker context; construct-valid accessible presentation; first-unaided evidence and actual exposure chronology; meaningful delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen communicative transfer; comparable or explicitly noncomparable methods and contexts; protected skill/review/exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators; event/version/confound permutations; and per-assertion verdicts. Without validated interpretations, reliable channel evidence, source-effective context or independent follow-up, affected claims remain **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, while independently established communicative evidence is retained.

A stronger claim that adaptive scope instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed multilingual, multivariety, accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with a credible comparator, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis, privacy review and reproducible independent outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners understand what was actually promised, prohibited or left uncertain, rather than rewarding confident but unsupported conclusions.**
