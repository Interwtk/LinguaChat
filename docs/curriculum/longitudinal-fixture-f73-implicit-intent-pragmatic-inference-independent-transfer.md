# F73 — Implicit intent, pragmatic inference and uncertainty-aware independent response

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a shipped pragmatic-intent classifier, validated intercultural inference evaluator, mind-reading system, CEFR certification, reproduced production defect or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F53 protects context-valid register and language variation; F54 tests clarification after breakdown; F58 protects learner initiative; F66 varies interlocutors; F71 tracks discourse references; F72 tests goal/constraint management. **F73 isolates source-grounded interpretation of indirect communicative intent:** understanding every literal word, using polite grammar or completing a conversation does not by itself show that the learner noticed a context-supported indirect request, refusal, concern or tentative suggestion. Equally, an indirect utterance may be genuinely ambiguous: the tutor must not invent a speaker's private intention, impose one culture's conventions or punish a source-valid clarification. F73 applies only where a real CEFR-linked source task actually requires pragmatic inference or an appropriate response; it adds no universal implicature gate to unrelated can-dos.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real versioned** CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, prerequisites, task/item, communicative goal, skill and actually available modality, interaction channel, speaker relationship, discourse context, source-supported intended meanings and reasonable alternatives, target-language/variety scope, evaluator/rubric, permitted clarification, assistance limits, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer, due reviews and exit gate. Freeze these before comparing instruction. For ambiguous utterances, record a *set of source-supported interpretations* and the conditions under which clarification is the best response; do not force one hidden 'correct' intention. Source-valid direct speech, indirect speech, refusals and hedges vary by context, speaker and language; no universal English phrase list, mind-state label, stereotype or inferred cultural identity is permitted.

Predeclare reversible instructional candidates CONTEXT_CONTRAST, INTENT_EVIDENCE_MAPPING, DIALOGUE_ROLEPLAY, CLARIFY_OR_ACT_PRACTICE, OPEN_UNSEEN_INTERACTION and BALANCED_DEFAULT. They may vary explanations, contextual examples, modality mix where genuinely supported, activity ordering, scaffold fading, practice dose and spacing while keeping the same source-defined objective, pragmatic burden and independent standard. Tutor-supplied intent labels, translations, answer-bearing paraphrases and scripted responses are **assistance**. An interlocutor's ordinary source-permitted reply to a learner-authored question is part of the interaction, not automatically tutoring. Continue sampling reasonable alternatives; do not assign permanent 'intuitive', 'visual', 'literal' or culture-based learner types.

Use deterministic pseudonymous longitudinal histories with injected clock/seed and immutable session-qualified event IDs. Record source, blueprint, rubric and evaluator versions; exact context, role, preceding turns, channel, topic familiarity and plausible intent set; speaker-authored utterance and learner-visible material; learner's first independent interpretation, evidence cited, uncertainty, chosen response and communicative effect; literal comprehension separately from inferred intent; tutor/interlocutor roles; actual selected/assigned/**rendered** instruction, hint/model/translation/caption exposure and trusted delivery chronology; retries and later revisions; accessible modality and observation quality; independently completed delayed and genuinely unseen probes; and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Do not infer voice prosody from plain text, culture from nationality, or latency from asynchronous messages. Preserve privacy and never claim an unavailable modality was tested.

Evidence labels: LITERAL_MEANING_UNDERSTOOD, CONTEXT_SUPPORTED_INDIRECT_INTENT, ALTERNATIVE_INTERPRETATION_VALID, PRAGMATIC_INTENT_MISREAD, EVIDENCE_BASED_UNCERTAINTY, SOURCE_VALID_CLARIFICATION, CONTEXT_APPROPRIATE_RESPONSE, CONTEXT_INAPPROPRIATE_RESPONSE, FIRST_INDEPENDENT_INTERPRETATION, TUTOR_INTENT_REVEALED, ASSISTED_RESPONSE, INTERLOCUTOR_REPLY_ALLOWED, INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL, INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_PRAGMATIC_TRANSFER, SCRIPT_EXPOSURE, CONTEXT_METHOD_CONFOUNDED, ACCESSIBILITY_CONFOUNDED, FOLLOWUP_MISSING, EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED, RUBRIC_VERSION_MISMATCH, MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE and UNSUPPORTED. Labels are evidence about events, never fixed properties of people.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Literal comprehension without an indirect request.** In a source-defined dialogue, an interlocutor says that the room is cold while looking toward a closable window; prior turns make a request for help a plausible interpretation. Learner A accurately translates the sentence but responds only with a weather fact; learner B independently offers to close the window or asks a source-valid clarifying question. Score literal understanding, contextual evidence, plausible alternatives and communicative response separately. If the frozen task lacks enough context to disambiguate a complaint from a request, do not penalize A for failing to guess the speaker's mind; mark the contested inference UNSUPPORTED.
- **B — Indirect refusal is not literal acceptance.** In a source-defined invitation exchange, the interlocutor replies 'I have to work early tomorrow' after an invitation, with preceding context supporting a polite decline but not logically proving it. A learner treats the utterance as firm acceptance; another acknowledges the possible refusal and responds tactfully or seeks clarification when source-permitted. Evaluate interpretation and response against the full task context, not an isolated keyword or a universal rule that excuses always mean 'no'. A direct refusal or a different valid social convention must remain acceptable when source-supported.
- **C — Hedging, suggestion and genuine uncertainty.** A speaker says 'That might be difficult' while discussing a proposed schedule. In one versioned context it signals a likely obstacle; in another it is a literal uncertainty pending more information. One learner confidently assumes a veto in both; another notices uncertainty, offers an alternative or asks which condition is problematic. Reward calibrated context-supported action where the rubric calls for it; do not award 'pragmatic competence' for overconfident speculation. Preserve all source-valid interpretations and avoid using sarcasm, irony or prosody as a text-only hidden answer key.
- **D — Tutor reveals intent before a polished response.** The learner initially misreads an indirect request, then sees the tutor's explicit intent label and a ready-made reply. The learner reproduces it fluently in the same conversation. In a separate source-equivalent unseen context days later, the learner either independently interprets the indirect move or does not. Keep first attempt, answer-bearing cue and actual delivery time, final assisted polish, distinct-session delay and unseen independent outcome separate. A scripted reply is not independent intent inference, and missing follow-up is not failure.
- **E — Method advantage reverses under matched contexts.** For the same versioned can-do, learner A initially transfers better after CONTEXT_CONTRAST than DIALOGUE_ROLEPLAY; learner B initially shows the opposite pattern. Later independent probes with unfamiliar topics and interlocutors reverse those apparent advantages. Match or disclose source difficulty, context informativeness, directness, role/power relation, variety, familiarity, prior script exposure, practice dose, modality/access and opportunity. Emit uncertainty, contrary evidence, exploration and a changed **actually rendered** next choice; never freeze a learning-style or cultural category. Interest-weighted scenarios may be more frequent but must coexist with adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts.
- **F — Replayed events, late hint and evaluator drift.** An intent explanation is delivered before the learner's revised response but uploaded after it; an offline session reuses a local turn ID; the displayed role/context changes while a cached scenario ID remains the same; a caption exposes answer-bearing information on one route; a delayed probe is offered but not completed; and an evaluator revision changes which interpretations are accepted. Replay multiple receipt orders using session-qualified immutable IDs, actual rendered-context hashes, trusted cue-delivery order, source/rubric versions and explicit migration policy. Deduplicate, quarantine irreconcilable chronology/context and avoid silently reinterpreting past independent credit. No phantom audio evidence or invented follow-up result.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-grounded pragmatic construct.** For A–F emit the actual versioned can-do, blueprint, task, role/context, channel, variety scope, rubric, acceptable meanings/responses, clarification rules, independence, delayed/unseen transfer, reviews and exit gate. Without source validation of pragmatic requirements or interpretation alternatives, disputed credit is UNSUPPORTED; do not add a universal indirect-intent mastery threshold.
2. **Literal and pragmatic evidence are distinct.** In A/B report literal comprehension, contextual clues, source-supported indirect meanings, first independent interpretation and response separately. Literal translation alone cannot prove pragmatic inference; a plausible alternative interpretation cannot be marked wrong just because it differs from one model answer.
3. **Uncertainty is not failure or mind reading.** In A/C emit the competing plausible readings and relevant context. A source-valid clarification, appropriately tentative response or explicit uncertainty may be correct. Reject both unjustified certainty and automatic penalization of noncanonical but reasonable readings; missing context is unknown, not a learner deficit.
4. **Social context and variety remain qualified.** In B/C/E preserve source-defined relationship, genre, channel, communicative purpose and legitimate target-language/variety conventions. Do not derive intention from nationality, ethnicity, accent or stereotypes, assume all hedges/refusals are equivalent, or use an unvalidated single-culture evaluator.
5. **First unaided intent versus revealed answer.** In D/F distinguish learner-authored initial interpretation, permitted ordinary interlocutor replies, tutor-intent labels, translations, captions, scripted answers and actual cue-delivery chronology. Copied replies after answer-bearing exposure are assisted, even if fluent; access accommodations are not automatically coaching but their construct effect must be assessed.
6. **Independent delayed and unseen transfer is decisive.** In D/E/F require meaningful intervening time/practice, genuinely separate sessions and source-equivalent unseen pragmatic contexts, with eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Immediate recognition, memorized dialogue, assisted polish and absent probes cannot establish durable independent competence.
7. **Outcome-driven reversible teaching, not fixed styles.** In D/E/F emit feasible alternatives, selected/assigned/actually rendered method, comparable independent delayed outcomes, uncertainty, counterevidence, exploration and next choice. Sparse, contradictory, inaccessible or confounded observations use BALANCED_DEFAULT; maintain CEFR objective, required receptive/productive coverage, due reviews and soft interest-context rotation.
8. **Rendered-context and replay invariance.** In F verify stable session-qualified IDs, source/role/context hashes, trusted tutor-cue delivery, deduplicated offline events and immutable rubric versions. Arrival-order permutations must preserve first/final independence, accepted interpretation set, follow-up missingness, review debt and next-choice trace; unsupported rubric migration or irreconcilable context fails closed.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F output source/evaluator versions, contextual evidence and alternatives, literal/pragmatic judgments, first/final assisted/independent turns, actual cue exposure, rendered instruction, delayed/unseen denominators, context/method/access confounds, topic/skill/review debt, replay equivalence, next-choice rationale and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. Documentation QA is not proof of a shipped inference engine or improved learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, seeded multi-session events with controlled clock, real source-resolved CEFR interaction tasks, validated multilingual/context-aware pragmatic rubrics with acceptable-interpretation sets, context and role versions, learner-visible utterances, first independent interpretation/response, actual tutor/interlocutor cue provenance, genuinely delayed unseen independent transfer and follow-up denominators, balanced topic/skill and due-review obligations, accessible modality boundaries, replay-order permutations, reversible instructional traces and per-assertion verdicts. Missing source intent validation, alternate-reading adjudication, modality, chronology or follow-up remains UNSUPPORTED/unknown rather than fabricated proficiency.

A claim that instruction about implicit intent **improves durable independent communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence versus a credible balanced comparator, validated multilingual pragmatic judgment with inter-rater uncertainty, ethically reviewed privacy/accessibility and subgroup-harm checks, and independent delayed unseen tasks. **A strong tutor teaches learners to use context, recognize uncertainty and respond appropriately themselves—not to memorize what a hidden speaker supposedly meant.**
