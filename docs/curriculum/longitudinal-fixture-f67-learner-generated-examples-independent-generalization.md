# F67 — Learner-generated examples must earn independent generalization

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced production defect, implemented example-generation policy, validated CEFR mastery, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 guards exposure and genuinely unseen transfer; F35 guards scaffold fading; F37 distinguishes assigned from delivered instruction; F50 preserves soft-interest topic breadth; F51 separates spontaneous from prompted repair; F56 covers feedback timing; F60 compares explanation depth and guided discovery; F62 separates receptive recognition from productive evidence. **F67 isolates the instructional act of generating, contrasting and checking one's own examples:** producing many plausible examples, copying a template or accepting a tutor's correction must not be mistaken for durable, independently generalizable command of the target form/function.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real versioned** CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, language function, task, modality, evaluator/rubric, required semantic/pragmatic criteria, allowed support, independent evidence, meaningful delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, due reviews and level exit gate. Freeze these requirements before comparing instructional options. Do not invent universal example-count thresholds, a requirement that every learner produce examples, or a text-to-speech/oral proficiency claim where the modality cannot be assessed.

Predeclare source-valid instructional candidates `TUTOR_WORKED_EXAMPLE`, `LEARNER_GENERATED_EXAMPLE`, `CONTRASTIVE_VALID_INVALID_EXAMPLES`, `GUIDED_EXAMPLE_REVISION`, `MIXED_GENERATE_AND_COMPARE` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. These are reversible teaching choices for a specific objective, not permanent learner types. The independent evaluation must use a new, source-equivalent communicative demand; repeating one's own previously generated example is exposure, not unseen transfer.

For each comparable learner/objective history, record frozen source/rubric versions, assigned and actually delivered option, displayed example/prompt content hashes, author of each example (learner/tutor/partner), first unaided learner-authored semantic attempt, novelty relative to prior prompts and templates, grammatical and pragmatic validity, meaning-preserving or meaning-changing errors, tutor validation/recast/model content and delivery chronology, learner-initiated versus cued revision, retries, hint/scaffold dependence, optional reliably observed latency, independent delayed retrieval and unseen transfer, and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Use session-qualified immutable event IDs and trusted event occurrence/delivery order, not upload order. Do not store personal stories or sensitive attributes when pseudonymous task-valid content is sufficient.

Predeclare labels `GENERATION_ASSIGNED`, `GENERATION_RENDERED`, `LEARNER_ORIGINAL_VALID`, `LEARNER_ORIGINAL_INVALID`, `TEMPLATE_PARAPHRASE`, `TUTOR_SUPPLIED_EXAMPLE`, `TUTOR_CORRECTED_REVISION`, `LEARNER_SELF_REPAIR`, `COUNTEREXAMPLE_CONSTRUCT_VALID`, `UNSEEN_CONTEXT_UNVERIFIED`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_TRANSFER`, `TOPIC_METHOD_CONFOUNDED`, `FOLLOWUP_MISSING`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These are observations, not learner identities.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Many generated examples, little transfer.** Learner A writes six near-identical sentences after seeing a tutor template and earns high immediate practice accuracy. Learner B writes two imperfect but genuinely distinct examples, revises one without a model and later independently performs the same source-defined function in an unfamiliar situation. A's quantity and polished final text do not outrank B's later independent evidence. Compare target exposure, opportunity, task demand and feedback before favoring a method.
- **B — A correct example can still fail the communicative objective.** A learner produces a grammatically valid conditional that changes the requested meaning; another generates a form-valid sentence with a source-inappropriate register; a third uses a legitimate language variety to convey the required condition. Preserve semantic intent, pragmatic context and source-valid variation. Surface-form similarity or evaluator preference cannot silently replace the frozen rubric.
- **C — Tutor correction masquerades as learner discovery.** A learner proposes an invalid example, the tutor displays the complete target sentence, and the learner copies it accurately. Another learner spots the error and independently revises it before any answer-bearing cue. Record first attempt, who supplied each word/meaningful correction, and actual cue delivery chronology. The corrected copy remains supported practice, not unaided generation or transfer.
- **D — Generation is not universally the best method.** Learner A's matched independent delayed probes favor learner-generated examples after balanced sampling; learner B's favor short worked examples followed by independent application. Later counterevidence for A reverses the ranking. Maintain uncertainty and an objective-specific reversible choice, with a balanced default under sparse evidence. Do not infer a fixed 'creative' or 'visual' learning type.
- **E — Interest topic and accessibility confound the result.** Generated examples use familiar sports situations, while worked examples use unfamiliar administrative language; another learner uses a permitted alternative input method that changes time-on-task. Counterbalance topic familiarity, source-valid language burden, modality and accessible interaction before attributing gains to generation. Rotate adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts and preserve all required can-dos, reviews and receptive/productive coverage.
- **F — Offline replay, prompt leakage and missing follow-up.** An example-generation activity is selected but never renders; a tutor answer is displayed before a revision but syncs later; two sessions reuse a local attempt ID; an old example is reissued with a new cosmetic identifier; the evaluator rubric changes between sessions; and an eligible delayed probe is offered but never completed. Reconstruct trusted chronology from session-qualified immutable IDs, content hashes and rubric versions, deduplicate replay, classify template exposure and keep missing follow-up unknown. Both receipt-order permutations must produce the same independent-evidence ledger and reversible next choice.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Frozen CEFR construct and noncompensatory gate.** For A–F emit the actual versioned can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, task, modality, rubric, semantic/pragmatic demands, allowed assistance, delayed retrieval, unseen transfer, review obligations and exit gate. No example quantity, learner preference or method choice may waive required independent evidence. Unresolved source mappings remain `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Original generation versus reproduction.** In A/C/F distinguish learner-originated meaning from template substitution, tutor-authored models, memorized variants and copies. Track actual rendered content and exposure hashes; cosmetic renaming or a new random seed does not establish novelty. Do not infer unseen transfer from a previously generated sentence.
3. **Semantic and pragmatic validity, not superficial form.** In B require source-defined meaning, register and legitimate variety checks as well as form. A grammatical sentence with wrong communicative intent does not earn objective mastery; a valid accessible or dialectal realization is not penalized without a source-valid criterion.
4. **First unaided attempt and correction provenance.** In A/C/F score the first learner-authored semantic attempt separately from tutor corrections, recasts, hints, learner self-repair, retries and final text. An answer-bearing model delivered before revision makes that revision assisted even if uploaded after it.
5. **Source-equivalent independent delayed generalization.** In A/D/F require meaningful elapsed/intervening practice, genuinely new source-equivalent demands and no answer-bearing assistance. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing probes; immediate example production, same-template reuse and incomplete follow-up cannot prove durable transfer.
6. **Comparable instruction and accessibility truth.** In D/E match or disclose differences in target exposure, opportunity, difficulty, topic familiarity, modality, accessibility, rubric and prerequisite readiness. An unsupported oral modality stays `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`; do not infer reliable latency from asynchronous text or classify a learner by disability, speed or preference.
7. **Actual delivery and replay-safe audit.** In C/F distinguish candidate, assigned, rendered, learner-attempted and completed options. Rebuild tutor/example exposure, first attempts and correction chronology from session-qualified immutable events and content hashes; replay duplicates and receipt-order permutations must not change evidence credit or next-policy choice. Unverifiable chronology fails closed.
8. **Reversible adaptation and protected breadth.** In D/E/F emit feasible options, selected versus delivered method, matched delayed outcomes, uncertainty, contradictory evidence and next reversible choice. Sample alternatives; preserve due reviews, required skill mix and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts. Interest preferences are soft context weights, not mastery evidence or topic exclusions.
9. **Assertion-level results without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/rubric IDs, rendered prompt/example provenance, original versus copied status, first/final semantic outcomes, assistance chronology, delayed/unseen outcomes, follow-up denominators, confounds, modality gaps, review/skill/topic debt, confidence, replay equivalence and per-assertion `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. Documentation-only CI is not a passing engine test or evidence of human learning improvement.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seeded multi-session histories and clock, actual source-resolved CEFR tasks/rubrics, real rendered examples with author and content provenance, first unaided semantic scoring and tutor cue chronology, source-equivalent delayed and unseen probes, eligible/offered/completed/missing follow-up counts, matched instructional opportunities or explicit confounds, protected review/skill/topic coverage, replay receipt-order permutations, reversible policy traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing evaluator, source mapping, rendered activity or follow-up remains unknown, not fabricated success.

A claim that learner-generated examples **improve durable independent generalization** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal outcomes against a credible balanced comparator, adequate independent delayed and unseen transfer, checks for sparse evidence, accessibility and individual harms, and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor judges what a learner can independently do with new meaning and context, not how many polished examples they can produce after seeing a model.**

**Boundary:** curriculum acceptance documentation only. No Pre-A1 edits, A1–C2 release, engine, Auth, cloud, infrastructure, production, Supabase, paid Claude, EvoLabs or auto-merge changes. Integration/watchdog alone aggregates owner actions.
