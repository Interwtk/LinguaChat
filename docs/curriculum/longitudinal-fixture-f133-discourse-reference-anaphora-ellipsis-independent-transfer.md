## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Explicit antecedent versus reversed recipient.** In an independently validated fictional English exchange, “Mara handed the notebook to Theo. **Theo** put it in the drawer.” The first referent of “it” is the notebook; the actor of the second sentence is Theo. Learner A independently says Mara put Theo in the drawer; learner B preserves both entity roles and produces “Theo put the notebook in the drawer.” Counterfactual: revise the source to “Mara put the notebook in the drawer before handing it to Theo”; the event actor/order oracle changes with the source, not with the evaluator's preferred story. An object-pronoun mapping alone must not be used to infer CEFR mastery outside the validated task.

**B — Real ambiguity is not an error to guess away.** A fictional note says, “Asha spoke to Bea after **she** finished the call.” Without independently validated disambiguating context, both antecedents may remain licensed in the specified English variety. Learner A asserts confidently that Bea finished the call; learner B says the note does not establish who finished and asks, “Who finished the call?” Counterfactual: “Asha spoke to Bea after **Bea** finished the call” resolves it; a validated earlier turn explicitly stating that Asha had just ended the call may make Asha a plausible but still defeasible interpretation depending on the complete discourse. The oracle must distinguish **entailed**, **plausible** and **ambiguous**; generic “I don't know” without identifying the referent is not automatically full-credit targeted clarification.

**C — Quotation, role shift and language-specific omission.** Fictional source: “Nia told Omar, ‘**I** will bring **you** the map.’” Within the quote, “I” refers to Nia and “you” to Omar; in a faithful report, Nia will bring Omar the map. Learner A switches the recipient; learner B tracks the quotation boundary. Counterfactual: “Omar told Nia ...” changes the oracle. A separate independently reviewed pro-drop-language example omits a subject where grammar/discourse permits it; the evaluator must use validated local constraints and preserve ambiguity when the omitted actor is not recoverable. English subject-pronoun rules, grammatical gender or automatic translations must not be treated as universal.

**D — Ellipsis, demonstratives and a changing topic.** In a fictional café dialogue: “I ordered the **tea**, and Leo ordered the **juice**.” “Mina did **too**” is presented only after a source-validated explicit antecedent clarifies whether Mina ordered tea, juice or simply placed an order; otherwise the object of the ellipsis may remain unresolved. In another dialogue, “The red folder is beside the blue folder. Please move **that one**,” no pointing gesture or further cue is delivered; the learner should ask which folder. Counterfactual: an actually delivered pointing gesture or a source-authored “the red one” changes the permitted referent. After a topic shift to a different folder, the learner must update the discourse state rather than reuse a stale salient entity. Source-version changes must invalidate prior reference oracles.

**E — Delivered channels, assistance chronology, accessibility and replay.** An authoritative fictional transcript explicitly says “**Bea** called Asha,” but the actually delivered caption says “**Asha** called Bea”; an automatic translation replaces an ambiguous pronoun with a named antecedent; a tutor highlight visually connects “she” to Bea before the learner answers; or a screen reader announces an answer-bearing label before the relevant utterance. Freeze **both** authoritative and actually delivered channels. Quarantine the affected construct as UNSUPPORTED for contradictory/inaccessible source delivery or ASSISTED when answer-bearing help was exposed. A first independently authored response at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED if a reference-chain diagram was queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01; copying it later is ASSISTED. Replay of the same immutable event ID cannot create a second independent first attempt. Accessible equivalents must preserve the reference distinction or explicitly record construct non-equivalence.

**F — Longitudinal reversal, matched difficulty and genuinely unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A resolves short pronouns immediately after colored antecedent arrows but fails delayed first-unaided role tracking in unfamiliar dialogue. After **actually delivered** CONTRASTIVE_ANTECEDENT_PAIRS with gradually faded cues on comparable tasks, A later independently resolves new reference chains and asks targeted clarification for a genuinely ambiguous unseen conversation. Fictional learner B instead improves delayed free-production coherence after SPEAKER_TURN_TRACKING with equivalent language load. Freeze baseline, CEFR descriptor, target language/variety, candidate-entity count, salience, reference distance, quote depth, ellipsis/ambiguity status, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity, hint exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If A receives two explicit named entities and B receives five long-distance ambiguous pronouns, mark METHOD_ENTITY_COUNT_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_REFERENCE_DISTANCE_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_AMBIGUITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Renaming actors in an identical trained antecedent graph is not unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided results may justify **actually changed next instruction**, with uncertainty, alternative sampling and reversal on contrary evidence. Preserve balanced skills, due reviews, shared CEFR gates and nonexclusive interests.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and language-specific source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, reviewed language/variety-specific reference and ellipsis oracles, source entity graph, rubric/evaluator, independent threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Complex zero-subject and long-distance anaphora are not silently assigned to beginners.
2. **Antecedent and role fidelity.** A/C preserve agent, recipient, object and quotation speaker; swapping actor, explicit antecedent or quotation roles changes the oracle. Cosmetic name substitutions with the same entity graph do not.
3. **Ambiguity and warranted clarification.** B/D preserve the complete independently reviewed licensed-referent set, separate entailment from plausible inference, and credit specific clarification where the source does not determine the answer. Neither forced guessing nor generic unsupported certainty earns independent mastery.
4. **Ellipsis, demonstratives and discourse updates.** D recovers omitted content only from validated linguistic/discourse evidence, respects actual pointing/context cues and updates reference after topic changes; missing antecedents remain unknown rather than invented.
5. **Multilingual validity and identity restraint.** C/D validate pronoun systems, zero anaphora, ellipsis, register and regional usage per target language; translations may be meaning-shifting stimuli, not ground truth. No demographic or identity inference from pronoun shape.
6. **Actual delivery, help chronology, accessibility and replay.** E verifies captions, translations, gestures, visual highlighting and screen-reader order against the authoritative source. Answer-bearing help **actually rendered before** first response contaminates independence; queued-but-not-rendered help does not. Event replay is idempotent and contradictory channels quarantine affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen communicative transfer.** A–F require separate real learning opportunities, new referent graphs/discourse shifts/recipients and first-unaided paraphrase, targeted clarification or free production without answer-bearing help. Renamed or cosmetically reordered trained chains are not unseen. Preserve eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
8. **Reversible adaptation and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** instruction, comparable entity count/reference distance/ambiguity, uncertainty, alternative sampling, contrary evidence and **observed change in later instruction** while preserving CEFR objectives, skill balance, reviews and exit gates. Interests only probabilistically bias examples; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/entity graph/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, delivered artifacts, first/help/event chronology, reviewed licensed-referent oracle, ambiguity/quotation/ellipsis/caption/gesture/source-version counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot establish a shipped evaluator, a reproducible runtime defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently validated target-language/variety reference, ambiguity and ellipsis judgments; accessible authoritative and actually delivered stimuli; independently attributable first responses and actual answer-bearing help chronology; delayed unaided retrieval and genuinely unseen reference-graph transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded instructional methods; protected skill coverage, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and antecedent/quotation/role/ambiguity/ellipsis/topic-shift/translation/caption/gesture/replay/source-version counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Unvalidated reference interpretations, conflicting delivered channels, inaccessible constructs, absent or missing follow-ups remain **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not fabricated learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive reference-tracking instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners keep track of who did what, recognize when a text does not say, and communicate that independently in new situations.**
