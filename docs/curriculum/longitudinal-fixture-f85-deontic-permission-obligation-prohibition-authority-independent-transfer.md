# F85 — Permission, obligation, prohibition and authority in independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a shipped rule-comprehension evaluator, legal/compliance advice, CEFR certification, reproduced production defect or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F31 guards against temporary learner-state effects; F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 distinguish assigned from actually delivered instruction and tutor-origin cues; F47/F48 preserve source-defined noncompensatory mastery; F49 accessibility equivalence; F50 broad topic rotation; F53 language varieties and contextual register; F54 clarification and repair; F72 task constraints; F73 pragmatic inference; F74 source attribution; F80 authoritative updates; F81 quantifier/negation/exception scope; F83 conditional commitments. **F85 isolates a different CEFR-linked communicative construct:** independently understanding and expressing **who is permitted, required or forbidden to do what**, by **which source or authority**, under **which source-specified conditions**, and appropriately clarifying uncertainty. It is not a general legal-reasoning, workplace-policy, morality, risk-taking or obedience test. Apply only where a **real versioned CEFR descriptor/can-do and language task** require communicating permission/obligation/prohibition; otherwise `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

## Controlled setup

Resolve a **real versioned** CEFR descriptor/can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, target language and accepted varieties, source-authored task and outcome, validated deontic expressions/readings, evaluator/rubric versions, assistance policy, independent threshold, required receptive/productive skills, due reviews, delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen transfer and exit gate. Freeze source utterances and provenance, speaker/addressee, subject and action, modality (`PERMITTED`, `REQUIRED`, `PROHIBITED`, `OPTIONAL`, `UNKNOWN`), polarity, negation scope, source authority and effective time **only when actually established**, exceptions, revocations, task-permitted clarifications and unresolved conflicts. These labels are evaluator annotations, **not** learner-facing grammar categories or universal interpretations. In particular, “you don't have to” is not automatically “you must not”; “you may” is not “you must”; “you must not” is not “you may choose not to.” Do not assume English modal mappings transfer unchanged across languages, dialects or contexts. A reported claim of permission is not automatically an authoritative grant.

Predeclare feasible, reversible instruction candidates `CONTRASTIVE_MODAL_MEANING`, `SOURCE_ROLE_DIALOGUE`, `SCOPED_EXCEPTION_RECONSTRUCTION`, `LISTEN_AND_DECIDE`, `OPEN_UNSEEN_RULE_EXCHANGE` and `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Vary only actually available and assessable listening/reading/writing/speaking/free production, explanation, scaffolding, pacing, ordering, repetition and spacing. Preserve identical CEFR objectives, task-defined source facts, independent threshold, source-required modalities, due reviews and exit gate. An answer-bearing permission table, tutor-rewritten final rule, highlighted exception or scripted compliant utterance contaminates the affected first learner move **only when actually rendered before that move**. A source-permitted request for clarification is legitimate independent communication; an interlocutor supplying the required deontic answer outside protocol is coaching. Interest examples remain soft, rotated across preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts; no fixed “rule learner” or “dialogue learner” label.

Use deterministic pseudonymous multi-session histories, injected seed/clock, immutable session-qualified event IDs, frozen source/task/rubric hashes, trusted cue-render chronology and validated target-language readings. Record source speaker/authority provenance, source version/effective time, first learner-authored interpretation or utterance, source-defined communicative outcome, assigned/selected/**actually rendered** instruction, first-attempt semantic quality, retries, hints, scaffold dependence, error type, optional calibrated latency, practice dose, language variety, modality, accessibility and topic familiarity. Follow with independent delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer; report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Separate linguistic misunderstanding from contradictory source rules, absent authority evidence, unreadable visual notices, hearing/ASR failure, missing translation validation, network delay and unfamiliar legal systems. A lucky yes/no response or copied rule is not independent deontic communication.

Evidence labels: `SOURCE_VALID_DEONTIC_TASK`, `MODAL_FORCE_PRESERVED`, `NEGATION_SCOPE_PRESERVED`, `SUBJECT_ACTION_SCOPE_PRESERVED`, `SOURCE_AUTHORITY_IDENTIFIED`, `EFFECTIVE_RULE_VERSION`, `EXCEPTION_APPLIED`, `UNCERTAINTY_CLARIFIED`, `FIRST_INDEPENDENT_DEONTIC_MOVE`, `TUTOR_RULE_CUE_EXPOSED`, `ASSISTED_DEONTIC_MOVE`, `INTERLOCUTOR_TASK_CLARIFICATION`, `INTERLOCUTOR_COACHING_LEAK`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_DEONTIC_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_DEONTIC_TRANSFER`, `AUTHORITY_CONFLICT_QUARANTINED`, `ACCESSIBILITY_CONFOUNDED`, `TOPIC_METHOD_CONFOUNDED`, `FOLLOWUP_MISSING`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `VERSION_CONFLICT_QUARANTINED`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Labels are bounded observations, never permanent learner traits.

## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Permission is not obligation.** A source-authored activity allows learners to submit either a voice note or a written note, with neither format mandatory. Learner A interprets “you may send a voice note” as a requirement and incorrectly tells a peer that writing is forbidden; learner B independently communicates the two valid choices in a source-accepted target-language variety. An optional action must not be scored as required just because the learner performs it. If the source never specifies whether the alternatives are exhaustive, preserve that uncertainty.
- **B — Lack of obligation is not prohibition.** A task notice states that participants **do not have to** register for a voluntary workshop; a separate source-defined notice says visitors **must not** enter a restricted room. Learner A collapses both into “not allowed”; learner B independently distinguishes an optional registration from a forbidden entry and communicates the difference. Validate equivalent meanings in the target language rather than English word-for-word modal translation.
- **C — Who, what and exception scope.** A source-defined community event requires **staff** to wear badges in a restricted area, permits **visitors** to enter only with an escort and exempts **staff working outside that area** from the badge rule. Learner A incorrectly applies the staff rule to all visitors or expands the exemption to everyone; learner B independently gives the right instruction to the specified recipient and asks a permitted clarifying question when the person's role is unknown. This tests modal force plus participant/action/exception scope, not general event-management skill.
- **D — A reported rule is not an authoritative change.** A peer reports “someone said the room is open,” while the frozen authoritative notice prohibits entry until a specified time. Later an authenticated source update changes the opening time. Learner A treats the hearsay as permission or repeats the superseded notice; learner B identifies the source and independently communicates the current, source-established rule. When two authoritative versions genuinely conflict without resolution, mark authority/time unknown and request clarification; do not invent a policy hierarchy or provide legal advice.
- **E — Immediate advantage reverses after delayed unseen transfer.** For the same source-validated can-do, `CONTRASTIVE_MODAL_MEANING` produces higher first-attempt scores than `SOURCE_ROLE_DIALOGUE` on matched immediate tasks. After a meaningful delay, new speakers, activities, permission verbs and exception placements show stronger independent performance after dialogue practice. Match or disclose language variety, reading load, authority complexity, topic familiarity, task difficulty, practice dose, modality, feedback and accessibility. Lower method confidence, sample alternatives and record the **actually rendered** next choice; do not label the learner as permanently preferring dialogue.
- **F — Soft interests, offline replays and missing evidence.** A learner performs well on familiar gaming-server permissions but cannot independently explain a neutral museum rule or an unfamiliar classroom exception. An inaccessible image-only notice cannot support a language-deficiency inference. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; an answer-bearing rule card was rendered before the first response but uploaded afterward; a source update and rubric version conflict; and a delayed unseen probe was offered but not completed. Deduplicate only genuine session-qualified replays, freeze source/rubric versions, quarantine unresolved conflicts, preserve missing follow-up and show identical verdicts and next-method rationale under receipt-order permutations. Text-only performance is not evidence of speaking/listening competence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real source-valid construct before scoring.** For A–F require versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, source task/outcome, target language and accepted varieties, validated deontic readings, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, independent threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing mapping or invented rule/authority yields `UNSUPPORTED`, not fabricated proficiency.
2. **Modal force and polarity.** In A/B/E distinguish permission, requirement, prohibition and absence of obligation using validated target-language meanings, including negation scope. Reject meaning-changing “may”→“must,” “not required”→“forbidden,” and “not permitted”→“optional” substitutions even when fluent.
3. **Recipient, action and exception scope.** In C/F preserve who the rule applies to, which action and setting it governs and the exact source-established exception. Reject globalized or reversed exemptions; accept semantically equivalent valid expressions and source-permitted clarification when the role/action is unresolved.
4. **Authority, attribution and effective time.** In D/F distinguish source-authored authoritative rules, reported claims, updates, superseded versions and irreconcilable conflicts. Do not invent legal hierarchy, consent, safety permission, effective dates or a missing exception. Conflicts remain unknown/quarantined until a versioned source resolves them.
5. **First independent move and actual cue exposure.** In A/C/D/F anchor the first learner-authored interpretation/utterance to trusted tutor/interlocutor render chronology. Answer-bearing rule cards, scripted corrections and highlighted exceptions rendered beforehand mark the affected move assisted; assigned-but-unseen cues do not. Distinguish task-licensed clarification from answer leakage.
6. **Delayed unseen independent communication.** In D/E/F require meaningful separation, genuinely new people/actions/modal expressions/authority contexts, first unaided production and source-defined communicative outcomes. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Immediate paraphrases, assisted repeats and absent follow-ups cannot establish durable independent mastery.
7. **Comparable adaptation, accessibility and broad coverage.** In E/F match or disclose task difficulty, language variety, authority complexity, topic familiarity, practice dose, feedback, modality and accessibility. Preserve shared CEFR objective, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation. Interests are context weights, never exclusions.
8. **Reversible hypotheses and deterministic provenance.** In D/E/F emit feasible options, selected/assigned/actually rendered instruction, independent later outcomes, uncertainty/confounds, alternative sampling and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicted evidence uses `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified deduplication, frozen source/rubric versions, trusted render chronology and receipt-order invariance must preserve assistance, modal interpretation, review debt and next choice; quarantine unresolved conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, speaker/authority/version/effective-time facts, first/final learner-authored moves, actual cues, communicative outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay-equivalence evidence and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is not proof of a shipped deontic evaluator, CEFR certification, legal compliance or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked deontic tasks, validated multilingual/variety-aware rubrics, frozen subject/action/authority/exception/time facts, trusted rendered-cue provenance, first independent utterances and source-defined outcomes, completed independent delayed unseen transfer with denominators, comparable instruction/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skill/review obligations, replay-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, reliable authority, validated language reading, accessible modality or follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

Any claim that adaptation **improves durable independent communication of permissions and obligations** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen interactions, multilingual/variety and accessibility evaluator validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps the learner communicate what is allowed, required or forbidden for the right person and situation—not merely recite modal verbs.**
