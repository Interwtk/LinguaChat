# F215 — Cross-language bridging, code-switching, transfer and independent evidence

**Status:** design-only synthetic *cross-cutting* longitudinal curriculum acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until an independently reviewed executable exact-head harness tests the specified choices and evidence. This is **not** a shipped multilingual tutor, a validated causal method comparison, a new CEFR can-do, a change to mastery, or proof of better human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F206 addresses biased instructional comparisons; F208 separates interests from method; F210 distinguishes assistance from independent performance; F212 checks generated-task validity; F214 tests whether a diagnostic is worthwhile. **Distinct question in F215:** when a learner uses another language for clarification, receives contrastive explanations or code-switches during practice, can the tutor use that language as a *revisable instructional bridge* without assuming a fixed language identity, mistaking translation-supported output for independent target-language ability, or overlooking genuine cross-language interference and later transfer?

## Fixed standards and pre-decision evidence

Bind each synthetic history to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do**, target language/variety, independently reviewed meaning-critical rubric, skill family, prerequisites, accessible supported modality, independent receptive/productive requirement, due reviews, context-rotation obligations and existing mastery/exit criteria. English and Spanish examples below are illustrative only, **not** released lessons. The unresolved B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix boundary remains **UNSUPPORTED**; translation or recognition cannot replace required independent target-language production.

At each decision boundary freeze pseudonymous learner, objective/rubric/policy versions; verified learner-selected interface and explanation languages when actually known; observed language use with uncertainty; target-language task and intended language of the response; first unaided semantic attempt; recurring meaning-critical errors across comparable contexts; offered and **actually delivered** translation, bilingual gloss, contrastive explanation, model, transcript, hints and examples; item familiarity; task difficulty and language burden; available alternatives and why some are unavailable; due/skill coverage debt; topic family and *separate* soft interest weights; evidence quality and selection rationale. Device locale, nationality, surname, location or inferred ethnicity are **not** evidence of first language, proficiency or preferred teaching method. Never assign a permanent “Spanish learner,” “translation-dependent” or auditory/visual type.

Keep an immutable event ledger with stable learner/session/run/event identities and trusted occurrence order: task offer, render, language setting, content version, language of instruction and response, whether a translation was shown before the first attempt, semantic rubric result, partial target-language production, code-switch scope, hint/model exposure, feedback, retries, independently reviewed delayed retrieval, genuinely unseen target-language transfer, missing/unavailable modalities and subsequent **actually delivered** instructional choice. Record due/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Unknown chronology or answer exposure is **UNKNOWN/quarantined**, not clean unaided evidence. Deduplicate offline replays.

A bridge is a *supported teaching option*, not a learning-style identity: a brief L1/L2 contrast, bilingual glossary, monolingual reformulation, example, or targeted practice may be selected if accessible, valid and appropriate to the objective. A learner can request another language for an explanation without being classified by it. Compare methods only when tasks, assistance, language burden, contexts and follow-up opportunities are sufficiently comparable; otherwise retain uncertainty and a balanced default. Record the actual choice and revisit after delayed independent outcomes. Preserve skill balance and preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topic rotation. Do not silently require translation-free classroom talk when the objective concerns target-language performance, nor silently credit assisted bilingual talk as the required independent target-language can-do.

Oracle labels: OBJECTIVE_FIXED, LANGUAGE_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN, REQUESTED_BRIDGE, BRIDGE_ELIGIBLE, BRIDGE_ACTUALLY_DELIVERED, BRIDGE_NOT_RENDERED, FIRST_UNAIDED, TRANSLATION_EXPOSED, MODEL_EXPOSED, ASSISTED_REPAIR, CODE_SWITCH_SCOPE_REVIEWED, TARGET_LANGUAGE_EVIDENCE_PARTIAL, TARGET_LANGUAGE_REQUIREMENT_OPEN, VALID_INDEPENDENT_TARGET_OUTPUT, FALSE_FRIEND_HYPOTHESIS_TENTATIVE, CROSS_LANGUAGE_INTERFERENCE_NOT_ASSUMED, LANGUAGE_BURDEN_MATCHED, INTEREST_METHOD_SEPARATE, CLEAN_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL, UNSEEN_INDEPENDENT_TRANSFER, HYPOTHESIS_REVISED, BALANCED_FALLBACK, EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED, FOLLOWUP_MISSING, NOT_COMPARABLE and UNSUPPORTED. These are event/evidence labels, never permanent person categories.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories and matched controls

**A — Locale is not a learner profile.** Two learners have the same fixed English can-do (independently requesting a schedule change), comparable unaided errors and due work. Both use a Spanish interface; only learner A explicitly requests a Spanish explanation, while learner B requests an English paraphrase. An unsafe tutor assumes both have Spanish as a first language and permanently selects bilingual instruction. Expected: LANGUAGE_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN, honor the two actual requests with eligible supported explanations, record what renders and retain uncertainty about which method improves later English transfer. In a matched control only device locale changes: neither target standard nor inferred language competence or instructional confidence may change. A bridge request is not a proficiency score.

**B — A bilingual hint can help without proving independent English.** In session 1 the learner independently writes “I have 20 years” for a reviewed English self-introduction objective. The tutor then actually displays a Spanish–English contrast and the model “I am 20 years old”; the learner copies the correction. Expected: retain the first meaning/form error, label the correction ASSISTED_REPAIR and record both translation/model exposures. In session 2 a distinct-session unseen, unaided English introduction may contribute independent evidence under the fixed rubric; a same-session near-copy cannot. Matched control: if the contrast was only planned and never rendered, do not invent exposure. Neither one error nor one copied repair establishes a stable “Spanish interference” diagnosis or a superior teaching method.

**C — Code-switching has different roles in instruction and assessment.** The learner asks in Spanish for a grammar explanation; the tutor may explain in Spanish without penalty. Later the required English open-production task asks for a complete polite request: “Could you pasar me the report by Friday?” The learner communicates part of the meaning but has not independently produced the full required target-language form. Expected: distinguish classroom help-seeking from target-language evidence, apply the pre-existing reviewed rubric (including any legitimate partial credit), preserve unmet independent English production and offer useful supported feedback without a fabricated pass. Matched control: a later unfamiliar unaided English request meeting the same rubric can count; recognition of the correct translation cannot replace it. Do not invent a blanket ban on multilingual classroom interaction.

**D — Apparent cross-language interference must be tested, not presumed.** The learner uses “actually” when intending “currently” in one English message. A tutor proposes that the Spanish word “actualmente” caused the error, although the learner has not identified Spanish as a language they use. Expected: FALSE_FRIEND_HYPOTHESIS_TENTATIVE, no inferred L1 or permanent label. A short valid contrast may be offered if the error recurs across reviewed independent contexts, but it is instruction/diagnosis exposure and cannot prove its own causal story. Matched control: the learner later independently distinguishes “actually” from “currently” in unfamiliar contexts; revise the hypothesis and spacing. Opposite control: meaning-critical confusion persists on delayed unaided tasks; retain targeted practice and uncertainty without changing CEFR thresholds.

**E — Translation and interest substitutions must not leak the answer.** For the same can-do, one proposed game-themed bilingual prompt includes a parenthetical English translation of the very phrase the learner must independently produce; a neutral prompt does not. An unsafe policy reports the game prompt as superior teaching or easier mastery. Expected: independently reject the pair as non-equivalent, record TRANSLATION_EXPOSED if actually displayed, quarantine contaminated first-attempt claims and use a validated equivalent or neutral fallback. In matched controls where language burden, information state, answer visibility and rubric are independently verified equivalent, varying only interest weights may change topic frequency but not the CEFR objective, method confidence or mastery. Maintain adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts.

**F — Evidence can reverse a tentative bridge preference without erasing history.** Session 1: a bilingual explanation is offered but never rendered. Session 2: a contrastive explanation renders, followed by an assisted correct answer. Session 3: an independently reviewed, unfamiliar English delayed task is completed unaided with a meaning-critical error. Session 4: a different supported English-only explanation actually renders, followed later by a genuinely unseen delayed independent success. Offline uploads arrive 4, 2, duplicate 2, 1, 3; one additional “unseen” item has unknown prior translation exposure. Expected: deduplicate by trusted event identity, distinguish offered/rendered/assisted/independent, quarantine unknown novelty, preserve due debt, and allow a tentative method hypothesis to update without claiming a causal winner from unmatched tasks or two outcomes. Paired control reverses session-3/4 independent outcomes under matched opportunities: the **next actually delivered** teaching option must be allowed to change in the opposite direction or fall back to balanced sampling, never remain locked to a guessed language profile. No invented retention gain from assisted practice.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR target and evidence standards.** Every history binds an existing versioned can-do, target language/variety, reviewed semantic rubric, supported modality, prerequisites, due reviews, independent skill/exit rules and meaningful-turn limits where relevant. No new can-do, altered threshold, B2 Arc 6 recognition shortcut or A1–C2 release.
2. **Language identity and preferences are observed, not presumed.** A records verified requests separately from interface locale and observed utterances; no nationality/locale/name-based L1 inference, fixed language-type label or permanent instruction assignment. Unsupported language options remain unavailable, not silently simulated.
3. **Bridge selection is revisable and auditable.** Publish pre-choice evidence, uncertainty, eligible options, rationale, requested language, actually delivered explanation and later outcomes. A stated preference or an immediate assisted success cannot establish method efficacy.
4. **Assistance cannot become independent mastery.** B preserves first unaided performance and records bilingual gloss, translation, worked model and copied repair as exposure. Only valid later independently reviewed target-language evidence can satisfy the unchanged requirement.
5. **Code-switch scope respects task intent.** C separates help-seeking/teaching language from target-language production; applies the existing rubric to partial communicative success without inventing a universal penalty or promoting recognition/translation to open-production mastery.
6. **Cross-language diagnoses require evidence.** D treats false-friend explanations as uncertain task-level hypotheses, tests plausible alternatives with reviewed tasks, and permits contradictory delayed results to revise them. No diagnosis from a single slip or assumed first language.
7. **Translation validity and interests remain separate.** E independently reviews answer leakage, difficulty, language burden and equivalent information state. Interest weights may influence context rotation but cannot change the method belief, diagnostic target, objective or rubric; neutral/unfamiliar sampling remains.
8. **Offline replay, unknown exposure and counterfactual outcomes are safe.** F publishes stable event IDs, occurrence/upload permutations, actual renders, deduplication, assistance and unknown-exposure quarantine, follow-up denominators, review-debt transitions and opposite-evidence controls. A policy must change *actual delivery* when justified, not merely log a different recommendation.
9. **Synthetic proof is not human efficacy.** Publish machine-readable A–F traces and matched controls, exact objective/rubric/policy/bridge versions, deterministic seed/clock, independently reviewed prompts and translations, event-level expected labels, option/exposure/coverage ledgers, next actual choice and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED output in an independently reviewed exact-head executable harness. A synthetic PASS proves only specified software invariants. Any claim of better durable learning requires a separate ethically reviewed longitudinal comparison against a balanced baseline with comparable opportunity and assessment exposure, delayed unaided retrieval, unseen transfer, missingness and subgroup analysis, and no hidden skill loss. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes or A1–C2 enablement.

**Fixture verdict:** F215 makes **multilingual help without language-identity stereotyping or evidence laundering** falsifiable. A strong tutor may explain through another language, yet must still establish what the learner can independently do in the target language and remain willing to change its teaching hypothesis. **UNSUPPORTED** until independently executed and reviewed; no real-world learning benefit is claimed.
