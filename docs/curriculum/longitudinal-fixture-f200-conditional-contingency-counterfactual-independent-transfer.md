An independently reviewed **conditional-world oracle** freezes exact utterance, speaker, addressee, channel, time, source authority and actual delivery; antecedent(s) and whether observed true/false/unknown; consequent type (plan, permission, obligation, prediction, possibility, hypothetical); deadlines; and actual evidence of decisions, bookings or execution. Review scope and pragmatic ambiguity of “if”, “only if”, “unless”, “might”, “could”, “would” and “as long as” for the language/variety. “Only if” expresses a necessary condition, not necessarily sufficient authorization. A stated intention does not prove execution. Unobserved conditions must not be silently treated as true or false. If natural usage or a policy admits multiple interpretations, accept scoped uncertainty or a targeted clarification rather than an unsupported rigid truth table.

A separate **recipient-action oracle** freezes what the addressee needs now: confirmed facts, pending conditions, responsible verifier, relevant deadline and next step. Accept concise, natural and variety-appropriate paraphrases rather than requiring formal logic notation. Distinguish fact, proposal, advice and speculation. Past hypotheticals may describe plausible alternatives, but do not establish an unobserved causal effect.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Unfulfilled condition is neither confirmed plan nor proven cancellation.** Organizer: “If Friday is confirmed by noon, we'll print flyers Friday afternoon.” At 12:05 no confirmation has been recorded. Nora asks whether printing is scheduled. Accept “We haven't received the confirmation required by noon, so Friday printing isn't confirmed; let's check with the organizer.” Reject “The flyers have been printed”, “Friday is definitely cancelled” and “They'll print because Friday was proposed.” A verified 11:45 confirmation later discovered changes the antecedent evidence, but still does not prove printing occurred. Freeze what was actually delivered before scoring.

**B — Necessary approval is not proof of booking or execution.** Club rule: “We may use Room 5 only if the coordinator approves it.” Theo reports that approval was granted; no room booking or actual meeting is recorded. Maya asks for a status update. Accept “The approval condition appears met, but we still need to verify the booking and meeting location.” Reject “The meeting happened in Room 5” and “Approval alone proves the room is booked.” Later visible booking confirmation supports booking, not a completed meeting. Do not assume every real-world action necessarily complied with a stated policy.

**C — Two conditions and an ambiguous exception.** Workshop note: “We'll hold the activity outdoors if the venue confirms the courtyard and the weather is safe.” Venue confirms; weather remains unverified. Accept “The courtyard is approved, but the outdoor plan still depends on safe weather.” Reject “The activity is outdoors” or “It is cancelled” without evidence. In a separate “Unless it rains, we'll be outdoors” variant, a rain forecast alone does not establish which indoor venue will be used or whether the event is cancelled. Review language-specific pragmatic ambiguity; ask a targeted question when the missing detail matters.

**D — Counterfactual possibility is not established causation.** The group sent invitations late; attendance was low. Maya: “If we'd sent them earlier, more people might have attended.” Asked to explain, accept “The invitations were late and attendance was low. Maya thinks earlier invitations might have helped, but we can't know from this alone.” Reject “Earlier invitations definitely would have doubled attendance” and “They actually sent them early.” Later credible comparative evidence may strengthen the inference; repeating the hypothetical cannot.

**E — Rendered help, failed audio and replay cannot manufacture independence.** At 09:01 the learner independently reads A and says “The flyers were printed.” Preserve the first-unaided error. At 09:02 the tutor renders a complete condition/status/action table; at 09:03 the learner copies a correct update. Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED_UPDATE, not independent mastery. A 09:04 replay is deduplicated. In an audio variant the source fails to play but captions render: no listening credit; a separately bound reading route may be valid. An unopened hint is not exposure. Later transfer requires genuine delay and a new condition structure, not Friday renamed Monday.

**F — Longitudinal choice, counterevidence and soft interests.** Learner A recognizes “if” in isolation but repeatedly reports conditional intentions as completed events. Across comparable real sessions CONDITION_VS_OUTCOME_CONTRAST, TIMELINE_CHECK and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated. Tentatively favor a method only if later first-unaided unseen updates improve under matched complexity, authority, deadline, language burden, channel, help, topic and delay. Learner B initially succeeds after a completed decision tree but later overgeneralizes “only if” as “if”; counterevidence lowers confidence and restores balanced exploration or a necessary-versus-sufficient contrast. Easier conditions, gaming familiarity, extra answer-bearing help or shorter follow-up imply confounding and **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up remains unknown. Gaming may softly weight one example; adjacent clubs, neutral libraries and unfamiliar workshops remain reachable. No CEFR objective or modality is skipped for interests.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **No invented CEFR gate.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed language/variety-specific conditional and recipient oracles; preserve prerequisites, burden, turn ceilings, rubric meaning, thresholds, skill coverage and exit gates; else NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Condition versus execution.** A separates antecedent, deadline, unconfirmed plan and actual printing; missing confirmation and later recovered confirmation do not prove cancellation or execution.
3. **Necessary versus sufficient.** B preserves “only if” approval as necessary and does not infer booking, meeting or completion from approval alone.
4. **Multiple conditions and uncertainty.** C preserves both conditions, avoids inventing an “unless” outcome and asks only justified clarifications.
5. **Actual versus hypothetical world.** D preserves observed facts and tentative possibility without turning an unobserved counterfactual into causal certainty.
6. **First authorship and modality.** E distinguishes independent error, rendered answer-bearing help, assisted copying, unopened hints, failed audio, valid caption route and replay; no phantom independent or listening credit.
7. **Matched reversible adaptation.** F compares actually delivered methods against later independent communication; complexity, authority, deadline, topic, help, channel and delay confounds reduce confidence and prevent causal claims.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Require genuinely new sessions and condition/recipient structures, first-unaided responses, explicit missing follow-up, no script leakage, balanced receptive/productive coverage and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation; never skip a can-do for interests.
9. **Honest evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic histories and generic CI are not executable conditional-language QA, a shipped tutor or proof of improved human learning. Require exact-head executable delivery/provenance/response checks, observed reversible choices and ethically reviewed delayed independent outcomes against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design histories, not observed learner behavior or executable tests. They specify how to communicate contingent plans and hypothetical alternatives without confusing what might happen, what was approved and what actually happened. Remain UNSUPPORTED until independently implemented and verified.
