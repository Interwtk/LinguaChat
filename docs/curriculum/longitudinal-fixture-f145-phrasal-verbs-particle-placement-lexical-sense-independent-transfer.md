## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Separable construction and pronoun placement.** In a reviewed standard-English equipment handoff, the manager says **“Pick up the parcel”**; **“Pick the parcel up”** is also acceptable for this construction/sense. With the pronoun object, **“Pick it up”** is the reviewed ordinary form; **“Pick up it”** is not. Learner A applies the noun-object ordering to every pronoun; learner B independently writes **“Please pick it up from reception”** to a recipient. Counterfactual: a different reviewed construction may be inseparable; do not conclude all English multiword verbs move their particles. If the task has not supplied which parcel **it** refers to, credit **“Which parcel do you mean?”** rather than guessing. The request itself does not establish collection.

**B — Prepositional construction is not a separable particle template.** A colleague asks **“Can you look after the plants while I'm away?”** In the reviewed standard-English care-for sense, **“look after them”** is acceptable, but **“look them after”** is not. Learner A transfers the `pick it up` pattern and produces **“I'll look them after”**; learner B independently replies **“Yes, I'll look after them until Friday”** in an appropriate context. Counterfactual: **“look up the number / look it up”** uses a different construction and sense; sharing `look` does not license transferring its object placement. The offer to care for plants is not proof the care was completed. Do not penalize a separately reviewed natural paraphrase such as **“I'll take care of them”** when the objective is successful recipient communication rather than forced reproduction of a target form.

**C — Lexicalized sense must follow the world context.** A fictional meeting update says **“I ran into Maya at the station.”** In the independently reviewed encounter sense, learner A reports a physical collision without contextual support; learner B writes **“I met Maya unexpectedly at the station.”** Counterfactual: a separate safety report says **“The cyclist ran into the barrier”**, where physical collision is the licensed interpretation. Another reviewed example contrasts **“turn down the music”** (reduce volume) and **“turn down the invitation”** (decline it). An isolated `turn down` prompt with no context may be ambiguous: credit a sensible clarification. Do not award correctness based on a keyword match that changes the real-world event.

**D — Pragmatic force, result and cross-language boundaries.** A fictional technician messages **“I'll turn off the projector after the presentation.”** Learner A reports **“The projector is already off”**; learner B independently preserves future intention and asks for confirmation before claiming completion. Counterfactual: **“The technician turned off the projector and confirmed the indicator went dark”** licenses a stronger result report. A separately reviewed Spanish translation may use **“apagar el proyector”**, not a literal separate word corresponding to `off`; a reviewed German task may require verb-particle position changes across clause structures. Such language comparisons must be reviewed for their own grammar, variety and communicative register and cannot be used to infer a universal cross-language particle rule. A natural formal paraphrase may be preferable to a colloquial multiword verb for a particular audience.

**E — Rendered particle/negation evidence, accessibility and authorship.** The authoritative spoken instruction says **“Don't turn it off; turn it down.”** An ASR transcript omits **don't**, captions replace **down** with **off**, a translation flattens both meanings into `stop`, or a screen reader reads an unlabeled color-coded answer before the learner responds. Compare the authoritative and **actually delivered** stimuli. If the delivered input loses the decisive distinction, mark **only the affected construct** UNSUPPORTED rather than penalize the learner. At 10:00 the learner independently asks **“Should I lower the volume or switch it off?”**; a model response queued at 09:59 but first rendered at 10:01 does not retroactively assist that answer. A corrected response at 10:02 after exposure is ASSISTED. Accessible text can support equivalent semantic understanding but must not be used to certify unobserved auditory discrimination. Replaying an identical session-qualified event cannot create new independent attempts or delayed successes.

**F — Longitudinal choice, confounds, delayed retrieval and unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A scores well immediately after NOUN_PRONOUN_PLACEMENT_SWAP but repeatedly chooses the wrong lexical sense on delayed first-unaided recipient messages. Comparable **actually delivered** VERB_PARTICLE_CONTEXT_CONTRAST and LITERAL_VS_LEXICALIZED_MINIMAL_CONTEXT trials precede a genuinely new unaided help-desk report using a different construction, recipient and purpose. Learner B instead improves delayed independent object placement after SEPARABLE_VS_INSEPARABLE_COMPARISON and LEARNER_GENERATED_INSTRUCTION. If one method uses common `pick up` with familiar nouns and another uses unfamiliar `brush off` with a figurative sense and formal audience, flag METHOD_LEXICAL_FREQUENCY_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_SENSE_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED and METHOD_REGISTER_CONFOUNDED: infer **no winner**. Replacing `parcel` with `box` in the same `pick it up` drill is not unseen transfer. Match baseline, construction frequency, sense, object type, audience, target variety, modality, topic, prior help, delay and actual follow-up opportunity. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing separately; missing is neither success nor failure. Only repeated comparable later **first-unaided** outcomes may justify a logged change in **actually delivered** instruction, retaining uncertainty, bounded alternatives and reversal after contradictory evidence. Interests in games, travel or music can influence examples but never remove neutral/unfamiliar contexts, required skills or CEFR objectives.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR-linked purpose and reviewed construction oracle.** A–F resolve actual versioned communicative objectives, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/variety/register, independently reviewed verb + particle/preposition senses and accepted paraphrases, accessible stimuli, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. No invented CEFR-specific phrasal-verb descriptor.
2. **Sense-specific object placement.** A accepts reviewed noun alternation and pronoun placement for `pick up` in the specified sense/register; it does not extend that pattern to all verbs or to unresolved pronoun referents.
3. **Inseparable preposition versus separable particle.** B rejects `look them after` for the reviewed care-for sense, accepts `look after them` and communicatively equivalent reviewed paraphrases, and never treats shared base verb spelling as proof of identical syntax.
4. **Context-sensitive lexical meaning and clarification.** C distinguishes reviewed encounter/collision and reduce/decline senses from source-world context, permits valid clarification when context is genuinely missing, and rejects fluent but meaning-changing paraphrases.
5. **Intent, completion and target-language boundaries.** D never converts a future promise/request into a completed event; separately reviewed Spanish, German or other target-language constructions are assessed on their own terms, without universal English word-order assumptions.
6. **Actually delivered accessibility and idempotent authorship.** E contrasts authoritative versus rendered audio/ASR/captions/translation/TTS/screen-reader/autocomplete, quarantines only unsupported claims, orders first authorship against **rendered** help and deduplicates immutable session-qualified events. Text-equivalent performance cannot stand in for unobserved listening evidence.
7. **Confound-aware, uncertain and reversible adaptation.** F compares feasible, accessible, actually delivered instruction under matched objective, lexical frequency/sense, object type, register, topic, modality, prior help, delay and follow-up. Sparse/confounded/contradictory data yields no winner and balanced fallback.
8. **Independent delayed retention, genuinely unseen transfer and broad skills.** Copied placement maps, assisted repairs, same-session repetitions and near-identical verb/frame substitutions do not earn independent delayed/unseen mastery. Require later eligible distinct sessions, first-unaided audience-correct messages and a new construction/context with reviewed oracle; record missingness, skill-family evidence and nonexclusive topic rotation.
9. **Evidence honesty and release boundary.** Passing document checks or CI is not executable engine support, a demonstrated change in delivered teaching, or proof of improved human retention. Such claims require exact-head executable tests tied to real objectives and longitudinal delivered-method choices with independent delayed/transfer outcomes against a balanced baseline, followed by ethically reviewed learner evidence.
