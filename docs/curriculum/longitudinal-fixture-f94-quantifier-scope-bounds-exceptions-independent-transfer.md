## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — “Not all” promoted to “none”; scope restricted to a subset.** A verified library notice says “Not all afternoon workshops are canceled; only the two outdoor workshops are canceled.” Learner A independently tells attendees “No afternoon workshops will run,” widening the cancellation. Learner B preserves the afternoon domain, identifies the two outdoor exceptions and advises checking the indoor timetable. A contrasting later notice says “All workshops are canceled,” and B reports the genuine universal rather than mechanically retaining exceptions. An unseen task changes the subset from workshop type to day of week and tests first unaided recipient-facing instructions.
- **B — Lower and upper bounds converted into exact quantities.** A venue message says “At least four volunteers are needed; no more than eight may enter.” Learner A announces “Exactly four volunteers will enter,” inventing both a total and an outcome. Learner B explains the minimum staffing need and maximum admission separately. A second notice says “Exactly six have registered, but approximately five may arrive,” requiring separation of exact registration from estimated attendance. Score faithful linguistic bounds and actionable instructions, not difficult calculations.
- **C — Exception attachment and collective versus individual scope.** A source-authored school notice says “Every student in Group B must bring a notebook, except students using the supplied tablets.” Learner A incorrectly exempts the entire Group B; learner B applies the exception only to tablet users. In a separate prevalidated dialogue, “The three teams need one key each” is explicitly distributive, while “The three teams share one key” is collective. Learner B communicates the correct number of keys without assuming all languages express the distinction with English “each.” If the source merely says “The teams need a key,” the rubric accepts a clarification request rather than fabricating scope.
- **D — “Most,” unknown denominators, negation and accessible correction.** An audio announcement says “Most seats are reserved, but some remain available; none of the front-row seats are available.” An inaccurate caption drops “front-row” and changes the final clause to “no seats are available.” Learner A reports the erroneous universal; learner B notes the conflict and requests an accessible verified transcript. Once the source-authorized correction is provided, B reports general availability with the front-row restriction. Neither learner may infer a numeric percentage or exact remaining count from “most.” Do not penalize a caption-dependent learner for inaccessible or conflicting stimuli.
- **E — Coached immediate winner reverses after delayed independent transfer.** In comparable early sessions, SCOPE_MINIMAL_PAIRS yields fluent correct immediate responses after visible answer models; SET_AND_SUBSET_MAPS produces slower and less polished first unaided distinctions. Across genuinely separate later sessions with unfamiliar source types, new group boundaries, bound reversals and real recipient decisions, the mapping condition yields stronger first-attempt independent domain/scope fidelity and fewer invented totals. Match or disclose baseline ability, source difficulty, feedback, practice dose, topic familiarity, register, language variety, modality and accessibility. Prefer later independent evidence over coached immediate correctness, keep exploration of feasible alternatives, and record the **actually rendered** next method; never label the learner permanently “visual” or “set-based.”
- **F — Interest overfit, incomplete follow-up and receipt-order conflict.** A learner handles “all/some” accurately in preferred sports-team announcements but misreads a neutral transit capacity notice and unfamiliar community sign-up rule. A required listening source lacks captions; an eligible delayed probe is offered but never rendered. Two real offline sessions reuse a local event ID; an answer-bearing tutor subset diagram was rendered before a learner response but its telemetry arrives afterward; another event carries a conflicting frozen rubric hash. Deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, quarantine unresolved versions and reconstruct trusted render chronology. Assistance, first-independent scope verdict, review debt, soft-interest rotation and reversible next-method rationale must remain invariant under receipt-order permutations. Written success cannot substitute for required listening or speaking evidence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid CEFR applicability.** A–F require real versioned can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/variety, source-authored task/provenance, recipient purpose, validated rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, threshold, required skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing mapping or unsupported language-specific quantifier analysis yields UNSUPPORTED/NOT_APPLICABLE, never invented proficiency.
2. **Domain, universals and negation scope.** A/C/D preserve who or what is quantified and where negation attaches. “Not all” must not become “none,” and a restriction on outdoor workshops or front-row seats must not become a restriction on all workshops or seats. Source-confirmed universals must still be reported as universal.
3. **Bounds, exactness and approximation.** B/D distinguish “at least,” “at most,” “exactly,” “approximately,” “some” and “most,” including whether a denominator or actual outcome is known. A lower/upper bound must not be turned into a fabricated exact total; fluent arithmetic-looking answers cannot override source meaning.
4. **Exceptions, distributivity and ambiguity.** C/D apply exceptions to their actual domain, distinguish source-specified individual versus shared obligations and allow source-appropriate clarification for ambiguous collective readings. Do not require one English quantifier syntax across target languages.
5. **First independent move and actually rendered cues.** A/D/F evaluate the first learner-authored recipient-facing quantity statement or clarification against trusted render chronology. A completed numeral, answer-bearing subset map or highlighted exception rendered beforehand makes the affected move assisted; selected-but-never-rendered instruction does not. Separate source-authored task stimulus from interlocutor coaching.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer.** A/B/C/E/F require separate real sessions, novel domains, reversed bounds and exceptions, different registers and recipient decisions, first unaided responses and observable outcomes. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; assisted revisions, copied models and missing follow-up cannot establish durable mastery.
7. **Comparable methods, accessibility and soft interests.** D/E/F match or disclose numeracy load, task difficulty, source clarity, language/variety, topic familiarity, feedback, practice dose, modality and accessibility. Preserve shared CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar context rotation. A missing caption or unknown denominator is not a learner language error.
8. **Reversible adaptation and receipt-order invariance.** E/F log feasible alternatives, assigned/selected/**actually rendered** instruction, later independent outcomes, uncertainty, confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicted evidence falls back to BALANCED_DEFAULT. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen versions and trusted render chronology produce identical assistance labels, quantity verdicts, review debt and next method across receipt permutations; quarantine unresolved conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** A–F emit exact source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen domain/operator/bound/exception fields, first/final learner-authored utterances, rendered cues, recipient outcomes, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. Documentation CI is not executable evaluator PASS, CEFR award or proof of improved human learning.
