# F38 — Tutor-origin errors must not become learner deficits or false method evidence

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. This is not a reproduced defect, shipped adaptive tutor, causal estimate, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 protects independent probes against exposed items, F34 separates bilingual help from independent evidence, F35 records scaffold visibility, and F37 separates assigned from delivered instruction. F38 isolates a different provenance failure: **a tutor can teach, demonstrate, score or correct something incorrectly, causing an apparent learner error or success that should not be attributed to the learner's language ability or used to rank instructional methods.** Tutor-origin content quality and evaluation validity must be audited independently of learner performance.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do**, actually supported skill route, prerequisites, calibrated rubric and independent mastery/exit gate. Compare eligible instruction A (`concise_explanation`) and B (`worked_example`) with a feasible fixed balanced baseline. Hold the objective, task demand, scoring rubric, independently verified answer key, novelty, timing, opportunity, supported modalities, review obligations and topic-context rotation constant. The fixture introduces *controlled tutor defects* in synthetic teaching/feedback events; do not imply that these defects exist in the deployed product or invent a live learner outcome.

Use a deterministic clock, seed, versioned `tutor_content_quality_policy`, immutable synthetic event ledger and separately adjudicated reference for the can-do and rubric. Record stable learner, objective/version, skill, session, activity, tutor-turn, asset/model/prompt version, instruction assignment and **verified delivery**, content-claim, feedback, evaluation, attempt, probe, item-family and event IDs; the actual tutor utterance or a privacy-preserving reviewed claim/answer-key reference; correctness/adjudication status and reviewer/version; whether a learner saw the misleading explanation, example, hint, model answer, translation, score or repair; first-attempt response and its pre-feedback timing; assistance, retry, independent transfer, delayed retrieval, intervening practice, actual delay, context, review debt, event versus upload time and missingness. Keep sensitive learner text out of analytics when not needed; synthetic payloads and auditable minimal evidence are sufficient.

Predeclare `TUTOR_CONTENT_VALID`, `TUTOR_CONTENT_INCORRECT`, `TUTOR_FEEDBACK_INCORRECT`, `ASSESSMENT_KEY_INVALID`, `EXPOSURE_UNKNOWN`, `LEARNER_EVIDENCE_VALID`, `LEARNER_EVIDENCE_QUARANTINED`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and `NOT_COMPARABLE`. Distinguish **what the learner actually produced**, **what the tutor claimed**, **what a validated rubric establishes**, and **what instruction the learner experienced**. Do not presume that every learner mistake is tutor-caused or that a later corrected answer demonstrates independent mastery.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Wrong worked example induces a patterned error.** A tutor's B example gives an incorrect target-language construction. A learner then repeats that construction in a first attempt. Independent adjudication confirms the example was wrong and that the learner actually saw it. Retain the literal learner attempt and the content defect, but quarantine any method-quality or stable learner-deficit inference that depends on this contaminated exposure. Offer a corrected explanation and a later clean, unfamiliar independent probe; do not automatically mark the later probe correct.
- **B — Correct learner answer is marked wrong.** A valid first-attempt answer satisfies the frozen CEFR rubric, but the tutor issues an incorrect negative correction and pushes the learner into an unnecessary retry. Preserve the original valid answer and its independent score from the adjudicated rubric, mark tutor feedback defective and exclude the erroneous correction/retry from evidence of learner weakness. Do not rewrite history as if the tutor had been correct.
- **C — Invalid answer key creates false success.** A tutor-generated answer key or scoring rule incorrectly accepts a response that does not meet the objective. The learner sees a model answer before a second try and subsequently reproduces it. Re-adjudicate the original attempt, keep the second response as assisted/exposed, and require a clean independently scored probe before mastery or method superiority can be claimed. An invalid key is not evidence of learner failure *or* success by itself.
- **D — Ambiguous usage and valid alternatives.** A tutor insists that one grammatical or pragmatic realization is the only acceptable one, although two context-appropriate forms satisfy the versioned rubric. A second case has a genuinely unacceptable form. Independent adjudication distinguishes acceptable variation from error without flattening dialect/register or changing the objective after the fact. Preserve both valid alternatives and genuine errors; flag unsupported claims about a single universally correct phrasing.
- **E — Corrected teaching and reversible hypothesis.** Earlier apparent A-versus-B differences arose from a verified defective A explanation and unequal exposure. After the defect is corrected, comparable verified delivery, calibrated unaided delayed retrieval and unseen-context transfer no longer support the old ranking; later independent outcomes may favor either option. Lower confidence, retract contaminated attribution, revise only objective/skill-specific hypotheses and continue balanced exploration. Do not infer that a content fix alone caused the outcome change.
- **F — Offline, duplicate and disputed tutor feedback.** An incorrect feedback event occurs offline before a learner retry but uploads afterward; one event replays; two tutor versions issue conflicting feedback; another correction occurs only *after* a completed clean probe. Deduplicate stable IDs, order trusted occurrence times, preserve immutable original attempts, record feedback/version conflicts and quarantine unresolvable visibility or chronology. Later feedback cannot retroactively contaminate an earlier independently verified probe.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Two-sided provenance ledger.** For A–F report objective/rubric versions, assigned and actually delivered method, tutor content/feedback/key versions, validated correctness and reviewer provenance, learner first attempts, exposure/assistance, later independent probes, actual delays, item novelty, eligibility/completion/follow-up denominators, uncertainty and missingness. Unknown quality or visibility is not proof of correctness or exposure.
2. **Tutor-origin contamination is explicit.** In A, preserve the observed learner response and verified exposure to incorrect teaching; do not silently promote a tutor-induced pattern into a stable learner weakness or infer that B is intrinsically inferior. Record `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` without awarding mastery or fabricating a counterfactual.
3. **False-negative feedback cannot erase valid evidence.** In B, an independently valid original first attempt remains valid even when the tutor mislabels it; unnecessary retries and tutor assertions do not overwrite the attempt or justify additional learner-deficit estimates.
4. **False-positive scoring cannot create mastery.** In C, invalid keys, revealed models and repaired answers cannot satisfy independent mastery, delayed retrieval or unseen transfer. Preserve re-adjudicated outcomes and require fresh clean probes under the unchanged rubric.
5. **Legitimate variation survives adjudication.** In D, contextually correct variants are not punished merely for differing from the tutor's preferred wording; genuinely invalid responses remain errors. Document the actual rubric, usage/register context and adjudication uncertainty without moving the CEFR goalposts.
6. **Method comparisons can be revised after quality repair.** In E, content quality and verified learner exposure are covariates, not interchangeable with method identity. Retract contaminated method rankings, allow uncertainty-aware hypothesis reversal on later comparable independent evidence, keep nonzero alternative sampling and never assign a permanent learning-style label.
7. **Replay-safe immutable chronology.** In F, duplicate and out-of-order uploads cannot change original first attempts, tutor-content version, feedback visibility, independent denominators or probe lineage. Conflicts and untrusted timestamps remain `UNKNOWN`; feedback delivered after a probe cannot explain that probe.
8. **Standards, availability and soft interests stay separate.** Every history retains the same versioned can-do, prerequisites, independent rubric, due reviews and supported receptive/productive obligations. Interest-weighted examples cannot excuse false explanations or eliminate neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar contexts. Unsupported listening/speaking remain `UNAVAILABLE`, never phantom successes.
9. **Conservative release gate.** If tutor content validity, feedback exposure, adjudicated rubric, comparable verified instruction, clean delayed retrieval or unfamiliar transfer cannot be established, classify the affected adaptation/learning claim `UNSUPPORTED` or `NOT_COMPARABLE`, retain narrower valid observations and use the shared balanced curriculum with normal supports. Neither corrected feedback nor immediate assisted success proves durable independent learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, deterministic seed/clock, full synthetic ledger, existing CEFR objective/skill mapping and immutable rubric, tutor content/feedback/key versions and adjudication references, assignment-versus-delivery provenance, original learner attempts and repair lineage, visibility of answer-bearing help, calibrated delayed independent unfamiliar probes, elapsed time and intervening practice, comparable exposure and follow-up denominators, review/skill/context coverage, replay invariance, uncertainty/reason codes and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for every assertion. Include a fixed balanced comparator with equivalent feasible opportunities. Preserve a distinct quality-incident/remediation record; do not turn synthetic fixture outcomes into claims of real-world tutor defects or causal method efficacy.

A deterministic fixture PASS establishes only a software acceptance invariant. A claim that personalized instruction improves durable independent learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal learner evidence, a comparable balanced baseline, verified clean delayed retrieval and unseen transfer, uncertainty calibration and unchanged CEFR coverage.
