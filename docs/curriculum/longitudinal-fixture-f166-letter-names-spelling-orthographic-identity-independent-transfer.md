**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F103 covers critical-detail readback; F109 code-switching; F111 ASR noise; F117 language variety; F131 phonemic lexical contrast; F152 uncertainty; F155 measurement units; F162 reference repair; F165 false-premise repair. **Distinct construct:** can a learner independently communicate, disambiguate and verify the **letter-by-letter orthographic identity of a fictional name, label or short reference**, when spoken letter names, visual glyphs, language variety or assistive delivery create a plausible alternative? Sound discrimination, ordinary vocabulary recognition and merely copying a displayed string do not establish this ability.

If the can-do does not require spelling or exact character identity, mark NOT_APPLICABLE. Prefer fictional library holds, clubs, classroom project labels and invented contact references; do not use real account credentials, real private contact details, medical identifiers, payment instructions or actual eligibility decisions.

Freeze a **versioned orthographic oracle** for each fictional source: authoritative intended string and its provenance; exact characters and order; letter-name pronunciations in the relevant language/variety; visually or acoustically confusable alternatives; whether capitalization, accent marks, spaces, punctuation, transliteration and Unicode normalization are identity-critical **for this task**; whether a shorter alias is explicitly accepted; acceptable natural readback, clarification and repair; and what remains unknown. The recipient's need for exact identity must be stated, not inferred from an arbitrary spelling preference. A written source can establish a visual string but not prove that a learner understood its unheard spoken letter names. An audio source can test listening only if the actually rendered sound preserves the required distinctions.

**Do not universalize spelling conventions.** English spoken **B** and **V** can be confusable in noisy audio; Spanish names for these letters and pronunciation conventions vary by region, and Spanish /b/–/v/ pronunciation is not a reliable orthographic discriminator. English **O** and **zero** and visual **O/0** or **I/l/1** need context-specific confirmation. Diacritics and transliteration may be essential in one reviewed identifier and optional in another; never assume a person's identity or nationality from a spelling. When a source cannot settle the intended character, a targeted clarification is a correct response, not a failure.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Spoken letter names require disambiguation, not a plausible guess.** A fictional club organizer spells a project code as English letter names **“B–E–A”**; the independently reviewed source-world code is **BEA**. In an actually audible, clearly articulated recording, **“B, E, A — is that B as in book?”** is a valid readback/clarification. **VEA** fails if the first letter is demonstrably **B**. Counterfactual: noisy audio makes **B/V** genuinely indistinguishable; **“Was the first letter B or V?”** is correct and an unsupported guess must not earn independent accuracy. If a text transcript reveals **BEA** before the response, that trial no longer proves unaided audio-letter recognition.

**B — Visual glyphs and exact short references.** A fictional library card label is visually printed **L0I** (capital L, digit zero, capital I), with a reviewed exact-match lookup requirement. The learner's recipient-facing readback **“capital L, zero, capital I”** is actionable; **“L-O-I”** is a different string and fails only when the original is legible. Counterfactual: a low-resolution image makes **0/O** indistinguishable; **“Is the middle character the digit zero or the letter O?”** is a valid targeted clarification. Never infer a numeric character merely because the string resembles a reference number.

**C — Diacritics, aliases and reviewed task-specific equivalence.** A fictional event list explicitly distinguishes two entries **“Jose”** and **“José”** as different display labels. For this particular reviewed exact-match list, **“José, with an accent on é”** is needed; silently dropping the mark may select the wrong entry. Counterfactual: another fictional source-world explicitly declares both spellings accepted aliases for one non-sensitive item; either may be acceptable. Unicode composed/decomposed representations of the same visible accented character may be equivalent only under a reviewed normalization rule. Do not generalize exact-match policies to human identity or penalize a legitimate language-variety spelling when the task does not require it.

**D — Spelling sequence and cross-language letter-name repair.** A fictional classroom project label is **M-A-R-A**, not **M-A-R-I-A**. The learner receives an English audio spelling and must confirm the four letters in order. **“M-A-R-A, four letters?”** succeeds; adding **I** fails. In a separate Spanish-variety dialogue, the listener hears an ambiguous spoken letter name for a fictional label containing **B** or **V**. Because regional letter names and pronunciations vary, **“¿Con be o con uve?”** (or a locally natural equivalent) is accepted if the source cannot disambiguate. A correct written name copied from a visible answer cannot certify listening or independent spelling production.

**E — Rendered help, accessibility and provenance change what is measurable.** At 09:00 an accessible original audio spells **L–0–I** with explicit **“zero”**; TTS substitutes **“oh”**, ASR emits **LOI**, and a screen reader reads a correction before the prompt. Quarantine any construct-changing rendition instead of scoring the learner against unheard or visually hidden characters. At 09:02 the learner independently asks **“Was the middle one zero?”**; at 09:03 an answer-bearing tutor spelling chart is actually displayed; at 09:04 the learner repeats **“L-zero-I.”** Preserve the 09:02 FIRST_UNAIDED targeted clarification, label the 09:04 response ASSISTED, and do not retroactively contaminate the earlier answer with help that was queued but not rendered. Autocorrect that inserts the target string before submission is recorded as answer exposure. Replayed event IDs do not create additional independent trials. Accessible text can assess accessible reading/clarification, but not unheard listening.

**F — Longitudinal adaptation, unseen transfer and topic independence.** Learner A succeeds immediately with a visible letter chart but fails delayed first-unaided spoken-code clarification. Over multiple matched **actually delivered** RECIPIENT_CONFIRMATION_ROLEPLAY opportunities, later first-unaided recipient readbacks improve on **new strings with different confusable positions**, speakers and neutral/unfamiliar contexts. This may cautiously favor roleplay while still sampling chart and sound-to-grapheme alternatives. Learner B initially benefits from roleplay but later independently confuses **zero/O**: lower confidence, move review forward and reopen exploration. If roleplay cases have three easy letters while chart cases have eight characters, diacritics and degraded audio, infer **no method winner**. Swapping a fictional music club for a game club while reusing the same target string and answer structure is **not** unseen transfer. Missing delayed probes are MISSING, not failures; improvement must not be inferred from selective follow-up.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing objective, task need and reviewed orthographic oracle.** A–F bind an existing versioned CEFR can-do, prerequisites, genuine recipient information gap, language/variety, task-specific exactness policy, source-world, actual accessible delivery, independent oracle/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; no new compulsory orthographic standard.
2. **Spoken-letter accuracy versus honest clarification.** A accepts verified **B–E–A** and a natural B/V clarification under noisy audio; it rejects a confidently invented first letter. Answer-bearing captions cannot count as unaided listening.
3. **Glyph identity and ordering.** B–D preserve character type, exact order and context-specific length; **zero/O**, **I/l/1**, **MARA/MARIA** are not silently normalized when the reviewed task requires distinction. Unresolvable input permits a targeted clarification.
4. **Language variety, diacritics and identity boundaries.** C–D honor independently reviewed accent, alias, transliteration, capitalization and Unicode-normalization policies. No universal claim about human identity, nationality or all Spanish B/V pronunciations is allowed.
5. **Recipient-actionable communication.** A–D accept concise, polite, natural spelling readbacks and targeted questions in the appropriate variety; they do not demand formal phonetic terminology or penalize valid alternative wording.
6. **Actual delivery, chronology, exposure and deduplication.** E audits original versus rendered audio/TTS/ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader order, quarantines construct-changing drift, protects pre-help first authorship, records actual autocomplete/model exposure, and deduplicates stable events. Reading and listening evidence are not interchangeable.
7. **Controlled, uncertain and reversible method selection.** F compares feasible **actually delivered** methods under matched character count/confusability, language/variety, modality, topic, help, source quality, prior exposure, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, conflicting, selective or confounded evidence yields no winner; delayed regression lowers confidence and reopens alternatives.
8. **Delayed independent genuinely unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Assisted spelling charts, same-session repetition, copied strings and topic-only swaps do not earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, a **new target string with changed confusable character or position**, changed recipient/context, reviewed oracle, first-unaided actionable spelling or justified clarification, and explicit missingness. Keep CEFR gates and modality balance fixed; preserve neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar topics.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Passing documentation CI is not executable orthographic QA, shipped adaptive tutoring or proof of human-learning improvement. Require exact-head executable assertions against real objectives and actually rendered teaching changes driven by longitudinal outcomes, followed by independently reviewed later transfer versus a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design/probe histories, not observed learner outcomes or executable QA. Preserve uncertainty, task-specific orthographic identity, accessible delivery and explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and longitudinal independent evidence exist.
