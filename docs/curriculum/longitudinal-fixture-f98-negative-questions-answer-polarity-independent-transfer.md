## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Negative wording does not invert the fact.** A source-authored English dialogue establishes that the meeting **is** happening on Tuesday (P=true). A participant asks, “Isn't the meeting on Tuesday?” Learner A says “No” intending to agree with the negative wording; the recipient cancels attendance. Learner B independently responds “Yes, it is on Tuesday,” or another source/variety-validated unambiguous equivalent. In a second dialogue P=false and the learner must independently say “No, it isn't; it's on Wednesday” only if Wednesday is actually source-supported. Later unseen tasks change event, interlocutor and surface question; scoring follows P and the recipient's understanding, not a bare yes-token.
- **B — Tag questions and correction of an expectation.** An English source-authored organizer asks “You haven't sent the update yet, have you?” The source shows that the update **was sent**. Learner A replies with an ambiguous bare “yes” that the organizer interprets as “yes, not sent.” Learner B answers “I have sent it” or an equally validated explicit correction. In a different source world the update was **not sent**; B preserves that negative fact without promising a delivery date. A later genuinely unseen tag reverses both surface polarity and the speaker's expectation. Do not award a result based only on matching a tag's particle.
- **C — Cross-language particle conventions and unknown facts.** Two validated target-language varieties or languages use different source-documented ways to contradict a negative question, such as an explicit echo answer versus a specialized response particle. Learner A translates English “yes/no” word-for-word and accidentally reverses the proposition. Learner B uses a source-validated form or explicitly repeats the proposition. In a third dialogue the answer is not known; B says so or asks a bounded question rather than fabricating a truth value. If the curriculum lacks verified conventions or accepted equivalents, mark UNSUPPORTED rather than inventing a universal cross-language rule.
- **D — Prosody, accessibility and genuinely ambiguous particles.** A source-authored spoken confirmation question has prosody that changes whether a short response is interpreted as correction or acknowledgement; an inaccurate caption omits the negative particle. Learner A is penalized despite not receiving the crucial cue. Learner B asks “Do you mean whether the train is running?” where source-permitted and then provides the known status. In a separate fully captioned dialogue an explicit echo answer is independently correct. Without reliable audio/captions, supported variety and unambiguous scoring context, mark confounded/UNSUPPORTED; do not treat hearing, speech fluency or cultural familiarity as language competence.
- **E — Fast coached particles versus delayed independent fidelity.** Across matched early sessions, `CONTRASTIVE_NEGATIVE_QUESTION_PAIRS` yields quick correct yes/no selections after a rendered answer-bearing chart, while `POSITIVE_PROPOSITION_ANCHOR` initially produces slower but unaided explicit answers. In later **separate real sessions** with genuinely unseen question/tag structures and recipient tasks, proposition anchoring yields more faithful independent responses for this synthetic learner. Match or disclose baseline proficiency, question difficulty, variety/register, feedback, practice dose, modality/accessibility, topic exposure and interlocutor familiarity. Prefer completed delayed unseen outcomes to assisted immediate correctness; retain exploration and uncertainty, and log the **actually rendered** next method without labeling a permanent learner style.
- **F — Interest overfit, late render telemetry and conflicting versions.** A learner succeeds with negative questions in favorite gaming chats but fails in neutral travel updates and unfamiliar volunteer coordination. A tutor's explicit P-anchoring model is rendered before the learner's first answer but arrives in telemetry afterward; another chart was selected but never rendered. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; a source correction changes P from true to unknown; a later rubric adds a validated variety-specific echo form; an eligible delayed probe was offered but never completed. Deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, quarantine incompatible source/rubric versions, reconstruct trusted render chronology, preserve unknown follow-up and review debt, rotate topics, and require identical verdicts and reversible next-method traces under receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR/task applicability.** A–F emit a real versioned can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/variety, source-authored polar exchange and recipient purpose, frozen P/truth state, validated response conventions, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, independent threshold, required skills, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing real source mapping or unvalidated particle convention yields NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED, never invented proficiency.
2. **Proposition anchoring versus surface negation.** A/B distinguish whether the answer affirms P, denies P or leaves P unknown despite negative interrogative wording or reversed tag polarity. The same “yes” token must not earn identical verdicts across source worlds with different conventions and recipient interpretations.
3. **Faithful recipient-facing answer.** A/B/C score whether the learner's independently authored response conveys the correct source-supported proposition and relevant uncertainty, without inventing dates, completed actions, consent, future promises or facts. Source-validated explicit echo answers and meaning-equivalent forms are accepted; token recognition alone is insufficient.
4. **Language variety, ambiguity and repair.** B/C/D/F validate the target-language and regional answer system, speaker expectation only where supported, legitimate ambiguity and clarification. Do not penalize an appropriate explicit restatement or clarification; do not treat missing prosody, inaccessible captions or unsupported response particles as learner errors.
5. **First independent move and render chronology.** A/D/F score the first learner-authored recipient-facing answer against trusted cue-render chronology. A worked proposition chart, correct echo answer, model translation or highlighted particle rendered before the move makes it assisted; selected-but-unrendered cues do not. Separate legitimate task stimuli from unsolicited coaching.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer.** A–F require distinct real sessions, source-authored new polar questions/tags and recipient outcomes, first unaided outputs and completed delayed independent probes. Emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; repeated questions, copied templates, assisted corrections and uncompleted probes cannot establish durable mastery.
7. **Comparable methods, accessibility and topic breadth.** C/D/E/F match or disclose baseline proficiency, P truth-state balance, question difficulty, particle convention, register, source familiarity, feedback, practice dose, modality/accessibility and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic exposure. Preserve shared CEFR goals, noncompensatory skills, due reviews and exit gates; never equate an unsupported language convention with low proficiency.
8. **Reversible adaptation and event-order invariance.** E/F record feasible methods, assigned/selected/**actually rendered** choice, independent later outcomes, uncertainty, confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicted evidence falls back to `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen versions and trusted render chronology must yield identical assistance labels, P-status verdicts, review debt and next method across receipt permutations; quarantine conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** A–F emit exact source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, P/truth/question/tag/response convention, first and final learner-authored answers, rendered cues, recipient interpretations, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. Documentation CI is not an executable evaluator PASS, CEFR award or evidence of improved real-learner retention.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, real source-linked CEFR polar-question tasks, validated target-language/variety answer systems, frozen P/truth states and rubrics, trusted cue-render chronology, first unaided recipient-facing answers, observable recipient interpretations, completed delayed genuinely unseen transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skills/reviews, receipt-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing real mapping, accessible channel, validated response convention, trustworthy chronology or independently completed follow-up remains UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

A claim that adaptation **improves durable independent question answering** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor teaches learners to answer the actual proposition, not merely to echo a yes/no token.**
