# F195 — Task ownership, delegation, progress reports, handoff and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable exact-head checks bind independently reviewed, target-language/variety-specific task-handoff oracles, actual source delivery, first-authorship provenance and genuinely delayed first-unaided unseen transfer to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a shipped task manager, work tracker, authority engine, legal duty detector, new CEFR gate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F72 covers goal/constraint tracking; F80 covers asynchronous update propagation; F83/F179 cover conditional commitments; F85 covers permission and authority; F169 covers offers/requests; F189 covers reported sources; F193 covers who received a message; F194 covers proposal versus group decision. **Distinct construct:** in an existing source-linked communicative task involving a small collaborative project, the learner must distinguish **a request for help, a volunteered offer, an explicit task acceptance, a source-confirmed assignment, a proposed versus accepted handoff, work in progress, a claimed completion and independently verified completion**. The learner then communicates who is expected to do what next, with appropriate uncertainty and without manufacturing obligation, acceptance or finished work. This is language-mediated status reporting, not judging whether participants are reliable. English examples are illustrative only.

## Fixed CEFR objective and independently reviewed task-status oracles

Bind only existing **versioned** CEFR interaction, mediation, listening, reading, writing, speaking or free-production can-dos whose source task actually requires requesting assistance, accepting a task, reporting progress, passing work to someone else or summarizing a simple collaborative handoff. Freeze can-do ID, prerequisites, level/turn ceiling, target language/variety, task/source and rubric/evaluator versions, accessibility route, assistance policy, first-independent and delayed/unseen evidence, thresholds, required receptive/productive coverage and exit gates. At lower levels, test only simple source-required phrases such as “I can bring the signs” and “Has the draft been sent?”; do not add advanced project-management concepts or extra language objectives. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE. Preserve the unresolved B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix disagreement, its 20-meaningful-turn ceiling and closed gate. Use fictional low-stakes library displays, student clubs and workshop posters; exclude employment/legal liability, finance, healthcare, emergencies, safety-critical work and private personal information.

An independently reviewed **task-and-utterance oracle** freezes the exact source utterance, speaker, addressee, channel, time, task identity/version, scope, expected deliverable, any deadline, relevant conditional clauses, visible attachments and surrounding turns. Distinguish REQUESTED_HELP, VOLUNTEERED_OFFER, ACCEPTED_TASK, SOURCE_CONFIRMED_ASSIGNMENT, PROPOSED_DELEGATION, ACCEPTED_HANDOFF, DECLINED_HANDOFF, OWNER_UNCONFIRMED, IN_PROGRESS, CLAIMED_COMPLETE, DELIVERABLE_RECEIVED, VERIFIED_COMPLETE, BLOCKED, CANCELLED and UNKNOWN. “Can someone make the signs?” requests help but names no owner; “I could help” is an offer, not necessarily acceptance of the entire deliverable; “I'll make the three signs” is a source-visible commitment by that speaker, not proof the signs exist. An organizer's assignment may be recorded as an assignment without assuming the recipient has seen, accepted or fulfilled it. Do not infer a person's thoughts, honesty, motivation or enforceable obligation.

A separate **handoff-and-status oracle** freezes who is permitted by the fictional task to assign or reassign work *if the source establishes that rule*, what work is being transferred, whether the recipient explicitly accepts, which version remains active and what source evidence proves the deliverable was produced or received. If no authority rule is stated, do not invent one. A proposed transfer does not silently remove the original assignee; a declined transfer cannot be reported as accepted. “I sent the poster draft” is evidence of a sender's report of sending, not independently verified delivery or approval. “I finished the draft” is a completion claim; a reviewer acknowledging receipt of the draft establishes receipt but not approval. A task can be finished while the overall event remains pending. Preserve superseded plans and distinguish actual evidence from reported status.

A **recipient-action oracle** freezes the communicative purpose: ask who can help, confirm only an unresolved handoff, give a precise progress update, relay a blocker, identify a missing deliverable, or request a next action from the correct recipient. Accept concise, natural, variety-appropriate alternatives and justified uncertainty. Do not demand redundant confirmation after a source-valid explicit acceptance and verified delivery. Do not penalize reasonable politeness variation, accent or disability accommodation.

An **actual-delivery and first-authorship ledger** records pseudonymous learner, fixed can-do/task/oracle/rubric versions, immutable session-qualified event IDs with replay deduplication, actual source messages/files shown and their chronology, rendered audio/captions/transcripts and accessible route, tutor hints/models/answers/feedback actually displayed, first eligible unaided recipient-facing message, subsequent assisted repairs, error category, optional reliable latency, topic familiarity, actual instructional option/dose and confidence, plus due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes. An unopened hint is not exposure; a displayed completed ownership table or ready-to-send progress update is answer-bearing help. A failed audio playback earns no independent listening; a reading fallback is credited only under a valid reading/mediation rubric. Replay, new fictional names, reopening the same task or a midnight boundary cannot create a new independent or delayed observation.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — A request and an offer do not automatically create an assignee.** In a fictional club chat Maya asks, “Can someone make three welcome signs?” Theo replies, “I could help with the lettering.” No one explicitly takes responsibility for all three signs. The learner must update Nora. Accept “Maya asked for three signs; Theo offered to help with lettering, but no one has confirmed who will make all three.” Reject “Theo has agreed to make all three signs” and “Nobody wants to help.” In a later visible continuation Theo says, “I'll make all three signs by Thursday”; update the status to explicit acceptance, not completed work. The later response does not retroactively make the first cautious summary wrong.

**B — Assignment and handoff have separate evidence.** A source-authored club plan explicitly assigns Maya to draft the event poster. Maya asks Theo, “Could you take over the draft?” Theo replies, “I can review it, but I can't write it.” The learner must explain the next action. Accept “Maya is still assigned the draft; Theo can review it, but he declined taking over.” Reject “Theo is now responsible for drafting” and “Theo refused to help at all.” In a separate branch Theo explicitly accepts the whole draft and the source-established coordinator confirms the reassignment: now the handoff is accepted. If the task gives no reassignment rule, do not invent who has authority; communicate the observed exchange and uncertainty instead.

**C — Claimed completion, receipt and approval are not identical.** Maya writes, “I've finished the poster draft and emailed it to Nora.” The learner needs to brief the group. Accept “Maya says she finished and sent the draft; Nora's receipt and approval aren't yet confirmed.” Reject “Nora approved the poster.” Later Nora replies, “I received the draft, but I haven't reviewed it yet”: record receipt, not approval. In a third source-valid continuation Nora writes, “I reviewed and approved version 2”; preserve version identity and do not treat version 1 as approved. The whole workshop remains unconfirmed if the source says the venue is still pending.

**D — Conditions and blockers constrain the next action.** Theo accepts responsibility for printing signs “by Friday if the club printer is working.” On Thursday he writes, “The printer is broken; I haven't printed them.” The learner must update Maya. Accept “Theo agreed to print them if the printer worked, but printing is blocked; we need another plan or a revised date.” Reject “Theo promised unconditional Friday delivery” and “The signs are ready.” A later note “I asked the library about its printer” is a request for help, not a completed print job. If the library confirms a machine is available, that still does not prove the signs were printed.

**E — Displayed ownership answers, failed audio and replay cannot earn independence.** At 09:01 the learner reads the actual task messages without a model. At 09:02 the tutor renders a completed table: “Maya: draft; Theo: review only; printing: unassigned.” At 09:03 the learner copies it into a status message. Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED; preserve any earlier eligible first unaided attempt and do not award independent mastery. Replaying the event at 09:04 cannot produce a second first attempt. In a separate listening route, playback fails and captions alone render: no independent listening credit; a reading/mediation route may be evaluated only if independently valid. An unopened hint does not count as exposure. Delayed independent transfer requires a genuinely new task/handoff/recipient configuration in a later distinct session without answer-bearing help.

**F — Longitudinal choice, counterevidence and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly treats “I could help” as an accepted assignment. Across comparable real sessions, REQUEST_OFFER_ACCEPTANCE_CONTRAST, WHO_OWNS_WHICH_DELIVERABLE_LEDGER and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated. A method may be cautiously favored only when later unseen first-unaided recipient messages better preserve task ownership, acceptance and evidence of completion under matched source complexity, roles, topic, help, channel and delay. Learner B initially benefits from ownership tables but later overcorrects by refusing to recognize even explicit accepted assignments; contradictory later independent errors reduce confidence and restore balanced sampling or acceptance-contrast practice. If one method had shorter tasks, explicit owner labels, favorite music topics, visible answer tables or shorter delays, mark confounding and **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up remains unknown, not failed retention. Music interests may softly increase examples, but neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops and all required CEFR objectives and modalities remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed source-defined CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos, independently reviewed task/handoff/recipient oracles and actual source delivery. Preserve prerequisites, target-language variety, turn ceilings, rubrics, thresholds, required skills and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Request, offer and acceptance.** A distinguishes an unassigned request, a limited offer, an explicit acceptance and verified completion; it neither invents a responsible person nor erases genuine willingness to help.
3. **Assignment and delegation.** B preserves source-authored assignments, the precise scope of a proposed transfer, a declined versus accepted handoff and authority uncertainty; a review offer is not acceptance of drafting.
4. **Progress evidence.** C distinguishes a sender's completion/sending claim, actual recipient receipt, explicit review/approval of a particular version and overall project status; it never silently upgrades one into another.
5. **Conditional status and blockers.** D preserves “if” conditions, deadlines, reported blockers and source-supported next actions; a proposed workaround is not a completed deliverable.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E separates visible source, displayed answer-bearing help, unopened hints, failed audio, captions, first unaided output, assisted copying and replay; no phantom listening or assisted independent mastery.
7. **Matched reversible adaptation.** F changes method hypotheses only when comparable actually delivered alternatives produce stronger later first-unaided transfer. Sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence lowers confidence; suitable alternatives continue to be sampled.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and soft interests.** Require genuinely distinct sessions, independently reviewed new task/recipient/handoff configurations, unaided messages, explicit missing follow-up, balanced receptive/productive coverage and nonexclusive preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts. Never skip required CEFR objectives because of interests.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable task-handoff QA, shipped ownership tracking or proof of human-learning gains. Require exact-head executable source/delivery/response/provenance tests, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should teach the learner to communicate which work was requested, offered, accepted, reassigned, blocked, received or verified complete, then independently transfer that distinction to unfamiliar collaborative conversations. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
