# A1 blueprint — reconciled against the shared A1-C2 curriculum master

**Task:** `LC-BP-A1` · **Lane:** `level-a1` · **Template used:** [`level-blueprint-template.md`](../level-blueprint-template.md) · **Master contract:** [`curriculum-master-a1-c2.md`](../curriculum-master-a1-c2.md)

This is a design artifact. It does not make A1 available and nothing in the product imports it — the same rule the template states in its own first line, and the same rule [`a1-map.md`](../a1-map.md) already followed.

**What this file is, and is not.** LinguaChat already has a live, checked A1 blueprint — [`a1-blueprint.json`](../a1-blueprint.json) (`a1.blueprint.v1`), narrated in [`a1-map.md`](../a1-map.md) and updated arc-by-arc in [`a1-authoring-contract.md`](../a1-authoring-contract.md). Runtime tooling (`check:a1-blueprint`, `check:a1-arc1` through `check:a1-arc5`, `check:curriculum-map`) validates against *that* file, not this one. This document does not replace it or redesign it. It reconciles it into the shared template and master contract that now govern A1-C2 as one dependency graph, so the cross-level audit (`LC-AUD-001`) and the pedagogical/psychology supervisors have one consistent shape to read across all six levels. Per CLAUDE.md's sources-of-truth order, the live blueprint still wins on any conflict; this file exists to translate it, not to out-rank it.

The structured data lives in [`a1.json`](a1.json). This file explains the decisions and is organized by the template's eighteen sections. Where a template requirement is answered by data already in the live blueprint, this file says so and points at it rather than re-deriving it. Where the template asks for something the live blueprint does not specify (CEFR references, the six-category evidence model, per-arc personalization allowances, a formal core-engine-requirements list, cross-level acceptance, human-pilot questions), this file adds it — and marks it clearly as new synthesis, never as an invented capability, pattern or episode. `a1.json.provenance` records exactly which fields are carried verbatim and which are new.

## 1. Level identity

- **Level:** A1
- **Blueprint version:** `a1.blueprint.foundry.v1` (reconciliation of live `a1.blueprint.v1`)
- **Status:** design complete (all seven arcs, twenty-one episodes fully specified) — **not** the same claim as "implemented". See §0 below.
- **Prerequisite level:** Pre-A1, frozen, 17 episodes, 6 arcs ([`pre-a1-freeze.md`](../pre-a1-freeze.md))
- **CEFR source references:** CEFR (2001); CEFR Companion Volume with New Descriptors (2020) — used as a *working correspondence* (master contract §3), not a certified alignment. See §3 below for the caveat this reconciliation is adding on top of the live blueprint.
- **Product availability:** false, until a separate release gate. Nothing in this reconciliation changes `implemented`, `available` or `contentStatus` on the level registry.

## 0. Implementation status (not a template section — stated up front because it is load-bearing)

The live blueprint plans all 21 episodes across 7 arcs. As of this reconciliation, **arcs 1-5 (`work_and_study`, `daily_rhythm`, `people_around_you`, `finding_your_way`, `paying_and_choosing`; episodes 18-33) have runtime content**, each proved by its own `check:a1-arcN` script and narrated arc-by-arc in `a1-authoring-contract.md`. **Arcs 6-7 (`what_you_can_do`, `making_arrangements`; episodes 34-38) remain designed-only** — no `check:a1-arc6`/`check:a1-arc7` exist yet, and they must fail closed exactly as CLAUDE.md requires. `docs/curriculum/blueprints/a1.json.implementationStatus` carries this as structured data; every arc and every "should"/designed-only capability in this document is tagged with it so a reader never has to cross-reference two files to know what actually runs today.

This blueprint reconciliation is content-neutral with respect to that boundary: it does not implement arcs 6-7, does not flip any availability flag, and does not weaken any arc-1-5 check.

## 2. Exit promise

Unchanged from the live blueprint (`a1.json.exitPromise`, itself `definition.canDo`/`cannotYet`/`interactionCeiling` in `a1-blueprint.json`). By the end of A1 a learner can, in the present tense, in one situation at a time: say what fills their days and ask the same of someone else; say who the people around them are; find their way and complete a small purchase; say what they can and cannot do; arrange to meet someone; and keep a conversation alive by asking what a word means or how to say one. The explicit ceilings — no past tense, no future beyond a present-tense arrangement, no multi-step directions, no subordinate-clause reasons, no productive third-person `-s`, no dates/spelling/contact details, no conversation that changes topic twice — carry forward unchanged. The interaction ceiling stays "one situation, one topic, 10-12 meaningful turns, repair available, support fading as evidence allows."

## 3. Required capability table

Full table: `a1.json.requiredCapabilityTable` (16 capabilities: 13 required, 3 should). Every `id`, `prerequisites`, `patterns`, `semanticNeeds` and evidence-target field is carried verbatim from `a1-blueprint.json.canDos`. Three fields are new in this reconciliation:

- **`cefr`** — a working correspondence to named CEFR Companion Volume scales (Information exchange, Obtaining goods and services, Interviewing, Conversation/social contact, Asking for clarification, Vocabulary range), chosen per capability from general CEFR A1 illustrative-descriptor knowledge, not copied from a licensed text and not independently verified against the Council of Europe document. It is offered as a working correspondence, in the sense the master contract requires ("map its capabilities to relevant CEFR Companion Volume descriptors"), not a certified alignment. **One capability is flagged as a genuine boundary question rather than a clean fit:** `arrange_to_meet` reads, in the strictest CEFR reading, closer to A2's goal-oriented co-operation family once any negotiation is involved. `a1.json.cefrCaveats.arrangeToMeet` records why LinguaChat still keeps it at A1 (a fixed day/time/place, no negotiation, all language already taught elsewhere in the level) and flags it as an open question for `LC-BP-A2` and `LC-AUD-001` to close: A2 must not silently restate the same capability under a different id.
- **`evidence.transferContexts`** — computed directly from the live blueprint's own `reuseMatrix` (an `R`/`C` mark in an arc after the capability's introducing arc), not invented. See §8/§9 below for why this is a faithful derivation rather than new design.
- **`vocabulary`** — deliberately *not* split per capability. The live blueprint budgets vocabulary per arc, not per capability, and inventing a per-capability split here would assert a precision the source design does not have. Each entry instead points at its arc's `vocabularyBudget`.

## 4. Skill families and arc map

Seven skill families, seven arcs — unchanged (`a1.json.skillFamilies`, `a1.json.arcs`). Per-arc data (situation, prerequisites, new/reused capabilities, vocabulary budget, conversation targets, mini-story decision, autonomy target, risk, episode numbers) is carried verbatim from `a1-blueprint.json.arcs`. This reconciliation adds, per arc, the four fields the template asks for that the live blueprint expressed only in prose scattered across `a1-map.md` and `a1-authoring-contract.md`:

- **`dominantFourStrand`** — see §12.
- **`personalizationAllowance`** (none / light / themed) — an *allowance* decision this reconciliation is making explicitly for the first time at arc granularity. The live system already declares `personalizationMode` per *episode* (`a1-authoring-contract.md` §"Interest personalization"), and this document does not claim to know what value each shipped episode currently uses — that would require reading runtime episode source, which is outside this blueprint-phase task's scope and would risk drifting from the actual code. What it does state is the *ceiling*: which arcs may safely allow light personalization (paying_and_choosing, finding_your_way, what_you_can_do, making_arrangements, and lightly work_and_study) and which must stay at none (`daily_rhythm`'s hosted story, `people_around_you` entirely, because the arc explicitly refuses family/relation personalization — `a1.json.factsToCapture` marks `relation_names` `store:false` for exactly this reason).
- **`neutralFallbackContext`** — the specific neutral value each arc already uses when no personalization applies (already present in the live blueprint's arc descriptions and episode `reusedLanguage`, just not labelled as "the neutral fallback" until now).
- **`transferTarget`** / **`delayedRetrievalTarget`** — see §8/§9.

## 5. Dependency graph

`a1.json.dependencyGraphValidation` records the check performed during this reconciliation: every required capability's prerequisite chain was traced by hand against `a1-map.md` §7's skill graph and `a1-blueprint.json`'s per-capability `prerequisites` arrays. Result: **no cycles**, **no orphan required capability** (every chain terminates in a Pre-A1 required-core capability or an earlier A1 capability already in the table), **no prerequisite absent from the current or an earlier level**. One item stays explicitly open rather than asserted closed: "no level exit requiring evidence the runtime cannot record" is **partially** open, because the per-episode evidence itself is already recordable for implemented arcs, but the A1-specific readiness/graduation derivation function does not exist yet (§15, `coreEngineRequirements.a1ReadinessFunction`) — the live blueprint already flagged this as phase-8, not-yet-built work, and this reconciliation preserves that honesty instead of claiming a check exists that does not.

## 6. Language infrastructure

**Patterns.** Nineteen new pattern groups, one new-pattern-group per episode except integration episodes — carried verbatim (`a1.json.patterns`), each with its communicative function, prerequisite, target reach (comprehension/guided/independent) and reuse arcs already in the shape the template asks for.

**Vocabulary budget.** Fifty new productive items, forty-six receptive, budgeted per arc, not per episode topic — carried verbatim (`a1.json.counts`, `a1.json.arcs[].vocabularyBudget`). The live blueprint's discipline here ("the Garden does not grow by token" — days of the week and numbers 11-100 are one item each) is preserved unchanged and is one of the clearest places the live blueprint already satisfies the master contract's "avoid vocabulary catalogues detached from a communicative need" (template §6) without this reconciliation needing to add anything.

## 7. Personalization plan

`a1.json.personalizationPlan` assembles: the per-arc allowances from §4; the compatible slot types already defined in the runtime contract (`object`, `activity`, `topic`, `subject` — from `a1-authoring-contract.md`'s "Interest personalization" section, carried verbatim, not redesigned); the neutral-fallback-required rule (verbatim from `a1-blueprint.json.personalization`); and the master contract's personalization invariant restated for A1 specifically (`canDoId`, evaluation intent, mastery threshold, required evidence, prerequisite graph, XP, and difficulty never change with personalization — master contract §7). Topic diversity (master contract §8 — explicit interests appear often enough, no interest dominates consecutive sessions, neutral contexts remain present) is explicitly named as **shared/core engine behaviour that A1 does not override**, not a level-specific policy this blueprint invents. The QA proof method (`check:interest-personalization`, the synthetic-template pattern, `invariantDrift`) is carried verbatim from `a1-authoring-contract.md` rather than re-specified.

## 8. Reuse / spacing matrix

The live blueprint's reuse matrix (`a1.json.reuseMatrix.rows`) is carried verbatim: 32 rows (16 Pre-A1 + 16 A1 capabilities) against the 7 arcs, `I`/`R`/`C` marked. This reconciliation adds one explanatory bridge the template needs and the live blueprint's legend does not have: the template's richer legend is `I` introduce / `G` guided reuse / `R` open retrieval / `T` transfer / `F` fluency / `D` delayed retrieval. `a1.json.reuseMatrix.legendMappingToTemplate` explains why the live matrix's stricter two-value reuse legend (`R`/`C`, evaluated-by-a-step only — `check:a1-arc2` through `check:a1-arc5` require every marked reuse to be *exercised*, not merely recognised) already implies the template's `T`/`F`/`D` by construction: any `R`/`C` mark occurring in an arc later than the introducing arc is a genuinely new communicative situation (master contract §6: "topics are contexts, not curriculum" — each arc *is* a different situation), so it satisfies transfer; a `C` mark additionally satisfies fluency (no new language, held together) and, on the closing arc, delayed retrieval across a real arc gap. No separate `G` row exists because the live matrix does not count guided-only mentions as reuse evidence at all — stricter than the template's floor, not weaker.

**Single-appearance risks**, carried verbatim: `ask_about_getting_somewhere`, `ask_the_price`, `buy_something` — all in the transactions branch, all with a stated mitigation (receptive-only return in the closing arc, carried by the shared review scheduler; the should-have transport episode is the first trim candidate).

## 9. Evidence and mastery

`a1.json.evidenceModel` is new synthesis, but every one of its six categories maps onto A1 primitives that already exist rather than inventing new engine behaviour:

| master contract category | A1 primitive it already is |
|---|---|
| recognition | scene/model/comprehension/choice steps |
| guided production | word_order/fill_blank/guided_reply steps |
| open assisted production | free_reply/recall attempts where the shared scaffolding engine recorded a hint/model used (`adaptive-support.md`) |
| open independent production | free_reply/recall/story-reply attempts with no support used — this **is** `canDos[].evidence.independent` in the live blueprint |
| transfer | an `R`/`C` mark in the reuse matrix later than the introducing arc (§8) |
| delayed retrieval | the shared spaced-review scheduler, extended from Pre-A1's overdue-review rule to the A1+Pre-A1 union, exactly as `exitCriteria.readinessDimensionsForA2` already states |

**The exact minimum for graduation is deliberately not stated as a number here.** The live blueprint says so explicitly — "the number is chosen when there is evidence from real journeys, not now" — and Pre-A1's own numbers were chosen the same way, from real journey data (`graduation.md`). Inventing a numeric A1 threshold in this reconciliation, with no journey data behind it, would be exactly the kind of guess CLAUDE.md's research-before-implementation rule and the evidence-supervisor contract both forbid. What A1 does have, and what this reconciliation confirms is already true: **a required capability cannot graduate the level on recognition or guided production alone** — every required capability's evidence path includes an independent-production target and a transfer context (§3/§8), satisfying master contract §9's rule structurally, even before the numeric threshold exists.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

Carried verbatim (`a1.json.autonomy`): early/mid/late phase expectations for model-answer availability, open productions per episode, learner-initiated questions and repair. Restated as what it already is under master contract §10 — an *authoring expectation*, not an override of the shared scaffolding engine, which keeps deciding from the individual learner's evidence exactly as it does for Pre-A1. No A1 arc bypasses central scaffolding logic to make its own content easier to pass.

## 11. Evaluation requirements

`a1.json.evaluationRequirements` carries the intent/subtype strategy and evaluation-locality rule verbatim (`intentStrategy`, `evaluationStrategy` — 14 new intents, 2 new repair subtypes, one level-aware quantity range, 11 deterministic + 3 hybrid functions, no capability requiring the provider to be reachable). This reconciliation deliberately does **not** author the per-intent example sentences the template's §11 checklist asks for (clearly correct / natural variant / near miss / wrong meaning / nonsense / correct-meaning-insufficient-form). Those examples already exist in the runtime evaluator and its test fixtures for arcs 1-5 (`check:hybrid-evaluation`, `check:repair-evaluation`, `check:edge-cases`); writing a parallel set here from memory would risk contradicting what actually ships, which is precisely what CLAUDE.md's "never invent curricular detail from memory" rule exists to prevent. The one flagged open evaluator problem is `can`-ambiguity — see §15.

## 12. Four Strands audit

New synthesis, but qualitative and directly derived from data already in the live blueprint rather than invented percentages (master contract §13 explicitly says "do not force equal percentages per episode... balance is a level/course property"):

- **Meaning-focused input** — present throughout via scene/comprehension/story steps, heaviest in `finding_your_way` (comprehending genuinely partly-unknown answers) and the hosted stories.
- **Meaning-focused output** — the level's dominant strand: every arc's stated evidence target is an unaided statement or question in a real frame.
- **Language-focused learning** — front-loaded: `work_and_study` introduces the core statement frame, `daily_rhythm` is the live blueprint's own admitted "densest moment" (three new pattern groups), density falls through the middle arcs and is near zero by the close.
- **Fluency development** — strongest in each arc's integration episode and across the whole of `making_arrangements`, which the live blueprint already describes as introducing "almost nothing new" while holding a whole arrangement together under a withheld model answer.
- **Out of scope** — listening, speaking and pronunciation remain future capabilities per master contract §13 and CLAUDE.md's voice/media boundary. A1 implements and tests reading/meaning-focused input, written meaning-focused output, chat/roleplay interaction, form-focused learning and written fluency development only.

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

Turn-taking and repair inherit Pre-A1's `ask_for_repair` and add the two new repair frames; repair is planted by design early/mid and learner-chosen late (§10). Conversation length grows from 6 meaningful turns (`work_and_study`) to 12 (`making_arrangements`), always inside the level's own single-topic ceiling — A1 explicitly does not attempt a topic change mid-conversation (`cannotYet`). **Mediation-like tasks (summarizing or simplifying text for another person) are not an A1 capability.** The master contract lists mediation as an available mode in principle for the text-first product (§13), but no A1 arc needs it and this reconciliation does not propose a mediation intent for A1 — that would be inventing scope the live blueprint never asked for. Register/pragmatics stay minimal: polite forms inherited from Pre-A1, no register distinction taught or assessed, consistent with the level's ceiling.

## 14. Explicit exclusions

Carried verbatim: `deferredToA2` (12 items, each with its own "why", including the deliberate-forever exclusion of personal contact details) and `optionalOrNotTaken` (4 items) — see `a1.json.explicitExclusions`. Nothing added, nothing removed. These are the exact boundaries `LC-BP-A2` must respect when it designs A2's own first capabilities.

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

`a1.json.coreEngineRequirements` lists five items and is explicit about which are **resolved** and which remain **open**, because conflating them would misinform whoever plans arc 6/7 work next:

- **OPEN — `canAmbiguity`.** Pre-A1's `Can you repeat, please?` (request) and A1's `Can you swim?` (ability) are the same surface words, different acts. The live blueprint already names this "the level's one real architectural debt" and proposes disambiguating by the semantic type of what follows the verb. It blocks arc `what_you_can_do` (episodes 34-35), which is designed-only — it does **not** block anything already implemented in arcs 1-5.
- **OPEN — `a1ReadinessFunction`.** No A1-specific readiness/graduation derivation exists yet (`levelMilestones.a1`, `MILESTONE_LEVELS` extension). Explicitly deferred to implementation phase 8 in the live blueprint; not blocking arc content authoring.
- **RESOLVED — `perArcChunkLoading`, `levelAwareCurriculumRegistry`, `sessionRunnerObjectiveFormatGuard`.** All three were phase-0/arc-2/arc-3 architecture work already completed and narrated in `a1-authoring-contract.md`. Recorded here only so a future reader of this reconciliation does not mistake them for still-open work.

No new core requirement is proposed by this reconciliation itself — it found nothing the live blueprint had not already surfaced.

## 16. QA acceptance

`a1.json.qaAcceptance` maps every template §16 checklist item to the concrete check or process that already exists for A1: structural validation (`check:a1-blueprint`), per-arc runtime gates (`check:a1-arc1`-`5`, explicitly noting `check:a1-arc6`/`7` do not exist yet), evaluator refusal (`check:hybrid-evaluation`, `check:repair-evaluation`, `check:edge-cases`), personalization invariant (`check:interest-personalization`), prerequisite/reuse (`check:curriculum-map` plus the per-arc reuse assertions), the ≥20-journeys-per-completed-arc requirement (`LC-PED-001`, `pedagogical-journeys-report.md`), assisted-vs-independent proof (`adaptive-support.md`), replay/idempotency (`check:episode-replay`), no-false-mastery (`check:learner-facts`, `check:activity-signals`), browser usability at 390px/1440px per CLAUDE.md's QA protocol, and two clean full cycles after the final change of any implementation sprint.

## 17. Cross-level acceptance

`a1.json.crossLevelAcceptance`. The two items resolved by this reconciliation: prior-level (Pre-A1) exit supplies every A1 prerequisite (§5), and no A1 capability duplicates a Pre-A1 one (checked directly against `preA1Inheritance`). Two items are explicitly left as **obligations for later tasks**, not resolved here, because resolving them requires content this task's write scope does not include:

- **`LC-BP-A2`** must confirm A1's exit genuinely supplies A2's first capability (`talk_about_what_you_did`) and must resolve the `arrange_to_meet` CEFR-boundary question (§3) rather than silently restating the same capability at A2.
- **`LC-AUD-001`** (the cross-level audit, which depends on this task) is where "graduation evidence attainable through real runtime paths" gets its final confirmation, once `coreEngineRequirements.a1ReadinessFunction` is built.

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Six questions this reconciliation surfaces that automation cannot answer, listed in `a1.json.humanPilotQuestions`: whether the ability/request `can` disambiguation feels natural once arc 6 ships; whether `arrange_to_meet` feels A1-appropriate to a real beginner; whether the two single-appearance transactional capabilities need earlier reinforcement than the review scheduler alone provides once real delayed-retention data exists; whether `daily_rhythm`'s admitted density causes measurable frustration; whether light personalization on the transactions/ability arcs feels personal rather than repetitive; and whether withholding the model answer by default in the late arcs measurably affects confidence or completion. None of these are answerable from the design alone — they are exactly the kind of claim CLAUDE.md's research-before-implementation rule reserves for real-learner pilot evidence.
