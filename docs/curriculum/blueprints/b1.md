# B1 level blueprint

This is a design artifact under the [level blueprint template](../level-blueprint-template.md) and the
[A1-C2 curriculum master](../curriculum-master-a1-c2.md). It does not make B1 available and no runtime
module imports it. The machine-checkable capability graph is
[`b1.json`](b1.json); this document is its narrative companion and the two must be read together — a
change to one without the other is an incomplete edit.

## 1. Level identity

- **Level:** B1
- **Blueprint version:** `b1.blueprint.v1`
- **Status:** planned
- **Prerequisite level:** A2
- **CEFR source references:** the global CEFR scale's B1 band (independent user), the Common European
  Framework Companion Volume's B1 descriptors for spoken production/interaction, discussion, mediation
  and pragmatic competence. Every capability below cites the specific descriptor it targets.
- **Product availability:** false until a separate release gate. This blueprint alone never flips
  `available` on any level.

## 2. Exit promise

At the start of B1, a learner can handle short, predictable exchanges (A1) and connected everyday
communication about the past, near future, comparisons and simple reasons (A2, expected — see section 5
on the prerequisite-reconciliation problem this blueprint is honest about).

By the end of B1, that same learner can reliably:

- **narrate a personal experience as a connected sequence**, including one moment that was already
  happening when something else interrupted it — not a list of separate sentences;
- **hold and defend an opinion**: state it with a reason, and agree or disagree with someone else's,
  politely;
- **compare more than two options on more than one quality** and justify a choice, then turn that
  judgment into a recommendation or a warning for someone else;
- **describe a place, event or service richly enough to be useful** — more than one attribute, plus how
  it made them feel;
- **raise and resolve an everyday problem**: explain clearly what went wrong, negotiate a solution, and
  stay cooperative even while frustrated;
- **talk about the future with real nuance**: choose correctly between a spontaneous decision, a fixed
  plan and a prediction, say what they hope for with no date attached, and say what happens under a real
  condition;
- **keep a conversation moving**: change topic politely, follow someone else changing it, ask a genuine
  follow-up question, and summarize what somebody said.

**Explicit ceilings — what stays out of reach until B2:** formal or deliberately-switched register; the
present perfect and any other perfect aspect; the passive voice; reported speech; a full second or third
conditional; sustained argument with counter-arguments and concession; indirect/embedded questions;
modals of deduction and speculation; abstract or specialist topics. Section 14 lists these with reasons,
and section 15 flags what the shared engine would need before any of them could move earlier.

this task. So every A2-facing prerequisite below is an **expected A2 exit capability**, not a verified
one — each is either an id A1 already coined in its own `deferredToA2` list, or, where no such id exists,
a plainly-labeled coined id derived only from the master contract's own prose description of A2 (section

## 6. Language infrastructure

### Patterns / grammar

Full table (form, prerequisite pattern, first arc, reuse arcs) is `b1.json`'s `patterns[]` (21 entries).
The shape worth stating in prose: **B1 adds almost no new tenses** — one (past continuous) — and instead
spends its grammar budget on **choices between forms the learner may already partly know** (will vs
going to), **connective language** (sequence connectors, because, topic-change phrases), and **fixed
functional frames** (opinion frame, negotiate frame, first conditional, if-I-were frame). This is a
deliberate reading of the master's progression note for B1 — "shift ... from highly predictable exchanges
toward flexible use of known language" (section 4) is a claim about *use*, not about a longer grammar
syllabus, and the pattern list reflects that: 21 new pattern groups against only 8 deferred grammar
systems (section 14), most of which (perfect aspect, passive, reported speech, second/third conditional)
are genuinely new systems rather than variations on B1's own.

Grammar introduced only because a traditional syllabus expects it at "B1" was explicitly rejected twice
during design: **third-person plural agreement in comparisons** and **a full set of discourse markers**
(furthermore, moreover, nevertheless) were considered and cut — the first because A1/A2 already produce
`is`/`are` correctly for the sentences B1 needs, the second because they belong to written/formal
register, which section 14 defers.

### Vocabulary budget

| | productive | receptive |
|---|---|---|
| total new | 82 | 52 |

No incidental scene language or multiword-unit-as-whole-vocabulary tracking is proposed as new
infrastructure at B1; the level reuses Pre-A1/A1/A2's existing vocabulary-catalogue mechanism. The
budget is deliberately connector-heavy rather than content-word-heavy: of the 82 productive items,
roughly 60% are discourse connectors, opinion/negotiation/topic-change frames and evaluative adjectives
that transfer across every arc, and roughly 40% are the small closed sets each arc's situation needs
(problem nouns, future-time words). This ratio is the concrete evidence for the "flexible use of known
language" reading above: the level teaches learners *how to connect and qualify* what they can already
say, more than it teaches them new things to say.

No B1 arc introduces a vocabulary catalogue detached from a communicative need — every item in every
capability's `productiveVocabulary`/`receptiveVocabulary` count in `b1.json` traces to a specific pattern
that specific capability's evidence path requires.

## 7. Personalization plan

Per the master's personalization invariant (section 7): personalization may change what the conversation
is about; it may never change `canDoId`, evaluation intent, mastery threshold, required evidence,
prerequisite graph, XP/reward, or difficulty.

- **Explicit interests allowed:** `which_one` (comparing/describing something the learner is actually
  into) and `keep_talking` (a topic-change conversation naturally wants a real interest to move toward).
- **Related contexts allowed:** `what_happened`, `looking_ahead` — a story or a hope framed near an
  interest without being about it exclusively.
- **Neutral-required:** `somethings_wrong` — a problem scenario personalized to an interest risks turning
  a negotiation into product placement for the learner's own hobby; every learner gets the same neutral
  problem shapes (a delivery, a booking, a purchase), which is also the most realistic frequency
  distribution for this situation in real life.
- **Compatible semantic slot types:** `activity`, `place`, `interest` (existing); the new `problem` type
  is deliberately **not** personalization-eligible — a problem is authored content, not a learner's
  stated interest.
- **Neutral fallback:** every personalized slot in `which_one` and `keep_talking` falls back to a neutral
  place/activity/experience when no safe personalized value exists, exactly as the A1 story-personalization
  contract already requires (`docs/curriculum/a1-authoring-contract.md`, "Interest personalization").
- **Recent-topic cooldown:** inherited from the shared personalization engine; no B1-specific cooldown
  rule is proposed.
- **How QA will prove two themes preserve identical capability/evidence/difficulty:** the existing
  `invariantDrift` mechanism (`docs/curriculum/a1-authoring-contract.md`) is the correct tool and needs
  no B1-specific extension — every B1 template that declares `slots` must pass it before content lands,
  exactly as A1 templates do. This is noted rather than re-specified because it is shared-core behavior,
  not level-owned.

No B1 blueprint capability personalizes `canDoId`, evaluator intent, evidence threshold, prerequisite,
reward or difficulty. `problem` scenarios in `somethings_wrong` and the future/hope statements in
`looking_ahead` are the two places a design pressure toward "make it about the learner's real life"
was strongest and was deliberately declined — a learner's real problems and real ambitions are exactly
the kind of personal content the product should not be capturing as gradable practice input.

## 8. Reuse / spacing matrix

The full matrix (7 arcs × 24 rows: 7 expected-A2 capabilities + 17 B1 capabilities) is `b1.json`'s
`reuseMatrix`. Reading the shape rather than every cell:

- Every one of the seven A2-expected capabilities is reused exactly once, in the B1 arc that most
  directly extends it (`talk_about_what_you_did` → `what_happened`, `express_an_opinion_with_a_reason` → `i_think_that`,
  and so on) — B1 does not open with a generic "review" episode; each A2 capability's B1 return is the
  moment it becomes the infrastructure for something new.
- Every required B1 capability is marked `D` (delayed retrieval) in `the_long_conversation`, the level's
  final arc, which runs at least one session after any capability's home episode by construction (it is
  the last arc).
- No required capability disappears for more than two arcs without a reuse row. The one which_one
  capability (`recommend_or_warn`, should-have) and the two looking_ahead should/optional capabilities
  (`talk_about_real_conditions`, `imagine_a_hypothetical`) do not carry a `D` mark into the capstone —
  see section 9 for why should/optional delayed retrieval is not required for graduation, matching A1's
  own precedent for its should-have capabilities.
- `describe_an_experience` and `give_an_opinion` are each reused inside two different later arcs before
  the capstone (`R` in `somethings_wrong`/`keep_talking` and `which_one`/`looking_ahead`/`keep_talking`
  respectively) — these are the level's two most load-bearing capabilities by reuse count, which matches
  their role as the justification layer nearly everything else in the level draws on.

## 9. Evidence and mastery

Inherits the master's six-category evidence model (section 9) unchanged: recognition, guided production,
open assisted production, open independent production, transfer, delayed retrieval. No B1 capability is
graduated on recognition or guided production alone.

- **Recognition evidence:** the learner correctly identifies which of two given sentences shows the
  target function (e.g., which of two sentences is a real opinion with a reason, not just a fact).
- **Guided production evidence:** the learner completes a frame with the missing piece (a connector, a
  form choice between will/going to) where most of the sentence is supplied.
- **Assisted open evidence:** the learner produces a full sentence/turn freely but with a model or hint
  visible on screen.
- **Independent open evidence:** the learner produces a full sentence/turn freely with no model on
  screen. This is the minimum bar the `independentEvidence` field in every required capability's JSON
  record states explicitly, and it is stated in numbers per capability (typically "two unaided X" or
  "one whole Y held unaided"), never as bare "the learner can do X."
- **Transfer evidence:** every required and should-have capability's `transferContexts` field names a
  genuinely novel realization — a topic, combination or scenario not modeled during teaching — that its
  independent evidence must be checkable against, not only the taught examples.
- **Delayed retrieval evidence:** `the_long_conversation` (arc 7) is the level's sole delayed-retrieval
  arc for every required capability, run at least one session after each capability's home arc.

**Minimum for graduation (stated now, revisable after real learner data, per the master's rule that the
exact threshold is chosen from evidence, not asserted in advance):** every **required** capability needs
at least the independent-evidence count stated in its own `b1.json` record, achieved across the learner's
own runtime history (a single arc pass plus, where the arc's own episode count does not reach the target
alone, a later replay/daily-session/capstone occurrence — the same "lifetime target, not an arc-exit
requirement" reading A1's `a1-authoring-contract.md` already establishes for `independent: 2`), **plus**
at least one transfer-context success and one delayed-retrieval success in `the_long_conversation`.
Should-have and optional capabilities are evidenced when the arc plays but are **not** a graduation
blocker if the arc's required capabilities already meet their own bar — exactly A1's own precedent for
its should-have capabilities (e.g., `ask_someone_about_ability`, `independent: 1`, no capstone
delayed-retrieval requirement).

Completion of every B1 episode is explicitly **not** mastery. A learner who has clicked through all
seven arcs without ever producing an unaided sentence has completed the level and evidenced nothing;
`hasRuntimeContent()`/`isLevelComplete()`-style distinctions (already drawn for A1 in
`a1-authoring-contract.md`) apply to B1 unchanged once content exists.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

No B1-specific scaffolding logic is proposed; the level uses the shared adaptive support engine
(`docs/curriculum/adaptive-support.md`) exactly as A1 does, per the master's scaffolding contract
(section 10).

- **Genuinely novel language:** past continuous (arc 1), the will/going to/prediction three-way choice
  (arc 5), and negotiation (arc 4) are the level's three highest-novelty moments; expect support to start
  higher there and fade faster elsewhere, since most of B1's grammar is a recombination of A1/A2 forms
  rather than new morphology.
- **Strong-prerequisite entry behaviour:** a learner who enters B1 with strong A2 evidence for
  `express_an_opinion_with_a_reason` and `describe_a_person_or_place` should see lower initial support in `i_think_that`
  and `which_one` than a learner whose A2 evidence for those same capabilities is weak or absent — this
  is the shared engine's existing evidence-based behavior, not a B1 override.
- **Expected support fade:** fastest in `keep_talking` (arc 6), because every capability it reuses has
  already been evidenced by the time the learner reaches it; slowest in `looking_ahead` (arc 5), because
  three related-but-distinct future forms compete for the same surface cues (`I'll`) and repeated strain
  there should restore help quickly rather than being read as level-wide difficulty.
- **Known high-strain task:** `negotiate_a_solution`, because it is the level's only capability that
  requires holding a multi-turn goal (get a specific outcome) against a partner who does not simply
  agree — a single failed negotiation should not be read as insufficient evidence for the underlying
  language, and repair should be available inside the same turn rather than only via retry.
- **Recovery after repeated failure:** standard shared-engine behavior — a single success/failure must
  not cause oscillation; this blueprint asks for nothing beyond what A1 already relies on.
- **Replay behaviour once capability is strong:** replaying `the_long_conversation` should route the
  learner through a different topic-change order and a different problem type than their first pass,
  since its evidentiary value is specifically that the combination is not memorized — this is a content
  authoring note for the eventual episode design, not a new engine capability.

**If B1 content design later discovers the shared engine cannot express one of these expectations, that
is a CORE requirement to raise through the master's authoring-architecture channel (section 14), not a
private per-arc workaround.** Section 15 below already identifies the two places this blueprint expects
that conversation will be necessary.

## 11. Evaluation requirements

Every new B1 intent (`b1.json`'s `intentStrategy.newIntents`, 14 total) needs, per the runtime authoring
contract's rule 6, an evaluator branch that rejects nonsense, and both a `PROMPT` and a `MODEL_ANSWER`
entry in the daily session runner. Representative examples for the level's two structurally hardest
intents:

**`state_future_intent` (subtype `intentForm: will_going_to`)** — the arc's own named architectural risk:

| case | example | why |
|---|---|---|
| clearly correct (decision) | "I'll have the pasta." | spontaneous choice, will is correct |
| clearly correct (plan) | "I'm going to visit my sister next week." | prior arrangement, going to is correct |
| natural variant | "I think I'll have the pasta." | hedge doesn't change the function |
| near miss | "I'm going to have the pasta." for a menu decision made on the spot | grammatically fine, wrong function — this is the case the evaluator must actually catch, not just accept as "a future sentence" |
| wrong meaning | "I had the pasta." | past, not future — should never pass as an attempt at this intent |
| nonsense | "I'll pasta tomorrow." | ungrammatical, must be rejected regardless of topic match |
| correct meaning, insufficient form | "have pasta" with no subject/auxiliary | meaning is inferable but the target form (will/going to) is not present; B1 evaluation must not accept bare-verb telegraphic speech the way A1's more forgiving evaluator might |

**`negotiate_solution`:**

| case | example | why |
|---|---|---|
| clearly correct | "Would it be possible to get a replacement instead?" | canonical negotiate frame |
| natural variant | "Could I possibly exchange this for a different one?" | different surface form, same function |
| near miss | "I want a replacement." | states the goal but skips the negotiation register the capability specifically targets — should be flagged as underdeveloped, not simply passed |
| wrong meaning | "I'd like to buy another one." | offers to buy, not to resolve the existing problem — a different speech act entirely |
| nonsense | "Replacement would I get." | rejected regardless of vocabulary present |
| pragmatically inappropriate | "Give me a replacement now." | grammatically valid, tone violates the arc's cooperative-negotiation ceiling — B1 is the first level where tone appropriateness, not just grammar, is part of what a correct answer means |

The last row is new relative to A1's evaluation requirements (A1 had no pragmatic-appropriateness
dimension) and is the concrete instance of the master's corrective-feedback progression note (section 11)
that "a C-level response may be grammatically correct and still fail the target if its register or
pragmatic force is inappropriate" — B1 is where that dimension first has to exist, even in a light form,
ahead of full C1/C2 pragmatic assessment.

## 12. Four Strands audit

Estimated level-wide balance (a course property, not an episode-by-episode requirement, per the master):

- **Meaning-focused input:** moderate-high — every arc's mini-story or partner turns carry language
  beyond the taught frame (an unmodeled connector, an unexpected offer in a negotiation, a partner's
  topic change), consistent with A1/A2's existing practice of receptive language exceeding productive
  budget.
- **Meaning-focused output:** the level's dominant strand by design — B1's whole premise is flexible
  production (opinions, negotiations, descriptions), not comprehension.
- **Language-focused learning:** lower than A1 relative to output — B1 introduces one new tense and
  spends most explicit attention on choosing between known forms (will vs going to) rather than on new
  morphology, matching the "flexible use of known language" reading in section 6.
- **Fluency development:** concentrated in `the_long_conversation` (arc 7) and in the should-have reuse
  rows across arcs 3-6 — known language returning under a genuinely new combination (a real conversation
  that was not scripted turn-by-turn) rather than a fixed drill.

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

This is the section where B1 most visibly earns the master's "shift from highly predictable exchanges
toward flexible use" framing (section 4):

- **Turn-taking and repair:** inherited from A1/A2's `ask_for_repair` mechanism unchanged; B1 adds no new
  repair type. What changes is that repair now has to survive across a topic change, not just within one
  fixed exchange.
- **Conversation length/topic changes:** the level's own `sustain_topic_change`/`ask_follow_up_questions`
  capabilities are the first place LinguaChat's curriculum deliberately teaches moving a conversation,
  rather than holding it to one topic by design (A1's `interactionCeiling` was explicitly "one situation,
  one topic"; B1's is "one to two related topics ... natural topic changes allowed").
- **Discourse coherence:** `narrate_connected_event`'s sequence connectors and `keep_talking`'s
  topic-change/follow-up/summary trio are B1's two discourse-coherence mechanisms; neither existed as a
  capability at A1/A2.
- **Summarization/reformulation:** `summarize_what_was_said` is the level's one explicitly mediation-shaped
  task (Nation's Four Strands / master section 13's "mediation-like tasks such as summarizing or
  simplifying text for another person") — a should-have, not required, since the arc's required
  independence is already evidenced without it.
- **Register/pragmatics:** introduced lightly and for the first time (section 11's pragmatic-appropriateness
  example), but B1 stays informal throughout by design — deliberate register switching is deferred to B2
  (section 14).

## 14. Explicit exclusions

What B1 deliberately does **not** teach, so a later worker does not silently expand scope:

- **Formal register / deliberate register-switching** — B1 stays informal throughout; register control is
  a B2 assessment dimension.
- **Present perfect and any other perfect aspect** — connecting past to present is a distinct aspect from
  B1's simple-past narration and deserves its own arc, not a late addition to `narrate_connected_event`.
- **Passive voice** — no B1 required capability needs an unnamed agent.
- **Reported speech / backshift** — `summarize_what_was_said` covers B1's mediation need without a full
  reported-speech system.
- **Second and third conditional (beyond the one fixed `imagine_a_hypothetical` frame)** — a genuinely
  productive hypothetical grammar is B2-scale load.
- **Deliberate hedging/softening as a register skill** (I suppose, sort of, to some extent) — belongs with
  B2's explicit pragmatic-appropriateness assessment.
- **Indirect/embedded questions** — a politeness upgrade on structures B1 already has direct forms for;
  not required for B1 independence.
- **Modals of deduction and speculation** (must be, can't have been) — inference language for evidence B1
  conversations do not yet require the learner to weigh.
- **Sustained argument with counter-arguments/concession** — needs concession language B1 does not have
  as a productive structure; deferred to B2 alongside advantage/disadvantage weighing.
- **Abstract or specialist topics outside everyday experience** — stays inside the same "familiar,
  everyday" ceiling A2 already establishes; B1's shift is in flexibility of use, not topic abstraction.

The complete list with individual rationale is `b1.json`'s `deferredToB2` (8 entries) and
`optionalOrNotTaken` (3 entries).

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

Two genuine shared-core questions surfaced while designing B1, neither of which this blueprint is
positioned to answer by itself (per the master's authoring-architecture rule, section 14: "when a level
worker discovers a shared-core need, it must raise the requirement instead of quietly implementing a
private workaround"):

### 15.1 Evaluation density shift toward hybrid

- **Problem:** 13 of B1's 14 new intents are marked `hybrid` in `b1.json`'s `evaluationStrategy`, against
  A1's 3 of 14. B1's whole point — opinions, comparisons, negotiations, discourse moves — is language too
  flexible for a small canonical-string evaluator to cover with confidence.
- **Why the current engine's shape may not already answer this:** A1's hybrid capabilities
  (`introduce_person`, `state_person_fact`, `ask_transport`) are a minority pattern there; nothing in the
  existing evaluator design has had to prove it stays *reliable*, not just *available*, at hybrid-majority
  density, or that the "conservative local verdict stands when the provider is unreachable" rule (which
  every A1/B1 capability depends on for offline QA) still produces a usably low false-reject rate when
  most of a level's intents rely on it.
- **Earliest level that needs it:** B1 (this blueprint).
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** before B1 content authoring begins, measure the existing local evaluator's
  conclusive-vs-inconclusive rate on a representative sample of B1-shaped open sentences (the near-miss
  and natural-variant examples in section 11 are a starting corpus), and decide whether the local
  fallback needs a genuinely richer heuristic layer or whether B1's evidence targets should be phrased to
  tolerate a higher `conclusive: false` rate gracefully.
- **Regression risk:** none to A1/Pre-A1 if the investigation is read-only; a regression risk only appears
  if whatever the investigation proposes changes shared evaluator code, which is exactly why it must be a
  separate serialized core task rather than something this blueprint or the eventual B1 content task
  quietly works around.
- **Tests required:** a measurement harness (not a shipped feature) over a representative B1-shaped
  sentence corpus, reported as a finding to whichever core task follows.

### 15.2 Discourse-level (multi-turn) evaluation for `sustain_topic_change` / `ask_follow_up_questions`

- **Problem:** judging whether a topic change was natural, or a follow-up question genuine, is a judgment
  about *this turn in relation to the previous one or two turns* — every other B1 (and A1) capability is
  evaluated on a single turn's content against a single turn's target.
- **Why the current engine's shape may not already answer this:** the existing evaluator interface, as
  far as this blueprint task can determine without touching runtime code (which is outside its write
  scope), is designed around one turn at a time. If it already carries enough conversational context to
  make this judgment, arc 6 needs no new engine capability and this item is moot; if it does not, arc 6's
  two required capabilities cannot be built to their stated evidence bar without one.
- **Earliest level that needs it:** B1 (arc `keep_talking`), the first level where a capability's
  correctness is a claim about discourse relationship rather than sentence content.
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** confirm, before `keep_talking` content is authored, whether the runtime
  evaluator call already receives the immediately preceding partner turn as context; if not, define the
  smallest extension that lets a step declare "judge this turn in relation to the last partner turn"
  without turning the evaluator into a full conversation-history reader.
- **Regression risk:** low if scoped to an opt-in field a step can declare, matching how `partner_name`
  was threaded through the pipeline for A1 arc 3 (`a1-authoring-contract.md`, "The subtype that had to
  travel") without touching turns that do not declare it.
- **Tests required:** synthetic step-level tests proving a genuine topic change passes, a non-sequitur
  fails, a genuine follow-up passes, and a restated-not-followed-up turn fails — the same "prove it
  rejects nonsense" bar every other intent already meets.

Neither item blocks this blueprint from being considered structurally complete; both must be resolved
(or explicitly re-scoped) before `LC-CONT-B1` can implement arcs 4-6 to their stated evidence bar.

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Claims automation cannot prove, to test later with real learners:

- **Perceived difficulty of the will/going to/prediction three-way choice** (arc 5) — does it feel like
  one coherent judgment or three separate rules to a real learner, and does the order (decision → plan →
  prediction, as currently sequenced) match how learners naturally discover the distinction?
- **Confidence during negotiation** (`negotiate_a_solution`) — does holding a multi-turn goal against a
  partner who does not simply agree feel like a fair, encouraging challenge or an anxiety-inducing one at
  this stage of learning?
- **Whether "flexible use of known language" actually reads as easier or harder than A1/A2 to a real
  learner** — the design bet in section 6 (fewer new tenses, more connective/functional language) is a
  hypothesis about what makes B1 feel different from A2, not a proven one.
- **Whether topic changes in `keep_talking` and the capstone feel natural or scripted** — the risk named
  in the capstone arc's own `risk` field ("could become a review episode with a new label") is exactly the
  kind of thing only a real learner's reaction can settle.
- **Usefulness of `express_frustration_politely` and `imagine_a_hypothetical`** as should/optional
  capabilities — do learners who skip them (because the arc's required capabilities already met their
  bar) feel the level was complete, or do they notice the gap?
- **Delayed retention of `narrate_interrupted_action`** — past continuous is the level's only new tense
  and its evidence path is comparatively thin (two unaided sentences plus one capstone recall); real
  spaced-retrieval data should inform whether that bar is sufficient before the threshold is treated as
  final.
- **Spontaneous transfer of `summarize_what_was_said`** outside the specific mediation framing taught —
  does the skill generalize to a learner's real conversations, which is the entire point of a
  mediation-shaped task, or does it stay tied to the taught frame?
