# LinguaChat C2 blueprint

This blueprint follows `docs/curriculum/level-blueprint-template.md` and obeys
`docs/curriculum/curriculum-master-a1-c2.md`. It is a design artifact only.
Nothing here is imported by runtime code and it does not change `A1`
availability, which stays gated by the existing A1 completion rules.

**Design note on prerequisites.** C2 was originally authored in parallel with the B1, B2
and C1 blueprints (`LC-BP-B1`, `LC-BP-B2`, `LC-BP-C1`), per the master
contract's parallel-authoring rule (§18), citing "assumed C1 exit capability"
placeholders quoting the C1 progression prose already frozen in the master
contract (§4) rather than inventing a C1 blueprint on C1's behalf. **Resolved
by `LC-AUD-001`** (`docs/curriculum/cross-level-audit.json` F3, F6) **and applied
by `LC-FND-002`**, now that `LC-BP-C1` has landed: six of the seven assumed
placeholders are renamed to C1's real ids (`c1.x` dot-notation), and C2's own
`reformulate_for_a_different_audience` — which had collided with C1's
identically-named, near-duplicate capability, with this document's "first
sustained mediation task" framing directly contradicted by C1 already owning
that id (F3) — is renamed to `reformulate_dense_source_for_a_new_audience`
and now correctly prerequisites `c1.reformulate_for_a_different_audience` as
a genuine escalation (dense source-text extraction plus audience
reformulation) rather than a re-declaration. One row

low-confidence finding and was deliberately left unresolved pending a human
curriculum-design decision. The full mapping is `c2.json`'s
`c1Inheritance.assumedExitCapabilities[].resolvedTo`.

## 2. Exit promise

At the end of C2, a learner can reliably, **in written/typed
interaction**:

- read a dense, abstract or argumentative text and reconstruct its claim,
  its support, and what the writer leaves unstated;
- reformulate that same material precisely for a different, named audience
  without flattening its nuance or inventing certainty the source lacks;
- notice when a message means more (or less, or something other) than its
  literal words — irony, understatement, hedged claims, indirect requests
  and indirect refusals — and respond in a way that shows they noticed;
- deliberately shift register (formal/neutral/informal, technical/lay) to
  fit a stated audience and purpose, and edit their own text when the first
  draft's tone is wrong for that purpose;
- build an extended, qualified argument; anticipate the strongest
  counter-argument; concede what deserves conceding without abandoning the
  position;
- keep a long, topic-shifting exchange coherent, repair a misunderstanding
  at the level of what someone *meant* rather than only what they *typed*,
  and stay functional inside an unfamiliar or high-ambiguity exchange rather
  than defaulting to a rehearsed script;
- mediate between two people, or two texts, who disagree — carrying each
  side's position faithfully to the other without editorializing, and
  closing the exchange usefully.

**Explicit ceilings — what C2 does not claim:**

- **Listening, speaking, live spoken interaction and pronunciation** are not
  taught or evaluated. C2 in this product means the implemented
  text/typed-interaction curriculum aligned to CEFR C2 progression, not
  certification of every CEFR mode, per master contract §3 and §13.
- C2 does not claim native-speaker-identical intuition, real-time
  simultaneous interpretation, or specialist professional registers this
  product never teaches (legal drafting, medical documentation, literary
  criticism as a genre — see §14 Explicit exclusions).
- C2 does not certify anything outside LinguaChat's own runtime evidence. A
  learner who exits C2 here has proven the capabilities below inside this
  product's tasks; it is not an external CEFR certificate.

## 4. Skill families and arc map

Eight arcs. Full per-arc fields (conversation targets, personalization mode,
neutral fallback, transfer target, delayed retrieval target, Four Strands
weighting, risk) are in `c2.json` → `arcs`. Summary:

1. **`dense_input_synthesis`** — "Making sense of dense material." The
   learner reads argumentative/abstract text and reconstructs claim,
   support, gaps.
2. **`precise_reformulation`** — "Saying it precisely, for someone else."
   Mediation: reformulating and summarizing without losing or inventing
   nuance.
3. **`implication_and_subtext`** — "Reading between the lines." Noticing
   and responding to what is implied, not only what is said.
4. **`register_and_pragmatics`** — "Saying it the right way for the
   moment." Deliberate register control, hedging/boosting, face-work.
5. **`argument_and_position`** — "Building and defending a nuanced
   position." Extended argument, rebuttal, precise qualification.
6. **`discourse_flexibility`** — "Keeping a long, unpredictable exchange
   coherent." Topic shifts, intention-level repair, unfamiliar exchanges.
7. **`stylistic_control`** — "Shaping your own language on purpose."
   Self-editing for tone/precision, lexical variety, genre adaptation.
8. **`integrated_mediation`** (capstone) — "Standing between two people who
   disagree." Combines synthesis, register, argument-tracking and repair in
   one extended high-ambiguity task; the level's `arrange_to_meet`-equivalent
   integrative proof.

Arcs 1–3 lean meaning-focused input; arcs 4, 5 and 7 lean language-focused
control; arc 6 is fluency/discourse management under real conditions; arc 8
integrates all of it and is where independent + transfer + delayed-retrieval
evidence for the required capabilities converges for graduation purposes.

## 6. Language infrastructure

### Patterns / discourse-pragmatic devices

C2 does not introduce new sentence-level grammar as its main driver — a
learner entering C2 (via the assumed C1 exit) already controls complex
clause structure. What C2 adds is **control over how form carries stance,
certainty, and social meaning**. The "patterns" below are discourse
devices, not grammar frames; each still has a communicative function,
prerequisite, first appearance, guided/independent target, and reuse, per
template §6. Full table in `c2.json` → `patterns`. Groups:

- **Hedging** (`it could be argued that`, `arguably`, `to some extent`,
  `not necessarily`) — softens a claim's certainty without weakening its
  content.
- **Boosting** (`undeniably`, `there is little doubt that`, `it is clear
  that`) — the hedging family's other pole; overuse of either is itself a
  C2-relevant error (sounding either mealy-mouthed or overconfident).
- **Concession-then-position** (`while X has merit, ...`, `granted that...,
  nevertheless...`, `even so`) — carries a rebuttal without dismissing the
  opposing point.
- **Evidentiality / stance marking** (`presumably`, `ostensibly`,
  `allegedly`, `according to X`) — signals how much the speaker vouches for
  a claim, distinct from the claim itself.
- **Reformulation connectors** (`in other words`, `to put it another way`,
  `put simply, for a non-specialist`) — the explicit signal that a
  restatement is coming, and for whom.
- **Register-shift lexis and syntax** (formal nominalizations vs
  conversational verb phrases; `I'm afraid I have to disagree` vs `nah, that's
  not right`) — matched pairs across formal/neutral/informal registers for
  the same proposition.
- **Face-saving disagreement** (`I take your point, but...`, `that's fair,
  though I'd push back on...`) — disagreement that preserves the
  relationship, contrasted with blunt disagreement that C2 must recognize as
  available but register-costly.
- **Cohesion at paragraph/multi-turn scale** (`insofar as`, `whereby`,
  `that said`, `by the same token`) — connectors that operate above the
  single sentence, needed once the learner is expected to sustain a
  multi-turn argument rather than one clean utterance.

### Vocabulary budget

Unlike A1–B2, C2's productive vocabulary growth is not chiefly about new
concrete nouns; it is about **precision upgrades to concepts the learner
can already express roughly**, plus register-paired lexis and the discourse
markers above. Per arc (declared per-arc in `c2.json`):

- **Productive:** 6–10 precision/collocation items or discourse markers per
  arc (e.g., moving from "very important" to "pivotal", "salient",
  "decisive" — each tied to a real discourse need in that arc, never a bare
  synonym list).
- **Receptive:** 12–18 lower-frequency, register-marked or domain-adjacent
  items per arc, since C2 reading input is deliberately denser than
  production demands.
- **Incidental scene language:** unbounded but never tested productively —
  dense source texts may contain vocabulary the learner is not required to
  produce.
- **Multiword units tracked whole:** the discourse-marker phrases above are
  tracked as single units, exactly as Pre-A1/A1 already track
  `full_greeting` or `cafe_order` as whole frames rather than word-by-word
  grammar.

No vocabulary catalogue is introduced without a capability that needs it,
per master contract §6.

## 7. Personalization plan

- **Arcs allowing explicit interests:** 1 (`dense_input_synthesis`), 2
  (`precise_reformulation`), 5 (`argument_and_position`) — source texts and
  debate topics can be drawn from the learner's stated interests (e.g., a
  gaming-industry controversy instead of a generic policy debate) as long as
  the underlying task (reconstruct claim/support, reformulate, argue) stays
  identical.
- **Arcs accepting related contexts:** 3 (`implication_and_subtext`), 7
  (`stylistic_control`) — adjacent subjects (a learner interested in film
  gets media criticism rather than literary criticism) keep material
  motivating without inventing a new capability.
- **Arcs requiring neutral, real-life situations:** 4
  (`register_and_pragmatics`), 6 (`discourse_flexibility`), 8
  (`integrated_mediation`) — register control, unfamiliar-exchange handling
  and mediation must be provable in neutral, non-hobby situations (workplace
  disagreement, a landlord/tenant dispute, a community-notice mediation)
  because these are the situations most learners will actually need C2 for.
- **Compatible semantic slot types:** `source_text`, `audience_profile`,
  `register_level`, `stance_marker` (see §9 below) all accept a neutral or
  interest-flavored value; the evaluator never inspects topic identity, only
  task completion, exactly as the master contract requires.
- **Neutral fallback:** every personalized slot has a documented neutral
  value (e.g., a `source_text` about workplace policy or a neighborhood
  issue) used whenever no safe interest-aligned value exists — carried over
  from the existing personalization engine's "neutral wins" rule.
- **Recent-topic cooldown:** identical to the existing engine's cooldown
  behavior; C2 introduces no new cooldown semantics.
- **QA proof of invariant:** two learners with different interests must
  receive different `source_text`/topic content but identical `canDoId`,
  evaluator intent, mastery threshold, required-evidence count and reward —
  proved the same way `check:interest-personalization` already proves it for
  A1, extended to C2's fixtures once `LC-CONT-C2` exists.

## 8. Reuse / spacing matrix

Full arc-by-arc reuse markers (`I`/`G`/`R`/`T`/`F`/`D`) are in `c2.json` →
`arcs[].reuseMap`. Structural guarantees this blueprint makes:

- Every required capability introduced in arcs 1–7 is reused (`G` or `R`) in
  at least one later arc before arc 8, and is required as `T` (transfer) or
  `D` (delayed retrieval) inside arc 8's capstone task.
- `recognize_implied_meaning` and `shift_register_deliberately`, being the
  two capabilities every other arc leans on, appear in every arc from their
  introduction onward — at minimum as `F` (fluency use), never disappearing
  for a full arc.
- No required capability's only two appearances are back-to-back arcs
  (immediate-only reuse does not count as spacing, per master contract §12).

## 9. Evidence and mastery

C2 evidence types follow master contract §9 exactly; no C2-specific
weakening is introduced. Because C2's whole point is nuance and judgment
under ambiguity, the mix skews harder than lower levels:

- **Recognition** — e.g., correctly identify which of three reformulations
  preserves the source's hedge; never sufficient alone.
- **Guided production** — reformulate with a supplied audience profile and
  a partially-scaffolded frame.
- **Open assisted production** — reformulate/argue freely but with a
  hint, model excerpt, or "consider mentioning X" nudge visible.
- **Open independent production** — the same task with no support surfaced.
- **Transfer** — the same capability proven on a source text/topic/register
  pair genuinely unseen in that arc's teaching material (not a reworded
  version of the taught example).
- **Delayed retrieval** — a required capability from an earlier arc,
  retrieved unaided inside arc 8 or a later session, with intervening
  material in between.

**Graduation minimum (draft, to be revised after real learner data per
master contract §9):** every required capability needs at least 2
independent-production instances and at least 1 genuine transfer instance;
`mediate_a_complex_disagreement_for_a_third_party` additionally requires at
least 1 delayed-retrieval instance of each of the other required
capabilities inside its own extended task, since it is the level's single
integrative proof. Recognition-only or guided-only evidence never counts
toward graduation for a required capability, and completion (finishing the
episode) is never treated as mastery.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

- **Genuinely novel language** at C2 is rarely a new grammar form; it is a
  new discourse device (a hedge, a register pair, a mediation frame) the
  learner has not evidenced yet. Support increases the same way the shared
  engine already increases it for novel forms at any level — via evidence
  and recent strain, not a hard-coded "C2 = less help" rule (master
  contract §10 explicitly forbids that shortcut).
- **Strong-prerequisite entry behaviour:** a learner who tested strongly on
  the assumed-C1 register/argumentation capabilities should need less
  initial scaffolding on `shift_register_deliberately` and
  `develop_an_extended_qualified_argument`, but novelty (a genuinely new
  discourse device, e.g., evidentiality marking) must still raise support
  regardless of that entry strength.
- **Expected support fade:** hedging/boosting and reformulation connectors
  fade fastest (closed, learnable sets); irony/implicature recognition and
  the capstone mediation task fade slowest, because ambiguity tolerance is
  the actual C2 skill being built and premature fading would just be
  guessing dressed as mastery.
- **Known high-strain tasks:** `recognize_irony_and_understatement`,
  `function_inside_an_unfamiliar_high_ambiguity_exchange`, and the capstone
  are expected high-strain points; the shared engine's "repeated strain
  restores help quickly" rule applies unchanged.
- **Recovery after repeated failure:** identical to shared engine behaviour;
  no C2-specific recovery logic is proposed.
- **Replay behaviour:** once a required capability has independent +
  transfer evidence, replay of its home episode must not silently regrant
  XP/Garden reward for reproducing the same surface answer — same
  no-false-mastery rule as every other level.

## 11. Evaluation requirements

New intents needing evaluator support (full list with example
correct/variant/near-miss/wrong-meaning/nonsense/pragmatically-inappropriate
sets to be authored during `LC-CONT-C2`, since worked examples are
runtime-content work, not blueprint work):

- `reformulate_for_audience` — correct: preserves claim + hedge, fits
  audience; near miss: preserves claim, drops the hedge (states more
  certainty than the source); wrong meaning: inverts or overstates the
  claim; pragmatically inappropriate: technically accurate but wrong
  register for the named audience (jargon for a lay reader).
- `synthesize_viewpoints` — correct: represents both sources fairly; near
  miss: represents one source, ignores the other; wrong meaning: invents a
  position neither source holds.
- `identify_stance` — correct: names the position and its basis; nonsense:
  a response that doesn't engage the text at all.
- `recognize_implication` — correct: states the implied meaning, not just
  the literal words; near miss: restates the literal words as if that were
  the implication; wrong meaning: infers something the text does not
  support.
- `shift_register` — correct: matches the named target register; near
  miss: shifts formality but keeps informal syntax (partial shift);
  pragmatically inappropriate: over-shifts into a register nobody asked for
  (e.g., needlessly bureaucratic for a friendly note).
- `qualify_claim` — correct: hedges/boosts proportionate to actual
  certainty in context; near miss: hedges everything or nothing
  indiscriminately.
- `rebut_counterargument` — correct: concedes what deserves conceding,
  rebuts the rest with a reason; near miss: rebuts without acknowledging any
  merit (register-costly, flagged per §11's pragmatic-appropriateness
  column); wrong meaning: rebuts a straw-man version of the counterargument.
- `repair_at_intention_level` — correct: addresses what the interlocutor
  meant, not only their literal wording; near miss: repairs the wording but
  misses the intention.
- `mediate_disagreement` — correct: represents both sides without
  editorializing and proposes a next step; near miss: silently favors one
  side while claiming neutrality.

Every intent above needs at minimum the clearly-correct, natural-variant,
near-miss, wrong-meaning, nonsense and pragmatically-inappropriate example
categories the template requires (§11); this blueprint commits to their
existence, `LC-CONT-C2` authors the actual example sets against real
runtime fixtures.

## 12. Four Strands audit

Level-wide estimate (not per-episode, per master contract §12/template
§12):

- **Meaning-focused input:** ~35% — dense source texts, multi-viewpoint
  material, implication-carrying dialogue the learner must read closely.
- **Meaning-focused output:** ~30% — reformulation, argument, mediation are
  all real communicative output, not drills.
- **Language-focused learning:** ~20% — explicit attention to hedging,
  register pairs, evidentiality and cohesion devices, since these are
  exactly the kind of form-meaning mapping learners at this level benefit
  from noticing explicitly (master contract §2 principle 4).
- **Fluency development:** ~15% — known C2 devices returning under easier
  conditions (arc 6, arc 8) once first taught, so control does not regress
  under real-time chat pace.

This skews more language-focused than A1 (which is almost entirely
meaning-focused for beginners) because C2's actual gap is rarely "cannot
communicate the idea" — it is "communicates the idea without the precision,
stance-marking or register control a C2 user needs," which is a
language-focused-learning problem by definition.

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

- **Turn-taking and repair:** C2 requires repair at the *intention* level
  (§9/§11), a strictly harder skill than A1–B2's word/form-level repair
  already in the product.
- **Conversation length/topic changes:** arc 6 explicitly targets sustained
  coherence across topic shifts inside one exchange — the first level where
  "the conversation changed subject and the learner kept it coherent" is
  itself graded evidence, not incidental.
- **Discourse coherence:** cohesion devices operate above sentence level by
  design (§6); evaluation must be able to look at a multi-turn span, not
  just the latest message, which is flagged as a possible core requirement
  (§15).
- **Summarization/reformulation:** the explicit subject of arc 2 and central
  to the arc 8 capstone; this is the product's first sustained mediation
  work (Pre-A1–B2 do not require it).
- **Register/pragmatics:** the explicit subject of arc 4 and load-bearing
  throughout; C2 is the level where "grammatically correct but pragmatically
  wrong" becomes a first-class failure mode (master contract §11).

## 14. Explicit exclusions

C2 in this product deliberately does **not** teach or evaluate:

- listening, speaking, live spoken interaction, pronunciation, or
  interpreting (voice/media are out of product scope entirely — CLAUDE.md
  infrastructure boundaries);
- real-time simultaneous or consecutive interpretation between languages;
- specialist professional registers this product has no domain expertise
  to validate (legal drafting, medical documentation, diplomatic protocol,
  literary criticism as an academic genre, peer-reviewed academic writing
  conventions of a specific discipline);
- creative/literary composition as an art form (poetry, fiction) — C2 here
  is about high-precision *communicative* control, not creative writing
  craft;
- native-speaker sociocultural intuition acquired only through lived
  immersion (regional slang currency, generational idiom, culturally
  specific humor that depends on shared lived context rather than
  learnable pragmatic pattern);
- assessment/certification claims beyond this product's own runtime
  evidence — LinguaChat's C2 completion is not equivalent to, and must
  never be marketed as equivalent to, an external CEFR examination
  certificate.

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

1. **Multi-turn/paragraph-scale coherence evaluation.** Problem: several
   C2 intents (`sustain_coherence_across_topic_shifts`,
   `repair_at_intention_level`, the capstone) need evaluation that looks
   across a span of turns, not just the latest learner message. Current
   engine (as understood from A1) evaluates per-turn. Earliest level that
   needs it: C2 (possibly late B2/C1, to be confirmed by those blueprints).
   Proposed shared behaviour: an optional span-evaluation mode the shared
   evaluator can invoke when an intent declares `evaluationSpan: "multiTurn"`.
   Regression risk: must not change per-turn evaluation for every existing
   level. Tests required: span evaluator unit tests plus a regression suite
   proving lower levels are unaffected when the new mode is unused.
2. **Nuance/register/irony judgment under the fake/local test provider.**
   Problem: provider safety (CLAUDE.md) requires zero real-model calls in
   QA/CI, but several C2 intents (`recognize_implication`,
   `shift_register`, `qualify_claim`) are hard to judge with pure string
   matching. Current engine's "hybrid" evaluation already escalates
   inconclusive replies to a remote judge for lower levels, but C2 will lean
   on this far more heavily, and CI must still be deterministic and
   provider-free. Proposed shared behaviour: extend the existing local/fake
   provider fixture set with deterministic canned judgments for C2's new
   intents (mirroring how `introduce_person` already has a hybrid path),
   so CI proves the *routing* is correct without ever depending on a live
   model. Regression risk: none to lower levels if implemented as additive
   fixtures. Tests required: fixture-driven tests proving every new C2
   intent has a working local/fake evaluation path with no real provider
   call, matching `ai/provider_policy.py`'s existing guarantee.
3. **`source_text` / `audience_profile` content objects.** Problem: no
   existing semantic type represents a multi-sentence reading passage or a
   named target audience for reformulation. Proposed shared behaviour: two
   new semantic types (§ below) plus scene-content support for presenting a
   longer passage than current levels' scene payloads assume. Regression
   risk: scene rendering/i18n must not silently break for shorter existing
   scenes. Tests required: scene-content length/rendering tests, i18n
   coverage for the new content shape.
4. **Delayed-retrieval-of-multiple-earlier-capabilities inside one task.**
   Problem: the capstone (arc 8) intentionally requires delayed retrieval of
   several earlier required capabilities inside a single extended task,
   which is a denser evidence-recording shape than any existing episode
   records today. Proposed shared behaviour: allow one episode/task to log
   multiple distinct `delayedRetrieval` evidence records against different
   `canDoId`s in a single completion. Regression risk: learner-model schema
   change: additive only, must not alter existing evidence records. Tests
   required: learner-model schema tests proving old single-capability
   episodes still record identically.

None of the above may be implemented by a level-owned workaround; each is a
CORE requirement to raise, per master contract §14, and must land through a
serialized core task before `LC-CONT-C2` depends on it.

### New semantic types (proposed)

- `source_text` — a multi-sentence passage the learner reads for synthesis
  or reformulation. Incompatible with short single-value slots like
  `activity`/`place`; needed because a source text carries a claim and
  support that must be extracted, not just named.
- `audience_profile` — who a reformulation targets (e.g., "a colleague
  outside the field", "a worried customer", "a child"). Incompatible with
  `person` (a person is an interlocutor identity, not a target-audience
  descriptor for a text).
- `register_level` — formal / neutral / informal / technical. Incompatible
  with free-text; must be a closed enum so the evaluator can check a
  requested shift actually occurred.
- `stance_marker` — a hedge/boost/evidentiality lexical item tracked as its
  own type so the evaluator can check presence/appropriateness
  independently of the proposition it attaches to.

## 16. QA acceptance

Same minimum bar as every level (master contract §16/template §16), applied
to C2 once `LC-CONT-C2` exists:

- structural blueprint validation (an eventual `check-c2-blueprint.mjs`
  mirroring `check-a1-blueprint.mjs`);
- curriculum authoring contract;
- evaluator/refusal tests per new intent (§11), including nonsense and
  pragmatically-inappropriate cases specific to C2;
- personalization-invariant tests (§7);
- prerequisite/reuse tests (§5, §8);
- at least 20 varied learner-shaped journeys per completed arc, per
  `LC-PED-001`/`LC-PED-002`'s existing standard — not 20 duplicate clicks:
  natural variants, near misses, retries, assistance, nonsense/refusal,
  replay/idempotency, delayed retrieval, transfer to a genuinely novel
  source text/audience/register;
- assisted vs independent proof (§9);
- transfer proof using a genuinely unseen source text/topic/register pair;
- delayed retrieval after interference, including the capstone's
  multi-capability delayed-retrieval task;
- replay/idempotency (no duplicate XP/Garden reward);
- no false mastery (recognition/guided evidence never counted as
  independent);
- representative browser usability at 390px and 1440px, light/dark, no raw
  i18n keys, no horizontal overflow;
- two consecutive full clean cycles after the last fix, restarted after any
  later fix, exactly as CLAUDE.md's QA protocol requires.

This blueprint itself only needs the design-level portion of this list —
structural review — before content authoring may begin, per template §15's
blueprint-before-content rule.

## 17. Cross-level acceptance

Before C2 can ever open (which additionally requires all seven listed gates
in the master contract, not this blueprint alone), `LC-AUD-001` must prove
(status: **done** for the id-graph/semantics-collision checks below — see
`docs/curriculum/cross-level-audit.json` findings F3, F6 and this document's
design note in §1; the audit's own disclaimer still applies — internal
structural consistency, not real-learner pedagogical efficacy):

- the assumed-C1 exit capabilities this blueprint cites in §1 really exist
  with compatible semantics in the merged C1 blueprint — reconciling ids,
  not just prose descriptions;
- no unprepared jump: every C2 "new discourse device" genuinely builds on a
  C1-proven capability rather than assuming skills C1 never taught;
- no duplicated "new" capability already taught at C1 (e.g., if C1 already
  teaches basic hedging, C2's hedging work must be framed as extension, not
  reintroduction, and this blueprint's `patterns` table must be corrected
  accordingly during the audit);
- C1's required capabilities continue to receive useful reuse inside C2
  (§8's spacing matrix; the audit should check this against the *real* C1
  arcs, not the assumed list);
- complexity rises coherently from C1 into C2 rather than oscillating;
- graduation evidence declared in §9 is attainable through real runtime
  paths once `LC-CONT-C2` exists, not just declared on paper.

That reconciliation is now done for six of the seven assumed-C1 prerequisites
(§1's design note); this blueprint's prerequisite side is provisional only
for the one remaining low-confidence row (`sustain_coherence_across_topic_shifts`),
pending a human curriculum-design decision. Its internal structure (arcs,
capabilities, evidence, exclusions) is self-consistent today.

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Claims automation cannot prove, to test with real learners once C2 content
and a pilot are possible:

- Does a C2-level learner actually experience the hedging/boosting and
  register-pair distinctions as meaningfully different, or do they collapse
  them into "more formal words" without real pragmatic control?
- Does the mediation capstone feel like a realistic high-stakes task, or
  does chat-mediated text make "disagreement between two people" feel
  artificial compared to how C2 learners actually need this skill (e.g., at
  work, in writing, not necessarily live)?
- Is irony/understatement recognition, taught through text alone, actually
  transferable to spoken irony once/if this product ever implements
  listening — or is text-only irony a genuinely different, narrower skill
  that should be labeled as such rather than assumed to generalize?
- Does explicit teaching of stance markers (`presumably`, `allegedly`)
  produce natural-sounding use, or does it produce learners who
  over-hedge/over-mark stance in a way that itself reads as unnatural (the
  opposite failure mode from the one the level is designed to fix)?
- Do learners perceive the capstone's delayed-retrieval-of-many-capabilities
  design as a fair integrative test, or as an unfair pile-up of difficulty
  at the very end of the level?
- Is 390px chat interaction actually a comfortable surface for dense
  `source_text` reading tasks at C2, or does the product need a distinct
  reading-mode presentation before real learners can use this level
  comfortably?
