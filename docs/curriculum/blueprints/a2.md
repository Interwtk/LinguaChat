## 2. Exit promise

A1 left a learner able to handle short, predictable exchanges in the present, in one situation at a time. By the end of A2, that same learner can, connecting clauses rather than only producing isolated frames:

- **say what already happened** — a short personal account, and a two-or-three-action sequence held together with a connector;
- **ask somebody what they did**, and understand an answer that uses an irregular verb they were not directly taught;
- **talk about near-future plans**, independent of any specific arrangement, and ask about somebody else's;
- **describe a person or a place with more than one attribute**, including the level's first productive third-person `-s`;
- **compare two things or two options**, and justify a preference with `because`;
- **follow a direction that takes more than one step**, and give a simple one back;
- **book a room or a table**: ask if something is available on a date, confirm the date, the number of people, and — when needed — spell a name, without ever handling real personal data;
- **report a problem** in a service situation and ask for it to be fixed;
- **invite somebody to do something**, accept or decline honestly with a reason, and keep the conversation going a little longer when the topic shifts once.

Explicit ceilings, deferred to B1: the present perfect and any tense that connects the past to now; past continuous or mixed-tense narration; conditionals and hypothetical language; reported speech; the passive voice; superlatives; directions that need a map or more than a handful of steps; multi-leg travel booking; deliberate register/negotiation; reason clauses joined by anything other than `because`. Real personal contact details are never collected, at any level — that is not a ceiling, it is a permanent product decision inherited unchanged from A1.

## 6. Language infrastructure

### Patterns / grammar

Twenty-five new pattern groups across twenty-three episodes (`a2.json#patterns`), one to three per episode depending on density — denser than A1's one-per-episode average because A2's mandate ("connect clauses") makes several patterns interdependent within a single arc (e.g. `what_happened` needs the regular past, the irregular closed set, a time expression and a connector before its capability is teachable at all). Each pattern record states what it enables, its prerequisite (an A1 pattern where the infrastructure is genuinely new, an A2 pattern where it is not), how far it is expected to travel, its first arc and where it returns.

Two grammar decisions worth stating directly, because a future author will ask the same questions A1's blueprint answered for itself:

- **Third-person `-s` becomes productive here, not before.** A1 taught it receptively for the whole level ("heard throughout A1, produced in A2" — A1's own deferral). `describe_a_person_or_place` is where it is produced for the first time, inside a frame (`he/she + verb + -s`) that already has a slot for it once description needs more than `is`.
- **Comparatives, not superlatives.** A2 needs two-way comparison for every situation in its capability table (two places, two plans, two solutions). Nothing in A2 needs a three-or-more-way comparison; superlatives are deferred to B1 with the rest of that structure family (§14).

### Vocabulary budget

Seventy-two new productive items and fifty receptive across the level (`a2.json#counts`), against A1's fifty granted and Pre-A1's fifty-nine before that — a larger budget than A1's, proportionate to a level that opens five new situational domains (past narration, plans, description/comparison, booking, problems) rather than extending existing ones. As in A1: productive means the learner is expected to say it; receptive means they will meet it and are not credited for it. The ten-verb closed irregular-past set is the level's one deliberately bounded vocabulary list, named explicitly in `simple_past_irregular_pattern` rather than left open-ended, for the same reason A1 bounded its relation words to three: an unbounded list invites a catalogue, not a capability.

## 7. Personalization plan

Per the master's personalization invariant (§7 of the master doc): personalization may change what an A2 conversation is **about**, never what the learner has to **do**, what counts as correct evidence, or how difficult a capability is.

| arc | mode | compatible slot types | neutral fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| `what_happened` | light | `activity` (what the learner did) | a shared neutral daily activity ("I worked", "I studied") — already A1's neutral set |
| `making_plans` | light | `activity`, `future_time` | "I'm going to relax this weekend" |
| `people_and_places` | themed | `place`, `person`, `quality` | a generic neutral café/neighbourhood pair, described and compared with the same attribute vocabulary regardless of theme |
| `getting_around` | none | — | the situation is the lesson, exactly as A1's `finding_your_way` stayed unpersonalized |
| `booking_a_stay` | light | `activity` (what is being booked: a table vs. a room), `date_ordinal`, `month` | a generic restaurant-table booking |
| `everyday_problems` | light | `problem` (drawn from a small neutral set: cold room, wrong order, lost item) | "the room is cold" |
| `lets_do_something` | themed | `activity` (what is proposed) | "go to the cinema" |

No arc personalizes `canDoId`, evaluator intent, evidence threshold, prerequisite, reward, or difficulty — every themed/light slot is a content-only substitution, exactly as `docs/curriculum/a1-authoring-contract.md`'s "Interest personalization" section already specifies for the shared engine, and A2 introduces no new personalization mechanism of its own. QA proof that two themes preserve identical capability/evidence/difficulty is the same `invariantDrift(storyA, storyB)` check A1 already uses; no A2-specific tool is required. Recent-topic cooldown expectations inherit the shared engine's existing cooldown, unchanged.

## 8. Reuse / spacing matrix

Full matrix, thirty-one rows (fifteen reused A1/Pre-A1 capabilities plus sixteen new A2 ones) against seven arcs, is in `a2.json#reuseMatrix`. Two findings from building it, stated the way A1's own equivalent section stated its findings:

- **Every required A2 capability reaches its home arc's `C` (consolidated) and returns at least once more**, except the closing arc's own three, which consolidate only at the level's very end — the same shape A1's `arrange_to_meet` had, and for the same reason: nothing is left to reuse it within a level that ends immediately after.
- **Two should-have capabilities go quiet after their home arc**, exactly as A1's `ask_about_getting_somewhere` did: `give_simple_directions` and `spell_a_name_for_a_booking`. Both are marked in `reuseMatrix.quietRows` with the reasoning, and both are the first trim candidates if the level needs it — again mirroring A1's own trimming precedent (§17 there).

## 9. Evidence and mastery

A2 keeps A1's evidence categories unchanged, per the master contract's ban on level-invented mastery definitions (master doc §9): recognition, guided production, open assisted production, open independent production, transfer, delayed retrieval.

What is new relative to A1's blueprint: **every required A2 capability's evidence record carries an explicit `transfer` and `delayedRetrieval` field**, not only `independent` (compare `a2.json#canDos` to `a1-blueprint.json#canDos`, which records `independent` but not the other two explicitly). This is not a stronger standard invented by this blueprint — the master contract already requires transfer and delayed retrieval targets per capability (§5, §9, §12) and A1's blueprint satisfies that requirement in prose (`transferContexts`, delayed-retrieval language in `a1-map.md` §14) rather than as a structured field. A2 makes the same requirement machine-checkable from the start, which is a stricter authoring discipline, not a stricter mastery bar. Should-have capabilities intentionally carry a lighter target (`independent: 1`, `transfer: 0`) exactly as A1's should-haves do.

No level exit threshold is set here. As A1's own map document states for itself: thresholds "need evidence from real journeys," and A2 inherits that same deferral — this blueprint states evidence types and per-capability targets, not a numeric graduation cutoff.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

A2 uses the shared adaptive support engine unchanged; nothing here overrides it (master doc §10).

Expected shape by arc phase, as author guidance only:

- **Early (`what_happened`, `making_plans`):** genuinely novel language (a new tense, then a symmetric new tense) keeps the model answer available through most of the arc; two-to-three open productions per episode; a deliberate contrastive design (episode 43 pairs a past recap with each new future item) rather than relying on the engine alone to prevent tense confusion.
- **Mid (`people_and_places`, `getting_around`, `booking_a_stay`):** the suggestion withheld on each arc's integration episode; repair required by design at least once per arc, since real multi-step answers and real booking confirmations will always outrun a learner's exact taught vocabulary somewhere.
- **Late (`everyday_problems`, `lets_do_something`):** unaided by default, support returning after a wrong answer, up to six open productions in the closing story, and — the one genuinely new expectation this level adds — the learner is expected to **decline and re-propose**, not only accept or ask, which is a support shape A1 never needed.

Known high-strain tasks, named rather than left implicit: episode 42 (holding a two-clause past sequence together for the first time), episode 47 (producing `because` immediately after two other new structures in the same arc), and episode 61 (the closing story's decline-and-repropose branch, which needs `accept_or_decline_with_a_reason` and `keep_a_longer_conversation_going` at once).

## 11. Evaluation requirements

Full per-intent examples belong to implementation, not this blueprint; `a2.json#intentStrategy` and `#evaluationStrategy` name the seventeen new intents and their deterministic/hybrid split. Two evaluator-design problems are flagged here because they are genuinely new relative to A1, exactly the way A1 flagged its own `can`-ambiguity problem as "the level's one real architectural debt":

1. **Two-clause sentences.** `narrate_past_sequence`, `describe_person_or_place`, `state_opinion_with_reason` and `make_booking` all need a clause-plus-connector-plus-clause shape judged locally, which A1's evaluator never had to do. Filed as a CORE requirement in `a2.json#coreEngineRequirements` with explicit nonsense-in-either-clause refusal tests demanded before any of these four intents ship.
2. **Higher hybrid share.** Roughly half of A2's new intents are hybrid versus A1's three-of-fourteen, because free word order genuinely increases once clauses connect and once a description can list attributes in any order. This is stated as a fact about the design, not hidden by relabeling hybrid intents as deterministic to make the count look more like A1's.

For each new intent, implementation must define (per the template): clearly correct, natural variant, near miss, wrong meaning, nonsense, correct-meaning-but-insufficient-form where form matters (e.g. a past event stated in the present tense), and — new at this level — correct-single-clause-but-missing-required-second-clause where a capability specifically requires the connected form (e.g. an opinion with no reason, when the turn asked for one).

## 12. Four Strands audit

Level-wide estimate, not per-episode (master doc §13 explicitly forbids forcing per-episode balance):

- **Meaning-focused input:** strong in `getting_around` (multi-step directions deliberately exceed first-pass production) and `what_happened`'s hosted story (irregular verbs received before produced).
- **Meaning-focused output:** the level's largest share — every arc's primary episodes end in an open production, and the two convergence arcs (`booking_a_stay`, `lets_do_something`) are almost entirely output-driven.
- **Language-focused learning:** concentrated in `people_and_places` (three new structures in four episodes) and the first half of `what_happened` (tense contrast); this is A2's densest language-focused stretch, comparable to A1's `daily_rhythm`.
- **Fluency development:** the four reinforcement/integration episodes (40, 48, 54, 57) and the closing story (61) are explicitly low-novelty, high-reuse — known language returning under easier conditions, per the master's fluency principle (§2.5).

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

A2 is where discourse-level demands genuinely begin, ahead of B1/B2's fuller treatment (master doc §77-79 progression table): the level's should-have `keep_a_longer_conversation_going` is the first LinguaChat capability whose entire evidence target is about **sustaining**, not only producing, an exchange — one topic change, held together with a connector, without the exchange falling apart. Turn-taking and repair inherit A1's mechanisms unchanged (repair is planted deliberately in `getting_around`, `booking_a_stay` and `everyday_problems`, exactly as A1 planted it in `finding_your_way`). Conversation length grows from A1's 10–12-turn ceiling to 14–18 turns in this level's closing arc. No mediation-like task (summarizing or simplifying for another person) is introduced in A2; that remains a later-level capability, since A2's own new structures (past, future, comparison, reason clauses) are demanding enough on their own. No register/pragmatics work is introduced — every A2 exchange stays informal and cooperative, per §14's exclusion.

## 14. Explicit exclusions

What A2 deliberately does not teach, so a later worker does not silently expand scope and break progression (full list with reasoning in `a2.json#deferredToB1`):

- present perfect and any past-to-present connection (`have you ever`, `I've just`) — the explicit first B1 capability;
- past continuous or mixed-tense narration;
- conditionals, hypothetical language, reported speech, the passive voice;
- superlatives (three-or-more-way comparison);
- multi-leg travel booking, flights, itineraries — booking stays to a room or a table;
- map reading or routes longer than a short verbal sequence;
- register management or negotiation — every exchange stays informal and cooperative;
- reason connectors beyond `because` (`although`, `even though`);
- **real personal contact details, at any level, permanently** — not a ceiling, a product decision A2 inherits unchanged from A1 and states again explicitly because `spell_a_name_for_a_booking` is exactly the kind of capability that could be misread as needing them (see `a2.json#canDos[].privacyNote` and the third `coreEngineRequirements` entry).

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

Three, filed in full in `a2.json#coreEngineRequirements` and summarized here per the template's required fields:

1. **The `day` (weekday) semantic type.** A1 already proposed it for its own designed-only arc 7; A2's `use_dates_and_months`/`book_a_room_or_table` are the first capabilities that would actually need it built. Proposed shared behaviour: register it once, wherever it is implemented first, with A1's already-published incompatibilities (rejects `time_point`, `relation`); a build-order guard should make a second, conflicting registration fail loudly rather than silently fork the type system.
2. **Two-clause evaluator judgment.** Four new hybrid intents need a clause-connector-clause shape judged locally. Proposed shared behaviour: extend the local evaluator with a declared two-clause canonical shape per intent, with mandatory nonsense-in-either-clause refusal tests before any of the four intents ship.
3. **A privacy boundary in fact capture.** `spell_a_name_for_a_booking` must never become a `FACT_TYPES` entry the way A1's `work_or_study` or `usual_time` are. Proposed shared behaviour: an explicit allowlist/denylist boundary in the shared fact-capture pipeline, treated as a CORE privacy policy decision, with a negative test asserting no fact type is ever created for spelling turns.

None of these are implemented by this blueprint. Each is a requirement to raise with the core lane before level content depends on it, per the master's authoring-architecture rule (§14): "When a level worker discovers a shared-core need, it must raise the requirement instead of quietly implementing a private workaround."

## 17. Cross-level acceptance

Before A2 can open, the integrated curriculum must prove (master doc §17, applied here):

- **A1's exit really supplies A2's prerequisites.** Every A1-prefixed id in §5's graph is drawn from A1's **required** thirteen, never from A1's should-have or optional/not-taken sets — a learner who graduated A1 on required evidence alone has everything this blueprint's prerequisite graph asks for. The one apparent exception, `ask_about_getting_somewhere` (A1 should-have), is deliberately NOT a prerequisite anywhere in A2; `follow_directions_with_more_than_one_step` depends on the required `ask_where_something_is` instead, precisely so A2 does not silently require an A1 should-have capability a learner may not have produced.
- **No unprepared language jump.** The past tense (A2's first capability) reuses A1's `i_do_life_pattern` frame and changes only the verb form; the future reuses A1's `time_at_pattern`/`arrange_to_meet` infrastructure; booking reuses A1's `buy_something` transaction shape outright. Nothing in A2's arc 1–2 introduces a structure with no A1 scaffold underneath it.
- **No duplicated "new" capability already taught earlier.** Checked against A1's full capability list (required, should and optional): no A2 id collides with an A1 id, and every A1 deferral this blueprint resolves (§`a1Inheritance.resolvesA1Deferral`) is resolved exactly once, not redundantly split across two A2 capabilities.
- **Old required capabilities continue to receive useful reuse.** `arrange_to_meet`, A1's own closing capability, reappears in three A2 arcs (`making_plans`, `booking_a_stay`, `lets_do_something`) — it is A2's single most-reused A1 capability, which is the intended outcome of A1 having spent its whole closing arc consolidating it.
- **Complexity rises coherently.** Turn ceiling: 10–12 (A1) → 14–18 (A2 closing arc). Clause structure: single-clause frames (A1) → connected two-clause frames (A2). Transaction complexity: an immediate purchase (A1) → a future-dated booking with a possible complication (A2). Each step reuses the previous level's shape rather than inventing an unrelated one.
- **Graduation evidence is attainable through real runtime paths.** Every required capability's evidence target names either an integration episode (§`a2.json#episodes`, `role: primary` closing an arc) or the level's own closing story (episode 61) as the path to independent/transfer evidence — no required capability's evidence target depends on a should-have or optional episode ever being played.

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Recorded per the master's standard (§19 acknowledges automation cannot prove literal absence of bugs, and the template asks explicitly what only real learners can answer):

- Does the tense contrast between `what_happened` and `making_plans` (taught two episodes apart) actually confuse learners in practice, or does the deliberate contrastive design in episode 43 suffice?
