## 2. Exit promise

By the end of B2 a learner can, without a script, and mostly at natural
conversational speed:

- **develop and defend an opinion** on an everyday-but-real issue, with reasons,
  and hold that opinion under a genuine counter-argument without switching to
  simple agreement or shutting the conversation down;
- **weigh several options against each other** and recommend one, stating the
  trade-offs rather than just picking a favorite;
- **handle something going wrong** — a cancelled booking, a faulty order, a
  missed appointment, a changed plan — by explaining the problem, justifying
  what they want done about it, and negotiating toward a resolution, including
  when the other side pushes back;
- **reason about the unreal** — what would happen if something were different,
  what must have happened given the evidence, what they would have done
  differently — using conditionals and modal speculation as thinking tools, not
  as a grammar drill;
- **mediate language for somebody else** — summarize a conversation or a short
  text for a person who was not there, reformulate something confusing into
  something clear, report what a third person said or thought;
- **read and use register** — notice when a conversation calls for more formal
  or more careful language, soften a strong claim or strengthen a weak one on
  purpose, and catch an implication that was not stated outright;
- **sustain a long, multi-topic conversation** that changes subject more than
  once, follow the change instead of forcing the old topic to continue, and
  reach an agreement even when the other party keeps objecting.

**Explicit ceilings — deferred to C1:**

- assessing pragmatic appropriateness and discourse control as the *primary*
  grading criterion (B2 notices and adjusts register; C1 is evaluated on
  register control as a first-class outcome, per the master contract §4);
- abstract, high-density text (academic argument, literary register, dense
  professional documents) as production input rather than the shorter,
  concrete-but-real situations B2 uses;
- reformulating with stylistic nuance rather than clarity as the goal;
- sustained monologue at length (a structured presentation) — B2 stays
  interaction-first;
- fully idiomatic, colloquially dense production — B2 recognizes and lightly
  uses idiom (`use_idiomatic_expressions_naturally`, optional), C1/C2 own
  fluent, natural idiomatic range.

**Escalation notes — where a B2 capability is close enough to an existing B1
capability that this document had to prove genuine escalation rather than
duplication (master contract §17):**

- `weigh_advantages_and_disadvantages` vs. `b1.compare_options_with_reasons`
  (required) and `b1.recommend_or_warn` (should) — B1 justifies a choice; B2
  requires the trade-off named explicitly (`advantage_disadvantage_frame`) as
  its own discourse move, and does not depend on `recommend_or_warn` since
  should-scope B1 capabilities are not guaranteed for every learner.
- `negotiate_a_resolution` / `negotiate_an_agreement_under_pushback` vs.
  `b1.negotiate_a_solution` (required) — B1 negotiates a single-round,
  reported problem; B2 escalates to justification-grounded proposals adjusted
  across genuine, repeated pushback.
- `express_frustration_diplomatically` vs. `b1.express_frustration_politely`
  (should) — B1's is a flat-politeness tone layer; B2 adds hedged,
  register-aware diplomacy sustained inside a live negotiation.
- `summarize_for_someone_else` vs. `b1.summarize_what_was_said` (should) — B1
  recaps for the same interlocutor inside the same conversation; B2 requires
  the genuine CEFR mediation move, summarizing for a third party who was not
  present.
- `hypothesize_about_unreal_situations` vs. `b1.talk_about_real_conditions`
  (should) and `b1.imagine_a_hypothetical` (optional, described in `b1.md` as
  "a light preview of B2's hypothetical reasoning, kept to one fixed frame") —
  both point directly at this capability, but neither is required in B1, so
  this required B2 capability's hard prerequisite stays the required
  `b1.talk_about_plans_and_intentions` rather than either should/optional cap.

## 4. Skill families and arc map

Six arcs, by communicative function, never by grammar topic. B2's arcs are
fewer and denser than A1's seven, which is a deliberate consequence of the
master contract's B2 progression note (§4): "B2 must substantially increase
discourse-level demands: coherence across turns matters, not only sentence
correctness." A B2 arc is not "finished" when a frame is produced once; it is
finished when the learner sustains it across a longer exchange.

### Arc 1 — `making_the_case`

- **Real-world situation:** contributing to a real decision — a workplace idea,
  a shared household decision, a community proposal — where the learner must
  argue for something and compare it with alternatives.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `develop_and_defend_opinion`,
  `weigh_advantages_and_disadvantages`, `concede_a_point_and_counter`.
- **Capabilities reused:** `b1.give_an_opinion`,
  `b1.compare_options_with_reasons`.
- **Prerequisites:** B1 exit.
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 10–12.
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** meaning-focused output.
- **Personalization mode:** light — the issue argued about may come from the
  learner's stated interests; the argument structure never changes.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** a shared household chore rota; which of two
  neutral local venues to choose for a group event.
- **Transfer target:** defend an opinion on an issue never rehearsed in this arc.
- **Delayed retrieval target:** defend an opinion, unaided, in arc 6's capstone
  conversation, at least one arc later.

### Arc 2 — `when_plans_go_wrong`

- **Real-world situation:** something has gone wrong with a booking, an order,
  or an appointment, and the learner must resolve it with the person
  responsible.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `justify_a_request_for_change`,
  `negotiate_a_resolution`, `express_frustration_diplomatically`.
- **Capabilities reused:** `b1.negotiate_a_solution`,
  `b1.give_an_opinion`, `develop_and_defend_opinion` (arc 1's
  reasoning frame, applied to a complaint).
- **Prerequisites:** `making_the_case`.
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 10–12.
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** meaning-focused output, with genuine
  interaction (negotiation only exists as a back-and-forth).
- **Personalization mode:** light — the service/booking/order context can match
  a stated interest (a concert ticket, a hobby-related delivery); the problem
  shape and required resolution do not change.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** a delayed home repair appointment; a wrong item
  in an online order.
- **Transfer target:** negotiate a resolution for a problem type not used during
  teaching (e.g., taught on a delivery, transferred to a cancelled reservation).
- **Delayed retrieval target:** justify a request for change inside arc 6's
  capstone, without the arc's own scaffolding.

### Arc 3 — `what_if`

- **Real-world situation:** reasoning about alternatives — advising a friend on
  a hypothetical decision, speculating about why something happened, looking
  back on a choice that turned out badly.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `hypothesize_about_unreal_situations`,
  `speculate_about_cause_and_effect`, `express_regret_about_a_past_decision`.
- **Capabilities reused:** `b1.talk_about_plans_and_intentions`,
  `b1.narrate_connected_event`.
- **Prerequisites:** B1 exit (independent of arcs 1–2; see §5).
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 9–11.
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** language-focused learning (the
  conditional/modal system is genuinely new machinery), balanced against
  meaning-focused output once the forms are usable.
- **Personalization mode:** light — the hypothetical scenario's topic may be
  interest-linked; the reasoning task never changes.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** "what would you do if your flight were
  cancelled the night before an important event"; "what probably happened,
  given what we know" about an everyday mystery (a missing package, a locked
  door).
- **Transfer target:** speculate about cause and effect for a novel scenario,
  combining a hypothesis with a deduction in the same turn.
- **Delayed retrieval target:** a hypothesis produced unaided inside arc 6.

### Arc 4 — `talking_around_a_subject`

- **Real-world situation:** acting as the bridge between two people or between
  a text and a person — summarizing what was said in a meeting for someone who
  missed it, explaining a confusing message, reporting a friend's opinion
  accurately.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `summarize_for_someone_else`,
  `reformulate_to_clarify`, `report_someone_elses_opinion`.
- **Capabilities reused:** `b1.narrate_connected_event`,
  `b1.sustain_topic_change`, `develop_and_defend_opinion`
  (recognizing an opinion well enough to report it).
- **Prerequisites:** B1 exit (independent of arcs 1–3; see §5).
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 9–11.
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** meaning-focused input (a source text
  or conversation to process) paired with meaning-focused output (the mediated
  version).
- **Personalization mode:** related — the source material's topic may be
  adjacent to a stated interest; the mediation task and its evidence standard
  never change.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** summarizing a short neutral news item; relaying
  a scheduling conversation to a third party.
- **Transfer target:** reformulate a passage the learner has not seen mediated
  during teaching.
- **Delayed retrieval target:** summarize a stretch of arc 6's own capstone
  conversation for a (simulated) latecomer, inside arc 6 itself.

### Arc 5 — `reading_between_the_lines`

- **Real-world situation:** noticing and controlling register — talking to a
  landlord versus a friend about the same problem, softening a criticism,
  strengthening a recommendation, catching a hint that was not said outright.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `adjust_register_to_context`,
  `soften_or_strengthen_a_statement`, `infer_implied_meaning`.
- **Capabilities reused:** `b1.sustain_topic_change`,
  `develop_and_defend_opinion` (softened/strengthened opinions).
- **Prerequisites:** `making_the_case` (register work needs an opinion to
  modulate).
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 9–11.
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** language-focused learning (register
  is a form-and-pragmatics system that needs deliberate attention) with
  meaning-focused input for the implication-recognition tasks.
- **Personalization mode:** none — register is about the relationship between
  speakers and the situation's formality, not about topic; forcing a personal
  interest into it would not change the skill and risks trivializing it.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** a message to a landlord versus a message to a
  flatmate about the same broken appliance.
- **Transfer target:** correctly register-shift a message about a situation not
  rehearsed during teaching.
- **Delayed retrieval target:** at least one register adjustment recognized or
  produced unaided inside arc 6.

### Arc 6 — `the_long_conversation` (capstone)

- **Real-world situation:** an extended, realistic conversation that changes
  subject more than once and ends in a negotiated agreement — the level's
  integrated evidence, the way A1's closing arrangement arc is A1's.
- **Capabilities introduced:** `sustain_a_multi_topic_conversation`,
  `handle_a_topic_shift_gracefully`, `negotiate_an_agreement_under_pushback`.
- **Capabilities reused/consolidated:** every arc-1–5 required capability,
  each exercised inside the same conversation rather than reviewed separately.
  `use_idiomatic_expressions_naturally` (optional) may appear here as
  recognition without being required for evidence.
- **Prerequisites:** all five preceding arcs.
- **Expected meaningful learner turns:** 16–20 (the level's longest single
  conversation, matching a genuinely B2 exchange rather than a short scene).
- **Dominant Four Strands contribution:** meaning-focused output and fluency
  development — known B2 language returning at natural pace under real
  conversational pressure, not new material.
- **Personalization mode:** themed — the overall scenario (e.g., planning a
  shared trip, resolving a shared-living disagreement, negotiating a group
  purchase) may vary by declared interest, provided every personalized variant
  still requires all three new capstone capabilities and all five reused
  families; see §7.
- **Neutral fallback contexts:** a full neutral variant (a shared-flat dispute
  that needs a plan, a complaint, a compromise, and a topic change to logistics)
  must exist and must be at least as capable of producing every required piece
  of evidence as any themed variant.
- **Transfer target:** the entire arc, by construction — nothing in it is a
  scripted repeat of an earlier arc's exact scenario.
- **Delayed retrieval target:** not applicable to itself; it *is* the delayed
  retrieval instrument for arcs 1–5.

Machine-checkable ids and `prerequisiteArcs` (the recommended walk) live in
`b2.json#/canDos` and `b2.json#/arcs`. Rules enforced by inspection today, and
by a future `check:b2-blueprint` once B2 has runtime content:

- no cycles — verified by hand above; every arrow moves strictly forward
  through arcs 1→2, 3 (independent), 4 (independent), 5, 6;
- no required capability without an evidence path — every required id in §3
  has an `evidence` block in `b2.json`;
- no hidden prosa-only prerequisite — every prerequisite named in §4's arc
  descriptions appears in the `prerequisites` array of the corresponding
  capability in `b2.json`;
- cross-level prerequisites (`b1.*`) are real B1 capability ids, reconciled
  against `b1.md`/`b1.json` after that lane merged (see the note at the top of
  this file); the still-open item is the full structural `LC-AUD-001` audit,
  not the identity of the ids themselves.

Three branches are independent of each other, same shape as A1's graph:
**argument** (arc 1), **hypothesis** (arc 3) and **mediation** (arc 4) share
only B1 prerequisites. **Negotiation** (arc 2) depends on argument. **Register**
(arc 5) depends on argument (it needs an opinion to modulate). The capstone
(arc 6) is the only true convergence, which is why it is last — identical
reasoning to A1's closing arrangement arc (`a1-map.md` §7).

## 6. Language infrastructure

### Patterns / grammar

Twenty new pattern groups, one to three per capability, none introduced because
"B2 textbooks cover it" — each is tied to the capability that needs it.

| pattern | communicative function | prerequisite | first appearance | later reuse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `opinion_stance_pattern` ("In my view / I'd argue that / As I see it") | stating an opinion as a position, not a fact | `b1.give_an_opinion` | `making_the_case` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `contrast_connector_pattern` ("whereas / on the other hand / by contrast") | holding two options in the same sentence | `opinion_stance_pattern` | `making_the_case` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `advantage_disadvantage_frame` ("the main advantage/drawback of X is that…") | naming a trade-off explicitly | `contrast_connector_pattern` | `making_the_case` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `concession_counter_pattern` ("that's true, but / I take your point, however") | conceding without surrendering the position | `opinion_stance_pattern` | `making_the_case` | `when_plans_go_wrong`, `the_long_conversation` |
| `justification_pattern` ("given that / since / the reason I'm asking is that") | grounding a request in a stated cause | `b1.give_an_opinion` | `when_plans_go_wrong` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `negotiation_proposal_pattern` ("would you be willing to / what if we / I'd suggest that") | proposing a solution, not just stating a problem | `justification_pattern` | `when_plans_go_wrong` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `diplomatic_hedge_pattern` ("I understand, but / I don't want to make a fuss, but") | expressing dissatisfaction without hostility | `negotiation_proposal_pattern` | `when_plans_go_wrong` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `second_conditional_pattern` ("If I were…, I would…") | reasoning about an unreal present/future | `b1.talk_about_plans_and_intentions` | `what_if` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `third_conditional_pattern` ("If I had…, I would have…") | reasoning about an unreal past | `second_conditional_pattern` | `what_if` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `mixed_conditional_pattern` ("If I had…, I would… [now]") | connecting a past cause to a present unreal result | `third_conditional_pattern` | `what_if` | — |
| `modal_deduction_present_pattern` ("must be / might be / can't be") | expressing certainty about a present state | `second_conditional_pattern` | `what_if` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `modal_deduction_past_pattern` ("must have / might have / could have") | expressing certainty about a past event | `modal_deduction_present_pattern` | `what_if` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `wish_past_perfect_pattern` ("I wish I had… / if only I'd…") | expressing regret | `third_conditional_pattern` | `what_if` | — |
| `reported_speech_pattern` (tense back-shift + reporting verbs: claim, mention, point out, suggest) | relaying what someone else said | `b1.narrate_connected_event` | `talking_around_a_subject` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `summary_connector_pattern` ("basically / the main point was / to sum up") | condensing a longer exchange | `reported_speech_pattern` | `talking_around_a_subject` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `reformulation_marker_pattern` ("in other words / what I mean is / put simply") | rewording without changing meaning | `summary_connector_pattern` | `talking_around_a_subject` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `register_marker_pattern` (formal/informal lexical and grammatical choice: "could you possibly" vs "can you") | matching language to the relationship/situation | `adjust_register_to_context`'s own first exposure | `reading_between_the_lines` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `hedging_pattern` ("sort of / tend to / I'd say / more or less") | softening a claim on purpose | `register_marker_pattern` | `reading_between_the_lines` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `intensifying_pattern` ("absolutely / without a doubt / I'm convinced that") | strengthening a claim on purpose | `register_marker_pattern` | `reading_between_the_lines` | `the_long_conversation` |
| `topic_shift_marker_pattern` ("anyway / speaking of which / that reminds me / before I forget") | moving a conversation from one subject to another without losing coherence | `register_marker_pattern` | `the_long_conversation` | — |

`pushback_response_pattern` ("I hear what you're saying, but / let's find a
middle ground") is deliberately **not** a new pattern group: it recombines
`concession_counter_pattern`, `negotiation_proposal_pattern` and
`diplomatic_hedge_pattern`, which is the point of the capstone arc — it proves
recombination, not new material.

### Vocabulary budget

- **Productive:** 88 new items, almost entirely functional multiword units
  (connectors, hedges, reporting verbs, negotiation frames) rather than topic
  nouns — continuing A1's rule that a functional phrase is one item, not one
  per word (`a1-map.md` §9). Budgeted per arc, summed in `b2.json#/counts`, not
  per capability — the same convention A1 uses (`a1-blueprint.json` `arcs[].vocabularyBudget`
  vs. its capability-level `canDos[]`, which carries no separate vocabulary field).
- **Receptive:** 67 new items — implication/idiom recognition vocabulary,
  additional reporting verbs (`concede`, `acknowledge`, `imply`), and the
  broader incidental lexicon that comes from processing longer mediation
  source texts.
- **Incidental scene language:** each arc's scenario contributes situational
  vocabulary (booking/service terms in arc 2, everyday-mystery vocabulary in
  arc 3) that is not separately budgeted, matching A1's convention that scene
  words are not Garden items.
- **Multiword units tracked whole:** every pattern in §6 above is a single
  Garden-trackable unit, not its component words — `contrast_connector_pattern`
  is one item even though it teaches three interchangeable connectors.

This is higher than A1's 50 productive / 46 receptive across seven arcs, and
should be: B2 packs three capabilities into most arcs instead of A1's roughly
two, over six arcs instead of seven, and B2's items are multiword functional
units (connectors, hedges, reporting frames) rather than A1's mostly
single-word or short-frame items. What the budget does **not** do is grow
because "B2 textbooks cover more grammar" — every item traces to a capability
in §3, which is the master contract's instruction that grammar/vocabulary
cannot be added "because a traditional syllabus says it belongs in the level"
(§2 principle 4, template §6).

## 7. Personalization plan

- **Arcs allowing explicit interests:** `making_the_case` (the issue argued
  about), `when_plans_go_wrong` (the booking/service/order context),
  `what_if` (the hypothetical scenario's topic), `the_long_conversation`
  (the overall themed scenario).
- **Arcs accepting only related contexts:** `talking_around_a_subject` (the
  source text/conversation topic may be adjacent to an interest, since a real
  mediation task needs authentic-feeling source material more than personal
  relevance).
- **Arcs requiring neutral, real-life situations only:** `reading_between_the_lines`
  — register is a property of the relationship and formality of a situation,
  not of a topic; personalizing it would not change the skill and risks making
  a serious pragmatic skill feel like a game.
- **Compatible semantic slot types:** `interest`, `activity`, `place` (existing);
  `stance`, `problem_type` (proposed, §6 continuation below) for slots that
  need an opinionated position or a categorized service failure rather than a
  concrete noun.
- **Neutral fallback for every personalized slot:** every arc above declares a
  neutral fallback context in §4; no themed variant may exist without one.
- **Recent-topic cooldown expectations:** inherits the shared interest-rotation
  cooldown (`docs/personalization/interests.md`); B2 adds no new cooldown rule.
- **How QA will prove two themes preserve identical capability/evidence/
  difficulty:** `invariantDrift(storyA, storyB)`, the same function A1 arc 1
  proved (`a1-authoring-contract.md` "Interest personalization"), applied to
  the capstone's themed variants. A themed capstone that changes which
  capability is exercised, how many unaided turns are required, or what counts
  as a resolved negotiation is refused, not shipped — exactly A1's rule.

No B2 blueprint capability personalizes `canDoId`, evaluator intent, evidence
threshold, prerequisite, reward, or difficulty. A learner arguing about a
concert they want to organize must clear the same bar as a learner arguing
about a shared chore rota.

## 9. Evidence and mastery

Inherits the shared evidence model in the master contract §9 without
weakening it:

- **recognition** — understands an opinion/negotiation/hypothesis/register
  shift when it is used by someone else;
- **guided production** — produces the target function with a supplied
  connector/frame visible;
- **open assisted production** — produces the function freely but with a hint,
  model, or the frame's connector list still available;
- **open independent production** — produces the function freely, with no
  supplied structure, at natural pace;
- **transfer** — succeeds on a genuinely new scenario/topic not used to teach
  the capability (§4's "Transfer target" per arc);
- **delayed retrieval** — succeeds again, unaided, after intervening arcs,
  primarily inside arc 6.

**Minimum for graduation (subject to revision once real learner journeys exist,
exactly as A1 declares its own thresholds provisional):** every required
capability needs at least one independent-open production **and** one
transfer-or-delayed-retrieval production before it counts toward B2 readiness.
Two are asked of arc-6's three new capstone capabilities, because the capstone
*is* the level's discourse-coherence proof and a single successful turn does
not establish that a learner can sustain 16–20 turns.

Recognition-only or guided-only evidence never satisfies a required
capability — the master contract's principle 11/12 (§2) applies unchanged, and
B2 needs it *more* than A1 does: a learner who can select the grammatically
correct hedge in a multiple-choice item has not demonstrated they can soften a
real claim they actually wanted to make.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

Uses the shared adaptive support engine; nothing here overrides it.

- **Genuinely novel language:** the conditional/modal system (arc 3) and
  register/pragmatics (arc 5) are the level's steepest new machinery and
  should expect the heaviest initial support — comparable to A1's densest arc
  (`daily_rhythm`, three new pattern groups at once, per `a1-map.md` §8).
- **Strong-prerequisite entry behaviour:** a learner arriving with strong B1
  opinion/comparison evidence should need less initial support in
  `making_the_case`, but novelty (arc 3's conditionals, arc 5's register) still
  earns its own support regardless of prior strength — echoing the master
  contract §10's rule that strong prerequisites reduce initial support but
  never erase novelty.
- **Expected support fade:** early (arcs 1–2) — model/frame visible while new,
  two to three open productions per episode, repair available. Mid (arcs 3–5)
  — the frame withheld on each arc's integrated episode, up to four open
  productions, at least one register or hypothesis task genuinely unaided.
  Late (arc 6) — unaided by default; support returns after a strained turn
  rather than being pre-emptively offered, matching A1's late-arc autonomy
  target (`a1-map.md` §12).
- **Known high-strain tasks:** the capstone's negotiation-under-pushback
  (multiple rounds of objection), and register-switching under time pressure
  (arc 5) — both should expect strain-triggered help to return quickly rather
  than being withheld on principle.
- **Recovery after repeated failures:** identical shared-engine behaviour;
  B2 declares no special-case recovery rule.
- **Replay behaviour once capability is strong:** a learner who replays an arc
  after reaching independent evidence should see support fade faster, exactly
  as the shared engine already does for any level.

If B2 content authoring later discovers a support need the shared engine
cannot express, it is a CORE requirement (§15), not a private workaround.

## 11. Evaluation requirements

The intent/evaluator strategy follows A1's rule (`a1-blueprint.json`
`intentStrategy`): **one intent per communicative function; variants travel as
a subtype**, not as a new intent. B2 proposes roughly fourteen new intents,
matching the fourteen A1 added, plus subtypes on existing repair/opinion
intents where a variant (concession, hedge, intensifier) is really the same
function said differently.

Representative examples per required capability — full sets belong to the
content-authoring phase, not the blueprint, but each required capability must
have at least this shape proven before content ships:

**`develop_and_defend_opinion`**
- clearly correct: "I think we should go with the later start time, because most people struggle to get here by eight."
- natural variant: "Honestly, I'd rather start later — getting here by eight is hard for most of us."
- near miss: an opinion stated with no reason at all ("I just think so") — recognized as an opinion, marked as missing the required justification.
- wrong meaning: agreeing with the counter-argument's position while using opinion-stating language.
- nonsense: a syntactically well-formed sentence with no evaluable stance ("The opinion is opinion.").

**`negotiate_a_resolution`**
- clearly correct: "Could you move my appointment to Thursday instead? I explained why Tuesday doesn't work."
- natural variant: "Would it be possible to reschedule for Thursday? Tuesday's a problem for me, for the reason I mentioned."
- near miss: a request with no justification and no proposed alternative ("Just fix it.") — recognized as dissatisfaction, not as a negotiation move.
- wrong meaning: accepting the original problem as final when the turn calls for a counter-proposal.
- nonsense: an unrelated proposal that ignores the stated problem entirely.

**`hypothesize_about_unreal_situations`**
- clearly correct: "If I were in your position, I'd ask for more time before deciding."
- natural variant: "Were I you, I'd want more time before deciding." (fronted inversion — accepted, not required)
- near miss: real-conditional form used for an unreal situation ("If I am you, I ask for more time") — recognized as an attempt, marked for form.
- wrong meaning: a hypothesis that answers a different question than the one asked.
- nonsense: a well-formed conditional with an impossible/unrelated result clause.

**`adjust_register_to_context`, correct-but-insufficient-form case (form matters here):**
- correct meaning, insufficient register: "gimme a sec, I'll check the contract" addressed to a landlord in a formal complaint turn — semantically fine, register-inappropriate, and per the master contract §11 this must be gradable as its own failure mode, not folded into "wrong."

**Pragmatically inappropriate but grammatically correct (B2 introduces this
category early; C1 owns it fully):**
- "That's completely wrong and you clearly don't understand the issue" as a
  response to a landlord during a request for a repair — grammatically
  flawless, pragmatically counter-productive to the negotiation goal, and must
  be distinguishable from a grammar error in the evaluator's output.

## 12. Four Strands audit

Level-wide estimate (not per-episode, per template §12):

- **Meaning-focused input:** substantial growth over A1/B1 — arc 4's mediation
  source texts/conversations, arc 6's extended capstone dialogue, and
  implication-recognition material in arc 5 all require processing real
  connected discourse, not single sentences.
- **Meaning-focused output:** the level's largest strand — every required
  capability is ultimately produced, not just recognized (§9); arcs 1, 2, and 6
  are output-heavy by design.
- **Language-focused learning:** concentrated in arcs 3 (conditional/modal
  system) and 5 (register/hedging), which are the level's genuinely new
  grammar-and-pragmatics machinery; lighter elsewhere, where B2 is mostly
  recombining B1 forms into longer discourse.
- **Fluency development:** concentrated in arc 6 — known B2 language returning
  at natural conversational pace under real pressure (topic shifts, pushback),
  which is fluency development in Nation's sense: getting faster/more
  automatic with what is already known, not adding anything new.

No strand is allowed to consume the level; the capstone's design purpose is
specifically to force fluency development to exist as its own strand rather
than being an afterthought, since B2's discourse-first nature could otherwise
crowd it out with more language-focused content.

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

- **Turn-taking and repair:** B2 keeps repair available throughout (asking for
  clarification, asking someone to rephrase) but expects it to be used more
  selectively than at A1/B1 — a B2 learner repairs when something is genuinely
  unclear, not by default.
- **Conversation length/topic changes:** grows from single-topic short
  exchanges (arcs 1–5, 9–12 turns) to the capstone's 16–20-turn, multi-topic
  conversation — the single largest jump the master contract asks of any
  level boundary in this document (A1→A2 doubles turn count roughly; B1→B2
  multiplies conversation length by design, per the master contract's B2
  progression note).
- **Discourse coherence:** explicitly evaluated from arc 6 onward — does a
  turn logically continue the argument/negotiation, not just "is this
  sentence correct." This is the level's primary new evaluation dimension and
  is declared as a CORE requirement in §15.
- **Summarization/reformulation:** arc 4's entire content — B2 is the first
  level where mediation (in the CEFR sense) is a required, evaluated skill
  rather than an incidental side effect of any other capability.
- **Register/pragmatics as appropriate to level:** arc 5 introduces register
  as an evaluated dimension for the first time in the curriculum; B2's version
  is deliberately lighter than C1's (§2, "Explicit ceilings").

## 14. Explicit exclusions

B2 deliberately does **not** teach yet:

- structured, sustained monologue (a prepared presentation/speech) — B2 stays
  interaction-first; sustained monologue as a primary skill is C1 territory;
- academic or highly abstract argumentation — B2's issues stay concrete and
  everyday-real (a household decision, a service failure), never an abstract
  policy debate;
- full idiomatic fluency — `use_idiomatic_expressions_naturally` is optional
  and receptive-leaning; idiom as a first-class productive skill is C1/C2;
- nuanced stylistic reformulation (matching a specific literary or professional
  style) — B2's reformulation goal is clarity, not style;
- pragmatic appropriateness as the *primary* grading criterion for every
  capability — B2 introduces it only for the capabilities that specifically
  need it (arc 5, the capstone); C1 generalizes it (master contract §4);
- written correspondence conventions (formal letters/emails as their own
  genre) — register is taught through spoken/conversational register-shifting,
  not letter-writing;
- listening/speaking/pronunciation as implemented, evaluated modes — unchanged
  from every other level in this document; B2's "conversation" and "mediation"
  capabilities are text-based interaction until the product implements and
  tests audio modes honestly (master contract §3, §13).

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

Four requirements, each declared rather than privately worked around, per the
master contract §14.

### 15.1 Discourse-coherence evaluation across turns

- **Problem:** `sustain_a_multi_topic_conversation`,
  `handle_a_topic_shift_gracefully`, and
  `negotiate_an_agreement_under_pushback` need to judge whether a turn
  coherently continues a multi-turn exchange, not just whether the turn
  itself matches a canonical intent frame.
- **Why the current engine cannot express it:** the evaluation architecture
  documented for A1 (`a1-blueprint.json` `evaluationStrategy`) judges a
  canonical frame per turn, locally, with hybrid escalation only for
  form-ambiguous single turns. It has no notion of "does this turn make sense
  given the last three turns."
- **Earliest level that needs it:** arguably B1 already needs a light version
  for "sustain conversations over topic changes" (master contract §4); B2 is
  the first level that must actually evaluate it, not merely allow it to
  happen.
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** a conversation-level evaluation pass that
  receives the recent turn history (already available to the planner/session
  engine) and grades coherence as an additional, separate signal from
  per-turn intent correctness — not a replacement for it.
- **Regression risk:** low for A1/Pre-A1 (single-topic, short exchanges,
  coherence is nearly guaranteed by construction); the risk is scope creep if
  this pass is built generically before B2's actual integrated-conversation
  format exists.
- **Tests required:** a synthetic multi-turn conversation that is
  turn-by-turn "correct" but incoherent overall (answers a different topic
  than the one active) must be flagged; a coherent conversation with one
  imperfect turn must not be penalized on the coherence dimension for that.

### 15.2 Register/pragmatic appropriateness as a separate evaluated dimension

- **Problem:** a semantically and grammatically correct reply can still fail
  a B2 register-sensitive turn (§11's landlord example).
- **Why the current engine cannot express it:** intents currently resolve to
  correct/incorrect (or inconclusive, escalated) on semantic/grammatical
  grounds; there is no separate axis for "right meaning, wrong register."
- **Earliest level that needs it:** B2 (arc 5, the capstone); the master
  contract names full pragmatic-appropriateness grading as a C1 requirement
  (§4), so B2's version must be built to extend cleanly rather than be
  reinvented at C1.
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** an optional secondary verdict alongside the
  primary semantic verdict — `registerAppropriate: true|false|not_applicable`
  — populated only for intents that declare a register-sensitive context, so
  A1–B1 intents are entirely unaffected.
- **Regression risk:** none to earlier levels if the field is additive and
  defaults to `not_applicable`; risk is in getting the B2 register rules
  right without becoming a second, competing evaluator.
- **Tests required:** the landlord example and its informal-register
  counterpart, both graded correct on meaning and differently on register;
  a genuinely register-neutral utterance must resolve `not_applicable`, not a
  false negative.

### 15.3 Honest fallback for open, paragraph-length argumentative turns

- **Problem:** several B2 capabilities (`develop_and_defend_opinion`,
  `weigh_advantages_and_disadvantages`, `negotiate_an_agreement_under_pushback`)
  are naturally longer, more variable free responses than most A1/B1 turns.
  A1's design principle — "no capability is designed to require the provider
  to be reachable" (`a1-blueprint.json` `evaluationStrategy.rule`) — cannot be
  honestly claimed for these B2 capabilities without either lying about
  offline capability or degrading the task into something narrower than the
  real skill.
- **Why the current engine cannot express it:** the existing hybrid path
  assumes a canonical frame is always locally judgeable and escalation is only
  for edge cases; B2's open argumentative turns are not edge cases, they are
  the median case.
- **Earliest level that needs it:** B2 (first level with paragraph-length
  required production as the norm rather than the exception).
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** a declared, honest three-tier fallback per
  affected intent — provider-graded when reachable; a narrower local check
  (does the response contain a stance marker and at least one connector from
  the required pattern, i.e. a structural floor, not full semantic grading)
  when not; and a visible, honest state to the learner ("this will be checked
  more closely when you're back online") rather than silently downgrading
  the task or silently accepting anything.
- **Regression risk:** none to earlier levels, which keep their existing
  fully-local evaluation; risk is that the structural-floor local fallback is
  mistaken for full grading if the "provider unreachable" state is not
  surfaced honestly.
- **Tests required:** provider-unreachable simulation must show the honest
  degraded state, must not silently mark a structurally-empty response as
  correct, and must not claim independent evidence was recorded when only the
  structural floor was checked.

### 15.4 Meaning-preserved reformulation/summary checking

- **Problem:** `summarize_for_someone_else` and `reformulate_to_clarify` need
  to judge whether a reworded sentence/summary preserves the source meaning,
  which is a different comparison from matching a canonical target frame.
- **Why the current engine cannot express it:** existing evaluation compares
  a learner's turn against canonical frames/patterns for a fixed intent; there
  is no existing "does paraphrase A preserve the meaning of source text B"
  comparison.
- **Earliest level that needs it:** B2 (arc 4, first mediation-as-capability
  arc in the curriculum).
- **Proposed shared behaviour:** provider-graded meaning-equivalence check as
  the primary path (this is inherently a semantic-equivalence judgment, not a
  pattern match), with the same honest-fallback contract as §15.3 when the
  provider is unreachable — a structural floor (did the learner produce a
  reformulation that is not a verbatim copy of the source and does contain a
  reformulation marker) rather than a claim of verified meaning-preservation.
- **Regression risk:** none to earlier levels.
- **Tests required:** a reformulation that changes the source's meaning must
  not pass on the structural floor alone when the provider is reachable; a
  verbatim copy must always fail, matching the existing rule that a
  model-visible answer copied verbatim is not independent evidence
  (`a1-authoring-contract.md`, "the model answer is authored content...
  copying it stores nothing").

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Recorded because automation cannot prove these and real learners eventually
must, per the master contract §19 and template §18:

- does the capstone's 16–20-turn length feel like a genuine conversation or
  like an endurance test — perceived difficulty and fatigue at the level's
  longest single task;
- does register-switching (arc 5) feel like a real, useful skill or like an
  arbitrary grammar rule — perceived usefulness;
- do learners actually notice implied meaning (`infer_implied_meaning`) being
  taught, or does it feel invisible/impossible to practice deliberately;
- confidence after `negotiate_an_agreement_under_pushback` — does simulated
  pushback feel like useful practice for a real disagreement, or does it
  provoke anxiety disproportionate to a language exercise;
- does the honest "checked more closely later" fallback state (§15.3) read as
  reassuring and transparent, or as the app admitting it does not really know
  if the learner was right;
- spontaneous transfer — do learners who complete B2 report using
  hedging/register/negotiation language in real conversations outside the
  product, at delayed follow-up;
- whether the themed vs. neutral capstone variants (§7) feel equally
  "real," or whether one reads as the true content and the other as a
  fallback.
