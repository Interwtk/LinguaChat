## 2. Exit promise

At the end of C1, a learner can reliably:

- take and defend a position on an abstract or complex topic, qualify it precisely, and
  concede a fair counterpoint without abandoning it;
- choose and adjust register on purpose — for the audience, and mid-conversation when the
  situation itself shifts — and notice and repair their own register slips;
- take in a longer or more complex message (a longer text excerpt, two differing accounts) and
  relay it accurately and usefully to someone who does not have the original;
- infer meaning that is not stated directly (hints, polite evasion, understatement) and mark
  their own claims with an honest degree of certainty instead of flattening everything to "yes"
  or "no";
- produce an extended, organized turn — several linked sentences with a beginning, a
  development and a close — and self-correct mid-turn without losing the thread;
- negotiate toward a mutually acceptable outcome when constraints genuinely conflict, ask a
  precisely targeted question when an instruction is ambiguous, and propose and defend an
  alternative when the first plan does not work;
- sustain a conversation across two or three related topic shifts, refer back to something said
  earlier without restating it, and close a longer interaction with a short accurate summary.

Explicit ceilings — what stays deferred past C1:

- no claim of complete CEFR C1 mastery: listening, speaking and pronunciation are out of scope
  per the master (§3, §13) and per the product's voice/media boundary; C1 here means the
  implemented textual/interactive curriculum aligned to C1 progression, not full certification;
- no dense literary, academic-citation or highly idiomatic/poetic register (deferred to C2);
- no live/professional interpretation, no specialized legal/medical register;
- no multi-party (3+ simultaneous interlocutors) negotiation; C1 negotiation stays two-party;
- no claim that irony/understatement recognition generalizes beyond the explicitly taught
  patterns — genuinely idiosyncratic humor stays out of scope;
- no formal long-form academic essay writing (structured argument stays conversational/short-text
  length, not essay-length with citation conventions).

## 4. Skill families and arc map

Grouped by communicative function, not grammar topic, per the master (§6).

### Arc A — "Taking a position" (`abstract_argument`)

- Real-world situation: debating an abstract or complex issue (technology and society, work-life
  balance, an everyday ethical dilemma) with a partner who pushes back in good faith.
- Capabilities introduced: `develop_a_structured_argument`, `qualify_a_claim_precisely`,
  `concede_a_counterpoint_gracefully`, `weigh_implications_of_a_position`.
- Capabilities reused: none (first arc).
- Prerequisites: assumed B2 argumentation exit.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 10–14, single topic, allowed to run longer than B2 because
  the topic is abstract rather than transactional.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: language-focused learning (hedging/concession forms) +
  meaning-focused output.
- Personalization mode: light — the abstract issue itself may be interest-adjacent (e.g. a
  learner interested in gaming argues about loot boxes/regulation; a learner interested in
  travel argues about tourism's local impact), never the difficulty of the argument.
- Neutral fallback contexts: work-life balance, screen time, a shared local resource.
- Transfer target: the same three capabilities argued about a topic never used in teaching.
- Delayed retrieval target: `qualify_a_claim_precisely` reused unprompted in a later arc's
  negotiation (Arc F) without being re-taught.

### Arc B — "Getting the tone right" (`register_and_diplomacy`)

- Real-world situation: a socially delicate exchange — giving difficult feedback, disagreeing
  with someone senior or unfamiliar, softening a request that might be refused.
- Capabilities introduced: `adapt_register_to_audience`, `hedge_and_mitigate_a_statement`,
  `disagree_diplomatically`, `repair_a_register_slip`.
- Capabilities reused: `qualify_a_claim_precisely` (G), `concede_a_counterpoint_gracefully` (R).
- Prerequisites: Arc A capabilities; assumed B2 register-management exit.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 8–12, one relationship-sensitive situation.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: language-focused learning (pragmatics/hedging devices) +
  meaning-focused interaction.
- Personalization mode: none — the interpersonal dynamic is the lesson; changing its topic would
  not change its difficulty, but a personalized boss/colleague/relationship risks manufacturing
  an inappropriate scenario, so this arc stays neutral by design (mirrors A1's precedent of
  declaring an arc's personalization mode `none` when the situation itself is the content).
- Neutral fallback contexts: workplace feedback, a shared-living disagreement, declining an
  invitation without damaging the relationship.
- Transfer target: diplomatic disagreement produced in a genuinely new relationship context
  (e.g. taught with a colleague, transferred to a landlord).
- Delayed retrieval target: `adapt_register_to_audience` reused unprompted in Arc E and Arc G.

### Arc C — "Making sense of it for someone else" (`synthesis_and_mediation`)

- Real-world situation: reading or hearing a longer, more complex message (an email, an article
  excerpt, two differing accounts of the same event) and relaying it accurately and usefully to
  someone who was not there — CEFR's mediation sense, in the product's implemented textual form.
- Capabilities introduced: `summarize_a_complex_message_for_a_new_audience`,
  `synthesize_two_conflicting_viewpoints`, `reformulate_for_a_different_audience`,
  `paraphrase_to_avoid_repetition`.
- Capabilities reused: `concede_a_counterpoint_gracefully` (R), `adapt_register_to_audience` (R).
- Prerequisites: assumed B2 summarization exit; Arc A/B capabilities.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 6–10 per message, may be split into a comprehension phase
  and a relay phase.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: meaning-focused input (a genuinely longer/denser text) +
  meaning-focused output (the relay).
- Personalization mode: light — source-text subject may be interest-adjacent (a review of a
  game, a film critique, a travel account); the two-viewpoint structure and the mediation task
  never change.
- Neutral fallback contexts: a workplace email thread, a neighborhood dispute, conflicting
  instructions from two sources.
- Transfer target: mediate a source never seen in teaching, to an audience type not used before
  (e.g. taught "explain to a colleague", transferred to "explain to a newcomer who knows less").
- Delayed retrieval target: `summarize_a_complex_message_for_a_new_audience` reused in Arc G's
  closing-summary capability.

### Arc D — "Reading between the lines" (`nuance_and_implication`)

- Real-world situation: a conversation where meaning is not fully explicit — a friend hints at a
  problem, a colleague is politely non-committal, a remark is understated or lightly ironic.
- Capabilities introduced: `infer_implied_meaning_in_unfamiliar_context`, `express_degrees_of_certainty`,
  `hold_a_nuanced_stance`, `recognize_understatement_or_irony`.
- Capabilities reused: `qualify_a_claim_precisely` (R), `concede_a_counterpoint_gracefully` (R).
- Prerequisites: assumed B2 spontaneous-conversation exit.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 8–12.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: meaning-focused input (inference load) + language-focused
  learning (certainty-marking devices).
- Personalization mode: none — implication and irony depend on shared context the engine
  controls; personalizing the trigger risks an ambiguous or unintentionally offensive remark.
- Neutral fallback contexts: a friend being vague about being busy, a colleague being diplomatic
  about a bad idea, a mildly self-deprecating remark about a plan going wrong.
- Transfer target: infer meaning from a hint pattern not used in teaching.
- Delayed retrieval target: `infer_implied_meaning_in_unfamiliar_context` reused unprompted as a prerequisite skill in
  Arc F (`clarify_an_ambiguous_instruction_precisely`) and Arc G.

### Arc E — "Saying more, in order" (`extended_structured_discourse`)

- Real-world situation: giving a structured account of some length — describing a situation,
  presenting a plan, explaining how or why something happened — in one sustained, organized
  turn, the way a learner would when a friend or colleague asks "so what happened?" and expects
  more than one sentence back.
- Capabilities introduced: `produce_an_extended_structured_explanation`,
  `use_cohesive_devices_across_a_turn`, `self_correct_without_losing_the_thread`,
  `open_and_close_an_extended_turn`.
- Capabilities reused: `develop_a_structured_argument` (R), `adapt_register_to_audience` (R).
- Prerequisites: Arc A.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 4–6 extended turns (each turn is itself several linked
  sentences, so raw turn count is intentionally lower than other arcs).
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: fluency development + language-focused learning (cohesion
  devices).
- Personalization mode: light — the situation/plan/process being explained may be
  interest-adjacent; the organizational demand (beginning/development/close, cohesion, self-
  repair) never changes.
- Neutral fallback contexts: explaining a scheduling mix-up, describing a process at work,
  recounting how a plan changed.
- Transfer target: produce an extended structured explanation about a topic never rehearsed.
- Delayed retrieval target: `use_cohesive_devices_across_a_turn` reused unprompted in Arc G's
  closing summary.

### Arc F — "Working it out" (`negotiation_and_complexity`)

- Real-world situation: a real situation with competing constraints — negotiating an outcome,
  resolving a scheduling/plan conflict, handling a complaint with no clean right answer.
- Capabilities introduced: `negotiate_a_mutually_acceptable_outcome`,
  `clarify_an_ambiguous_instruction_precisely`, `propose_and_defend_an_alternative`,
  `handle_an_unexpected_complication`.
- Capabilities reused: `disagree_diplomatically` (R), `develop_a_structured_argument` (R),
  `infer_implied_meaning_in_unfamiliar_context` (R), `qualify_a_claim_precisely` (D — delayed retrieval target from
  Arc A).
- Prerequisites: assumed B2 negotiation exit; Arc B and Arc D.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 10–14, may include a genuine mid-conversation complication.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: meaning-focused output + fluency development.
- Personalization mode: light — the negotiated good/service/plan may be interest-adjacent (a
  refund on a game, a rescheduled trip); the negotiation structure and required constraints never
  soften for a personalized topic.
- Neutral fallback contexts: a returned purchase, a shared calendar conflict, a service booking
  gone wrong.
- Transfer target: negotiate an outcome for constraints never seen in teaching.
- Delayed retrieval target: `clarify_an_ambiguous_instruction_precisely` reused unprompted in
  Arc G.

### Arc G — "Staying in it" (`sustained_interaction`)

- Real-world situation: a longer conversation that changes topic and tone more than once —
  small talk that turns into a serious request, a catch-up that moves through two or three
  related subjects — requiring the learner to track what was said and adjust.
- Capabilities introduced: `sustain_a_conversation_across_topic_shifts`,
  `refer_back_to_earlier_discourse`, `shift_register_within_one_conversation`,
  `close_a_complex_interaction_with_a_summary`.
- Capabilities reused: nearly every earlier-arc capability, by design (see §8 reuse matrix) —
  this is the level's integrated conversation arc, structurally analogous to A1 arc 6/26's
  whole-exchange episodes.
- Prerequisites: Arc B, Arc D; assumed B2 spontaneous-conversation exit.
- Expected meaningful learner turns: 14–18, spanning 2–3 topic shifts and at least one register
  shift.
- Dominant Four Strands contribution: fluency development, integrating all four strands.
- Personalization mode: themed — topic-shift content may draw on the learner's stated interests
  for two of the three subjects, with the third always a neutral life context, per the master's
  topic-diversity policy (§8).
- Neutral fallback contexts: a catch-up conversation, a mixed personal/practical phone-call-style
  exchange (text-mediated, consistent with the product's no-voice boundary).
- Transfer target: the full integrated skillset exercised in a conversation shape never rehearsed.
- Delayed retrieval target: this arc IS the level's delayed-retrieval arc for nearly every
  required capability introduced earlier (see §8).

| `self_repair_discourse_pattern` ("what I mean is...", "or rather...", "let me put that differently") | repair mid-turn without losing the thread | `cohesive_connector_pattern` | Arc E | 2 | 2 | Arc G |
| `anaphoric_reference_pattern` ("that point you raised", "going back to what we said about...", "as for the other thing") | refer back without restating | `cohesive_connector_pattern` | Arc G | 2 | 2 | — |
| `conditional_alternative_pattern` (mixed/hypothetical conditionals for "if that doesn't work...") | propose an alternative and justify it | assumes B2 second conditional | Arc F | 2 | 2 | Arc G |

"Guided-use target" and "independent-use target" are per-episode occurrence counts within a
capability's home arc, not level-wide totals; level-wide independent evidence targets are in §9.

Grammar is introduced only because a capability above needs it — no pattern here exists for
syllabus-completeness reasons, per the master (§6).

### Vocabulary budget

C1 is deliberately not a raw-vocabulary-count level: precision, register range and collocation
matter more than new headword count, consistent with the master's rejection of vocabulary
catalogues detached from communicative need (§6).

- **Productive items**: ~90–110 new lexical/phrasal items across the level, weighted toward
  hedging/certainty/register vocabulary (roughly 40) and argumentation/negotiation vocabulary
  (roughly 50), with the remainder split across the arcs' concrete nouns/verbs as needed by
  scenario content.
- **Receptive items**: ~160–200, including the vocabulary needed to understand the source
  texts/messages in Arc C and the implied-meaning cues in Arc D, which are deliberately denser
  than what the learner must produce.
- **Incidental scene language**: unbounded but never graded — scenario color (a workplace name,
  a service type) the way earlier levels already treat incidental vocabulary.
- **Multiword units tracked whole** (~35–45): collocations and discourse markers such as "make a
  compelling case", "play devil's advocate", "read between the lines", "on balance", "for what
  it's worth", "meet halfway", "find common ground", "a fair trade-off", "that said", "having
  said that" — tracked as single vocabulary items because their meaning is not the sum of their
  parts, the same rule the product already applies to Pre-A1's multiword phrases.

Exact counts are finalized during `LC-CONT-C1` against the actual authored episodes; these are
budget ceilings, not a pre-committed catalogue.

## 9. Evidence and mastery

Definitions inherit the master's evidence model (§9) without redefinition:

- **Recognition**: the learner correctly judges a register choice, an implied meaning, or a
  certainty level in a closed-choice or short-judgment task (e.g. "which reply is too blunt for
  this situation?").
- **Guided production**: the learner produces the target function using a supplied frame,
  discourse-marker bank, or sentence starter (e.g. a hedging-phrase bank for
  `qualify_a_claim_precisely`).
- **Open assisted production**: the learner produces freely but uses an on-screen hint or model
  answer.
- **Open independent production**: the learner produces freely, unaided, no model on screen.
- **Transfer**: independent success in a genuinely new lexical/contextual realization of the
  capability (per capability, declared in §3/`c1.json`).
- **Delayed retrieval**: independent, unprompted retrieval after intervening arcs, per the reuse
  matrix's `D` marks.

**Minimum for graduation** (provisional, per the master's instruction that exact thresholds may
be revised after real learner data, §9, §19):

- every required capability: **independent = 3** (higher than A1's `independent: 2`, because C1
  evidence includes a pragmatic/register dimension that a single or double success does not
  reliably establish — this is a design choice pending real-learner evidence, not a proven
  number);
- every required capability: **transfer = 2**, at least one of which occurs in Arc G's
  integrated conversation;
- every required capability: **delayed retrieval = 1**, after at least one intervening arc;
- `should` capabilities: independent = 2, transfer = 1, no mandatory delayed-retrieval count;
- C1 cannot graduate a learner using only recognition or guided production, per the master (§9)
  — every required capability's evidence path in `c1.json` includes at least one open-independent
  turn.

A C1 response may be grammatically correct and still fail its target if register or pragmatic
force is inappropriate, per the master (§11). This is why §11 below defines
`register_appropriateness` as an evaluation dimension distinct from grammatical correctness for
every intent in the register/diplomacy/negotiation family.

## 10. Scaffolding expectations

Describes likely support shape; does not override the shared adaptive-support engine (master
§10).

- **Genuinely novel language**: hedging/certainty-marking devices (Arc A/D) and cohesion
  connectors (Arc E) are the level's most novel forms and are expected to need the most initial
  support — a device bank on first exposure, faded as evidence accumulates.
- **Strong-prerequisite entry behaviour**: a learner arriving with strong B2 register control
  (per the master §10, "strong prerequisites may reduce initial support, but never erase
  novelty") should see less framing on `adapt_register_to_audience` but still receive full
  support on the genuinely new `repair_a_register_slip`, since noticing one's own slip is a new
  skill even for a strong entrant.
- **Expected support fade**: frames/hint banks fade once two independent evidence instances are
  recorded for a capability, consistent with the engine's evidence-based fade rule.
- **Known high-strain tasks**: Arc F (multiple simultaneous constraints) and Arc D (irony/
  understatement recognition) are flagged as likely high initial strain; both are non-`I`-heavy
  arcs elsewhere in the reuse matrix so a strained learner is not simultaneously meeting brand-new
  vocabulary.
- **Recovery after repeated failures**: repeated register slips or repeated failure to infer
  implied meaning should restore a lightweight "register check" / "what might they really mean?"
  cue quickly, per the engine's "repeated strain restores help quickly" rule.
- **Replay behaviour once capability is strong**: once a capability's independent target is met,
  replay of its home episode should drop the supporting frame and increase topic novelty rather
  than repeat the original scenario verbatim, matching the master's "no generic review unit"
  principle (§12).

No C1-specific scaffolding bypass is proposed; if the shared engine cannot express something C1
needs, it is raised as a CORE requirement in §15, not implemented privately.

For each intent, example categories (per template §11):

- **`state_structured_argument`** — clearly correct: claim + reason + example, organized;
  natural variant: reason before claim, still coherent; near miss: claim with no support;
  wrong meaning: argues the opposite of the stated position; nonsense: unrelated statement;
  correct meaning but insufficient form: a bare opinion with no reasoning when reasoning was the
  target; pragmatically inappropriate: an aggressive/absolutist claim in a context that called
  for a measured position.
- **`qualify_claim`** — correct: precise hedge matching the intended certainty; variant:
  different hedge, same certainty band; near miss: hedge present but vague ("kind of"); wrong
  meaning: hedge reverses the claim's direction; nonsense: hedge attached to an unrelated
  statement; insufficient form: correct content stated as an absolute when qualification was the
  target.
- **`concede_point`** — correct: acknowledges the specific opposing point, then holds position;
  variant: different acknowledgment phrasing; near miss: vague acknowledgment with no specific
  point named; wrong meaning: concession that actually abandons the position; nonsense: ignores
  the counterpoint entirely; pragmatically inappropriate: a concession phrased as sarcasm.
- **`adapt_register`** — correct: register matches audience; variant: a different but equally
  appropriate register choice; near miss: register roughly right but one lexical choice too
  casual/formal; wrong meaning: content correct, register inverted (formal where informal was
  needed or vice versa); nonsense: register markers with no coherent content; pragmatically
  inappropriate: technically polite phrasing that is condescending for the relationship.
- **`hedge_statement`** — correct: softened, face-preserving, and still clear; variant: different
  mitigation device, same effect; near miss: softened but the actual point gets lost; wrong
  meaning: hedge inverts the statement; nonsense: unrelated hedge; pragmatically inappropriate:
  over-hedged to the point of seeming evasive, or under-hedged to the point of rudeness.
- **`summarize_message`** — correct: accurate gist at the right grain for the audience; variant:
  different valid emphasis, still accurate; near miss: accurate but includes an unnecessary
  detail the audience does not need; wrong meaning: misstates the source; nonsense: unrelated to
  the source; insufficient form: accurate content but copied verbatim rather than reformulated
  when reformulation was the target.
- **`synthesize_viewpoints`** — correct: states agreement and disagreement between both sources
  accurately; variant: different valid framing; near miss: captures one source's view only; wrong
  meaning: reverses which source said what; nonsense: unrelated; pragmatically inappropriate: n/a
  (this intent is content-accuracy focused, not register-focused).
- **`infer_meaning`** — correct: infers the intended meaning; variant: a differently worded but
  equivalent correct inference; near miss: partially correct inference (right topic, wrong
  specific meaning); wrong meaning: literal reading of a non-literal remark; nonsense: unrelated
  interpretation.
- **`extended_explanation`** — correct: organized, cohesive, self-corrects cleanly if needed;
  variant: different valid organization, still cohesive; near miss: correct content as a flat
  list with no connectors; wrong meaning: explanation contradicts the situation; nonsense:
  disconnected sentences; insufficient form: correct content, but abandons the thread after a
  self-correction rather than recovering it.
- **`negotiate_outcome`** — correct: proposes/adjusts toward a genuinely mutual outcome; variant:
  different valid proposal that still satisfies both constraints; near miss: proposal satisfies
  only one side's constraint; wrong meaning: proposal contradicts a stated constraint; nonsense:
  unrelated proposal; pragmatically inappropriate: a technically valid proposal delivered as an
  ultimatum.
- **`clarify_ambiguity`** — correct: targeted question that resolves the specific ambiguity;
  variant: differently worded, equally targeted question; near miss: a generic repair request
  ("what?") that does not target the ambiguity; wrong meaning: question about the wrong part of
  the instruction; nonsense: unrelated question.
- **`track_discourse`** — correct: accurate reference to earlier content, register shift lands
  correctly, or summary is accurate; variant: differently worded, still accurate; near miss:
  vague reference ("that thing you said") without enough specificity to be useful; wrong meaning:
  misattributes what was said earlier; nonsense: unrelated to the conversation history.

## 13. Interaction / discourse / mediation

- **Turn-taking and repair**: C1 repair moves beyond Pre-A1/A1's "I don't understand, say it
  differently" into targeted, precise repair (`clarify_ambiguity`) and self-repair
  (`self_correct_without_losing_the_thread`) — the learner both asks better questions and fixes
  their own mid-turn errors without derailing.
- **Conversation length/topic changes**: Arc G is the first arc in the whole product (across all
  levels designed so far) to require 2–3 genuine topic shifts inside one conversation; earlier
  levels keep "one situation, one topic" (A1) or connect clauses within one topic (A2/B1
  progression per master §4).
- **Discourse coherence**: explicitly evaluated via `use_cohesive_devices_across_a_turn` and
  `track_discourse`, not assumed as a side effect of grammatical correctness — this is the
  concrete implementation of the master's "C1 must assess pragmatic appropriateness and discourse
  control in addition to grammatical form" (§4).
- **Summarization/reformulation**: Arc C is a dedicated mediation-like arc (summarize, synthesize,
  reformulate, paraphrase), consistent with the master's inclusion of "mediation-like tasks such
  as summarizing or simplifying text for another person" among what the text-first product can
  test (§13).
- **Register/pragmatics**: Arc B is dedicated to it, and it is load-bearing across Arc E/F/G — C1
  is the first level where register is a first-class, separately evaluated dimension rather than
  an incidental correctness detail.

## 14. Explicit exclusions

C1 deliberately does not teach, and a later worker must not silently add:

- listening comprehension, speaking, pronunciation or any live/voice interaction — out of scope
  per the master (§3, §13) and the product's infrastructure boundary until those systems exist
  and are honestly tested;
- dense literary or highly idiomatic/poetic language, academic citation conventions, or
  professional legal/medical register — deferred to C2 or out of the product's text-first scope
  entirely;
- multi-party (3+ simultaneous interlocutors) negotiation or discussion — C1 negotiation stays
  two-party;
- essay-length formal writing with citation/structure conventions — C1 "extended structured
  explanation" stays conversational/short-text length, not essay length;
- generalized humor/wordplay recognition beyond the explicitly taught understatement/irony
  patterns — C2's territory per the master (§4: "manage implication/irony/rhetorical choices"
  at a broader scale is explicitly a C2 descriptor);
- any claim of complete CEFR C1 certification — this blueprint covers the implemented
  textual/interactive curriculum aligned to C1 progression only, per the master's CEFR-honesty
  rule (§3).

## 15. Core-engine requirements discovered by blueprint

Per the master (§14), these are raised as requirements for serialized CORE work, not implemented
privately by a level worker.

1. **Register-appropriateness scoring as a distinct evaluation dimension.**
   - Problem: existing evaluation (per the visible A1 precedent) primarily judges whether an
     answer matches an expected intent/pattern. C1 needs a response to be marked wrong (or
     partially credited) for being the *wrong register*, even when the intent and grammar are
     correct.
   - Why current engine cannot express it: no `register_appropriateness` scoring dimension is
     documented in the A1 authoring contract's evaluation model.
   - Earliest level that needs it: C1 (Arc B onward).
   - Proposed shared behaviour: the hybrid evaluator gains a register-appropriateness signal,
     scored independently of intent-match correctness, so "correct meaning, wrong register" is a
     distinguishable outcome from both "correct" and "wrong meaning" — this is exactly the
     `state_life_fact`-style near-miss table the product already uses elsewhere, extended with a
     new axis.
   - Regression risk: low for A1/A2/B1 if the new dimension defaults to "not scored" below C1;
     must not retroactively fail existing lower-level answers that were never register-sensitive.
   - Tests required: register-mismatch fixtures per intent in §11's table; confirms lower levels
     are unaffected.

2. **Discourse-coherence scoring for extended (multi-sentence) turns.**
   - Problem: `use_cohesive_devices_across_a_turn` and `track_discourse` need to evaluate whether
     several sentences in one turn cohere, not just whether each sentence individually passes.
   - Why current engine cannot express it: existing per-turn evaluation, as documented, appears to
     be single-utterance-oriented (one step, one expected intent).
   - Earliest level that needs it: C1 (Arc E).
   - Proposed shared behaviour: an evaluator path that accepts a multi-sentence turn and returns
     both an overall coherence signal and per-capability credit, rather than requiring the turn be
     pre-split into single-intent steps.
   - Regression risk: moderate — touches shared evaluator plumbing; must not change scoring for
     any existing single-utterance step type.
   - Tests required: coherent vs. flat-list vs. contradictory multi-sentence fixtures.

3. **Conversation-state tracking across topic shifts, for `refer_back_to_earlier_discourse`.**
   - Problem: crediting an accurate reference to something said 6–10 turns earlier in the same
     conversation requires the runtime to retain and check against that earlier turn's content.
   - Why current engine cannot express it: not documented in existing session/planner behaviour;
     the closest analog (Pre-A1's `full_conversation`) is a fixed-script roleplay, not a
     dynamically referenced conversation history.
   - Earliest level that needs it: C1 (Arc G).
   - Proposed shared behaviour: a bounded conversation-history buffer the evaluator can check a
     later turn's reference against, scoped to a single session/episode run.
   - Regression risk: low if additive and opt-in per episode; must not change memory/session
     behaviour for levels that do not use it.
   - Tests required: correct-reference, vague-reference and misattributed-reference fixtures
     against a scripted conversation history.

4. **A `negotiated_item` (or similarly named) semantic slot type for Arc F personalization.**
   - Problem: personalizing "the negotiated good/service" (a returned game, a rescheduled trip)
     does not cleanly fit existing semantic types documented in the A1 precedent (`object`,
     `place`, `activity`, `topic`, `subject`).
   - Why current engine cannot express it: no slot type for a personalizable transactional
     item/service with a neutral fallback.
   - Earliest level that needs it: C1 (Arc F), though A2/B1/B2 transactional arcs may also want it
     — `LC-AUD-001` should check whether a sibling blueprint already requested something
     equivalent before this is built twice.
   - Proposed shared behaviour: a new semantic type with the same neutral-fallback contract as
     existing personalizable slots.
   - Regression risk: low, additive.
   - Tests required: personalization-invariant tests per §7's two-theme proof requirement.

None of these four are implemented in this PR. They are raised here per the master's instruction
that "when a level worker discovers a shared-core need, it must raise the requirement instead of
quietly implementing a private workaround" (§14), and are also recorded in
`.ai/foundry/requests/LC-BP-C1.md` for the core lane to pick up.

Before C1 can open, per the master (§17) and template (§17):

- previous-level (B2) exit evidence must actually supply every assumed prerequisite in §0 — the
  single largest open item, since B2 does not exist yet;
- no unprepared language jump: `LC-AUD-001` must confirm B2's actual exit vocabulary/pattern
  ceiling is compatible with C1 Arc A's entry assumptions;
- no duplicated "new" capability already taught earlier: `LC-AUD-001` must confirm none of C1's
  28 capabilities overlaps semantically with something A2/B1/B2 already teaches under a different
  name (a particular risk for `weigh_implications_of_a_position` vs. an assumed B2
  advantages/disadvantages capability — flagged in §3 as a deliberate deepening, not a duplicate,
  but this claim needs the real B2 blueprint to verify);
- old required capabilities continue to receive useful reuse: satisfied within C1 itself by §8;
  cross-level reuse of B1/B2 capabilities inside C1 cannot be verified until those blueprints
  exist;
- complexity rises coherently: C1's abstraction (Arc A/D), register control (Arc B) and discourse
  span (Arc E/G) are each a genuine step up from the master's B2 description (§4), not merely
  "more of the same" — but this is a design claim, not yet independently audited;
- graduation evidence is attainable through real runtime paths: not yet testable without runtime
  content.

This blueprint cannot itself satisfy §17 — it can only state what a satisfied §17 would need to
show. `LC-AUD-001` is where that verification actually happens.

## 18. Human-pilot questions

Recorded per the master's instruction that automation cannot prove real-learner efficacy (§19,
research-before-implementation rule):

- does an abstract-topic argument (Arc A) feel meaningfully harder than a B2 familiar-topic
  argument, or does the jump feel arbitrary?
- do learners perceive register feedback (Arc B, Arc G) as useful correction or as unwelcome
  social judgment — this matters for the master's "confidence-preserving correction" principle
  even outside the older-adult-specific age-adaptation rule;
- does inferring implied meaning (Arc D) feel like a legitimate skill or like guessing what the
  app "wants to hear" — a specific false-mastery risk for any capability graded on interpretation
  rather than a checkable fact;
- does the negotiation arc's (Arc F) mid-conversation complication feel like a fair complexity
  increase or like the app moving the goalposts;
- does Arc G's personalized topic-shift content feel personally relevant, or does stitching two
  interest-based topics and one neutral one into a single conversation feel contrived;
- delayed retention: do learners who pass a capability's independent target in its home arc still
  produce it correctly, unprompted, in Arc G weeks later — this is the actual test of the §8
  reuse matrix's `D` marks, not just the presence of the marks in this document;
- does the `independent = 3` threshold (§9) feel appropriately rigorous or needlessly repetitive
  compared to A1's `independent = 2` — the master is explicit that this number is provisional
  pending real evidence, and this blueprint's choice of 3 should be revisited once C1 has real
  learner journeys, not treated as settled.
