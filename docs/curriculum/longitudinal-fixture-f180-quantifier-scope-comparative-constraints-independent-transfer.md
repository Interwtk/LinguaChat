**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F179 covers conditional permission and plans; F178 covers source and epistemic certainty; F173 covers prosodic grouping. **Distinct construct:** preserving quantifier scope, inclusion/exclusion, comparison direction and exact-versus-approximate strength when a learner conveys a message to another person. ## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Minimum is not an exact total.** A fictional club message says, “At least six volunteers have confirmed.” The learner tells a coordinator, “Exactly six volunteers confirmed.” Mark UNJUSTIFIED_EXACTNESS: six is a lower bound, and additional confirmations remain possible. “Six or more have confirmed; the exact total wasn't given” preserves the evidence. In a separately reviewed variant, “No more than six volunteers can attend” sets an inclusive upper bound, not a lower bound. The recipient cannot infer that six people actually attended from either sentence. A later independently confirmed exact count supports a **new** report, not retroactive credit for the earlier overstatement.

**B — Not all is not none; some is not necessarily only some.** A librarian says, “Not all the workshop handouts are ready.” The learner reports, “None are ready.” Reject NOT_ALL_AS_NONE: the original asserts that at least one is not ready but does not specify whether any are ready. In another message, “Some handouts are ready” does not logically establish “Some are not ready,” even if that conversational implication is common. A correct recipient-facing report preserves the limited claim or asks, “How many are ready?” Do not force a single scalar implicature when source context is absent.

**C — Inclusive/exclusive limits and approximations.** A fictional event notice says, “Up to eight people may join each practice group,” and a later organizer says, “We expect about eight.” The learner reports, “Every group must have exactly eight.” Reject the conflation: “up to eight” allows smaller eligible group sizes, while “about eight” is an approximate expectation, not a fixed requirement. In a separate variant, “fewer than eight” excludes eight, whereas “eight or fewer” includes it. Preserve whether a statement is permission, capacity, prediction or observation; no group size is fabricated from a capacity limit.

**D — Quantifier scope and comparison reference.** A fictional message says, “Each volunteer reviewed a poster.” With no additional context, the same poster and different posters can both satisfy the utterance. The learner says, “All volunteers reviewed the very same poster.” Mark INVALID_SCOPE_INFERENCE; accept “Each reviewed at least one poster; I don't know whether it was the same one” or targeted clarification. A separate comparison says, “The morning session had more participants than the afternoon session.” The learner says, “The morning session had more than ten people.” Reject INVALID_COMPARATIVE_INFERENCE because no absolute count or ten-person baseline was provided. A new message with independently established numbers may support a new claim, not repair the original evidence.

**E — Actual delivery, first authorship and accessible alternatives.** At 09:01 an audio-only fictional message says, “No more than five folders are needed,” but playback fails. The learner asks for text. Do **not** score an unaided listening failure or hallucinate a heard number. At 09:02 a transcript and highlighted upper-bound explanation actually render; at 09:03 the learner correctly says “Five or fewer folders.” Record ASSISTED and ANSWER_EXPOSURE for the measured reading/mediation construct, not unaided listening. An unopened optional diagram is not exposure. At 10:15 a new unassisted, separately delivered “fewer than five” message may be evaluated as a **new** attempt; keep all timestamps, delivered-asset versions and event IDs distinct and deduplicate replayed events.

**F — Longitudinal adaptation, reversal and independent transfer.** Learner A repeatedly confuses “at least” and “at most” in first-unaided messages to new recipients. Across matched sessions, actually delivered LOWER_VERSUS_UPPER_BOUND_CONTRAST and NUMBER_LINE_WITH_FADE alternate with BALANCED_DEFAULT. Later first-unaided unseen library, neutral club and unfamiliar workshop reports preserve inclusive endpoints and exactness after meaningful intervening material/time. Only comparable repeated delayed probes justify cautiously raising the probability of a helpful method while continuing to sample alternatives. Learner B initially improves after NUMBER_LINE_WITH_FADE but later independently converts “not all” into “none” in a new context; lower confidence and explore NOT_ALL_VERSUS_NONE_REPAIR or PRAGMATIC_VERSUS_ENTAILED_INFERENCE. If one method used easier numbers, fewer scope ambiguities, highlighted answers, familiar interests, more hints, shorter delay or more follow-up, record **no causal winner**. Assisted repeats, copied number lines, same-scenario paraphrases, next-calendar-day boundaries without meaningful delay and absent follow-ups do not establish durable independent transfer. Preserve listening/reading and productive/interactive balance.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR and reviewed quantity/recipient oracles.** A–F bind existing versioned relevant can-dos, independent quantity/scope and recipient-action review, actual-delivery provenance and unchanged mastery standards. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; no invented gate.
2. **Bounds and exactness.** A rejects “exactly six” from “at least six” and distinguishes an inclusive upper bound from an observed or guaranteed count; later evidence cannot rewrite first authorship.
3. **Negation and pragmatic implication.** B rejects “none” from “not all” and rejects a categorical “not all” from bare “some” without corroboration; accept justified uncertainty and targeted clarification.
4. **Endpoint and approximation strength.** C distinguishes “up to,” “fewer than,” “or fewer,” “about” and actual attendance, preserving inclusive/exclusive endpoints and statement purpose.
5. **Distributive scope and comparative baseline.** D rejects a shared-poster inference when same-versus-different is unknown, and rejects an absolute ten-person inference from a relative comparison.
6. **First authorship, rendered help and accessibility.** E records earliest eligible unaided output, only actual help/answer exposure, audio failure as unavailable evidence, accurate construct labels and replay deduplication; assisted repairs cannot become independent mastery.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F may change instructional probabilities only after comparable actually delivered methods and later eligible independent outcomes. Sparse, confounded or contradictory observations prevent causal-winner claims and retain exploration.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and separate soft interests.** Require genuinely later distinct sessions, new independently reviewed referents/quantities/recipients, first-unaided reports or clarification and explicit missing follow-up. Rotate adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar topics while preserving fixed can-dos and balanced modalities.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI do not establish executable quantifier-oracle QA, shipped outcome-driven adaptation or human retention. Require exact-head executable stimulus/scope/first-authorship assertions, observed decision changes and independently reviewed delayed unaided transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, threshold weakening, A1–C2 enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable QA. Preserve bounds, negation scope, comparison reference, ambiguity, justified clarification and reversible instruction. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independently verified learning evidence exist.
