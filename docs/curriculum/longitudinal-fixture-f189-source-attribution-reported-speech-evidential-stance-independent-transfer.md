Not a shipped source-checking engine, a universal reporting convention, a new CEFR gate or evidence of human learning gains. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F183 concerns presupposed premises; F185 distinguishes causal explanation from evidence; F187 concerns concession; F188 concerns conversational clarification. **Distinct construct here:** a learner communicates *who said or observed what, through which available channel, with which degree of certainty*, while separating quoted/reported propositions from established facts, preserving modal force and updating an attribution after an explicit correction. Reported information must not silently become the learner's own verified assertion. English examples are illustrative only; this fixture does not teach journalism or authorize factual verification beyond the provided evidence.

A frozen, independently reviewed **attribution oracle** records exact stimulus wording, speaker/writer identity when known, channel (heard speech, read notice, forwarded message), directness (firsthand observation, direct report, secondhand report), claim boundaries, quoted versus paraphrased wording, source reliability **only if actually established**, explicit certainty/negation/conditions and what remains unknown. A source label does not certify a claim. “The notice says the room is available” does not entail “the room is available”; “Maya heard that Theo said ...” must not be silently shortened to “Theo confirmed ...”. An anonymous note remains anonymous; do not invent an author, a timestamp or a verification.

A frozen **semantic and speech-act oracle** accepts multiple natural target-language/variety-specific reporting formulations that preserve propositional content and stance, not one English-style tense backshift or punctuation convention. It distinguishes exact quotation from a faithful paraphrase, a speaker's prediction from an observed event, a suggestion from a commitment, and a question from an assertion. Do not force indirect-speech grammar beyond the existing can-do. Correct grammar cannot compensate for lost attribution, certainty or source chain. A justified “the source is unclear” is valid where the source is genuinely missing.

An **actual-delivery oracle** records immutable versions/hashes of the precise audio, written messages, captions, transcripts, examples, hints, feedback and accessibility alternatives actually rendered before each attempt. An unopened hint is not exposure; a displayed model report is answer-bearing help. Failed audio cannot count as independent listening; a readable fallback may support the reading/mediation route actually used, never phantom listening/speaking. The learner must not receive hidden information from an unrendered source.

A **first-authorship and longitudinal ledger** retains pseudonymous learner, fixed can-do/oracle/rubric versions, stable session-qualified event IDs, replay deduplication, first eligible unaided source-preserving response, source-confusion/error category, actual delivered instruction/help, later assisted revisions and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes. Preserve elapsed time, genuinely distinct sessions and independently reviewed unseen source/claim combinations. Optional hesitation is weak evidence, never a timed mastery gate. Assisted copying cannot overwrite the first-independent response.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — A report is not verification.** A fictional library notice reads, “The club organizer says the rehearsal room is available on Friday,” but no booking confirmation is shown. The learner reports, “According to the organizer, the room is available on Friday.” Accept DIRECT_SOURCE_PRESERVED and CLAIM_NOT_VERIFIED. Reject “The library confirmed the room is booked” as CLAIM_ENDORSED_WITHOUT_EVIDENCE plus invented source and commitment. If the learner says “I don't know whether it is booked,” accept justified uncertainty when the can-do permits it; do not require an unrelated real-world fact-check.

**B — Preserve possibility and suggestion.** In a club message, Nora says, “I might bring a spare microphone, and we could try it.” “Nora said she might bring one and suggested trying it” is a faithful report. Reject “Nora promised to bring a microphone and we will use it”: the learner has inflated possibility into certainty and suggestion into commitment. The target-language oracle must allow valid local reporting forms without enforcing English tense-backshift rules.

**C — Do not collapse a secondhand chain.** Maya says, “Theo told me that the workshop may move upstairs.” The learner reports, “Maya said Theo had told her it might move upstairs,” preserving the source chain and uncertainty. Reject “Theo confirmed the workshop is upstairs.” If the actual stimulus is only “Someone said it may move,” mark SOURCE_UNKNOWN; do not fabricate Maya, Theo or a source timestamp. A later actual statement from Theo can update the ledger but cannot retroactively rewrite what Maya originally said.

**D — Quotation, correction and scope.** The first notice says, “The posters should arrive by Tuesday.” A later signed correction says, “Correction: the invitations, not the posters, should arrive by Tuesday.” A valid response attributes the earlier claim to the first notice and the corrected claim to the later notice, without asserting that anything already arrived. Reject “The posters arrived on Tuesday” or “The first notice always said invitations.” Exact quotation requires the original words; a paraphrase can be accepted if attribution, scope, uncertainty and chronology remain faithful. Do not confuse a changed report with a verified delivery.

**E — Visible model, inaccessible audio and event replay.** At 09:01 a spoken source fails before playback. At 09:02 an accessible text transcript appears with the answer-bearing example, “According to Maya, the room might be available.” At 09:03 the learner copies it. Record ACTUAL_STIMULUS_RENDERED, ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED on the actual reading/mediation route; award no independent listening or free production. An unopened hint is not exposure. Replaying the same stable event at 09:04 does not create a second first attempt. A distinct later session with a genuinely new independently reviewed source and no answer-bearing help may produce valid independent evidence; synonym replacement or crossing midnight alone is not delayed unseen transfer.

**F — Reversible instruction, matched evidence and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly turns secondhand “might” claims into confirmed facts on first-unaided tasks. Across comparable real sessions, WHO_SAID_WHAT_MAPPING, CERTAINTY_AND_MODAL_CHECK and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated with matched source depth, help and delays. Later independent unseen reports may cautiously favor a method linked to more faithful source and certainty preservation while alternatives remain sampled. Learner B initially improves with SOURCE_CHAIN_TRACING, then overattributes every anonymous notice to a named speaker; contradictory evidence lowers confidence, reverses a preference or returns to balanced sampling. If one method had shorter source chains, familiar gaming topics, visible examples, more hints, easier modality or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up and assisted repetitions cannot prove retention. Gaming interests may increase example probability but cannot exclude neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops or required skills.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific attribution, semantic, delivery and evidence oracles. Preserve prerequisites, turn ceilings, thresholds, rubrics and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Report versus established fact.** A preserves a named source and does not turn an unverified claim into a confirmed booking, an invented institutional endorsement or a promise.
3. **Epistemic and speech-act fidelity.** B preserves possibility, suggestion, negation and conditional scope. Reject certainty inflation or manufactured commitments regardless of grammatical fluency.
4. **Secondhand provenance.** C retains available source chains and unknowns; a secondhand claim cannot be recast as a firsthand confirmation or supplemented with an invented author.
5. **Quote, correction and chronology.** D distinguishes exact quote, faithful paraphrase, earlier claim and later correction without retroactively altering source content or treating a prediction as a completed event.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E separates failed audio, accessible text, displayed model exposure, unopened help, assisted copying, deduplicated replay and valid first-independent evidence without relabeling modalities.
7. **Uncertain, matched and reversible adaptation.** F updates method probabilities only from comparable actually delivered options and later independent source-faithful reports; sparse, contradictory or confounded data cannot name a causal winner and alternatives continue to be sampled.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and topic diversity.** Require genuinely distinct sessions, independently reviewed new source/claim/stance combinations, first-unaided reports, explicit missing follow-up and original receptive/productive balance. Interests only influence probabilities; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain reachable.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable interaction QA, shipped adaptive teaching or demonstrated learning gains. Require exact-head executable stimulus/source/provenance checks, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should help learners faithfully report another person's words and uncertainty, then independently transfer that ability to unfamiliar sources. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
