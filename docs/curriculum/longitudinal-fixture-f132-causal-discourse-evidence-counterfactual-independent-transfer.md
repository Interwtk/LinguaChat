## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Sequence alone does not assert cause.** A fictional English note says, “The lights went out **after** the rain began.” Learner A reports “The rain caused the outage” as a confirmed source fact; learner B independently states that the events are ordered but the note gives no cause. Counterfactual: an independently validated revised note says, “The lights went out **because** rainwater damaged a cable.” The revised text **asserts** a cause; the evaluator credits accurate attribution of that assertion, without treating it as independently verified physical evidence. Replacing names or dates alone must not change the oracle. Validate actual connective semantics for other target languages.

**B — Correlation, reported explanation and source certainty.** A fictional school newsletter reports, “More umbrellas were sold on rainy days.” Learner A concludes that buying umbrellas makes it rain; learner B describes an association and, if asked for a cause, says the newsletter does not establish one. A second source says, “The shopkeeper **believes** rain increased umbrella sales.” The learner must preserve **who believes what** rather than convert the claim to a verified cause. Counterfactual: an independently reviewed fictional report explicitly states that a controlled test supports a specific causal effect; the learner may report the **report's conclusion and evidence level**, not universal certainty beyond it. Do not score statistical or scientific truth from linguistic plausibility alone.

**C — Direction, necessary conditions and multiple causes.** Fictional instructions say, “The gate opens **only if** the badge is valid; a valid badge does not guarantee opening because the gate may be locked.” Learner A reverses the condition (“a valid badge always opens it”); learner B explains that validity is required but may not suffice. Counterfactual: change the validated source to “The gate opens **if** the badge is valid and the gate is unlocked”; the accepted conditional model must change with the explicitly supplied conditions. If the lock status is unknown, a learner may ask. A separate source listing two possible causes does not license selecting one without evidence. No real security operation is performed.

**D — Purpose, result, negation and unrealized counterfactual.** A fictional message says, “Lena left early **to avoid** traffic, but she **still arrived late**.” Learner A says she avoided traffic and arrived on time; learner B distinguishes the intended purpose from the observed result. Another fictional speaker says, “**If** the bus had arrived earlier, we **would have** reached the concert on time.” This is a hypothetical contrast, not evidence that the bus did arrive earlier or that the concert was reached on time. Counterfactual: “The delay was **not caused by** the weather” reverses a cause attribution relative to “The delay was caused by the weather”; an automatic translation dropping **not** changes the intended proposition. Target-language counterfactual morphology and appropriate CEFR level require independent validation.

**E — Delivered stimulus, accessibility, help chronology and replay.** An authoritative fictional audio explanation says “X did **not** cause Y,” but the rendered caption omits **not**, a translated handout replaces “after” with “because,” and a screen reader announces an arrow label before the relevant cause/effect description. Freeze both source and **actually rendered** channels. Quarantine affected causal-meaning evidence as UNSUPPORTED when the delivered stimulus is corrupted or construct-inaccessible; preserve other valid observations. A first unaided interpretation submitted at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED if the tutor's answer-bearing causal diagram was queued at 09:59 but **rendered** at 10:01. Copying it later is ASSISTED. Replaying the same immutable event ID never manufactures a new independent attempt. Accessible alternatives must preserve the assessed construct or explicitly mark non-equivalence.

**F — Reversible instruction, matched difficulty and genuinely new transfer.** Fictional learner A can copy a tutor's cause/effect arrows immediately but later confuses chronology with cause in first-unaided explanations. After **actually delivered** TEMPORAL_VS_CAUSAL_CONTRAST on comparable tasks, A independently explains an unseen fictional transport delay to a new listener days later, preserving uncertainty. Learner B instead improves delayed independent explanation after SOURCE_CLAIM_VS_EVIDENCE_SORT with equivalent language load. Freeze baseline, target descriptor, language/variety, causal relation type, clause count, source reliability, evidence burden, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity, hints, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If A receives explicit two-clause “because” statements and B receives long ambiguous reports, mark METHOD_CLAUSE_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_EVIDENCE_BURDEN_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Renaming actors while retaining the same causal graph and answer cues is not unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups: missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may justify **actually different next instruction**, with uncertainty and continued alternatives; contrary evidence must reduce confidence. Preserve skill balance, due reviews, CEFR gates and nonexclusive topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, validated language/variety-specific propositions and causal constructions, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Complex counterfactuals are not silently imposed on beginners.
2. **Chronology, correlation and attributed cause remain distinct.** A/B distinguish event order, association, an author's asserted explanation and externally established evidence. Changing “after” to a validated “because” changes the source-claim oracle; cosmetic names do not. The evaluator never silently fact-checks or upgrades claims.
3. **Causal direction, condition and sufficiency.** C preserves necessary-versus-sufficient meaning, unknown conditions and multiple possible causes. Reversing implication or supplying a previously missing condition changes the expected explanation.
4. **Purpose, negation and hypothetical truth status.** D separates intention from achieved outcome, respects the scope of “not” and does not treat an unrealized “if ... would have” as a historical event. Validated polarity and modality counterfactuals must change the oracle.
5. **Warranted uncertainty and independent communication.** A–D reward source-faithful explanation, calibrated attribution and targeted clarification where evidence is absent; unsupported causal certainty, generic hedging and copied diagrams do not earn independent mastery.
6. **Actual delivery, help timing, accessibility and replay.** E validates audio, captions, translation, negation, connective, diagram direction and screen-reader sequence. Only answer-bearing help **actually rendered before** the first authored response contaminates independence; replay is idempotent and corrupted channels quarantine only affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningful separate learning opportunities, new causal relation structures and contexts/recipients, first-unaided explanation or free production without answer-bearing help, and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Reworded or renamed trained causal graphs are not unseen.
8. **Reversible method selection and soft interests.** F logs feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** options, comparable evidence burden and clause complexity, uncertainty, exploration, contradictory outcomes, protected CEFR skill coverage and due reviews. Interest examples are probabilistic only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain available.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/proposition/relation/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, source-effective and actually delivered artifacts, first/help/event chronology, validated linguistic oracle, polarity/condition/source-version counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot establish a shipped evaluator, a reproducible runtime curricular defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently validated target-language/variety-specific causal language and source-attribution oracles; accessible authoritative and actually delivered stimuli; independent first attempts and help chronology; delayed unaided retrieval and genuinely unseen explanatory transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skill coverage, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and chronology/correlation/cause/condition/purpose/negation/counterfactual/caption/translation/diagram/replay/source-version counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Unvalidated constructions, contradictory source channels, inaccessible tasks, unknown evidence or missing follow-ups leave affected claims **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, never fabricated learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive causal-discourse instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners explain what a source does and does not establish—independently and in unfamiliar contexts—not merely repeat a confident causal story.**
