## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Borrowing, custody and title are not equivalent.** A reviewed checkout log states: 'Priya borrowed a tablet from the media desk at 09:00. She still has it at noon. The record does not identify its legal owner.' Learner A reports 'Priya owns the tablet'; learner B independently reports 'Priya currently has a tablet borrowed from the media desk; ownership is not established.' Counterfactual: a separately authenticated source explicitly states that the library owns the tablet, licensing an attributed library-title claim but not transferring title to Priya. Another counterfactual logs a completed 13:00 return, so 'still has it' is no longer licensed after that time. A source saying 'Nina lent her charger' does not by itself independently prove Nina's legal title or authorization to lend.

**B — Relational possession is not property.** In a family-and-work introduction, 'My sister works at our university; her manager edited the department's guide.' Learner A reports that the speaker owns their sister and university and that the manager personally owns the guide. Learner B independently distinguishes kinship, institutional affiliation, editorial role and department association. Counterfactual: a reviewed rights notice explicitly credits the manager as sole author, permitting that narrower authorship attribution; editing alone never proves authorship or copyright ownership. In independently reviewed Spanish 'mi casa' may describe the speaker's home/residence without documenting legal title; Japanese '私の会社' can indicate employer/affiliation. Do not grade the absence of a literal English ownership paraphrase as a semantic error.

**C — Nested reference and ambiguous antecedents.** A lost-property message says 'Alex left Jo's sister's bag in Pat's car.' With identities and a reviewed reference graph, the bag is associated with Jo's sister and the car with Pat; the message alone does not establish title to either. Learner A says 'Alex's bag is in Jo's car'; learner B independently preserves the nested relation and asks where to collect the bag. Counterfactual: 'Alex told Sam that their badge was missing' is ambiguous without contextual antecedent resolution; credit 'Whose badge—Alex's or Sam's?' rather than an invented owner. Singular 'their' is possible in reviewed English varieties. A clarified source naming Sam supports a determinate report. Do not confuse physical location, speaker reference and title.

**D — Group possessives, item counts, consent and handoff.** A team note states 'The three interns are using the lab's two cameras with the coordinator's permission; one camera is checked out to the group until Friday.' Learner A infers six cameras, one owned by each intern, permanent individual ownership and blanket permission for other lab devices. Learner B independently reports **two** lab-associated cameras, permission limited to the specified use and an outstanding group checkout. Counterfactual: a separately reviewed inventory and permission record can establish named ownership and broader rights, but not otherwise. 'The students' projects' does not by itself prove that each student authored every project. If recipient action depends on whether a particular intern may take a camera offsite, clarification is correct.

**E — Delivered channel, authorship, accessibility and replay.** Authoritative audio says 'This is **not** my charger; I borrowed it from Mei.' ASR omits 'not', captions misattribute 'Mei's' to the speaker, machine translation changes 'borrowed' to 'bought', or an unlabeled visual owner arrow is read in the wrong screen-reader order. Compare authoritative and **actually rendered** inputs; quarantine only the affected ownership/custody construct when the learner was not given adequate evidence. At 10:00 the learner independently asks 'Did Mei lend it to you?'; a hint queued at 09:59 but first rendered at 10:01 cannot retroactively assist that question. A later correction after exposure is ASSISTED. An accessible reviewed transcript may provide equivalent **reading** evidence but does not certify unheard listening. Autocomplete suggesting 'Mei owns it' is assistance, not learner-authored proof. Replayed session-qualified IDs cannot generate new first attempts, independent sessions or delayed successes.

**F — Longitudinal methods, confounding, delayed transfer and interest diversity.** Fictional learner A aces an immediate entity-arrow diagram after ENTITY_RELATION_GRAPH_THEN_FADE but later incorrectly attributes title in first-unaided lost-property messages. Comparable actually delivered POSSESSION_VS_TITLE_SOURCE_CONTRAST and BORROW_LEND_RETURN_TIMELINE_THEN_FADE trials precede a genuinely unseen independent equipment handoff with new people, items, audience, relation depth and purpose. Learner B improves delayed independent kinship-versus-ownership reporting after KINSHIP_AFFILIATION_VS_PROPERTY_CONTEXT_PAIRS and LEARNER_GENERATED_CHECKOUT_MESSAGE. If one option uses explicit title documents and two familiar people while another uses unresolved title, four people and nested genitives, mark METHOD_TITLE_EVIDENCE_CONFOUNDED and METHOD_RELATION_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED: infer **no winner**. Changing only names in an otherwise identical bag-location sentence is not unseen transfer. Match baseline, oracle clarity, reference depth, lexical familiarity, variety, modality, topic, help, interval and follow-up. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing distinctly; missing is neither success nor failure. Only repeated comparable later **first-unaided** outcomes justify a logged change in **actually delivered** instruction; maintain uncertainty, alternatives and reversibility when later evidence contradicts an early apparent benefit. A learner interested in music may encounter instrument loans more often, but neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar equipment/family/work contexts and all CEFR objectives remain reachable.
