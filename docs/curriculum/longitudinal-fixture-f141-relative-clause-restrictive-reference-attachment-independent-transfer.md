## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Restriction is not an assertion about everyone else.** In a fictional club roster with mentors M1 and M2 marked as workshop attendees and M3 not marked, the reviewed English notice says **“The mentors who attended the workshop received the schedule.”** The sentence reports schedule receipt for attending mentors; it does **not** entail that M3 did not receive the schedule through another channel. Learner A concludes “M3 definitely received nothing”; learner B independently reports the known recipient set and says M3's receipt is **not established**. Counterfactual: replace the source with **“Only mentors who attended the workshop received the schedule”**; the exclusivity claim now excludes nonattendees from the recipients under the reviewed scope, without claiming every attendee received it. Another counterfactual changes the predicate to “may request the schedule”; permission must not be upgraded to receipt. A highlighted roster showing the answer before authorship is assistance.

**B — Restrictive versus supplementary relative information.** In a fictional English written handoff with an already identified group of **all four coordinators**, compare **“The coordinators who completed training will lead the tour”** with **“The coordinators, who completed training, will lead the tour.”** In the reviewed written context, the first describes the qualifying coordinators; the second presents training as supplementary information about the already identified coordinators and says the group will lead. Learner A treats both as necessarily the same subset instruction; learner B independently explains the contrast and preserves uncertainty about nonqualifiers in the first sentence. Counterfactual: remove the known four-person referent set or change the medium to spontaneous speech without clear prosodic evidence; the supplementary interpretation may no longer be securely recoverable and must be marked **UNSUPPORTED/ambiguous**, not inferred solely from a model's imagined comma. Independently reviewed language-specific alternatives must remain acceptable.

**C — Relative-clause attachment and warranted clarification.** Fictional message: **“Please contact the assistant of the director who called yesterday.”** Without further context, the relative clause may be understood as modifying **director** or **assistant**. Learner A confidently names one caller and invents a contact; learner B independently asks **“Was it the assistant or the director who called yesterday?”** Counterfactual: a preceding reliable message explicitly states “The director called yesterday; the assistant did not”; only then is the intended attachment resolved. A diagram that connects the clause to one noun is answer-bearing help if rendered before the first response. Do not treat a plausible preferred reading as logically unique or score a warranted clarification as failure.

**D — Only, negation, indefinite reference and multilingual boundaries.** Fictional opt-in notice: **“Only email parents who have opted in.”** This restricts permissible recipients but does **not** command emailing every opted-in parent. **“Do not email parents who have not opted in”** prohibits emailing non-opted-in parents; it also does not obligate sending mail to others. Learner A converts either into “email all opted-in parents”; learner B independently communicates the restriction without inventing an obligation. Counterfactual: replace “only email” with **“Email every parent who has opted in”**; now the positive universal instruction is present. Separately reviewed Spanish written pairs such as **“Los tutores que llegaron temprano…”** and **“Los tutores, que llegaron temprano, …”** may express restrictive/supplementary contrasts in specified contexts; Japanese prenominal modification and other target-language systems must be judged on their own syntax and discourse conventions, not on English comma placement. An indefinite **“a tutor who speaks Spanish”** does not, by itself, identify one unique tutor.

**E — Delivered punctuation, prosody, translation and authorship chronology.** The authoritative English written notice is **“The guides, who completed training, will help.”** A delivered caption removes the commas, a TTS voice suppresses the relevant phrasing, a translation recasts the clause as restrictive, a screen reader loses clause boundaries, or a visual diagram highlights only a subset. Preserve source and **actually delivered** representations; if the delivered contrast no longer supports the target interpretation, quarantine **only the affected scope judgment** as UNSUPPORTED rather than mark the learner wrong. A learner's independent written interpretation authored at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED when a parse was queued at 09:59 but **rendered** at 10:01; the revision at 10:02 after seeing it is ASSISTED. A silent autocomplete rewrite of the learner's relative clause is not independently authored production. Replayed event IDs cannot manufacture attempts, new sessions, delayed retrieval or improvement. Accessibility alternatives must preserve the assessed distinction without exposing the answer before the independent probe.

**F — Longitudinal methods, confounds and unseen recipient transfer.** Fictional learner A scores immediately after **BRACKETED_STRUCTURE_THEN_FADE** but repeatedly misidentifies referents in delayed unaided handoffs. After comparable, **actually delivered** REFERENT_SET_FIRST and AMBIGUITY_CLARIFICATION_DIALOGUE trials, A later independently clarifies a **new** ambiguous volunteer assignment for an unfamiliar recipient. Fictional learner B instead improves delayed written audience descriptions after RESTRICTIVE_SUPPLEMENTARY_CONTRAST and LEARNER_GENERATED_REFERENT_DESCRIPTION. Compare baseline reference tracking, antecedent salience/count, clause embedding, lexical familiarity, punctuation/prosody availability, negation/only scope, modality, topic familiarity, delay and follow-up opportunity. If one method uses a two-person unambiguous written notice while another uses four nested antecedents in noisy audio, flag METHOD_EMBEDDING_DEPTH_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_REFERENT_SALIENCE_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_PUNCTUATION_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Replacing “mentors” with “guides” in the same sentence and referent diagram is **not** unseen transfer; require a new antecedent configuration, communicative purpose or audience demand with an independently reviewed oracle. Track eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes; missing is neither success nor failure. Only repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may support an **observed subsequent change in delivered instruction**, with uncertainty, exploration and reversal after contradictory evidence. Music, games and travel may bias example probability but cannot exclude neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar topics or required CEFR objectives.
