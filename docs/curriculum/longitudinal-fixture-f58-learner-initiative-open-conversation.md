# F58 — Learner initiative in open conversation must be distinguished from tutor-driven fluency

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped initiative evaluator, personality assessment, CEFR certification or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F54 concerns clarification and repair after communication breakdown; F57 separates reported confidence from demonstrated competence. F58 concerns **learner-authored conversational initiative** when the task permits open interaction: starting a relevant exchange, introducing or developing a topic, asking purposeful follow-up questions, sustaining mutual exchange, changing direction appropriately, and closing or handing over the floor without a supplied script. Initiative is not raw talk time, extroversion, speed, assertiveness, message count, confidence, engagement, compliance with a chatty tutor or a universal CEFR requirement. The source-defined task/rubric determines which initiative behaviors, if any, are assessable. A learner can be competent while concise, reserved or choosing not to disclose personal information.

## Controlled setup

Resolve the **actual versioned** CEFR can-do, blueprint, interaction task, audience/role, modality, evaluator/rubric, permitted partner contributions, independence conditions, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, due review and exit gates before any credit. Distinguish a genuinely open interaction from a constrained information-gap, receptive-only task or scripted exchange; do not invent an initiative requirement for objectives that do not contain one. Freeze communicative goal, task difficulty, language burden, available information and evaluator version across method comparisons. If spoken interaction is required but live audio or its evaluator is unavailable, report `UNAVAILABLE` instead of treating a text chat as oral mastery. Source-permitted natural partner replies are not automatically tutor scaffolds; an answer-revealing starter, question bank or supplied next turn is.

Use deterministic pseudonymous synthetic histories, injected clock/seed, distinct real learning sessions, stable session-qualified event IDs and immutable event streams. Record the learner's **first unprompted opportunity**, actual first authored move, relevance and semantic adequacy, topic initiation/development, question specificity, uptake of partner information, reciprocity, source-valid topic shifts and closing, plus conversational outcome. Separately record partner/tutor role, assigned versus **actually delivered** opening prompt, follow-up cue, model, transcript, translation, accessibility aid, retries, answer exposure and subsequent revisions. Record optional hesitation/latency only when measured and contextually interpretable; silence, turn length and speed are not by themselves evidence of inability, disability, disinterest or lack of mastery. Preserve explicit opt-outs, missing observations, asynchronous context and privacy-minimized transcripts. Never infer personality, mental health, protected attributes or a fixed “initiator type.”

Predeclare `OPEN_OPPORTUNITY_VERIFIED`, `LEARNER_INITIATED`, `LEARNER_DEVELOPED_TOPIC`, `SOURCE_VALID_PARTNER_REPLY`, `TUTOR_CUED_INITIATIVE`, `MODEL_SUPPLIED_NEXT_TURN`, `ASSISTED_CONTINUATION`, `INDEPENDENT_DIALOGUE_TRANSFER`, `NO_OPEN_OPPORTUNITY`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These are evidence-provenance states, not stable learner identities or invented scoring thresholds.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Same fluent conversation, different authorship.** Two learners complete source-matched open planning dialogues. A independently opens with a relevant proposal, asks a specific follow-up based on the partner's reply and negotiates a change. B produces equally polished final turns only after the tutor displays an opening sentence and suggests each next question. Preserve both outcomes and first attempts, but credit independent initiative only where authored before answer-revealing cues. Do not erase B's assisted practice benefit or misclassify an ordinary partner reply as coaching.
- **B — Quiet but effective versus verbose without uptake.** A concise learner waits for a legitimate conversational opening, then asks one well-targeted question, incorporates the answer and completes the task. A verbose learner repeatedly launches unrelated questions without responding to partner information. Where the versioned rubric values relevant initiative and reciprocity, distinguish their communicative adequacy; do not use word count, silence, latency, accent or self-described confidence as a proxy. If the task does not require initiating, do not add a new failure gate.
- **C — Apparent independence created by an invisible starter.** A UI “conversation suggestion” and an audio model are delivered before the first learner message, but one analytics stream logs only the final message. Another learner has the suggestion merely assigned, never displayed, and independently proposes a topic. Reconstruct verified **delivery** and answer exposure, not assignment, and keep the two initiative histories distinct. If delivery chronology is uncertain, quarantine disputed independence instead of awarding it by default.
- **D — Short-term guided fluency versus later open transfer.** Scaffolded topic cards yield many smooth follow-up questions in familiar role-play. On a genuinely later session with intervening material and an unseen, source-comparable context, the learner cannot initiate or sustain the target exchange without cues. Another learner makes more first-attempt errors during practice but later independently negotiates an unfamiliar arrangement. Report immediate assisted participation separately from eligible, completed and missing delayed independent probes; do not certify durable initiative from guided conversational smoothness.
- **E — Adaptive method reversal without topic capture.** For the same versioned objective and standard, learner A initially shows stronger later independent open exchanges after concise explanation plus one unguided attempt; learner B initially benefits from short scaffold-fading role-plays. Counterbalance partner behavior, task order, topic familiarity and difficulty; sample viable alternatives. Later independent transfer reverses B's earlier pattern, requiring a lower-confidence, revisable instructional choice. Gaming or music interests may influence examples but must not exclude neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar exchanges or any required receptive/productive objective.
- **F — Offline turn ordering, consent and modality fences.** An offline independent opening is recorded before a tutor cue, but the cue event arrives first at sync; a duplicate opening is uploaded, a new session reuses a local turn ID, and an older rubric differs on acceptable partner prompts. A separate learner declines a personal-topic invitation and instead proposes a task-equivalent neutral topic; another needs a source-permitted accessible response mode; a required oral probe has no supported speech evaluator. Replay trusted occurrence/delivery order with session-qualified IDs and immutable rubric versions. Preserve the independent opening, mark only later cue-exposed continuations assisted, honor a safe opt-out without automatic failure, and return `UNAVAILABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED` for unvalidated modality or rubric equivalence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Objective and rubric govern initiative.** For A–F emit exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, task, modality, audience/partner permissions, evaluator/rubric, permitted support, independent evidence, delayed retrieval, transfer, due-review and exit requirements. Credit initiative only where source-defined; missing rules or unsupported modalities produce `UNSUPPORTED`/`UNAVAILABLE`, not invented thresholds.
2. **Authorship precedes apparent fluency.** In A, compare the learner's first open opportunity, self-authored opening, follow-ups, uptake and actual tutor model/cue exposure. Identical polished final transcripts must not collapse independent and coached initiative into the same evidence.
3. **Relevance and reciprocity outrank verbosity.** In B, separate relevant initiation, response to partner meaning, task completion and language accuracy from word count, turn count, hesitation, self-reported confidence or willingness to disclose. Do not penalize concise, source-valid exchanges or add a new initiative requirement to unrelated can-dos.
4. **Assigned prompts are not delivered prompts.** In C, track selected, displayed, heard and acknowledged starter/question/model events before each learner turn. Classify known exposure; do not infer exposure from assignment or independence from missing analytics. Disputed chronology is explicitly unknown.
5. **Delayed independent transfer is indispensable.** In D, preserve first-attempt semantic quality, assistance dependence and later unaided retrieval in distinct meaningful sessions and unseen contexts. Report probe eligibility, completion and missingness; assisted fluent chat or a date boundary alone is not durable mastery.
6. **Instructional choices are uncertain and reversible.** In E, log available, chosen and verified-delivered explanation, role-play, scaffold fading and free-production options, plus subsequent independent outcomes and uncertainty. Allow opposite learners and later contradictory evidence to change provisional decisions; continue feasible exploration and balanced skill coverage without fixed “outgoing/quiet” learner types.
7. **Interests, agency and accessibility are independent constraints.** In E/F, keep soft topic weighting separate from instructional choice, rotate adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts, and preserve all source-required objectives. Accept task-equivalent neutral topic alternatives and supported accommodations without labeling them failure or unauthorized help; do not invent oral/listening evidence from text.
8. **Replay cannot rewrite initiative provenance.** In F, receipt-order permutations, duplicate events, session-local ID reuse, late cue delivery and rubric changes must converge on the same first-turn authorship, independent/assisted ledger, due reviews and policy inputs. Preserve original rubric versions, quarantine uncertain chronology and never retroactively promote coached continuations.
9. **Release claims require executable and human evidence.** For every history produce an auditable ledger of source/rubric IDs, verified open opportunities, first/final turns, partner role and replies, prompt delivery, semantic outcome, independent versus assisted initiative, delayed/transfer eligibility and results, method choice/uncertainty, topic/skill coverage and per-assertion `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. Documentation CI is not an executable longitudinal evaluator, shipped adaptive tutor or evidence of improved real-world learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

To claim an **executable fixture PASS**, publish the exact tested engine HEAD and command; injected seed/clock; source-resolved CEFR task, blueprint and evaluator versions; immutable synthetic event streams with receipt-order permutations; verified tutor/partner roles and actual cue delivery; the learner's first independent opportunity and authored turns; rubric-defined initiative/reciprocity outcomes; independently completed delayed and unseen-context probes with eligible/completed/missing denominators; topic/modality balance; reversible instructional choices; and assertion-level verdicts. Unknown source rules, prompt delivery, chronology, transfer, privacy-safe alternatives or modality equivalence must remain visible as unknown or unsupported, never manufactured successes.

Any claim that adaptive initiative practice improves **durable independent conversational competence** requires separately reviewed longitudinal evidence with a credible balanced comparator, sufficient genuinely delayed unaided retrieval and unseen-context transfer, harm checks for quiet/concise and accessibility-supported learners, and an ethically reviewed real-learner pilot. **The tutor should create room for the learner to lead when the task calls for it, not script a conversation and call it independent.**
