# F59 — Adaptive challenge must optimize durable independent learning, not easy wins or unproductive struggle

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped challenge scheduler, validated cognitive-load measure, CEFR certification or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F44 protects against engagement-proxy reward hacking; F56 concerns feedback timing; F58 concerns learner-authored initiative. F59 isolates **practice challenge calibration**: the tutor may provisionally adjust practice complexity, scaffold availability, pacing, example distance and sequencing to support later unaided retention and transfer. A difficult attempt is not inherently beneficial, and an easy successful attempt is not inherently durable. Challenge is a **revisable instructional choice**, not a fixed learner trait, an inferred emotion or permission to change CEFR evidence requirements.

## Controlled setup

Resolve an **actual versioned** CEFR can-do, curriculum blueprint, prerequisite graph, task/skill/modality, evaluator and rubric, source-permitted supports, required independent evidence, delayed retrieval, unseen-context transfer, due reviews and exit gates. Keep these standards fixed. Separate (1) **practice challenge** (e.g. amount of context, distractors, linguistic novelty, task length, scaffold fade, time between reviews), (2) **assessment difficulty and construct**, (3) **topic familiarity**, and (4) **accessibility and safety supports**. A practice variant is not source-equivalent merely because it has the same title. Predeclare which variants genuinely target the same can-do, which are preparatory, and which require separate scoring; do not manufacture a universal “optimal difficulty” percentage, latency cutoff or frustration score. An unsupported speaking/listening evaluator is `UNAVAILABLE`, never replaced by text evidence.

Use deterministic pseudonymous synthetic learner histories with injected clock/seed, stable session-qualified event IDs, immutable event streams, and genuinely distinct learning sessions. Record objective/rubric versions, candidate challenge options and their source-valid mappings, **assigned versus actually delivered** challenge, item exposure and familiarity, activity order, first-attempt semantic quality, recurring error categories, optional measured hesitation/latency, retries, hints/models/scaffold delivery, stop/skip/decline events, self-initiated repair, independent completion, meaningful delay/intervening material, unseen-context transfer, and eligible/completed/missing probes. Separate natural task difficulty from inaccessible presentation, missing prior instruction, technical failure, partner behavior and fatigue that cannot be reliably inferred. Never infer anxiety, disability, intelligence, motivation or a permanent “struggles best” learning style from pauses, mistakes or engagement. Respect privacy-minimized logs and source-permitted accommodations.

Predeclare `PRACTICE_CHALLENGE_DELIVERED`, `CHALLENGE_ASSIGNED_NOT_DELIVERED`, `SCAFFOLD_AVAILABLE`, `SCAFFOLD_ACTUALLY_USED`, `PRODUCTIVE_FIRST_ATTEMPT`, `REPEATED_UNPRODUCTIVE_FAILURE`, `ASSISTED_PRACTICE_ONLY`, `INDEPENDENT_DELAYED_RETRIEVAL`, `INDEPENDENT_UNSEEN_TRANSFER`, `CHALLENGE_CONTEXT_CONFOUNDED`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `MODALITY_UNAVAILABLE`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. These describe evidence and decisions, not people.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Easy fluent practice versus later failure.** Learner A repeatedly completes familiar, heavily cued examples with high final accuracy and low retries. The first attempt on an unseen source-matched independent task omits a meaning-critical condition; a later genuinely delayed unaided probe also fails. Learner B makes more correctable first-attempt errors on varied, source-valid practice and later succeeds unaided on the same target construct. Preserve assisted practice benefit and first-attempt quality, but do not prefer easy practice or award mastery from final coached correctness. A challenging variant that changes the assessed construct cannot be used as the comparison.
- **B — Challenge helps one learner but overloads another.** On counterbalanced equivalent practice for the same can-do, learner A shows repeated later independent improvement after a modest increase in linguistic novelty with optional non-answer-revealing prompts. Learner B repeatedly cannot complete first attempts at that challenge, requires answer-revealing models and shows no later independent gain; smaller steps plus scaffold fading yield better later transfer. Adapt choices provisionally in opposite directions with uncertainty, retain exploration and protect the unchanged eventual independent standard. Do not call repeated failure “productive struggle” by definition.
- **C — Apparent success is really a topic and item-exposure effect.** Harder-looking tasks use a learner's familiar music vocabulary and previously seen answers, while easier-looking tasks use unfamiliar administrative contexts. The apparent high-challenge winner disappears when topic familiarity, language burden, item novelty, rubric and order are counterbalanced. Keep soft interests separate from challenge; retain adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts and all required skills. A recycled item is not unseen transfer.
- **D — Accessibility, agency and task safety.** A learner uses source-permitted captions or an accessible input method; another requests a pause after several failed practice attempts; a third needs a missing prerequisite explicitly taught. Do not interpret accommodation use, slower response or a pause as lower competence, withhold accessibility support to intensify challenge, or force repeated failure to collect experimental data. Provide a valid smaller-step practice route or nonpunitive pause while keeping independent evidence pending. If no construct-equivalent assessment route exists, report `UNSUPPORTED`/`UNAVAILABLE`, not success.
- **E — Delayed reversal and protected coverage.** A provisional policy favors harder free-production practice after early independent gains, but later unaided retrieval falls while balanced review and mixed reading/writing practice improve transfer. Reduce confidence, reverse the provisional challenge/spacing choice and continue feasible alternatives. Do not crowd out required listening/speaking opportunities, due reviews, weaker can-dos or unfamiliar contexts merely because an easier or preferred skill produces a higher short-term score. Where a modality is not implemented, record its coverage gap.
- **F — Offline delivery, replay and rubric drift.** A challenging item is assigned but never displayed before the first response; a hint is delivered before a later revision but syncs afterward; a retry is uploaded twice; a new session reuses a local attempt ID; a rubric changes; and one delayed probe was offered but not completed. Replay trusted occurrence/**actual delivery** chronology with session-qualified IDs and immutable source versions. Preserve the original independent first attempt, classify cue-exposed revisions as assisted, count missing follow-up honestly, quarantine ambiguous exposure and reject unsupported cross-rubric equivalence. Receipt order must not change the resulting challenge hypothesis.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **CEFR source and evidence gates stay fixed.** For A–F output the exact versioned can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, modality, task, evaluator/rubric, permissible supports, independent requirements, delayed retrieval, unseen transfer, due-review and exit obligations. Practice difficulty may change; assessment construct, mastery threshold and required skill coverage may not. Missing source mapping or modality is `UNSUPPORTED`/`UNAVAILABLE`.
2. **Practice challenge is not final-answer accuracy.** In A, distinguish first unaided semantic performance, actual challenge and hint/model exposure, retries, assisted final accuracy, delayed unaided retrieval and unseen-context transfer. Equal polished final answers must not imply equal durable independent learning.
3. **Both underchallenge and overload are testable.** In B, a higher challenge is not always better: compare repeated, source-comparable later independent outcomes and meaningful error/assistance patterns. A persistent run of nonproductive failures triggers a safer, reversible teaching choice, not an invented psychological label or an automatic lowered mastery gate.
4. **Assigned difficulty is not delivered difficulty.** Across B/F, log available, selected, rendered and actually experienced challenge/scaffold options and answer exposure before each attempt. Undelivered assignments, missing telemetry and uncertain chronology must not masquerade as instructional evidence.
5. **Topic, exposure and order do not explain away method effects.** In C, counterbalance source-valid difficulty, topic familiarity, prior item exposure, order, evaluator and linguistic load; compare independent unseen transfer. Interest weighting remains soft, with adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar context rotation and no objective exclusions.
6. **Agency and accessibility are nonnegotiable.** In D, support valid accommodations, pauses, opt-outs and prerequisite repair without shaming or coercion. Neither slow responses nor accommodation use are failure proxies. A source-unsupported oral or accessible assessment route remains explicitly unavailable.
7. **Adaptation remains uncertain, reversible and balanced.** In B/E, preserve per-objective hypotheses, alternatives, delivered choices, uncertainty, feasible exploration, review debt and required receptive/productive opportunities. Contrary delayed evidence must be able to reverse challenge, scaffold and spacing choices; never assign a permanent challenge-tolerance or learning-style type.
8. **Event replay preserves evidence provenance.** In F, receipt-order permutations, duplicate attempts, reused local IDs, late hints, incomplete follow-ups and rubric changes yield identical first-attempt, assisted/independent, review and policy ledgers. Do not promote missing delayed evidence to a pass or retroactively erase cue exposure.
9. **No efficacy or release inflation.** For every history emit source/version IDs, practice/assessment equivalence map, challenge options and actual delivery, first/final attempts, errors, help exposure, eligible/completed/missing delayed probes, unseen transfer, topic/skill balance, provisional policy and uncertainty, and per-assertion `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED`. A green documentation QA is not an executable longitudinal evaluator, a shipped adaptive tutor or proof of improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

To claim an **executable fixture PASS**, publish the exact tested engine HEAD and command, injected seed/clock, immutable synthetic events and replay permutations, source-resolved CEFR task and rubric versions, valid practice-to-objective mapping, challenge and scaffold assignment/**delivery** records, first-attempt semantic outcomes, hint/retry exposure, independently completed delayed retrieval and unseen-context probes with eligible/completed/missing denominators, counterbalanced topic/item/difficulty/order, skill coverage, reversible policy trace and assertion-level verdicts. Unknown equivalence, exposure, missing follow-up or unsupported modality must stay visible as unknown or unsupported.

Any future claim that challenge adaptation improves **durable independent retention and transfer** requires separately reviewed longitudinal real-learner evidence against a credible balanced comparator, sufficient delayed unaided probes and unseen-context tasks, safeguards against repeated unproductive failure and accessibility harms, and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor offers a meaningful challenge, adjusts support when the evidence warrants it, and measures what the learner can later do alone.**
