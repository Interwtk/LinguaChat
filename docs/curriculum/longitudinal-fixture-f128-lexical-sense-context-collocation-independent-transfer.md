This document does **not** ship a word-sense-disambiguation engine, multilingual lexicon, CEFR mastery, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 protects genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 protect actual help chronology; F49 protects accessible delivery; F50 keeps interests nonexclusive; F104 isolates method from task difficulty; F107 guards unreliable tutor feedback; F117 protects language variety and register; F118 guards conditional/negation scope; F119 guards clarification; F120 guards evidence certainty; F126 guards speaker perspective. **Distinct construct:** can a learner infer, explain and independently use the **intended meaning of a word or short expression from its linguistic and communicative context**, rather than selecting a memorized translation or the most frequent dictionary sense? This includes sense selection, collocational constraints, domain/variety dependence, cross-language false-friend traps and justified clarification when the available context is insufficient. F128 does not equate unfamiliar vocabulary with a language-learning deficit or impose English lexical categories on other languages. Beginner tasks may use two explicit, concrete senses with supportive context; advanced ambiguity, figurative language and cross-variety distinctions require their own genuine CEFR-linked prerequisites.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — One surface word, two source-supported senses.** In an English-language fictional story, “We left our bags on the **bank** beside the river” means the sloping land at the water's edge; “The **bank** closes at four, so deposit the cheque now” refers to a financial institution. Learner A mechanically translates both as a financial institution; learner B uses the adjacent river/deposit context to select and paraphrase each intended sense. Counterfactual: change the first sentence's context to “We deposited our savings at the bank” while keeping the lexical span unchanged; the expected sense changes. Do not demand knowledge of cheques from an A1 learner unless the linked objective and prerequisites actually support it. The English example is not a universal multilingual equivalence claim.

**B — Collocation and communicative action.** In English, “**take a photo** of the sign” means capture an image; “**take a break** after class” means rest. Learner A infers that “take” always means physically carry away; learner B explains the two actions in context and produces a new natural request. Counterfactual: replace “photo” with “break” while holding the grammatical frame constant; a valid interpretation changes. Validate target-language natural collocations independently: a literal translation of “take” may be unnatural or misleading in another language. A synonym that preserves intended action may pass even if it differs from the fixture's preferred surface wording.

**C — Polysemy, register and domain boundaries.** Fictional English notices say “Please **charge** the tablet before the workshop” (supply electrical power) and “The club will **charge** a small entry fee” (request payment). Learner A confidently explains the first as a payment request; learner B identifies the action required in each context. Counterfactual: remove “tablet” and “entry fee” and present only “They will charge it”; without a validated preceding context the intended sense may be genuinely unresolved. Accept a targeted clarification and mark unsupported forced single-sense scoring; do not reward indiscriminate hedging when a reliable source makes the meaning clear. Different legal or technical uses of “charge” are not silently introduced into beginner prerequisites.

**D — Language-pair-specific false friend, not universal error.** In an independently validated **English-to-Spanish** fictional dialogue, “I'm **actually** free on Friday” communicates a correction or contrast such as “en realidad estoy libre el viernes,” not necessarily “actualmente estoy libre.” Learner A translates the English adverb as a Spanish temporal word regardless of context; learner B preserves the speaker's intended correction. Counterfactual: change the authoritative English utterance to “I'm **currently** free on Friday” in a validated context, and re-evaluate the intended time/contrast meaning rather than reusing the “actually” key. Do not mark “actualmente” universally wrong outside this specific contrast, and do not project this false-friend pair onto other source/target languages. Independently validate register, regional alternatives and any legitimate paraphrases.

**E — Delivered-stimulus fidelity, help chronology and replay.** An independently validated short dialogue contains enough surrounding text to disambiguate “bank,” but a cropped caption removes “beside the river”; an automatic translation picks the financial sense; a screen reader skips the preceding disambiguating sentence. Record authoritative and **actually rendered** versions and quarantine affected sense-selection evidence as UNSUPPORTED, retaining separately valid language evidence. At 10:00 a learner independently paraphrases the source correctly; a sense menu queued at 09:59 but **first rendered** at 10:01 does not contaminate the first attempt. A subsequent answer copied from the displayed menu is ASSISTED. Offline replay of the same immutable event ID must not create another first attempt; identical wording in a new genuine turn remains a distinct event. Do not infer a learning weakness from a defective delivery channel.

**F — Longitudinal method choice and genuinely unseen recipient transfer.** Fictional learner A performs well when copying word lists but later chooses the wrong sense in an unaided conversation. After **actually delivered** CONTEXT_CLUE_NOTICING on comparable CEFR tasks, A independently explains an unseen fictional transport notice containing a different ambiguous expression to a classmate after a meaningful delay. Learner B instead benefits from COLLOCATION_IN_SENTENCE on independently comparable new tasks. Compare baseline proficiency, target/source language and variety, lexical frequency and familiarity, number of candidate senses, contextual cue strength, collocation predictability, sentence length, domain, topic familiarity, modalities, accessible delivery, assistance exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If one method always receives familiar, richly cued phrases and the other rare words without context, mark METHOD_VOCABULARY_FAMILIARITY_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_CONTEXT_RICHNESS_CONFOUNDED and infer **no method winner**. Renaming characters while preserving the same ambiguous word, cue pattern and answer path is not genuinely unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups: missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed first-unaided outcomes may warrant **actually different next instruction**, with reversible uncertainty; contrary evidence lowers confidence. Keep protected skill balance, due reviews, alternative sampling and nonexclusive topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR, source and lexical linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored span/sense/context/collocation IDs, language/variety, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Beginner objectives never silently inherit advanced figurative or technical vocabulary.
2. **Context-sensitive sense binding.** A/C bind meaning to source-authored surrounding cues rather than a global one-word translation. Changing only authoritative disambiguating context changes the expected interpretation; cosmetic changes do not.
3. **Natural collocation and communicative purpose.** B/C distinguish what action an expression actually requests from literal component meanings; accept validated equivalent paraphrases and natural variety-specific constructions, not arbitrary word-for-word matches.
4. **Language-pair and variety-specific validity.** D validates false-friend contrasts only for the specified source/target pair, domain, register and varieties; acceptable alternatives are reviewed, and a target-language translation is never treated as an automatic lexical oracle.
5. **Uncertainty and warranted clarification.** C/E distinguish genuinely underspecified context from a learner's incorrect reading of clear context. A precise question or bounded interpretation is credited when the source lacks enough evidence; unearned certainty and gratuitous hedging are not.
6. **First-unaided chronology, accessible stimulus and replay.** E freezes source-effective and **actually delivered** captions, audio, translation, reading order, crop, learner answer and help render times. Queued/failed/post-answer hints do not retroactively contaminate a first attempt; copied models are assisted; replay is idempotent. A broken modality quarantines only affected constructs.
7. **Delayed retention and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningful separate learning opportunities and source-authored unfamiliar expressions, changed contextual cue structures and new communicative recipients, without answer-bearing help. Publish eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; relabeling the same lexical contrast does not count.
8. **Reversible adaptation, confounds and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** instruction, baseline lexical familiarity, cue strength, task complexity, topic familiarity, uncertainty, alternative exploration and contradictory outcomes. Interests increase example probability only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain available, and no required CEFR objective or protected skill is skipped.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/span/sense/context/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, delivered accessible artifacts, first/help/event timeline, validated sense/collocation oracle, counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI does not establish a shipped lexical evaluator, curricular defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independently validated language/variety-specific sources, senses, collocations and accessible equivalents; authoritative and actually delivered contexts; first-unaided evidence and real help chronology; delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen recipient transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded instructional alternatives; protected skills, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and source-context/collocation/false-friend/translation/caption/crop/screen-reader/version/replay counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Missing context, unvalidated variety, defective accessible delivery or absent follow-up leaves the affected claim **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not an invented learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive lexical instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis, and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners recover meaning from context and communicate it independently—not memorize one translation per word.**
