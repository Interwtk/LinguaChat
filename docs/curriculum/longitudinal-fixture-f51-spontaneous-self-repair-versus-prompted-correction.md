# F51 — Spontaneous self-repair is not the same evidence as prompted correction

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped correction classifier, CEFR certification, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F50 keeps explicit topic boundaries separate from interest weights. F51 isolates **learner-initiated, unaided self-monitoring and repair versus externally cued or assisted correction**. A learner may catch and correct their own error before feedback; that behavior can be pedagogically meaningful, but it must not rewrite the first-attempt trace or automatically prove durable mastery. Conversely, a polished answer after the tutor says “check the tense” is not spontaneous self-repair merely because the learner typed the replacement. Neither speed nor a permanent “self-correcting learner” label establishes instructional superiority.

## Controlled setup

Resolve an actual versioned CEFR can-do, source blueprint, item and rubric/evaluator before each scenario. Freeze the intended receptive/productive construct, scoring of revisions within an attempt, permitted preparation and feedback, independence criteria, delayed retrieval, transfer and exit obligations. **Do not invent a universal self-repair bonus, fluency penalty, allowed pause length or mastery threshold.** If the source rubric does not establish how a self-revised response is evaluated, preserve the trace and report `UNSUPPORTED` for the disputed scoring inference; never award unearned CEFR credit.

Use a deterministic clock/seed, immutable synthetic event stream across genuine sessions and a balanced instructional comparator. Model an attempt as an ordered, provenance-rich sequence of draft segments, corrections, explicit final submission and feedback exposures. Distinguish a correction made **before any linguistic cue** from one made after a model, hint, targeted follow-up, peer answer, transcript, autocorrect, evaluation marker or other content-bearing signal. A neutral invitation to continue may be distinct from an error-localizing prompt only when the source policy explicitly validates that distinction. Separate `attempt_first_observation`, `attempt_final_response`, `self_repair_event`, `cue_exposure`, `evaluation` and `later_independent_probe`; no retroactive overwrite of the first observation.

Capture pseudonymous learner, level-qualified objective and rubric versions, task/item/context and novelty, run/session/attempt/event IDs, trusted event occurrence and receipt order, draft and final boundaries, cue content/type and actual delivery time, revision span and initiator, any device/ASR/autocorrect transformation, first-attempt semantic quality, independent final quality, retries and scaffolds, eligible later independent retrieval and unseen transfer, review debt, missingness, instructional option and topic family. Treat optional latency as weak context-dependent evidence, not a universal clock-based cutoff. Do not store unnecessary sensitive voice recordings or infer diagnoses or fixed learning styles.

Predeclare `SPONTANEOUS_SELF_REPAIR`, `CUED_SELF_REPAIR`, `ASSISTED_CORRECTION`, `FIRST_OBSERVATION_PRESERVED`, `EXPOSURE_UNKNOWN`, `REVISION_ORIGIN_UNKNOWN`, `INDEPENDENT_PROBE_PENDING`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED`, `RUBRIC_MISMATCH`, `UNAVAILABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. Unknown cue provenance must never silently become proof of unaided repair.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Spontaneous correction before feedback.** In an otherwise independent, source-valid free-production task, the learner begins with an incorrect past-tense phrase, notices it and revises it without any tutor/peer/device linguistic cue before submitting. Preserve the original error, self-initiated revision and final answer as separate observations. Where the source rubric permits revision within one unaided attempt, evaluate the final response accordingly while retaining the first-observation diagnostic; do not manufacture a flawless first attempt or award delayed mastery.
- **B — Targeted prompt followed by an identical repair.** The learner makes the same initial error and final correction as A, but the tutor first says “check the verb tense” or highlights the incorrect segment. The correction is useful assisted practice, not spontaneous evidence, even if the learner supplies every corrected word. A generic praise message or post-submission feedback must be ordered by actual delivery, not by log upload time; ambiguous cues are `EXPOSURE_UNKNOWN`.
- **C — Streaming speech, recognition errors and accessible input.** A learner pauses, restarts and repairs a spoken utterance without a tutor cue; another transcript changes because ASR revised its hypothesis; a third correction comes from keyboard grammar/autocorrect suggestions. Do not count ASR instability or device-generated replacement as learner-initiated language monitoring. Use source-defined spoken/written construct maps and verified input provenance. If audio, turn boundaries or source-equivalence are unavailable, report `UNAVAILABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED`, not invented independent oral evidence.
- **D — Strong immediate repair, weak later retrieval.** A learner repeatedly self-repairs an objective during guided practice and seems fluent, but later makes the same error on a genuinely novel, unaided delayed probe. Retain self-monitoring as a useful signal while keeping independent retention unproven or negative as actually observed. Adapt explanation, scaffold fade, review spacing and error-targeted practice as reversible hypotheses; do not convert frequent repaired correctness into durable mastery.
- **E — Opposite learners and method reversal.** Two learners share a fixed CEFR can-do, rubric and comparable topic/difficulty. Learner A later transfers independently after concise feedback plus opportunities to self-monitor; learner B later transfers after a worked example and gradual scaffold fade. Counterbalance instructional choices and context familiarity; both continue balanced receptive/productive and unfamiliar-topic sampling. After further independent observations contradict the earlier ranking, decrease confidence and reverse a method preference where justified. Neither learner is permanently categorized by self-repair frequency.
- **F — Out-of-order offline correction and replay.** An initial draft is logged, an error-specific hint is delivered, the learner repairs, then the hint event arrives late; the correction is uploaded twice and a previous-session spontaneous repair shares a local attempt label. Deduplicate stable event IDs, use trusted occurrence/delivery chronology and session-qualified attempt IDs, quarantine ambiguous cue provenance and stale rubric versions, and preserve completion-only eligibility. Replaying the same events in two different arrival orders must yield identical first observations, cue classifications, independent evidence, review debt and policy hypotheses.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-specific scoring contract.** For A–F emit the exact versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, item, rubric/evaluator, response construct and source rules for within-attempt revision, independent credit, delayed retrieval and exit. A missing rule is `UNSUPPORTED`, never a made-up self-correction score.
2. **Original observation is immutable.** In A and B, preserve the initial error, exact revision sequence, final submission and feedback/cue timeline separately. Neither a correct final response nor a replay may rewrite first-observation quality to “correct on first attempt”.
3. **Spontaneous means no prior linguistic cue.** In A, classify learner-initiated repair as spontaneous only with verified pre-cue provenance. In B, targeted tutor prompts, highlighted errors, examples, peer answers or other delivered linguistic cues prevent the same repair from being counted as unaided self-monitoring. Do not infer spontaneity from learner authorship of the replacement text.
4. **Do not over-penalize valid independent revision.** When a source rubric allows an unaided within-attempt revision, retain its valid final-response evaluation without inventing a first-attempt bonus or arbitrary hesitation penalty. When the rubric is silent or the attempt boundary is uncertain, withhold disputed mastery claims and request curricular review.
5. **Input and construct integrity.** In C, distinguish learner speech/writing from ASR revisions, predictive text and assistive transformations; apply task-specific source-approved accessibility equivalence. Do not count unverified generated corrections as independent language production or confuse written and oral constructs.
6. **Self-repair is not delayed retention.** In D, spontaneous corrections and short-term polished responses may inform error diagnosis and instruction but cannot replace later eligible independent retrieval, unseen transfer or source-required review. Report eligible/completed/missing follow-ups and actual elapsed delay, without a calendar-boundary shortcut.
7. **Uncertain, revisable adaptation.** In E, method hypotheses change only from comparable repeated later independent outcomes; sample alternative methods, separate topic effects, preserve common CEFR requirements and respond to contradictory evidence. No fixed learning-style or “natural self-corrector” profile is inferred.
8. **Replay and cue chronology are deterministic.** In F, stable IDs, source-qualified objectives, actual delivery timestamps, session/attempt boundaries and rubric versions produce identical classifications, evidence ledgers and review obligations across duplicate/out-of-order replay. Unknown exposure or version mismatch fails closed rather than laundering assisted repair.
9. **Evidence and release honesty.** Across A–F output spontaneous/cued/assisted/unknown repair counts with provenance, first-observation and final-response quality, verified instructional choices, skill/topic coverage, independent later retrieval/transfer, missingness and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion. A fixture PASS on an exact engine HEAD is not proof of shipped support, CEFR mastery, causal instructional benefit or authorization to open levels.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, injected clock/seed, immutable event sequences in both replay orders, source blueprint and rubric versions, original/final attempt boundaries, actual delivered cues and revision origins, accessibility/ASR provenance, independent/assisted classification, policy choices with uncertainty, later independent retrieval and unseen transfer with eligible/completed/missing denominators, unchanged review/exit gates and per-assertion results. If cue order, source scoring or construct validity cannot be resolved, report `UNSUPPORTED` rather than force a PASS.

Any claim that teaching students to self-monitor improves durable learning requires separate reviewed longitudinal evidence with balanced instructional comparators, genuine unaided retention/transfer and ethically reviewed learner evaluation. **The tutor can value a learner's own repair without confusing it with unprompted first-attempt accuracy or lasting independent command.** Documentation alone does not implement a self-repair detector or validate a learning benefit.
