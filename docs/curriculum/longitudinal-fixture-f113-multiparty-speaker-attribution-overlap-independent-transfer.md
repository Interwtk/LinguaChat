## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Two overlapping claims are not consensus.** In a source-approved two-speaker meeting, coordinator Ana says “We can open at nine,” while technician Bo simultaneously says “The system **cannot** open before ten.” A learner hears a valid audio artifact with distinguishable speakers. Learner A reports that *both* approved nine; learner B independently attributes the conditional proposal to Ana and the safety restriction to Bo, then requests confirmation before announcing an opening time. A matched control has Bo explicitly agree to nine **after** the restriction is lifted, so a later confirmed nine is now source-valid. If overlap masks the negative and no authorized evidence resolves it, mark that binding unknown; never award certainty from a transcript alone.

**B — Role and authority are not acoustic identity.** A customer says “I approve the refund” while an authorized supervisor says “I need to review it first”; a third speaker is an interpreter relaying the supervisor's words. Learner A attributes the customer's approval to the supervisor and initiates an unauthorized action. Learner B distinguishes speaker, **role authority**, relay versus original commitment and the still-pending decision, or asks a legitimate clarification when a source cue is missing. A separate source world delegates authority explicitly to the customer, changing the valid outcome. A voice that sounds older, deeper, more confident or uses a prestigious accent does not confer authority.

**C — Machine diarization swaps speakers; source audio governs.** A source-valid group clip includes Lia saying “I sent the file” and Rui saying “I **haven't** sent mine.” ASR transcribes both sentences correctly but diarization swaps speaker labels; a second pipeline collapses both into one speaker. A learner independently attributes each claim correctly using authorized acoustic/context evidence. Do not mark the learner wrong because the transcript is mislabeled. Another learner sees a color-coded caption that reveals the correct speaker mapping **before** answering: accurate response may be assisted rather than independent audio attribution. If the source audio is missing and only conflicting machine labels remain, return UNSUPPORTED for the listening speaker-binding claim.

**D — Accessible alternatives and genuine ambiguity.** A task source has two similar-sounding voices talking over each other; even adjudicators cannot reliably identify who uttered “cancel it.” Learner A asks “Who requested cancellation?”; learner B confidently attributes it to a speaker without evidence. An authorized accessible transcript with source-verified role tags is supplied to another learner for an explicitly validated **reading/mediation** objective; it may assess that construct, not silently certify auditory speaker discrimination. A task-valid alternative with distinct source-authored verbal self-identification can preserve the intended listening goal **only when source-approved as equivalent**. Neither a hearing-related accommodation nor a valid accent/variety is a deficit.

**E — Fast segmented practice versus durable group-dialogue transfer.** Across matched fictional sessions, learner A initially excels after SOURCE_VALID_SPEAKER_TURN_SEGMENTS that make every voice obvious; learner B initially struggles in UNSEEN_GROUP_DIALOGUE. At a later distinct session, with new speakers, neutral contexts, equal intelligibility and unfamiliar but source-equivalent overlaps, A's first-unaided attribution and recipient action are worse than after a feasible alternative method; another learner shows the reverse. Match or disclose baseline, source difficulty, number of speakers, overlap burden, audio quality, speaker distinguishability, exposure dose, rendered help, modality/accessibility, topic familiarity and follow-up attrition. Record uncertainty and the **actually rendered** revised next method; do not declare a stable “group-listening learner” type or maximize coached segment scores.

**F — Delayed offline uploads and speaker-map version drift.** Two genuine offline sessions reuse a local utterance ID; a retry upload duplicates one attempt; the first learner response occurred before a tutor's role map was rendered but reaches the server afterward; a later assisted retry follows the rendered map; a regenerated audio URL has new speaker order while the cached clip uses an earlier artifact; and a later authorized correction changes the coordinator's decision. Use immutable session-qualified IDs, trusted occurrence/render chronology, source/audio/speaker-ledger/rubric hashes and effective source times. Deduplicate only true replays; never reconstruct old audio from the new URL, relabel earlier independence from packet arrival order, or rewrite an earlier correct source-time answer because of a later authorized change. Quarantine irreconcilable versions and preserve unknown follow-up as missing, not failure.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source and CEFR applicability.** A–F require actual versioned descriptor/can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored multiparty goal, speaker/role/authority map, approved acoustic or equivalent route, language variety, rubric/evaluator, independent threshold, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Without source-valid multiparty attribution obligations return NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED, not invented mastery.
2. **Speaker-to-claim binding with ambiguity.** A/B/D separate audible speaker, reported words, role, authority, polarity, modality and correction state. Two simultaneous statements do not imply consensus; an acoustically unresolved span cannot be assigned by confidence or last-speaker heuristics. Source-valid clarification can be the correct action.
3. **Channel and diarization attribution.** A/C/D distinguish actual authorized audible source from ASR/diarization/transcript guesses, overlap masking, playback issues and missing media. A machine speaker label is not ground truth; unsupported source attribution is not a learner-origin error.
4. **Recipient consequence and role authorization.** A/B/F judge whether a recipient should announce, delay, verify, refuse or act based on the source-authorized speaker's **actual** commitment and role. Preserve relay/interpreter status, source-effective corrections and legitimate uncertainty; fluent paraphrase alone cannot pass a consequential action.
5. **First unaided versus rendered help.** C/E/F retain source stimuli, source-approved introductions, tutor role maps, color-coded captions, answer-bearing transcripts, model phrases, feedback and learner response chronology. Assistance rendered before the relevant attribution affects its independence; later help never retroactively contaminates an earlier genuine first response; assigned but unseen help is not exposure.
6. **Consent, accessibility and construct fidelity.** B/C/D/F respect minimal audio retention, consent and approved adjudication, and never infer identity from a voiceprint or protected traits. Source-validated alternatives may satisfy their declared equivalent objective; reading role tags does not automatically fulfill a source-required auditory discrimination or live interaction construct.
7. **Delayed independent retention and unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningfully separated sessions/material, new source-authored speaker sets, contexts, roles and overlap patterns, and first-unaided recipient-facing outcomes. Distinguish repeated clip recognition and coached role mapping from unseen transfer; emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators and protect all due CEFR skills/reviews.
8. **Reversible method choice, topic breadth and replay invariance.** E/F log feasible versus assigned versus **actually delivered** teaching, comparison uncertainty, audio quality, speaker count, overlap difficulty, dose, source and topic confounds, alternative sampling and revised next choice. Preserve preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts without topic exclusion. Deduplicated retries, late uploads and audio/rubric changes cannot fabricate evidence or change historic assistance labels.
9. **Assertion-level reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit source/CEFR/rubric/evaluator hashes, audio/speaker-ledger provenance, consent/access, verified-versus-inferred speaker labels, audible overlap/ambiguity, actual cue exposure, first/assisted responses, recipient action, delayed/unseen denominators, replay/version permutations and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Synthetic documentation CI is not evidence of shipped diarization, CEFR mastery, improved human learning or a reproduced defect.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; deterministic clock/seed; real versioned CEFR-linked multiparty tasks with source-approved speaker/role/authority ledger; authorized consent-respecting audio or validated construct-equivalent access; independent speaker-turn/overlap adjudication and explicitly bounded ambiguity; actual transcript/caption/tutor-cue render chronology; first-unaided attribution and recipient decisions versus assisted retries; meaningfully delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen group-dialogue transfer; comparable or explicitly noncomparable methods, audio and topic conditions; protected skill/review/exit gates; eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators; and receipt/replay/version permutations with assertion-level verdicts. Without source-valid acoustic attribution, consent, trustworthy chronology or independent follow-up, the affected claim stays UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

A stronger claim that multiparty-aware adaptation **improves durable independent communication** requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator or ethically reviewed study, multilingual/variety and accessibility adjudication, measurement-error and attrition analysis, subgroup-harm checks and privacy safeguards. **A strong tutor teaches the learner to distinguish who actually committed to what, and to ask when a crowded conversation does not justify certainty.**
