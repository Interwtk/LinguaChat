# F203 — Clarification-question selection, underspecified requests and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific pragmatic, question-selection and recipient-purpose oracles, actual prompt delivery, first-authorship provenance, executable exact-head tests and genuinely delayed first-unaided unseen transfer bind to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a new CEFR objective, autonomous decision engine, safety policy, shipped adaptive tutor or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F193 distinguishes who received information; F199 repairs an expressed misunderstanding; F200 tracks conditions; F201 attributes conflicting claims; F202 summarizes information for a recipient. **Distinct construct:** when a request lacks information needed for an appropriate communicative response, can the learner decide *whether to ask, what single question matters most, to whom, and how to proceed after an answer*, without guessing or asking needless questions? The goal is language-mediated clarification, not teaching a universal one-question rule or allowing the tutor to make real-world decisions for the learner.

## Fixed objective, ambiguity and response oracles

Bind only applicable **existing versioned** CEFR can-dos for asking for clarification, checking instructions, negotiating meaning, making arrangements, requesting missing information or equivalent communication. Freeze objective ID/version, prerequisites, level-appropriate linguistic burden, target language/variety, scenario/rubric/oracle versions, accessible channel, help policy, meaningful-turn ceiling where applicable, first-unaided and delayed/unseen standards, thresholds, receptive/productive balance and exit gates. For an existing lower-level can-do, a short question such as “Which room?” can suffice; multi-constraint prioritization is NOT_APPLICABLE unless already supported by that can-do. No new graduation gate. Preserve the unresolved B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix disagreement; B2 remains unavailable.

An independently reviewed **ambiguity and consequence oracle** freezes the exact requester utterance, recipient and roles, task purpose, plausible interpretations, known versus missing facts, which missing fact changes the immediate response, and the cost of proceeding on an assumption. Distinguish genuinely missing decision-relevant information from merely optional preferences, facts already provided, unavailable facts and conflicting sources. Do not infer real-world consent, safety, authority or completion from a linguistically plausible answer. The oracle must state when a reasonable conditional reply or explicit assumption is acceptable, when a targeted clarification is necessary, and when a requester cannot resolve the missing fact. High-stakes scenarios require separately reviewed domain policy and are out of scope here.

A separate independently reviewed **clarification-response oracle** accepts natural, culturally appropriate and target-language/variety-specific ways to ask one or more relevant questions, with polite register appropriate to the relationship. It scores information gain and usefulness, not a magic phrase or fixed question count. “Which Friday?” may be better than asking for every event detail when only the date is blocking a reply. A bundled question can be appropriate when two inseparable details are required and the existing can-do/turn budget allows it. Do not reward a vague “Can you clarify?” when a precise question is feasible; do not penalize justified uncertainty or a concise conditional response. An answer actually received may remove the ambiguity; an unanswered question does not count as resolved.

The **actual-delivery and first-authorship ledger** records pseudonymous learner, stable session-qualified event/replay IDs, fixed objective/scenario/oracle/rubric versions, exact utterances and updates actually rendered, requester identity/role as presented, available constraints, first eligible unaided learner clarification or conditional reply, question specificity and information gap addressed, later answers actually delivered, help/examples/model questions actually rendered, assisted repairs, optional reliable latency, recurring error type, instructional choice/dose/confidence, accessible modality route, and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes. Unopened help is not exposure; rendered answer-bearing model questions are. Failed audio yields no listening credit; displayed text may support a separately bound reading route. Duplicates, copied revisions and midnight boundaries cannot create independent mastery or delayed retention.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Ask for the one missing fact that blocks a response.** A coordinator says “Please tell the guests which room the workshop is in,” but the learner has only the confirmed date and time, not a room assignment. Accept “Which room should I tell them?” or “I know the time, but which room is confirmed?” Reject “It's in Room 3” without evidence and “What time is it?” because the time is already given. If the coordinator replies “Room 5” and that answer is actually delivered, the learner can then relay Room 5. Merely asking does not prove the room was confirmed or the guests received the update.

**B — An optional preference does not justify blocking a useful answer.** A friend asks “Can you send me the address for the library meetup?” The address is already in a delivered notice; the friend did not ask about refreshments or seating. Accept providing the known address directly, perhaps with the already-known start time. Reject delaying the answer to ask “What snacks do you prefer?” or fabricating a new venue. A follow-up about wheelchair access may be legitimate if it is raised by the friend, but the fixture does not presume an accessibility need from identity or appearance.

**C — A conditional reply can preserve uncertainty without excessive questioning.** A teammate asks “Can you finish the slides by Friday?” The learner knows their own availability only if the missing dataset arrives Thursday. Accept “I can finish by Friday if the dataset arrives by Thursday; has it been sent?” or a concise conditional statement if the teammate cannot answer. Reject “Yes, they're finished” or a firm promise with no condition. The question should target the dataset, not irrelevant slide colors. An unanswered question remains unresolved; no task completion is inferred.

**D — Ask the person who can actually resolve the gap, with appropriate register.** A volunteer tells a visitor “The entrance might be north or east; the organizer will confirm.” The visitor asks the learner which entrance is final. Accept “The entrance isn't confirmed yet; could we check with the organizer?” and, when addressing the organizer, “Could you confirm whether visitors should use the north or east entrance?” Reject demanding that the visitor choose an entrance or treating the volunteer's speculation as official confirmation. A polite formal variant and a concise familiar variant may both satisfy the same language objective if the independent pragmatic oracle accepts them.

**E — First authorship, delivered answers, modality and replay matter.** At 10:01 the learner reads A and independently writes “Tell them Room 3.” Record FIRST_UNAIDED_QUESTION as absent and UNSUPPORTED_ASSUMPTION. At 10:02 a complete example question is rendered; at 10:03 the learner copies “Which room is confirmed?” Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED_REPAIR, not independent question-selection mastery. At 10:04 the same prompt is replayed and deduplicated. If an audio request fails but a transcript renders, do not award listening credit; a separately bound reading route may count. An unopened hint is not exposure. A later probe requires meaningful intervening time and a different ambiguity/role/consequence structure, not renamed rooms.

**F — Reversible method selection, contrary evidence and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly guesses a missing detail instead of asking; across comparable completed sessions ASK_VS_ASSUME_CONTRAST, INFORMATION_GAP_HIGHLIGHT and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated. Tentatively favor a method only when later first-unaided unseen clarification becomes more relevant under matched ambiguity, register, language burden, help, channel, topic and delay. Learner B initially asks excellent questions after a complete question template but later asks unnecessary questions when all essential facts are present; lower confidence, restore balanced exploration and test RELEVANT_VS_OPTIONAL_SORT. An easier prompt, friendly requester, extra hint, familiar gaming scenario or shorter delay is confounding, not proof of a winning method. Missing follow-up remains unknown. Gaming can softly weight an example, but adjacent sports, neutral libraries and unfamiliar volunteer settings remain available; required can-dos and modalities are never excluded.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Shared CEFR objective, unchanged evidence standard.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed ambiguity and response oracles, preserving prerequisites, linguistic burden, turn ceilings, thresholds, accessibility, skill coverage and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Target the actual information gap.** A asks for the unknown room, does not re-ask a supplied time and never invents a room or assumes that a request for information means the recipient received it.
3. **Do not over-clarify complete requests.** B answers using already available information and does not treat unrelated optional preferences as blockers.
4. **Conditionals and unanswered questions retain uncertainty.** C permits a bounded conditional response, targets the missing dataset and never turns a conditional plan or unanswered question into completion.
5. **Recipient, authority and register matter.** D routes verification to the organizer rather than an uninformed visitor, accepts independently reviewed appropriate registers and preserves unresolved status until an answer is actually delivered.
6. **First authorship, help and modality integrity.** E preserves initial guessing, rendered answer-bearing help, assisted copying, unopened hints, failed audio, possible text route and replay deduplication; no phantom listening or independent credit.
7. **Matched reversible instructional adaptation.** F compares actually delivered options with later independent clarification; ambiguity, consequence, register, help, channel, topic and delay confounds lower confidence and prevent causal-winner claims.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Require distinct genuine sessions, changed ambiguity and requester structures, first-unaided output, explicit missing follow-up, no script leakage, balanced receptive/productive coverage and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation; never skip objectives for interests.
9. **Honest evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic histories and generic CI are not executable clarification QA, a shipped tutor or proof of improved human retention. Require exact-head executable prompt/delivery/provenance/response checks, observed reversible teaching decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent outcomes against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic design histories, not observed learner behavior or executable tests. They specify how to select useful clarification questions and avoid both guessing and unnecessary interrogation. Remain UNSUPPORTED until independently implemented and verified.
