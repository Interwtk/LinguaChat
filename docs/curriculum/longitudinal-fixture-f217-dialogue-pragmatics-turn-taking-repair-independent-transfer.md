# F217 — Dialogue pragmatics, turn-taking, repair and independent transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic *cross-cutting* longitudinal curriculum acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until an independently reviewed executable exact-head harness tests the specified histories and actual decisions. This is **not** a shipped dialogue tutor, new CEFR can-do, conversational AI evaluation certification, proof of causal instructional superiority, or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F210 covers feedback timing and assistance; F212 covers invalid/ambiguous generated tasks; F213 covers learner agency; F215 covers code-switching; F216 covers unreliable speech/ASR evidence. **Distinct question in F217:** can a tutor teach *context-sensitive conversational action*—taking a relevant turn, requesting clarification, repairing misunderstanding, maintaining or appropriately closing an exchange—without mistaking the tutor's own leading turns, sociocultural assumptions, interface latency or a long friendly conversation for independent communicative mastery? A realistic dialogue may be engaging but still supply the learner with the answer.

## Fixed objective, rubric and pre-decision ledger

Bind each synthetic history to an **existing versioned CEFR can-do**, target language/accepted variety, independently reviewed task-specific communicative intent, role and interlocutor relationship, meaning-critical pragmatic/linguistic requirements, acceptable alternatives, required receptive/productive skill evidence, prerequisites, accessible supported channel, due-review and coverage debt, meaningful-turn limits where applicable, and unchanged mastery/exit standards. Illustrative English utterances below are **not** released lessons. Do not invent a generic politeness score, require a single accent or culturally preferred script, infer personality or nationality, or silently turn text-chat success into speaking/listening evidence. The B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix boundary remains **UNSUPPORTED**.

Freeze a pre-decision record containing pseudonymous learner, session/run/turn/event IDs, trusted occurrence order, objective/rubric/task/policy versions, conversational roles and legitimate ambiguity, available teaching options, expected language burden and difficulty, soft interest weights and context family, prior comparable first attempts, feedback and model exposure, independently assessed delayed retrieval/unseen transfer, confidence/uncertainty, missingness, accessibility/channel availability, due skill coverage and the **next actually delivered** option. Distinguish tutor **offered** turn from turn **actually rendered/heard**, learner first independent turn from post-prompt repair, semantic content from discourse function, and learner-initiated clarification from tutor-provided wording. Keep an explicit trace of every answer-bearing hint, completion, correction, transcript, translation, recast and suggested response before/after the learner's first attempt. A transcript or auto-completion that reveals the target is assistance even if the tutor calls it a “conversation.”

The evidence ledger must record offered → rendered → interlocutor prompt and context → first learner response or legitimate clarification → rubric/uncertainty → actual repair feedback or model → retries → conversational outcome → later distinct-session delayed unaided dialogue → genuinely unfamiliar interlocutor/context transfer → next delivered instruction. Include due/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators, comparable opportunity counts, stable event deduplication and quarantined unknown chronology. Missing learner response, dropped network turn, slow assistive typing, interrupted audio or absent interlocutor context is **UNKNOWN**, not failure or mastery. Latency is weak context-sensitive telemetry only when genuinely captured; it cannot independently establish hesitation, anxiety or competence.

Use a **reviewed meaning/function rubric**, not word-for-word matching. An appropriate clarification (“Do you mean Friday or Saturday?”) may be more competent than guessing. A direct answer may be appropriate in a close-friend context yet need a different register in a formal request; the context must be established rather than inferred from a name or accent. Conversational success is not a count of turns, compliments, tutor enthusiasm, or the tutor's own successful reformulation. Adapt scaffolding, feedback timing, role-play difficulty, pacing, examples, modalities and review spacing only from eligible evidence. Keep shared CEFR requirements, balanced skill coverage and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts; sample viable alternatives when uncertainty remains.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories and matched controls

**A — Clarifying a genuinely ambiguous request is competent communication.** An existing asking-for-information can-do places a learner in a text dialogue: “Can you move the meeting to next Friday?” The shared task context does not disambiguate which Friday. The learner independently asks “Do you mean this Friday or the one after?” The tutor's unsafe evaluator marks the response wrong because it expected a date. **Expected:** CLARIFICATION_VALID and FIRST_UNAIDED_TURN under a reviewed ambiguity-aware rubric; no invented date, no punitive failure and no automatic CEFR pass for unrelated language demands. Matched control: the interlocutor has already supplied a unique date, and a learner responds with a relevant independently formed confirmation; evaluate against that different but explicitly versioned context. A tutor-supplied “Ask which Friday” before the learner's turn makes the same clarification assisted, not clean independent evidence.

**B — A leading tutor cannot earn mastery for the learner.** An existing can-do requires independently making a polite request and negotiating a feasible alternative. In session 1 the learner says “I need another time.” The tutor then renders “Could you say: Would Thursday afternoon work instead?” The learner copies the sentence; the simulated interlocutor accepts, and the tutor congratulates the learner. **Expected:** preserve the first attempt and actual prompt exposure; label TUTOR_PROMPT_EXPOSED, ASSISTED_REPAIR and ANSWER_BEARING_RECAST, not independent mastery. In a later distinct session a new, nonleading interlocutor presents an unfamiliar schedule conflict and the learner independently proposes a suitable alternative: this may support CLEAN_DELAYED_DIALOGUE_RETRIEVAL and UNSEEN_INTERLOCUTOR_TRANSFER under the fixed rubric. Matched control: the tutor merely asks a neutral “What would you suggest?” without leaking wording; a genuinely self-produced response can be eligible if other independence conditions hold. Do not assume a neutral prompt makes a familiar repeated item “unseen.”

**C — Length, friendliness and tutor completion are not independent ability.** Two synthetic learners complete ten-turn role-plays for the same existing can-do. Learner A gives short but relevant independent replies and handles an unexpected clarification; learner B produces long, fluent-looking text only after the tutor autocompletes clauses and recasts each response. The unsafe policy ranks B higher by tokens, turn count and praise. **Expected:** score meaning/function and independence under the same rubric, count actual answer-bearing exposures, preserve the meaningful-turn ceiling and avoid inventing conversational proficiency from a verbose assisted transcript. Matched control: B later independently resolves an unfamiliar interaction without autocompletion; only that later evidence may change the tentative estimate. If a dialogue is cut off by the existing turn ceiling before the required function is elicited, retain incomplete/UNKNOWN evidence and coverage debt rather than declaring success or failure.

**D — Register and cultural variation require an established role, not a stereotype.** The same fixed communicative objective is assessed in two explicitly described relationships: a familiar peer asking to borrow notes, and a formal email-like request to an unfamiliar administrator. The learner says “Can you send me the notes?” in the peer context and “Could you please send me the form?” in the formal context. An unsafe tutor enforces one memorized polite phrase everywhere, penalizes a legitimate variety, or infers cultural background from a name. **Expected:** independently reviewed APPROPRIATE_REGISTER_REVIEWED and RUBRIC_VARIANTS_REVIEWED only when role/context and linguistic intent support that finding; acceptable variation is not an error. Matched control: remove the relationship description; register suitability is REGISTER_UNKNOWN until context is clarified, not a demographic guess. A meaning-critical rude or incoherent request may be flagged only under a documented task-specific rubric with reviewed alternatives. A role-play conducted by text cannot automatically certify oral pragmatics.

**E — Network, accessibility and topic changes must not change competence standards.** Two matched learner histories have the same objective, clean independent first turns, feedback and later transfer. In one, a slow screen reader and network buffering delay turn delivery; in the other, the interface is immediate. The unsafe policy interprets slower timing as poor turn-taking and narrows practice to the learner's favorite gaming scenario. **Expected:** distinguish learner-authored timestamps from rendered delivery and network latency, use LATENCY_NOT_COMPETENCE, avoid penalizing accessible interaction, preserve the same meaning/function rubric and skill obligations, and keep adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics eligible. Matched control changes **only** interest weights (gaming → cooking): examples may change but the instructional-method confidence, objective, assessment demands and balanced context rotation cannot. If speaking or listening is unavailable, do not fabricate corresponding skill evidence from text chat.

**F — Contradictory delayed conversations must revise actual instruction without double counting.** Session 1: an independently valid request in a familiar context; session 2: a model-assisted repair after a misunderstanding; session 3: a clean, unfamiliar interlocutor transfer success; session 4: a different unfamiliar dialogue with a meaning-critical unresolved misunderstanding. The offline upload order is 4, 2, duplicate 2, 1, 3; a fifth alleged success lacks trusted exposure chronology. **Expected:** EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED; quarantine session 5 as UNKNOWN; retain both clean later outcomes, meaningful-turn limits, missing follow-ups and coverage debt. Reconsider the hypothesis about whether brief recasts, clarification prompts or independent role-play better support later unaided transfer; log uncertainty, available alternatives, the selected option and its **actual rendered delivery**. A paired control reverses the clean delayed outcomes under matched difficulty/opportunities and requires reversible choice or justified balanced exploration, never a permanent “conversational learner” label. Because tasks, feedback and exposure can differ, neither synthetic history establishes a causal winner.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR and role-sensitive rubric:** A–F bind existing versioned can-dos, meaning-critical communicative functions, role/interlocutor context, acceptable target-language varieties, prerequisites, evidence modality, meaningful-turn ceiling and unchanged mastery/exit criteria. No new can-do, Pre-A1 change, B2 Arc 6 resolution or A1–C2 release.
2. **Clarification versus guessing:** A publishes ambiguity, first independent response and reviewed rubric outcome. Appropriate clarification can be valid evidence; a tutor-suggested clarification cannot be misrepresented as unaided performance.
3. **Prompt/recast provenance:** B preserves exact offered-versus-rendered prompts, first attempt, answer-bearing suggestions, model/recast exposure, assisted repair and later clean unfamiliar dialogue. A successful tutor-led negotiation does not automatically become learner mastery.
4. **Meaningful interaction, not engagement proxy:** C rejects raw turn count, length, praise, auto-completed text and artificial extra turns as independent CEFR evidence. Incomplete objectives retain visible UNKNOWN/debt.
5. **Role, variety and fairness:** D applies reviewed meaning/function and register criteria to explicitly known relationships, accepts legitimate variants, abstains where role is unknown, and never infers nationality, personality or culture from proxies.
6. **Access and channel integrity:** E distinguishes network/device/accessibility delays from learner competence, preserves accessible alternatives and skill-family evidence boundaries, and does not silently count text as speaking/listening.
7. **Separate soft interests from instructional adaptation:** Matched E controls alter topic probability only; fixed objective, rubric, difficulty, method confidence, skill coverage and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation remain auditable.
8. **Longitudinal replay, contradiction and actual decisions:** F publishes stable IDs, trusted occurrence order, duplicate/unknown handling, due/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators, first attempts, delayed and unseen dialogue outcomes, alternative options, uncertainty and **actually delivered** next instruction. Opposite later evidence must be able to revise a hypothesis or justify balanced sampling.
9. **Executable evidence and honest efficacy boundary:** Publish machine-readable A–F histories and matched controls, deterministic seed/clock, exact objective/rubric/policy/task/channel versions, independently reviewed semantic/pragmatic oracles, per-turn exposure and skill-evidence ledger, coverage/missingness and selection/delivery traces, and per-assertion PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED from an independently reviewed exact-head executable harness. Synthetic PASS demonstrates invariants only. Claims of improved durable human learning require a separate ethically reviewed longitudinal pilot versus a balanced accessible baseline, delayed unaided dialogue, genuinely unseen interlocutor/context transfer, missingness and fairness analyses, and no hidden loss in other required skills. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** F217 makes **independent pragmatic dialogue performance versus tutor-led conversational fluency** falsifiable. A strong tutor can guide, clarify and encourage, but must not confuse its own helpful words with what the learner can independently do in a later unfamiliar conversation. **UNSUPPORTED** until independently executed and reviewed; no real-world efficacy claim is made.
