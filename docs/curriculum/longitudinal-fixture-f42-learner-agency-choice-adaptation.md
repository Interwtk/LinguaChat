# F42 — Learner agency and activity choice cannot be mistaken for instructional efficacy

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped adaptive tutor, causal estimate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 is frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F23 separates topic from teaching method; F24 guards against policy-selection bias; F37 distinguishes assigned from delivered instruction; F39 considers outside teaching; F41 guards against selective reporting. F42 isolates **learner agency**: a good tutor may offer choices, accept an explicit request to change explanation, permit an accessible alternative or honor a temporary pause. The learner's choice is not a fixed learning style, and a self-selected option's outcome cannot automatically prove that option is more effective than alternatives the learner did not receive. Adaptation must respond to demonstrated later independent learning while preserving meaningful control, dignity, CEFR requirements and uncertainty.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to one **existing versioned CEFR can-do**, supported skill, prerequisites, frozen rubric and independent mastery/exit gate. Compare feasible instruction A (`concise_explanation`), B (`worked_example`) and the shared balanced comparator. Fix independent assessment demand, calibrated item families, context novelty, actual retrieval delay, due reviews, feasible skill opportunities and access constraints. Use an injected clock/seed, immutable synthetic ledger across distinct sessions/days, and a versioned `learner_agency_policy` whose allowed request/override/deferral states are declared before inspecting outcomes.

Log stable learner/objective/skill/session/run/event IDs; objective and rubric versions; policy version; eligible options and availability; system recommendation, displayed choices, learner request and timing, whether the choice was free, constrained or defaulted; selection mechanism and known propensity (**unknown stays unknown**); verified delivered exposure, duration and scaffolds; independent first attempt, retries/hints, actual delayed retrieval and unseen transfer; due-review/skill/context debt; missing follow-up and reason; event versus upload time; and whether an assessment was genuinely independent. Do not require an explanation for a learner's choice, infer protected traits, or record unnecessary sensitive reasons.

Distinguish `LEARNER_CHOICE`, `EXPLICIT_OVERRIDE`, `ACCESS_CONSTRAINED`, `TEMPORARY_DEFERRAL`, `PREFERENCE_ONLY`, `CHOICE_CONFOUNDED`, `UNDELIVERED`, `FOLLOWUP_UNKNOWN`, `NOT_COMPARABLE` and `UNSUPPORTED`. A choice may validly affect the **next activity** without becoming evidence that the selected method improves durable retention. Keep (1) learner autonomy and engagement, (2) actual delivered instruction, (3) independent mastery evidence and (4) uncertain method-effect hypothesis separate.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Expressed preference conflicts with later evidence.** A learner repeatedly asks for B because worked examples feel reassuring, but comparable verified A occasions yield stronger later unaided retrieval and unfamiliar free production. Respect a current explicit B request when feasible, offer noncoercive A opportunities later and revise only the uncertain learner/objective method hypothesis from valid independent outcomes. Never assign a permanent "worked-example learner" type or claim the learner's preference is false.
- **B — Self-selection masquerades as a winning method.** More prepared learners choose A and less prepared learners choose B. A has better raw delayed outcomes, but baseline readiness, choice availability and opportunity differ. Report who was offered what, who chose what and what was actually delivered. Flag `CHOICE_CONFOUNDED`/`NOT_COMPARABLE`; do not attribute A's raw advantage to teaching efficacy or invent randomization/propensity.
- **C — Accessibility and availability constraint.** A learner cannot use a proposed audio/speaking route in the current environment, and another explicitly requests text-only work. Where audio/speaking is unsupported, the option is `UNAVAILABLE`, never a refusal or poor listening performance. Offer genuinely supported text practice without inventing CEFR listening/speaking completion. Record the feasible choice set; no sensitive diagnosis is needed.
- **D — Temporary pause, refusal and later return.** A learner declines a difficult production task during one session, chooses a shorter explanation and returns for the required can-do later. Treat the deferred task as an outstanding obligation, not an incorrect independent answer, a permanent avoidance preference or proof of incapacity. Do not force the task in that session, silently graduate the learner, or bury it indefinitely; offer a reasonable later opportunity with normal scaffolds.
- **E — Informed option sampling and reversible choice.** A learner initially chooses A, later asks to try B, then chooses the balanced option. With comparable verified exposure and genuinely later independent unfamiliar probes, the learner/objective hypothesis changes direction. Log offered versus chosen versus delivered options and uncertainty; continue nonzero feasible alternative sampling, without making a mandatory experiment or claiming a causal winner.
- **F — Offline replay, misleading click and answer contamination.** A stale offline request for B arrives after a newer explicit A override; a duplicate click is replayed; an auto-selected B card was displayed but never started; a model answer appears before a purported independent probe. Stable IDs and trusted occurrence order preserve the actual request/delivery history. Do not overwrite the newer explicit choice, count a display/click as teaching exposure, double-count delivery or credit the answer-exposed probe as independent mastery. Quarantine ambiguous chronology without erasing valid learner evidence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Four distinct ledgers.** For A–F report versioned can-do/rubric, available/offered/recommended/requested/chosen/verified-delivered options, valid independent outcomes and method-hypothesis uncertainty separately. Choice, click, preference and delivered instruction are not synonyms.
2. **Autonomy without fixed labels.** In A, a feasible explicit learner request is respected without converting a subjective preference into a permanent style classification or a superior-method claim. Contradictory independent delayed/transfer outcomes can revise the method hypothesis while leaving the learner free to choose.
3. **Selection-bias disclosure.** In B, show baseline readiness, eligible/offered options, learner-choice mechanism, verified exposure and follow-up denominators. Mark self-selected unequal groups `CHOICE_CONFOUNDED`/`NOT_COMPARABLE`; do not manufacture known selection probabilities, missing counterfactuals or causal effects.
4. **Supported accessibility route only.** In C, availability constraints affect the offered feasible option set, not CEFR requirements or ability scores. Never record phantom audio/oral outcomes, infer disability or demand sensitive justification for an accessible choice.
5. **Deferral is not failure or completion.** In D, a refused/deferred task is neither a failed first attempt nor successful mastery. Preserve the due objective and review/skill obligations with a later accessible opportunity; no coercive immediate completion or indefinite silent omission.
6. **Revisable decisions and noncoercive exploration.** In E, record explicit choices, real delivery, independent calibrated later retrieval and unfamiliar transfer. Update an uncertain learner/objective hypothesis only with valid evidence; maintain feasible balanced alternatives without overriding explicit current requests to manufacture experiments.
7. **Replay and contamination integrity.** In F, deduplicate stable events, retain trusted event versus upload order and original choice/delivery versions, and quarantine unknown chronology or answer-bearing assessment exposure. Displayed, selected and actually delivered instructions remain distinct under both replay orders.
8. **Shared curriculum and interest boundary.** Across A–F preserve fixed can-do/prerequisites/rubric/independent mastery gates, due reviews, productive/receptive opportunities and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation. Interest context weights remain soft and independent of instructional agency; unsupported routes stay unavailable.
9. **Conservative release and efficacy claims.** Without comparable verified exposures, adequately observed alternatives, calibrated unaided delayed retrieval, unseen transfer, follow-up and an appropriate treatment of self-selection, mark method superiority `UNSUPPORTED`/`NOT_COMPARABLE`. Preserve legitimate mastery evidence and normal learner control. A synthetic PASS proves a software invariant only, not improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

For A–F publish the **exact tested engine HEAD**, executable command, deterministic seed/clock, full immutable synthetic ledger, real supported versioned can-do and frozen rubric, versioned agency policy, offered/eligible/selected/delivered choices, baseline/opportunity differences, assistance and first attempts, actual independent delayed and unseen-context probes, missingness and follow-up denominators, event/data-use lineage, replay invariance, due review/skill/context coverage, uncertainty and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Do not invent random assignment, selection probabilities, missing outcomes, unsupported modalities or protected-trait explanations.

An eventual claim that adaptive instruction improves durable independent learning requires separately reviewed longitudinal learner evidence against a credible balanced comparator, adequate independently delayed retrieval and unfamiliar transfer, uncertainty and heterogeneity analysis, plus an ethically sound learner pilot. Respecting learner choice is itself a product requirement, **not** evidence that a chosen method caused better learning.
