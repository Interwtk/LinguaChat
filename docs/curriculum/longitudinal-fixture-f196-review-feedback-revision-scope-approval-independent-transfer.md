# F196 — Review feedback, revision scope, approval conditions and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only synthetic longitudinal curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable exact-head checks bind independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific review-feedback oracles, source delivery, first-authorship provenance and genuinely delayed first-unaided unseen transfer to existing versioned CEFR can-dos. Not a shipped document editor, quality-assurance system, grading authority, universal workplace approval rule, new CEFR gate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F89/F164 cover contrastive corrections; F97 covers respectful refusal; F188 covers clarification; F189 covers source attribution; F191 covers partial answers; F193 covers information receipt; F194 covers proposal versus authorized decision; F195 covers task ownership and completion. **Distinct construct:** for a source-linked collaborative language task, the learner must understand and communicate **what feedback evaluates, which revision is merely suggested versus explicitly requested, what version and detail the feedback refers to, whether a change has actually been made, and whether approval is conditional, pending or explicitly granted**. A positive remark is not blanket approval; a requested edit is not proof of an edit; a revised artifact is not automatically approved. This tests communicative interpretation and response, not the aesthetic quality of the artifact. English examples are illustrative only.

## Fixed CEFR objective and independently reviewed review-status oracles

Bind only **existing versioned** CEFR interaction, mediation, reading, listening, writing, speaking or free-production can-dos whose source task genuinely involves understanding, requesting, responding to or summarizing feedback and revisions. Freeze can-do ID, prerequisites, level/turn ceiling, target language/variety, source/task and rubric/evaluator versions, accessibility route, help policy, first-independent and delayed/unseen evidence, thresholds, receptive/productive coverage and exit gates. At lower levels use only simple task-required exchanges (for example, “Please change the date” / “I changed the date”); do not add advanced editorial, organizational or legal approval concepts. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE. Preserve the unresolved B2 Arc 6 recognition-versus-open-retrieval blueprint/reuse-matrix disagreement, its 20-meaningful-turn ceiling and closed gate. Use fictional low-stakes library posters, student-club invitations and workshop leaflets; exclude regulated, medical, legal, financial, safety-critical, employment-discipline and private personal materials.

An independently reviewed **feedback-and-scope oracle** freezes each source utterance, speaker, addressee, channel, timestamp, artifact identity/version, referenced element, surrounding context, language/variety, exact force of the expression and any conditions. Separate PRAISE, OBSERVATION, SUGGESTION, EXPLICIT_REVISION_REQUEST, CLARIFICATION_REQUEST, REVIEWER_PREFERENCE, REVIEWER_OBJECTION, CONDITIONAL_APPROVAL, EXPLICIT_APPROVAL, REJECTION, AMBIGUOUS_FEEDBACK and UNKNOWN. “The heading looks good, but the date is wrong” endorses the heading while identifying a date problem; it does not reject the whole document. “Could the title be shorter?” may be a suggestion or a polite request depending on source context and locally reviewed conventions; do not universally equate grammatical interrogatives with optionality or mandatory instructions. A bare “Nice!” does not establish a formal sign-off. No mind-reading, cultural stereotypes or inferred private intentions.

A separate independently reviewed **revision-and-authorization oracle** freezes the source-visible current artifact/version, the requested change and its scope, any required reviewer or approval procedure **only if explicitly established by the source**, whether the revision was claimed, visibly made, received, reviewed or approved, and any unresolved conflict between reviewers. Distinguish REVISION_REQUESTED, REVISION_PROPOSED, REVISION_CLAIMED, REVISION_VISIBLE, REVISION_RECEIVED, REVIEW_PENDING, APPROVED_FOR_SPECIFIC_VERSION and APPROVAL_RULE_UNKNOWN. If Nora approves version 2, do not transfer approval to a materially changed version 3. If two reviewers disagree and no decision rule is given, communicate the disagreement rather than inventing whose preference wins. “I updated the date” is a sender's claim until the revised artifact is source-visible; “I saw the update” confirms visibility, not necessarily approval.

A **recipient-action oracle** freezes the actual communicative purpose: acknowledge a precise suggestion, request only necessary clarification, summarize requested edits, report the status of an actual revision, ask a source-authorized reviewer for a pending decision, or courteously explain a justified constraint. Accept concise natural variety-appropriate alternatives and warranted uncertainty. Do not require a lengthy response when a simple accurate acknowledgment suffices; do not force an unnecessary second approval after source-valid final sign-off.

An **actual-delivery and first-authorship ledger** records pseudonymous learner, fixed can-do/task/oracle/rubric versions, immutable session-qualified event IDs with replay deduplication, source messages and artifact versions actually displayed, audio/captions/transcripts and accessible route actually rendered, tutor examples/hints/answer-bearing revision checklists actually displayed, first eligible unaided recipient-facing response, assisted revisions, error types, optional reliable latency, topic familiarity, delivered instructional option/dose and confidence, and due/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes. A hint that was offered but never opened is not exposure; a rendered complete “edits required / approval pending” table is answer-bearing assistance. Failed audio earns no independent listening; captions may count only for a valid reading/mediation route. Replaying a source, renaming reviewers or crossing midnight cannot generate a second independent attempt or a genuine delayed transfer observation.

Task-local labels: CEFR_OBJECTIVE_BOUND, FEEDBACK_ORACLE_REVIEWED, REVISION_ORACLE_REVIEWED, PRAISE_NOT_BLANKET_APPROVAL, SUGGESTION_SCOPE_PRESERVED, REQUEST_FORCE_CONTEXT_BOUND, REQUEST_NOT_IMPLEMENTATION, VERSION_IDENTITY_PRESERVED, REVISION_CLAIM_NOT_VISIBLE_PROOF, VISIBLE_EDIT_NOT_APPROVAL, CONDITIONAL_APPROVAL_PENDING, EXPLICIT_VERSION_APPROVAL, APPROVAL_RULE_UNKNOWN, CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED, JUSTIFIED_CLARIFICATION, UNNECESSARY_CONFIRMATION, FIRST_UNAIDED_RESPONSE, ACTUAL_SOURCE_RENDERED, ACTUALLY_RENDERED_HELP, ANSWER_EXPOSURE, ASSISTED, EVENT_REPLAY_DEDUPED, DELAYED_UNAIDED_RETRIEVAL, UNSEEN_UNAIDED_TRANSFER, SAME_SCRIPT_LEAKAGE, METHOD_TASK_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_TOPIC_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_HELP_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_VERSION_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_AUTHORITY_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_CHANNEL_CONFOUNDED, METHOD_DELAY_CONFOUNDED, FOLLOWUP_MISSING, NOT_APPLICABLE, UNSUPPORTED. ## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Positive feedback and one correction are not blanket approval.** A fictional student-club poster reviewer Nora writes, “The heading looks good, but the event date should be Friday, not Thursday.” The learner must update Maya. Accept “Nora likes the heading but asked us to correct the date to Friday; the updated poster has not yet been approved.” Reject “Nora approved the whole poster” and “Nora rejected the heading.” A later explicit “I approve poster version 2 with Friday's date” changes the status for **version 2 only**, provided that exact approval and version were actually delivered. The initial cautious report remains correct for its original evidence window.

**B — A suggestion, a source-required change and a clarification need distinct replies.** Theo says “Maybe make the title shorter”; the source gives no mandatory edit rule. Maya separately writes “Please replace Room 4 with Room 5 on the invitation.” Accept “Theo suggested a shorter title; Maya requested a room-number correction.” Reject “Both edits have already been completed” and “Theo formally rejected the title.” In a target-language variety where the polite question “Could you change Room 4 to Room 5?” functions as a clear request in context, the oracle accepts that request interpretation; it must not mechanically classify every question as optional. If two rooms appear and the target of “change it” is unclear, accept one targeted clarification instead of guessing.

**C — A claimed change, visible revision and review are different events.** Maya writes “I fixed the room number and sent the new poster.” No new artifact is visible. Accept “Maya says she corrected and sent it; we haven't checked the new poster.” Reject “The revised version has been independently verified and approved.” A later visible version 2 indeed shows Room 5; record REVISION_VISIBLE, not approval. Nora then says “I received version 2, but I haven't reviewed it yet”: record receipt and pending review. Only a later explicit source-valid approval can establish approval for that version. A still-unresolved event booking cannot be inferred complete from poster approval.

**D — Reviewer disagreement and changed versions do not create invented authority.** Nora comments on version 2, “Keep the blue background.” Theo says “I prefer green.” The task provides no final design decision rule. The learner must brief Maya. Accept “Nora wants blue and Theo prefers green; we need to confirm who decides before changing the approved design.” Reject “The majority chose green,” “Theo has final authority,” or “Both reviewers approved green.” In a different source-authored continuation, the designated coordinator explicitly selects blue for version 2; a concise recap may report that choice without needless re-voting. If version 3 later changes the background, do not silently carry version-2 approval to version 3.

**E — Displayed answer, missing audio and event replay cannot earn independent mastery.** At 09:01 the learner reads Nora's real comments with no answer model. At 09:02 the tutor displays a complete checklist: “Heading praised; date correction requested; approval pending.” At 09:03 the learner copies it into a message. Record ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED; preserve any earlier eligible unaided response, but do not award independent mastery for the copy. Replaying the same event at 09:04 cannot produce another first attempt. In a separate listening variant playback fails and only captions render: no independent listening credit; a bound reading/mediation route may be evaluated only if valid. An unopened hint is not exposure. Delayed independent transfer requires a later genuinely distinct session, unseen comments/artifact/version and recipient, and no answer-bearing help.

**F — Longitudinal method selection, counterevidence and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly treats “Looks good overall, but please change the date” as unconditional approval. Across comparable real sessions, PRAISE_VS_REQUEST_CONTRAST, REVISION_VERSION_LEDGER and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually delivered and alternated. Cautiously favor an option only when later unseen first-unaided messages better preserve feedback scope, requested changes and approval status under matched task complexity, reviewer roles, language burden, topic, help, channel and delay. Learner B initially benefits from a version ledger but later overcorrects by refusing to recognize an explicit approval of the correct version; new independent errors lower confidence and restore balanced sampling or targeted contrast. If one method used shorter feedback, a visible answer checklist, familiar music posters, a clearly authorized reviewer or shorter delays, mark confounding and **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up is unknown, not failed retention. Music interests may softly weight examples; neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops and all required CEFR objectives/modalities remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed source-defined CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos, independently reviewed feedback/revision/recipient oracles and actual source delivery. Preserve prerequisites, target-language variety, level-specific turn ceilings, rubrics, thresholds, required skills and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Praise and correction scope.** A distinguishes positive feedback on one element from a correction request and whole-artifact approval; neither false blanket approval nor false rejection earns credit.
3. **Suggestion, request and ambiguity.** B preserves source- and variety-sensitive speech-act force, the exact requested element and justified clarification without treating every polite question as optional or every suggestion as binding.
4. **Revision evidence and approval.** C separates claimed edit/sending, source-visible revision, recipient receipt, pending review, explicit version-specific approval and unrelated event status.
5. **Conflicting reviewers and version changes.** D does not invent authority, majority rule or consensus, preserves disagreement and source-authored decisions, and never silently transfers approval to a materially changed version.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E distinguishes rendered sources, answer-bearing help, unopened hints, failed audio, captions, first unaided response, assisted copying and replay; no phantom listening or assisted independent mastery.
7. **Matched reversible adaptation.** F updates uncertain method hypotheses only when comparable actually delivered alternatives support stronger later first-unaided transfer; contradictory, sparse or confounded evidence lowers confidence and preserves exploration.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and soft interests.** Require genuinely distinct sessions, independently reviewed new review/recipient/version combinations, unaided responses, explicit missing follow-up, balanced receptive/productive coverage and nonexclusive preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar contexts. Interests never skip required CEFR objectives.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable review-feedback QA, shipped adaptive tutoring or proof of human-learning gains. Require exact-head executable source/delivery/response/provenance tests, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should teach the learner to communicate exactly what feedback asks for, what revision evidence exists and whether a particular version has actually been approved, then independently transfer that distinction to unfamiliar review conversations. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
