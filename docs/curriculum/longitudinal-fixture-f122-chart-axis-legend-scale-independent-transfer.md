## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Legend/series binding rather than value spotting.** A fictional library chart plots books borrowed by Group A (solid line) and Group B (dashed line) across Monday/Tuesday. At Tuesday, A=12 and B=8. Asked “How many books did Group B borrow on Tuesday?”, learner A says “12” after following the correct day but the wrong series; learner B says “8” and identifies the dashed line. A counterfactual swaps the solid/dashed legend mapping while keeping plotted coordinates, question and objective unchanged: the expected answer changes. If color alone is used in the original, independently validate a shape/line-style and accessible equivalent. Do not reward a correct value found on the wrong series or infer an immutable “visual learner” deficit.

**B — Truncated origin, tick intervals and unsupported precision.** A fictional bar chart shows Room A=48 and Room B=50 visitors, with a vertical axis starting at 45 and ticks every 1. A learner describes B as “many times larger” solely because its bar looks much taller; another says “B has two more visitors”. Source-valid communication preserves the numeric difference and does not confuse displayed bar height with the measured magnitude. Counterfactual: redraw the **same data** with a zero origin; the valid numeric answer must stay unchanged. In a separate chart with unlabeled/interpolated values, an answer that asserts an exact 48.37 from pixels is UNSUPPORTED_PRECISION. Distinguish the chart-language construct from an advanced arithmetic requirement not present in the CEFR task.

**C — Units, rate/absolute counts and missing values.** Two fictional venues show visitors **per day** in one chart and visitors **per week** in another; an annotation says one week has no reported data. Asked “Which venue received more visitors on Tuesday?”, learner A compares a weekly total with a daily value and claims a daily winner; learner B notices the unit mismatch and asks for the relevant daily data. A counterfactual changes a source-validated “per week” label to “per day” while preserving the plotted values: the acceptable comparison changes accordingly. Blank/no-report is not zero. Where a source author provides a validated conversion and the level permits it, accept an appropriately qualified comparison; otherwise preserve bounded uncertainty.

**D — Trend, observation and forecast.** A fictional plant-growth line chart has measured heights for weeks 1–3 and a dotted **forecast** for week 4. The task asks what was observed in week 4. A learner reporting the dotted point as an observed measurement is wrong; a learner stating “Week 4 is only predicted; no observation is shown” is source-valid. Counterfactual: the source changes the week-4 point to a measured observation with an authoritative legend update; the answer must change. Do not teach that every rising line establishes a cause, or that a dotted line always means forecast without the chart's own legend.

**E — Render/accessibility corruption versus learner difficulty.** The authoritative chart has two series, but a cropped phone view hides the legend; a translation changes “thousands” to “units”; OCR moves an x-axis category label to a different tick; and a color-only rendering is inaccessible to a learner who needs a validated noncolor representation. Capture the actual rendered stimulus and its timestamp. Quarantine affected error attribution as RENDER_CROP, TRANSLATED_UNIT_CONFLICT, OCR_AXIS_CONFLICT or inaccessible representation rather than diagnosing deficient interpretation. A validated text/audio description that faithfully binds categories, values, series and units can supply equivalent **communicative comprehension** evidence when the task permits, but not retroactively claim unaided visual chart decoding. An alternative screen-reader table must be independently validated against the same effective chart data, not assumed equivalent.

**F — Reversible adaptation, source versions, replay and unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A gets assisted chart questions right after axis highlighting but fails delayed first-unaided comparisons; later A improves on genuinely different charts after **actually delivered** AXIS_LABEL_CHECK. Learner B instead improves after LEGEND_SERIES_CONTRAST on comparable charts. Compare baseline, CEFR objective, language/variety, chart type, number of series, axis complexity, scale, unit, accessible route, topic familiarity, assistance exposure, review interval and follow-up completion. If axis instruction is always paired with simple single-series charts and legend instruction with dense multi-series charts, label METHOD_CHART_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no** winner. Source v1 shows a fictional Friday attendance count of 18; source v2 corrects it to 16: keep answers scored against the effective source version. Deduplicate replayed offline attempts by session-qualified event ID; identical local chart IDs across versions are not globally identical. Tutor annotation rendered **after** first response cannot contaminate that earlier first attempt, but a copied later answer is assisted. Merely recoloring an identical chart with identical question/answer path is not unseen transfer; a new source-authored chart with different scale, series binding, question purpose and distractors can qualify after a meaningful interval. Missing follow-up is FOLLOWUP_MISSING, never fabricated success or failure. With sufficient comparable repeated outcomes, the next **actually delivered** method can differ for A and B; contradictory outcomes reduce confidence and allow reversal. Keep due reviews, skill balance, alternative exploration and neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Versioned CEFR and authoritative chart linkage.** A–F resolve real descriptor, blueprint, prerequisites, source-effective chart and underlying values, source-approved language/variety interpretations, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accessible equivalence, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No fabricated advanced numeracy prerequisite.
2. **Axis, legend and category binding.** A/B require correct series, category, axis origin, tick intervals and question-target alignment; changing source-authoritative legend or ticks changes the expected interpretation where appropriate, while merely restyling the same values does not.
3. **Units, scale, missingness and warranted claims.** B/C distinguish chart-height illusion from numeric difference, daily versus weekly units, reported versus absent values and warranted approximation versus invented precision. Preserve source-valid clarification/uncertainty.
4. **Observed, forecast and causal scope.** D keeps predictions separate from observations and does not infer causes from visual trends. Source-authoritative legend changes must propagate to the correct expected answer.
5. **Accessible, multilingual and rendered-stimulus validity.** A/C/E validate language-specific labels, noncolor encodings, reading order and source-equivalent text/audio/tactile alternatives. Crop, OCR, translation or inaccessible display conflicts quarantine affected learner-deficit claims.
6. **First-unaided and source/event provenance.** E/F bind each answer to source-effective version, chart-qualified element IDs and actual rendered assistance chronology; queued/post-answer help is not retroactive assistance; duplicate uploads do not double count evidence.
7. **Delayed independent retention and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningful time/intervening learning, a new source-authored chart with noncosmetic scale/series/question/distractor variation, no answer-bearing help and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Recoloring or label renaming alone is insufficient.
8. **Reversible method choice and soft topic rotation.** F records feasible, assigned and actually delivered methods, comparability, uncertainty, exploration, conflicting evidence, skill balance, due reviews and diverse topic contexts. Neither interest nor a fixed “visual learner” type may gate CEFR objectives.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/chart/source/rubric/evaluator hashes, authoritative and rendered representations, first/assisted answers, render/event ledger, counterfactuals, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Docs-only CI is not an executable chart evaluator, reproducible product defect or evidence of learning gains.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires named exact engine HEAD and runnable command; real versioned CEFR-linked tasks; independently validated multilingual chart data/encoding, scale, units and accessible equivalents; source-effective versions and actually delivered stimuli; first-unaided evidence; meaningful delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen chart-to-communication transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded instructional methods; preserved protected skill/review/exit obligations; eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators; render/OCR/translation/scale/legend/version/replay counterfactuals; and per-assertion verdicts. Missing source validation, unreliable rendering, unavailable modality or absent follow-up makes affected claims **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**; preserve independently established partial communication evidence.

Any stronger claim that adaptive chart interpretation **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, accessibility-validated multilingual real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparison, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor teaches the learner to explain what the chart actually communicates, not merely to read a tall bar or repeat a highlighted number.**
