## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Mass nouns, count nouns and useful partitives.** In a fictional English-language volunteer supply request, the reviewed intended message is **“We need some advice and three pieces of information.”** Learner A writes *three informations* and *an advice* in the reviewed standard-register context after assuming all conceptual items take ordinary count plural; learner B independently writes **“Could you send me three pieces of information and some advice?”** The oracle judges the message in its **reviewed target variety/register**, not by English rules projected onto other languages. Counterfactual: replace **information** with **messages**; “three messages” becomes natural, but does not establish the same countability for “information.” An article/partitive chart rendered before authorship makes the response assisted. Independent successful communication matters more than identifying “uncountable” on a quiz.

**B — Substance, serving, kind and contextual coercion.** In a fictional café dialogue, **“Two coffees, please”** is a reviewed natural request for **two servings**, while **“There is coffee on the floor”** denotes substance. Learner A marks “two coffees” always wrong or infers an exact volume; learner B independently confirms **“Two coffees—what sizes would you like?”** Counterfactual: in a warehouse request for **two kinds of coffee**, “two coffees” may denote varieties under a separately reviewed context. The oracle must preserve the difference between portions, types and substance, and refuse to infer serving size or total volume without a stated measure. A picture showing two cups before the response is potentially a delivered scaffold, not independent evidence.

**C — Container versus content and underdetermined quantities.** A fictional pantry note says **“Bring two bottles of water and one bag of rice.”** Learner A reports **“two liters of water and one kilogram of rice”** although no bottle or bag sizes were supplied; learner B independently requests **“What size bottles and what size bag?”** Counterfactual: append **“Each bottle holds 500 mL and the bag is 1 kg.”** Only then may the recipient calculate one liter of water and one kilogram of rice (assuming the bottles are filled as specified). Preserve the actual task's language and numeric notation. No measurement-conversion proficiency should be inferred from simply repeating a memorized phrase, and no unstated package size may be invented.

**D — Language-specific classifier and agreement boundaries.** In a fictional Japanese-language classroom request, the independently reviewed oracle contrasts a conventional **hon**-counter construction for long cylindrical objects (e.g., pencils) with an inappropriate substitution of a generic item counter **in the specified context**. Another separately reviewed Spanish task checks determiner/noun agreement in **“dos botellas pequeñas de agua”**; the plural adjective agrees with **botellas**, not with the mass noun **agua**. Learner A mechanically copies English “two waters” or universalizes a Japanese classifier into Spanish; learner B independently uses each language's **own reviewed** expression for its recipient. Counterfactual: change the referent, dialect, colloquial setting or noun sense; a reviewer must re-evaluate acceptable classifiers/agreements rather than freezing one global rule. Do not assert that every target language requires classifiers or that one classifier is correct across all objects and varieties.

**E — Delivered quantity distortions, authorship timing and accessibility.** The authoritative instruction says **“three pieces of information”**, but a caption drops **three**, ASR substitutes **thirteen**, a translation removes the partitive, an inaccessible graphic alone supplies the container size, or autocorrect silently rewrites a learner's quantity. Log the **actually delivered** stimulus and authorship history. A learner response authored at 10:00 is FIRST_UNAIDED when an example was queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01; a revision after seeing the example is ASSISTED. If the delivered caption/audio changes the target quantity, quarantine the affected quantity judgment as **UNSUPPORTED**, not learner failure. Offer accessible equivalent quantities without leaking the answer before a first-unaided probe. Replayed duplicate events must not manufacture extra independent attempts, review completions or improvement.

**F — Longitudinal adaptation, matched alternatives and unseen transfer.** Fictional learner A scores immediately after **DETERMINER_AGREEMENT_CONTRAST** but repeatedly invents package sizes on delayed unaided requests. Comparable, **actually delivered** **CONTAINER_AND_PARTITIVE_PARAPHRASE** and **AMBIGUITY_CLARIFICATION_DIALOGUE** trials are followed by a genuinely new supply request; A independently asks for missing size information. Learner B instead improves delayed independently written requests after **REAL_CONTEXT_FIRST** and **LEARNER_GENERATED_ORDER**. Compare baseline number competence, lexical familiarity, item sense, quantity magnitude, portion-context explicitness, modality, objective, review delay and opportunity for follow-up. If one method received easier café servings and another ambiguous warehouse packaging, flag METHOD_PORTION_CONTEXT_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Changing “two bottles” to “three bottles” with the same sentence template and recipient is **not** sufficient unseen transfer; require a new lexical/communicative demand with a reviewed oracle. Track eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed probes; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable independent delayed evidence may support a **subsequent observed change in actually delivered teaching**, with uncertainty, ongoing alternatives and reversibility. Food/travel/game interests may influence examples but cannot exclude neutral, adjacent and unfamiliar contexts or any required CEFR objective.
