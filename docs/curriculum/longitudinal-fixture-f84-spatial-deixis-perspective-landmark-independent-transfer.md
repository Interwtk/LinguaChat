## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — “On your left” changes with viewpoint.** A source-defined wayfinding exchange places two people facing opposite directions in the same corridor. Learner A repeats the speaker's “left” as if it were the listener's left and sends the listener to the wrong door. Learner B independently asks which way the listener faces or supplies the correct addressee-relative turn using source-validated expressions. If the orientation is genuinely unknown, accept a task-permitted clarification rather than inventing a heading. Do not treat choosing the correct door by chance as proof of comprehension.
- **B — Landmark relation versus geographic expertise.** A source-defined instruction distinguishes “the desk behind the reception counter” from “the desk beside the counter,” with landmark identities and a validated frame. Learner A repeats a fluent instruction but swaps the relation; learner B independently identifies the intended desk and communicates a usable relation in an accepted target-language variety. If a floor plan or landmark identity is absent, keep it unknown. F71's pronoun/reference resolution alone does not establish spatial relation competence.
- **C — A perspective shift and an updated route.** A learner first independently gives a correct route from the entrance. The task then switches the addressee to a second entrance with a different orientation and provides an authoritative source update that closes a corridor. Learner A repeats the first route; learner B independently reframes the directions from the new starting point and avoids the closed corridor. If the tutor already displayed the completed revised route before the first replan, the replan is **assisted**, even when that cue is uploaded afterward. A permitted interlocutor asking “Which entrance are you at?” is not automatically coaching.
- **D — Immediate diagram advantage reverses on delayed unseen tasks.** On matched first unaided tasks, `VIEWPOINT_CONTRAST` appears more effective than `LANDMARK_DIALOGUE_REHEARSAL` for one learner. After meaningful delay, genuinely new spatial instructions with changed orientations and landmarks yield stronger independent outcomes after dialogue rehearsal; the diagram advantage disappears. Match or disclose number of turns, frame complexity, landmark salience, reading load, modality, accessibility, language variety, topic familiarity, practice dose, feedback and rubric. Reduce confidence, explore alternatives and record the **actually rendered** next method; do not label the learner a permanent diagram learner.
- **E — Favorite maps do not replace broad communicative ability.** A learner successfully directs a character around a familiar game map but miscommunicates a neutral library location and cannot independently describe an unfamiliar public-building route. A second learner can independently use an accessible text-based landmark description but has no valid audio/oral evidence; a visual-only diagram was inaccessible. Rotate favorite, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts while preserving due reviews and all source-required skill evidence. Do not count inaccessible visual failure as linguistic deficiency, text performance as proven speaking, or an answer-bearing caption as unaided success.
- **F — Offline replay, conflicting frames and missing follow-up.** Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; a device clock drifts; an answer-bearing arrow was **rendered** before the learner's first instruction but uploaded later; two source versions disagree about the landmark's location; a rubric revision changes accepted relative-frame readings; and a delayed unseen probe is offered but never completed. Deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, freeze source/rubric versions, use trusted render chronology, quarantine irreconcilable source/frame conflicts and keep follow-up unknown. Receipt-order permutations must preserve identical assistance labels, spatial state, review debt and next-method rationale.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid spatial construct before scoring.** For A–F emit versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, task/outcome, target language/variety, source spatial facts, accepted frames/readings, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, independent threshold, delayed/unseen probes, due reviews and exit gate. Missing mapping, invented map/geometry requirements or unsupported frame yields `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Perspective and reference-frame meaning.** In A/C/F distinguish speaker-relative, listener-relative, object-relative and source-valid absolute frames where applicable. Correctly re-anchor directions when orientation changes; do not infer orientation from device coordinates, learner geography, English word order or an assumed “north-up” diagram.
3. **Landmark relations and communicative outcome.** In B/C/E assess whether a learner independently preserves the source-required relation and produces a usable location/route for the intended recipient. Accept meaning-equivalent target-language expressions; reject fluent but destination-changing substitutions and lucky binary turns without sufficient evidence.
4. **Unknown facts, legitimate ambiguity and variety.** In A/B/F preserve unresolved frames, missing landmarks and source-valid alternative readings. Accept permitted clarification and validated language varieties; never fabricate source facts or force one perspective when the task permits more than one.
5. **Actually rendered cues and interlocutor provenance.** In C/E/F anchor the first learner-authored spatial move to trusted tutor-cue render chronology. A displayed solved map, arrow, route or answer-bearing caption makes the affected move assisted; assigned-but-unseen cues do not. Distinguish ordinary task clarification from an interlocutor leaking the answer outside protocol.
6. **Independent delayed unseen transfer with denominators.** In C/D/F require meaningful session separation, genuinely new starting points, landmark configurations and frames, first unaided instructions, completed source-defined communicative outcomes and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-up denominators. Immediate rotations of a memorized map, tutor-supplied turns and missing probes cannot establish durable independent mastery.
7. **Comparable adaptation and broad, accessible coverage.** In D/E match or disclose frame difficulty, landmark familiarity, geography knowledge, map availability, practice dose, feedback, modality, language variety, accessibility and topic familiarity. Preserve shared CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation; interests remain soft context weights.
8. **Reversible hypotheses and deterministic replay.** In C/D/E/F emit feasible methods, selected/assigned/actually rendered method, later independent outcomes, uncertainty/confounds, alternative sampling and next-choice rationale. Sparse or confounded data use `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Deduplicate session-qualified replays; freeze source/frame/rubric versions; prove receipt-order invariance for assistance, spatial interpretation, skill/review debt and next choice, quarantining conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen frame/landmark/orientation facts, first/final learner-authored instructions, actually rendered assistance, observed communicative outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay equivalence and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI alone does not prove a shipped spatial evaluator, CEFR certification or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock and multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked spatial tasks, validated multilingual/variety-aware rubrics, frozen reference frames and authoritative landmarks, trusted rendered-cue provenance, first independent spatial instructions and source-defined outcomes, independently completed delayed unseen transfer with denominators, comparable instruction/topic/modality/access exposure, protected skill/review obligations, replay-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing source mapping, validated frame, reliable modality, accessible task or follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown, never invented proficiency.

Any claim that adaptive teaching **improves durable independent spatial communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen interactions, multilingual/variety and accessibility evaluator validation, human adjudication, privacy and subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps a learner tell another person where to go from *their* perspective—not merely repeat directions from a memorized map.**
