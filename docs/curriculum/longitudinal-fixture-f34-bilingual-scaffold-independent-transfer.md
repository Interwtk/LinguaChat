# F34 — Bilingual scaffolds and cognate cues must not masquerade as independent target-language transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against an exact engine HEAD. Not a reproduced defect, shipped adaptive-tutor capability, causal finding or evidence of improved learning. Pre-A1 remains frozen; A1–C2 remain unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F29 guards against instructional carryover, F30 against assessment drift, and F33 against item/answer exposure. F34 addresses a distinct contamination channel: **L1 translation, bilingual explanations, cognate transparency and cross-language answer cues** can make target-language performance look more independent or generalizable than the evidence warrants. Such support can be useful instruction; it is not inherently a defect. The defect to prevent is silently promoting cue-dependent correctness to unaided delayed retrieval, unseen transfer or proof that a teaching method is superior.

## Controlled setup

Resolve `goal-1` to one existing versioned CEFR can-do and supported skill. Compare eligible options A (`target_language_explanation`) and B (`bilingual_bridge`) while holding the required objective, prerequisites, language demand, independent mastery threshold, assessment rubric, overdue reviews and skill coverage constant. Keep interest-driven topics a separate soft sampling layer.

Use a deterministic seed/clock and append-only synthetic event ledger with stable learner, objective, skill, session, teaching exposure, probe, attempt, item-family and event IDs; actual event versus upload times; instructional method/eligibility/offer/completion; **explicitly recorded** explanation language(s), translation visibility, glossary/transcript/hint/model exposure, cognate/false-friend cue provenance, assessment prompt and response language, first attempt, retries, assistance, actual retrieval delay, intervening practice, item/rubric version, unseen-context status, and missing/unknown provenance. Record learner language preferences only if actually supplied and appropriate; never infer L1 or language competence from nationality, locale or accent.

Predeclare a versioned `cross_language_evidence_policy` distinguishing `INSTRUCTIONAL_BRIDGE`, `ANSWER_BEARING_CUE`, `LEXICAL_TRANSPARENCY`, `VERIFIED_UNAIDED`, `UNKNOWN_CUE_PROVENANCE` and `NOT_COMPARABLE` as applicable. These are evidence classifications, not fixed learner types. Translation provided **during instruction** is not automatically contamination of a later independent probe; an answer-bearing translation available **during assessment** is. Cognates alone are not cheating or necessarily invalid language knowledge: a correctly understood cognate may be valid evidence for the specific item, but a cognate-heavy probe cannot establish broad transfer to less transparent contexts without additional calibrated evidence. Do not invent universal cognate similarity thresholds or assume a hidden glossary was absent merely because telemetry is missing.

## Six synthetic histories

- **A — Visible translation at assessment.** B provides a correct L1 translation beside the target-language listening or reading probe; the learner answers correctly on the first click. Preserve observed success and the answer-bearing cue, but do not count it as unaided target-language comprehension, delayed retrieval or evidence that B is superior.
- **B — Cognate-only apparent mastery.** A and B receive equally difficult-looking prompts, but B's follow-ups contain transparent cognates while A's contain opaque yet CEFR-equivalent vocabulary. B's raw score is higher. Report lexical transparency and assessment comparability; do not generalize B's score to unfamiliar noncognate production or claim a method winner without calibrated anchors.
- **C — Legitimate bridge, clean transfer.** B uses L1 comparison and a bilingual worked example *during teaching only*. After a documented delay, the learner completes calibrated, unfamiliar target-language-only prompts, including independent free production, without accessible translation or hints. Keep this valid evidence and allow a provisional uncertainty-aware hypothesis rather than excluding all bilingual teaching.
- **D — False-friend trap and code-switch.** A learner correctly recognizes a familiar cognate but misinterprets a documented false friend; another produces an L1 response to a target-language production objective. Preserve item-level evidence, intended-language/rubric requirements and error type. Do not turn semantic similarity, code-switching or partial recognition into an automatic independent productive-skill pass; avoid blanket penalties when code-switching is explicitly allowed by the objective.
- **E — Reversal and balanced ability.** Early bilingual-assisted and cognate-heavy scores favor B; later comparable delayed target-language-only retrieval and unseen noncognate transfer favor A, with contradictory valid observations. Reduce confidence and revise the objective/skill-specific teaching hypothesis; continue sampling both eligible approaches and required receptive/productive skills, without a permanent 'bilingual learner' label.
- **F — Offline exposure and unknown provenance.** An offline bilingual glossary view is uploaded after an assessment but occurred before it; a hint event is duplicated; one probe lacks reliable visibility records and another contains only ordinary cognates. Deduplicate stable IDs, order by occurrence time, quarantine unknown cue availability and avoid classifying ordinary cognates as answer leakage solely by resemblance. Replaying upload orders must not change results.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Cue and language provenance.** Expose, per option, eligible/offered/exposed/assessed counts, teaching-language and probe-language provenance, translation/hint visibility, lexical transparency strata, independent first attempts and unknown cue counts. Missing telemetry must not be treated as proof that cues were absent.
2. **No assessment-translation laundering.** In A, an answer-bearing L1 translation or bilingual hint accessible during the probe blocks classification as `VERIFIED_UNAIDED` retrieval or unseen target-language transfer, regardless of correctness or first-click status.
3. **Cognates are evidence, not a shortcut to generalization.** In B, preserve valid item-level recognition but report cognate/noncognate and false-friend strata, calibrated comparability and uncertainty. A transparent lexical item alone does not prove robust cross-context or productive transfer; ordinary cognates are not automatically contamination.
4. **Legitimate bilingual teaching remains eligible.** In C, a prior bilingual instructional bridge does not invalidate later verified independent, target-language-only, first-attempt delayed retrieval and unfamiliar transfer. Report actual delay, intervening practice, item-family novelty and sample sizes.
5. **Objective-sensitive language scoring.** In D, distinguish receptive recognition, target-language free production and objectives that explicitly permit multilingual output. Preserve rubric meaning and documented false-friend errors; never lower the CEFR can-do/mastery gate or invent oral/listening evidence for unavailable modalities.
6. **Reversible uncertain hypotheses.** In E, compare only eligible calibrated independent anchors; contradictory later evidence can reverse a provisional method preference. Continue exploration and balanced skill exposure; never assign permanent learning-style or L1-based categories.
7. **Event-time idempotency.** In F, deduplicate stable event IDs and reconstruct cue availability from occurrence times, not upload order. Unknown visibility or ambiguous linkage stays `UNKNOWN_CUE_PROVENANCE`; replay order cannot alter independent denominators or decisions.
8. **Interest and coverage separation.** Interest weights may change examples, but not language/rubric demands, cue classifications, required CEFR objectives, overdue reviews, productive skills or neutral/adjacent/unfamiliar topic rotation. A bilingual example cannot become an exclusive topic or modality filter.
9. **Conservative release claim.** When translation exposure, lexical comparability, independent provenance or delayed unseen evidence is missing, report method efficacy `NOT_COMPARABLE`/`UNSUPPORTED`, preserve valid narrower observations and use the shared balanced curriculum. Never claim durable independent improvement from cue-assisted correctness.

For histories A–F provide exact tested engine HEAD, executable command, deterministic seed/clock, versioned policy, complete synthetic ledger, versioned CEFR can-do and supported skill, method eligibility/assignment/exposure, teaching and probe languages, cue visibility and source, cognate/false-friend strata, assessment calibration, first-attempt/assistance provenance, actual retrieval delays, item novelty, target-language-only independent retrieval and transfer, denominators/exclusions, missingness, uncertainty/reason codes, outstanding review and coverage debt, and PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion. Include a legitimate bilingual-teaching success, a cognate-only false winner, a false-friend case, a later hypothesis reversal and idempotent offline replay.

If cue visibility or assessment comparability cannot be verified, mark the affected method contrast **UNSUPPORTED** without discarding separately valid can-do evidence. A deterministic PASS establishes only a software invariant. Claims that personalized teaching improves durable independent learning require separately reviewed longitudinal learner outcomes versus a balanced baseline, comparable independent opportunities, calibrated unfamiliar transfer, delayed retrieval, uncertainty and unchanged CEFR standards.
