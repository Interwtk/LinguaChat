## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Actual arrival versus unrealized alternative.** Source states that a participant arrived at 09:20, the appointment began at 09:00, and the participant *imagines* leaving home earlier. Learner A says “they arrived before nine” after hearing “If they had left earlier, they might have arrived on time”; learner B independently says the actual arrival was late and an on-time arrival is only a possible alternative. Neither may assert that earlier departure **would certainly** have solved the problem without licensed source evidence. Later interlocutor switches from reporting the real event to discussing a hypothetical plan for next week: preserve the world change.
- **B — Premise, consequence and negation scope.** A source-authored speaker says, “If the notice had **not** been sent, fewer people might have attended,” but the notice **was** sent. Learner A reports “the notice was not sent” or “fewer people attended”; learner B preserves actual notice status, unrealized premise, possibility marker and whose claim it is. An independent follow-up asks whether the speaker proved the notice caused attendance; the appropriate answer distinguishes speculation from evidence. Validate natural target-language alternatives rather than literal English order.
- **C — Regret, wish and indirect speech.** One person says “I wish I had asked for clarification”; another reports “she wishes she had asked.” Learner A treats the wish as evidence that a question was asked, or converts the reported wish into the reporter's own regret. Learner B independently identifies the unfulfilled wish and correct speaker, and can respond tactfully without treating regret as a factual causal explanation. A task with no reliable attribution must allow clarification, not fabricate a speaker.
- **D — Mixed time frames and repair.** A source says a route was closed yesterday; today it is open; a participant asks what **would have happened yesterday** had it been open, then what **could happen tomorrow** if it closes again. Learner A merges both into a present closure or asserts a definite alternative outcome. Learner B preserves each time/world boundary and independently repairs a misleading first report. Source facts must be sufficient for the required report; unsupported consequences are expressed conditionally.
- **E — Immediate assisted correctness versus delayed independent transfer.** On matched first sessions, `ACTUAL_VS_IMAGINED_TIMELINE` produces more immediately correct, scaffolded utterances than `COUNTERFACTUAL_DIALOGUE_RECAST`. A later separate session uses novel speakers, unfamiliar conditions, a negated unrealized premise and open-ended follow-up; the dialogue condition produces stronger **first unaided** distinctions and source-faithful communication. Compare or disclose baseline, source complexity, practice dose, feedback, topic familiarity, modality, variety and accessibility. Lower confidence in the apparent immediate winner, retain exploration and log the **actually rendered** next method, not a fixed learner classification.
- **F — Interest bias, missing probes and out-of-order cues.** A learner handles gaming-related hypothetical upgrades but confuses an unfamiliar community event's unrealized contingency. A color-only timeline is inaccessible; a delayed probe is eligible but not rendered; an offline retry reuses a local event ID in a distinct real session. An answer-bearing tutor “what really happened” cue was rendered before the response but uploaded afterward, while the source/rubric version differs between offline and online records. Deduplicate only true session-qualified replays; quarantine conflicting versions, reconstruct trusted render order, mark missing follow-up honestly and preserve identical assistance labels, review debt and next-method rationale across receipt-order permutations. Text-only performance does not certify speaking/listening.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-valid CEFR applicability.** A–F require a real versioned can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, target language/varieties, source-authored world facts, task/outcome, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, thresholds, skill requirements, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing mapping, ambiguous world premise or unvalidated target-language expression returns `UNSUPPORTED`/`NOT_APPLICABLE`, never invented CEFR proficiency.
2. **Actual and imagined worlds cannot collapse.** A/B/C/D preserve what actually happened, what was merely wished, supposed or contrary to fact, and which outcomes were possible rather than observed. Grammatical fluency with a false factual report fails the communicative assertion.
3. **Premise, consequence, negation, time and speaker scope.** B/C/D preserve condition versus consequence, negation location, speaker attribution and time frame. Do not import English-only tense rules or penalize validated variety-appropriate paraphrases.
4. **Unsupported causal certainty and appropriate repair.** A/B/D accept source-faithful hedging and clarification where the alternative consequence is underdetermined; reject unsupported “would definitely,” invented causes and a repair that silently changes the source. A source revision creates a versioned new observation, not retroactive proof.
5. **First independent move and actual cue exposure.** C/F use trusted render chronology and the first learner-authored world distinction. A worked counterfactual, answer-bearing timeline or interlocutor coaching rendered beforehand marks the affected move assisted; selected but never rendered instruction does not. Legitimate source facts and ordinary clarification are not answer leakage.
6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent transfer.** D/E/F require separate real learning sessions, novel source events/speakers/world switches, first unaided target-language interpretation or production and observable communicative outcome. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing counts. Same-session paraphrase, coached repetition, prior-item recall and uncompleted follow-ups cannot establish durable transfer.
7. **Comparable methods, skills and soft interests.** E/F match or disclose source complexity, world-switch difficulty, practice dose, register, variety, modality, accessibility, feedback and topic familiarity. Keep CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, reviews and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar context rotation intact. Topic interests are soft probabilities, never filters.
8. **Reversible longitudinal policy and replay invariance.** E/F emit feasible methods, assigned/selected/**actually rendered** instruction, subsequent independent outcomes, uncertainty, confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Sparse/conflicted evidence uses `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen versions and trustworthy render chronology yield receipt-order-invariant world labels, assistance, review debt and policy choice; quarantine unresolved conflicts.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** A–F emit exact source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, frozen actual/hypothetical facts, learner's first/final moves, rendered cues, outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is not an executable evaluator PASS, a CEFR award or proof of improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic clock/seed, multi-session histories, real source-resolved CEFR-linked tasks, validated target-language/variety-aware rubrics, versioned actual and imagined worlds, trustworthy rendered-cue chronology, first unaided world distinctions, source-defined outcomes, completed delayed genuinely unseen transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality exposure, protected skill and review obligations, receipt-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing mapping, accessible modality, reliable source, independently completed follow-up or trustworthy chronology remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

Any claim that adaptation **improves durable independent counterfactual communication** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, delayed unseen tasks, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners explain the difference between what happened and what could have happened—without mistaking an imagined possibility for a fact.**
