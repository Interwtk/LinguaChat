**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F182 addresses indirect requests and commitments; F183 addresses repair of *presupposed premises*; F187 covers concession and counterargument. **Distinct construct:** when an exchange is misheard, ambiguous or underspecified, the learner requests the minimum useful clarification, checks a candidate interpretation without turning it into a fact, integrates the partner's actual response and continues without inventing a date, referent, permission or commitment. Do not turn this into a general requirement to ask questions when the source is already clear. English examples are illustrative only.

Distinguish repetition after failed hearing, meaning clarification, a candidate-understanding check, correction of a wrong assumption and acceptance of a partner's answer. “Do you mean the room upstairs?” is a **hypothesis**, not proof. “No, downstairs” must update common ground; silence or an unrelated reply cannot confirm. Accept multiple natural locally reviewed formulations and justified uncertainty; do not require one phrase, accent, indirectness pattern or cultural convention.

A **turn-by-turn semantic/commitment oracle** distinguishes what the learner asked, what the partner answered, what is now jointly established and what remains unresolved. Preserve given times, negation, conditions, referents and uncertainty. If “next Friday” lacks a reference date, do not silently choose a calendar date. ## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Request repetition, not guess.** In a fictional library task, audio containing a shelf number genuinely fails before playback. The learner says, “Sorry, could you repeat the shelf number?” Accept REPETITION_REQUEST_VALID if the existing can-do covers it. Reject “It was shelf seven” as UNSUPPORTED_GUESS. Do not award listening accuracy from failed audio. A readable alternative can be scored only as its actually delivered reading route.

**B — Candidate check and partner correction.** A classmate says, “Let's meet in the room near the stairs,” where two rooms fit. The learner asks, “Do you mean upstairs?” Accept a candidate check, but do **not** record upstairs as confirmed. The partner replies, “No, downstairs.” “Okay, downstairs” integrates the correction; “Great, upstairs is booked” invents a fact. If no reply arrives, the room remains unresolved.

**C — Repair only when necessary.** An organizer clearly says, “The workshop starts at three in the library's main room,” with no conflicting cue. Answering “Three” to a time question is correct; repeated “Which room?” prompts do not automatically improve the response. If a later turn introduces two competing main rooms, a targeted clarification may become justified. Conversely, “next Friday” without a known reference date must not be converted to a specific date by guessing.

**D — Clarification is not permission.** A fictional study-group note says, “You can use the projector if the coordinator approves.” The learner asks, “So it is approved already?” The partner replies, “No, approval is pending.” The learner's “I'll ask first” preserves the condition. Reject “We have permission” or “The coordinator promised approval.” Without an actual partner answer, no confirmation can be claimed.

**E — Model exposure, failed audio and replay.** At 09:01 repair-task audio fails before playback. At 09:02 an accessible text alternative appears **with** an answer-bearing example “Could you repeat the room number?” At 09:03 the learner copies it. Count ACTUAL_STIMULUS_RENDERED, ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED on the reading/interaction route, not independent listening or free production. An unopened hint is not exposure. Replaying the same stable event at 09:04 cannot create another first attempt. A later real distinct session with a new reviewed ambiguity, responsive partner and no model may provide independent evidence; a synonym swap or midnight boundary alone is not delayed unseen transfer.

**F — Reversible method choice and soft interests.** Learner A repeatedly guesses ambiguous locations in first-unaided tasks. Across comparable real sessions, actually delivered NOTICE_MISSING_INFORMATION, TWO_TURN_REPAIR_ROLEPLAY and BALANCED_DEFAULT alternate with matched partner responses and help. Later independent unseen exchanges may cautiously favor a method associated with better targeted questions **and correct uptake**, while alternatives remain sampled. Learner B initially benefits from clarification cards, then asks unnecessary questions after clear answers; lower confidence, reverse the preference or sample REPAIR_ONLY_WHEN_NEEDED. If one method had easier ambiguities, friendlier replies, familiar music topics, a visible model, extra hints or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up and assisted copies cannot prove retention. Music interests can increase example frequency but never exclude neutral libraries, unfamiliar workshops, adjacent contexts or required modalities.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed objectives and reviewed common ground.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific common-ground, semantic, turn and delivery oracles. Preserve prerequisites, ceilings, rubrics and evidence gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Repair instead of unsupported guessing.** A rewards a targeted repetition request after a real breakdown and rejects invented heard content; listening evidence depends on audible delivery.
3. **Candidate uncertainty and uptake.** B treats a check as a hypothesis until answered, records the partner's correction, requires independent integration and never upgrades unanswered questions to confirmation.
4. **Necessary versus unnecessary repair.** C accepts direct answers when sufficient, identifies genuine underspecification and avoids maximizing question counts.
5. **No manufactured commitments.** D distinguishes conditional permission, a question and actual approval even after a partner's explicit correction.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E separates failed audio, accessible text, visible answer-bearing examples, unopened help, first-independent evidence, assisted imitation and deduplicated replay without modality relabeling.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F changes method probabilities only from comparable actually delivered options and later independent repair-plus-uptake; sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence cannot name a causal winner and exploration persists.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Require genuinely distinct sessions, independently reviewed new partners, ambiguities, responses and contexts, first-unaided uptake, explicit missing follow-up and original receptive/productive balance. Interests change topic probabilities only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain reachable.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI do not prove executable interaction QA, shipped adaptive instruction or improved human learning. Require exact-head executable stimulus/turn/provenance checks, observed reversible instructional decisions and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should help learners restore shared understanding with the least necessary repair and later do so independently in new exchanges.
