## Reviewed source-world, linguistic and evidence contract

If no existing objective requires reporting another person's words or stance, mark NOT_APPLICABLE; never invent a CEFR requirement for evidential philosophy or force sophisticated backshifting on all beginners. Use low-stakes fictional club notices, workshop planning, study groups, community events, library desks and volunteer handoffs. Do not infer real legal, health, employment or financial facts from fictional messages.

Freeze a **versioned attribution-and-stance oracle** per stimulus: original utterance and exact wording or reviewed paraphrase; source speaker/role and recipient; channel, language/variety/register and timestamp; quoted versus paraphrased material; the utterance's speech act (assertion, question, request, prediction, offer, promise, denial, guess or correction); its epistemic force (confirmed by source, claimed, possible, uncertain, disputed, unknown); pronoun/person/deictic anchors; time and location anchors; negation, quantifier and modality scope; source authority within the fictional task; subsequent replies/acknowledgments and effective corrections; whether the underlying world fact is **independently established** or only reported; acceptable recipient-facing reports, clarification and explicit unknowns. Separate **(i) fidelity to what was said, (ii) whether the speaker actually committed to it, and (iii) independent truth of the proposition**. A faithful report of a false or unverified statement can be correct as reporting; an unqualified assertion of its truth is not thereby warranted.

Direct quotes require actual wording fidelity, including negation, hedges and speaker identity. Indirect speech may legitimately shift person, tense, deixis or register when anchored to the original speech time and reporting time; do not enforce one English backshift formula or treat every grammatical variation as a semantic error. In Spanish and other supported target languages, review natural sequence-of-tenses and attribution variants with language specialists; accept meaning-preserving alternatives and recipient-appropriate clarification, not a single privileged translation. A question is not an assertion; a prediction is not a completed outcome; an offer is not necessarily acceptance; “I heard that” is not independent verification. Distinguish an authoritative revised notice from a later person's unsupported retelling.

Latency is weak evidence only when actually observed and comparable. Accessible text can support written source interpretation, but cannot certify unheard listening.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Reported claim versus established fact.** A fictional club coordinator Mina says **“I think the art room opens at nine, but I haven't checked the timetable.”** No official timetable is provided. Learner A tells a volunteer **“The art room opens at nine”** as confirmed fact and fails the handoff. Learner B says **“Mina thinks it opens at nine, but she hasn't checked; please confirm”** and preserves source and uncertainty. Counterfactual: a later reviewed official notice confirms **09:30**, effective from that notice's timestamp; a recipient-facing update distinguishes Mina's earlier guess from the newer official schedule. Do not retroactively rewrite Mina's original words or award a world-fact claim before the notice.

**B — Questions, offers and commitments.** A fictional student asks **“Can we move the practice to Friday?”** and the organizer answers **“I'll check the room.”** A learner reports **“The organizer agreed to move it to Friday”** and fails: a request and promise to check are not an accepted change. Counterfactual: the organizer later says **“Friday is confirmed”** and sends the group an updated schedule; a report may now attribute confirmation to that later message. **“I might be able to help”** is not **“I promise to help”**. Credit a targeted recipient question when confirmation is missing.

**C — Direct/indirect speech and anchored deixis.** On fictional **Monday 5 October**, Lucia tells a colleague **“I will send my notes tomorrow.”** On Wednesday 7 October, the learner must relay the original statement. **“Lucia said on Monday that she would send her notes on Tuesday”** is faithful; **“Lucia sent the notes on Tuesday”** invents completion. Quoting **“I will send my notes tomorrow”** without identifying Lucia and the original Monday context can mislead the Wednesday recipient. Counterfactual: if Lucia later says **“I sent them on Tuesday”**, the learner may report that Lucia **said** she sent them, but cannot claim independent receipt unless the source-world includes it. Accept natural language-specific tense/deixis variants that preserve time and attribution.

**D — Conflicting speakers and scoped corrections.** In a fictional library planning thread, Ana says **“All six seats are taken.”** Ben says **“I think two seats remain, but my list may be old.”** A learner reports **“There are two seats free”** as verified and fails; the reviewed handoff is **“Ana says all six are taken; Ben thinks two remain but may have an outdated list; please check the current roster.”** Counterfactual: the designated roster owner later posts a timestamped corrected count. Use the authoritative update prospectively, retain the original conflicting reports for provenance and do not infer that either earlier speaker was deliberately dishonest. A copied quote with its negation removed is not faithful.

**E — Accessible delivered channels and authorship chronology.** A fictional audio message says **“Nora said the hall might be available; she did not confirm it.”** ASR omits **“not”**; captions drop **“might”**; translation removes **“Nora said”**; screen-reader order omits the speaker label. Reconcile the **actually delivered** stimulus and quarantine construct-changing channel corruption rather than penalizing a learner for a false transcription. A learner independently asks **“Did Nora confirm it, or only say it might be available?”** at 10:00; an answer-bearing tutor model renders at 10:01. The original clarification remains FIRST_UNAIDED; a copied report at 10:02 is ASSISTED. A replayed event is not new evidence or a new learning day. An accessible corrected text can support text comprehension but cannot prove unheard listening. A source correction at 10:03 does not change the 10:00 oracle.

**F — Longitudinal method reversal and nonexclusive interests.** Fictional learner A performs well immediately after DIRECT_INDIRECT_REPORT_CONTRAST, but later independently reports a tentative offer as a firm promise in an unfamiliar community handoff. Repeated matched, actually delivered SPEAKER_SOURCE_READBACK opportunities precede better delayed first-unaided recipient-correct reports across new speakers, acts and contexts. Only repeated comparable outcomes may cautiously favor readback while retaining exploration. Fictional learner B initially benefits from MULTISPEAKER_ATTRIBUTION_MAP, then on delayed transfer treats a disagreement as settled fact; confidence drops, review becomes due and alternatives resume. If readback receives one short clear speaker but the map receives four conflicting speakers, or only one method shows a completed model, declare **no winner**. Replacing a gaming topic with music while preserving the same dialogue and source structure is not unseen transfer. Missing follow-up is MISSING, not failure. Interests cannot exclude neutral/unfamiliar sources or skip required objectives.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR objective and independently reviewed attribution oracle.** A–F require a versioned communicative can-do, prerequisites, source authority/version, speaker/recipient and information gap, reviewed speech-act/stance/quotation oracle, target language/variety/register, accessible delivered stimulus, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; no invented universal evidential-logic CEFR requirement.
2. **Claim/source/truth boundaries.** A and D credit faithful attribution of an unverified claim without promoting it to independently established fact; distinguish source authority and effective updates, preserve disagreements and uncertainty, and never equate disagreement with deception.
3. **Speech act and commitment boundaries.** B distinguishes questions, requests, offers, tentative willingness, promises, acceptance and confirmed changes. Neither a request nor a promise to check proves the requested event happened; clarification is creditable when warranted.
4. **Quotation, stance and temporal/deictic fidelity.** C preserves speaker, direct quote wording, person, temporal anchor, modality, negation and reporting-versus-completion. Accept natural reviewed indirect-speech variants rather than one mechanical tense backshift; reported future action is not evidence of completion.
5. **Multiple-source revision without retroactive rewriting.** A/D bind each message to its source/version/effective time, treat official fictional corrections only within their authority and preserve historical statements. Contradictory reports do not silently collapse into a single confident answer.
6. **Actually delivered accessible chronological evidence.** E reconciles original and rendered audio/ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader speaker/hedge/negation structure, quarantines meaning-changing corruption, preserves first authorship before actual help, deduplicates stable events and distinguishes access-equivalent from different constructs. Text cannot prove unheard listening.
7. **Controlled uncertain reversible instructional adaptation.** F compares feasible actually delivered methods under matched speaker count, quote length, stance burden, source completeness, time/deixis load, modality, language, topic, help, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, contradictory, selectively observed or confounded evidence yields no winner; delayed regression lowers confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed first-unaided unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Copied quotations, hinted source maps, immediate repetition and topic-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, new source/speaker/recipient/context **and changed speech act, attribution or stance structure**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided actionable handoff and explicit follow-up missingness. Preserve shared CEFR objectives, receptive/productive balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic rotation.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Green documentation CI is not a shipped attribution evaluator, an outcome-responsive tutor or proof of improved human retention. Require exact-head deterministic executable assertions against real objectives, logged delivered instructional changes, later independent transfer versus a balanced baseline, and separately reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs work, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are fictional design/probe cases, not observed learner histories or executable QA. Preserve speaker attribution, speech-act force, original wording, time and uncertainty; maintain explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and learner-evaluation evidence exist.
