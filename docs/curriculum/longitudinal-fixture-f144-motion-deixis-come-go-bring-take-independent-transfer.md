## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Addressee-centered “come” is not automatically wrong.** A fictional caller in the office says to a colleague at home: **“Can you come to my office at three?”** The office is the caller's intended center. The colleague replies **“Yes, I'll come to your office”** in a reviewed English conversational context; a rigid speaker-only rule would incorrectly reject this addressee-oriented use. Learner A mechanically rewrites every addressee-oriented **come** as **go**; learner B independently produces a reviewed recipient-natural answer and confirms the destination. Counterfactual: the same learner tells an unrelated third party **“I'll go to the office at three”** with no licensed addressee/shared-center reading; that **go** may be the appropriate reviewed choice. Neither sentence proves that the trip occurred. If the intended center is missing, credit **“Do you mean your office or mine?”** rather than guessing.

**B — Current versus shared future center.** Two colleagues are now at different locations but plan to meet at a conference tomorrow. One asks **“Are you coming to the conference?”** where the conference is a shared future conversational center. Learner A assumes **come** is impossible because neither is there now; learner B interprets the invitation correctly and independently writes a natural response. Counterfactual: the speaker is not attending and is merely describing a third party's unrelated travel; an independently reviewed **go** construction may be preferable. Record speaker attendance, projected future presence and discourse commitments **only when source-provided**; an invitation does not entail attendance. Changing **tomorrow** to **today** without changing the center does not automatically create an unseen transfer task.

**C — Carrying an object and completing delivery are different.** In a fictional workshop, a manager at Room B tells a technician at Room A **“Please bring the projector to Room B.”** The task source licenses the manager's Room B center. The technician can respond with a reviewed **“I'll bring it to you”**; a third-party status report may instead say **“I'll take the projector to Room B.”** Learner A asserts **“The projector has been delivered”** immediately after agreeing; learner B independently distinguishes intended motion from completed handoff and asks for receipt confirmation when needed. Counterfactual: **“The projector was taken from Room B”** reverses the source/destination relation; **“I showed the projector to the manager”** does not prove possession transfer. Track agent, carried theme, goal, time and recipient independently. Do not mark every **bring/take** difference wrong without reviewed discourse context.

**D — Quotation and role switches require reanchoring.** A coordinator forwards a quoted message from a visitor to a receptionist: **“I'm coming to your desk now.”** The original **I** is the visitor, **your** is the receptionist, and the destination is the receptionist's desk. Learner A forwards the quote as a first-person statement from the coordinator without attribution, changing who will move; learner B independently reports **“The visitor said they're coming to your desk”** or a separately reviewed equivalent that preserves attribution and destination. Counterfactual: the coordinator later speaks directly as the traveler, changing the licensed deictic center. A copied tutor-generated pronoun/center map before first authorship makes the result assisted. A quote whose speaker or recipient is not identified requires clarification, not invented identity.

**E — Actual delivery, accessibility, uncertainty and replay.** The authoritative audio says **“Bring the sample to me, not to the lab.”** An ASR transcript substitutes **take** for **bring**, captions omit **not**, a translation switches the center, a screen reader presents unlabeled speaker arrows, or a tutor diagram points to the correct destination. Compare the **actually delivered** stimulus with the authoritative version. If the delivered material does not support a valid center/destination distinction, quarantine **only the affected judgment** as UNSUPPORTED, not a learner-wide failure. At 10:00 the learner independently asks **“Who is ‘me’?”**; a model answer queued at 09:59 but first rendered at 10:01 cannot retroactively contaminate that response. An edited answer at 10:02 after the model is ASSISTED. A validated accessible text equivalent can test the same linguistic contrast, but text-only performance must not certify unobserved listening ability. Replaying the same event ID must not manufacture first attempts, new sessions or delayed retention.

**F — Outcome-driven adaptation, confounds and genuinely new transfer.** Fictional learner A answers immediate center-swap drills correctly after SPEAKER_ADDRESSEE_CENTER_SWAP but repeatedly confuses destination on delayed unaided real-message simulations. After comparable **actually delivered** PHONE_INVITATION_DIALOGUE and CURRENT_VS_FUTURE_CENTER_TIMELINE trials, A independently handles a new multi-party equipment pickup with a future shared meeting point, different verb and recipient. Learner B instead shows better delayed unaided messages after MOTION_AGENT_VS_CARRIED_THEME_CONTRAST and LEARNER_GENERATED_HANDOFF. If one method uses a simple two-person **come** invitation and another uses three speakers, quotation and unfamiliar **deliver/collect** verbs, mark METHOD_SPEAKER_COUNT_CONFOUNDED and METHOD_VERB_FREQUENCY_CONFOUNDED: infer **no winner**. A name swap in the same office invitation is **not** unseen transfer. Match baseline ability, center complexity, time shift, lexical load, language variety, accessibility, prior help, topic, modality, delay and actual follow-up opportunity; record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing probes. Missing is neither success nor failure. Only repeated comparable later **first-unaided** outcomes can justify a logged change in **actually delivered** instruction, with calibrated uncertainty, continued alternatives and reversal if subsequent evidence contradicts the hypothesis. Interests in games, music or travel may influence example frequency but cannot exclude neutral or unfamiliar settings or required objectives.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR-linked purpose and independently reviewed deictic oracle.** A–F resolve actual versioned objectives/blueprints/prerequisites, target language/variety/register, speaker/addressee/third-party roles, source-authored center/time/path, reviewed accepted readings, accessible stimulus, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Do not invent a motion-deixis CEFR descriptor.
2. **Licensed centers, not a universal speaker-only rule.** A accepts independently reviewed speaker- and addressee-oriented uses in context; an ambiguous center earns source-valid clarification. The evaluator cannot enforce `come = toward the current speaker` as a universal English or cross-language law.
3. **Future shared center versus actual attendance.** B distinguishes current from future conversational center and accepts a licensed invitation reading without inventing presence, travel completion or agreement to attend.
4. **Motion roles, delivery and possession entailments.** C preserves motion agent, carried theme, source, destination and intended recipient; a request or promise to bring/take does not prove arrival, delivery, acceptance or possession transfer.
5. **Quoted-speaker reanchoring and attribution.** D preserves whose motion is described when first-person forms and recipient pronouns are quoted, reported or revoiced; genuinely unresolved identities require clarification.
6. **Actually delivered evidence, accessibility and replay.** E contrasts authoritative versus rendered ASR/captions/translation/TTS/visual/screen-reader/autocorrect, quarantines only unsupported constructs, orders first authorship versus **rendered** help and deduplicates session-qualified events. Text-equivalent access must not be mislabeled as oral evidence.
7. **Uncertain, confound-aware and reversible method choice.** F compares feasible, accessible, actually delivered methods under comparable center/time/speaker complexity, verb familiarity, language variety, modality, topic, prior help and follow-up; sparse/confounded/contradictory evidence yields no winner and a balanced fallback.
8. **Independent delayed retrieval, genuine unseen transfer and broad coverage.** Copied center maps, same-session repetition, assisted repairs, identical frame/name substitutions and topic familiarity cannot earn delayed or unseen mastery. Require later eligible distinct sessions, first-unaided messages to a new recipient/world configuration and a reviewed oracle. Missingness remains unknown; preserve each required skill family and nonexclusive interest rotation.
9. **Evidence honesty and release boundary.** Passing a document/CI check is **not** evidence of executable engine support, outcome-driven delivered-method changes or improved human retention. Those claims require exact-head executable tests tied to real objectives and observed longitudinal independent delayed/transfer outcomes against a balanced baseline, then ethically reviewed learner evidence.
