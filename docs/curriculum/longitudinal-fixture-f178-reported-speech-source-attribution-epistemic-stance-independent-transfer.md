## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Belief versus confirmation.** A fictional volunteer says “I **think** the library room is available, but I haven't checked.” The learner reports to an uninformed organizer “The room is definitely available.” That is an **unjustified certainty upgrade**, despite fluent grammar. “Maya thinks it may be available; nobody has confirmed it” preserves attribution and status. A later actually delivered availability confirmation can support a later updated report but must not retroactively validate the earlier independent overstatement.

**B — Direct quotation versus paraphrase.** A workshop organizer's verified written message is “We **might** start at nine.” The learner presents “The organizer said, ‘We will start at nine.’” The quotation is not verbatim and strengthens possibility to certainty. “The organizer said they might start at nine” is a faithful paraphrase; exact quotation requires the exact reviewed words. In a separately reviewed variant, quotation marks are not delivered and the speaker cannot be distinguished; accept a targeted “Was that the organizer's exact wording?” rather than assuming verbatim evidence.

**C — Nested source chain and hearsay.** In a fictional study club, Theo says “Nina **heard from Omar** that the posters may be ready.” The learner tells a recipient “Omar confirmed the posters are ready.” This collapses the chain and invents confirmation. “Theo says Nina heard Omar may have the posters ready; let's check with Omar” preserves the limited claim. A different stimulus where Omar directly and audibly confirms completion may warrant a stronger report; grade the **actually delivered** source chain, not the hidden world oracle alone.

**D — Negation and scope under reporting.** A fictional tutor says “I **didn't say** the session was canceled; I said it **might be postponed**.” The learner must not report “The tutor canceled the session,” nor infer that the session is definitely happening. A faithful recipient-facing summary is “The tutor denied announcing a cancellation and mentioned a possible postponement; the schedule is unconfirmed.” Distinguish denial of a previous assertion from a positive claim about the schedule. If the negation was acoustically obscured, targeted clarification is valid and the listening task must reflect signal recoverability.

**E — First authorship, help, accessibility and correction.** At 09:02 an audio report plays with no visible speaker label; the learner first asks “Who said that, and was it confirmed?” Preserve FIRST_UNAIDED_INTERACTION and TARGETED_SOURCE_CLARIFICATION. At 09:03 a speaker-labelled transcript and answer-bearing summary **actually render**; at 09:04 the learner correctly reports the source and certainty. The latter is ASSISTED and ANSWER_EXPOSURE, never a replacement for 09:02. A help control offered but not opened is not exposure; retry of 09:04 with the same session-qualified ID is deduplicated. If audio failed to play, do not score unaided listening. Transcript-first accessibility remains valid under its **actual** measured construct.

**F — Reversible adaptation, independent transfer and confounding.** Learner A can identify individual speakers but repeatedly overstates uncertain reports in independent multi-turn messages. Across matched sessions, actually delivered CERTAINTY_LADDER_WITH_SCOPE_CHECK and RECIPIENT_REPORT_BACK_ROLEPLAY alternate with BALANCED_DEFAULT. If later first-unaided reports to new recipients preserve source, modality and action status across **new speakers, reports, neutral/unfamiliar contexts and meaningfully later sessions**, cautiously raise the better-supported method's probability while continuing alternative sampling. Learner B initially copies source-chain diagrams successfully, but later collapses an unseen nested hearsay report; lower confidence and explore REPORT_THEN_VERIFY_CLARIFICATION. If one method had explicit speaker labels, simpler source chains, more answer-bearing transcripts, stronger original certainty, familiar topics or more follow-up, record **no causal winner**. A calendar boundary, paraphrase of the same memorized report, assisted re-answer or missing follow-up does not establish delayed unseen transfer.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Fixed CEFR and reviewed source/claim oracles.** A–F bind existing versioned relevant can-dos, independent source-world and claim-strength review, actual-delivery provenance, unchanged rubric and due/delayed/unseen probes; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED. No new level gate.
2. **No certainty inflation.** A rejects “definitely” when the actual source only believes something might be true; a later confirmation cannot rewrite the earlier first attempt.
3. **Quotation fidelity and scope.** B distinguishes exact verified quotes from permissible faithful paraphrases, preserving “might” and recognizing missing quotation evidence.
4. **Nested attribution is not direct confirmation.** C preserves Theo → Nina → Omar and hearsay status; a recipient-facing report must not silently promote indirect information to Omar's confirmed fact.
5. **Negation, correction and recipient action.** D preserves the denial's scope and the possible postponement without inventing cancellation, certainty or an actionable schedule; obscured cues require honest clarification.
6. **First authorship, actually rendered help and accessibility.** E keeps the earliest independent report/clarification, records only delivered exposure, deduplicates retries, distinguishes failed audio from language error and reports accessibility under the real construct.
7. **Matched, uncertain and reversible adaptation.** F updates instructional probabilities only using comparable **actually delivered** options and eligible later independent results; sparse, confounded or contradictory outcomes block causal-winner claims and retain exploration.

Preserve modality balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topics without changing CEFR objectives.
