## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Perception must bind sound to meaning, not written hints.** In an independently validated English variety with an applicable vowel contrast, source audio says “ship” or “sheep” in a minimal contextual exchange. Learner A chooses correctly only when written options reveal the answer or the tutor previously plays a labeled model; learner B identifies the independently presented audio and chooses the corresponding lexical meaning without answer-bearing text. A no-audio condition and a transcript-only condition are **not** evidence of listening discrimination. Counterfactual: the source changes the vowel while holding speaker, gain, prompt and context comparable; the oracle must change accordingly. Other varieties/languages require their own validated contrasts, not a universal English phoneme table.

**B — Production intelligibility versus machine transcription.** In a fictional task, a learner requests a “ship” but produces an utterance that an appropriately blinded, qualified listener consistently understands as “sheep” under controlled context. A recognizer returns “ship” from a strongly primed phrase; that machine output cannot override the independently supported communication outcome. Counterfactual: the recognizer fails on a validated acceptable regional realization that human listeners understand; do not penalize the learner for ASR bias. Mark missing, distorted or non-consented audio as UNSUPPORTED for production. A response understandable despite a non-native accent may meet the communicative rubric; native-like pronunciation is not the gate.

**C — Variety validity and individual error hypotheses.** Two independently validated target-language varieties differ in an acceptable vowel realization, and a learner produces one approved realization intelligibly. The evaluator must not enforce one region's acoustic template as the sole correct answer. A second learner repeatedly confuses a meaning-changing contrast across independently verified items, but that observation is an **uncertain, task-local instructional hypothesis**, not a permanent auditory/visual style or an inference about nationality or first language. Counterfactual: replacing an approved accent with a genuinely meaning-changing production alters the scored communication outcome; swapping equivalent acceptable varieties does not.

**D — Orthography and lexical familiarity are not phoneme evidence.** A learner selects the correct spelling after reading a word and later fails to discriminate the spoken contrast with text hidden. The first task measures orthographic/lexical knowledge, not unaided listening. Conversely, a learner accurately discriminates a sound in unfamiliar but source-validated meaningful words while misspelling them; do not erase valid auditory evidence. Counterfactual: a supposedly new item repeats the exact trained recording with a different file name; it is **not** unseen transfer. A truly unseen, validated lexeme and new speaker can probe generalization only if task difficulty and vocabulary prerequisites are controlled.

**E — Delivered channel, assistance chronology, accessibility and replay.** A source-authored contrast is intelligible in the authoritative recording, but the actually delivered audio clips the distinguishing segment; an automatic caption displays the answer before the learner hears it; a slowed playback changes the target contrast; or the screen reader reads labeled choices before the unaided attempt. Quarantine the affected construct as UNSUPPORTED or ASSISTED according to what was **actually rendered**. A learner's first response at 10:00 remains FIRST_UNAIDED when the tutor's answer-bearing waveform was queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01; a later copied answer is ASSISTED. Replaying the same immutable event ID must not create a fresh attempt. When auditory evidence is inaccessible, offer a validated accessible learning path and record construct non-equivalence rather than fabricating an equivalent listening score.

**F — Reversible method selection and delayed independent transfer.** Fictional learner A shows assisted immediate imitation but poor later first-unaided listening discrimination across new speakers. After **actually delivered** auditory-contrast practice on comparable tasks, A independently distinguishes unseen source-validated lexical pairs days later and produces an intelligible request to a new listener. Learner B instead benefits more from articulatory cues plus communicative recasts under comparable conditions. Freeze baseline, target contrast, language/variety, lexeme frequency/familiarity, speaker, device, signal quality, task length, review interval, accommodations, topic familiarity, hints and follow-up opportunity. If method A receives clear recordings of common words while method B receives noisy recordings of rare words, mark METHOD_SIGNAL_QUALITY_CONFOUNDED / METHOD_LEXICAL_FAMILIARITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no winner**. Distinct audio filenames with identical trained tokens do not establish unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed independent outcomes may warrant **actually different next instruction** with uncertainty, alternative sampling and reversible updates; contrary evidence reduces confidence.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and phonological source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor, blueprint, prerequisites, language/variety, validated lexical contrast, accepted realizations, source audio, rubric/evaluator, independent threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No advanced phonetic target is silently imposed on beginners.
2. **Perception versus visible-answer contamination.** A/D require source-validated sound-to-meaning discrimination with no pre-exposed transcript, orthographic cue or labeled tutor model. A phoneme-changing audio counterfactual changes the oracle; filename or spelling cosmetics do not.
3. **Communicative intelligibility over ASR or accent mimicry.** B/C distinguish independently supported meaning-changing listener confusion from machine-recognition errors and legitimate variety differences. ASR output alone never certifies or denies pronunciation mastery.
4. **Distinct listening, production and orthographic evidence.** A/B/D separately attribute receptive discrimination, production intelligibility and spelling/reading performance. Success in one cannot automatically compensate for missing evidence in another; accessible alternatives must be explicitly construct-qualified.
5. **Validated varieties and non-diagnostic hypotheses.** C accepts independently reviewed pronunciation variants, records only task-local error patterns and never labels a learner's nationality, disability or permanent modality style from speech.
6. **Actually delivered channel, help timing and replay.** E checks waveform integrity, rate, captions, screen-reader ordering, model exposure and actual render times; duplicate events are idempotent; broken signals are quarantined rather than converted into learner failure.
7. **Delayed retrieval and genuinely unseen phonological transfer.** A–F require distinct real learning opportunities, new validated words/speakers/contexts, first-unaided evidence without answer-bearing help, and eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Renamed recordings or immediate model repetition do not qualify.
8. **Reversible method selection and nonexclusive interests.** F logs feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** methods, comparable contrast/lexeme/talker/signal difficulty, uncertainty, alternative sampling, contrary outcomes and protected CEFR skills/reviews. Interest examples increase probability but do not exclude neutral, adjacent or unfamiliar vocabulary.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named exact engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/contrast/audio/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, delivered artifacts, first/help/event timeline, blinded listener or validated oracle evidence, counterfactuals, confounds, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Documentation-only CI cannot establish a shipped phonological evaluator, reproducible runtime defect or improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked objectives; independent language/variety and audio validation; accepted pronunciation alternatives and communication-focused rubrics; authoritative and actually delivered accessible stimuli; independently attributable first listening and speaking responses; actual answer-bearing help chronology; reliable human-listener or independently validated intelligibility evidence rather than ASR alone; delayed unaided retrieval and genuinely unseen lexeme/talker transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded methods; protected skills, due reviews and exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and phoneme-change, accent-variant, ASR/TTS, clipping, caption, rate, chronology, replay and source-version counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Missing audio, unvalidated phonetic contrasts, inaccessible tasks, absent consent or missing follow-ups leave affected claims **UNSUPPORTED/unknown**, not invented learner failure.

A stronger claim that adaptive phonological instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual, variety-fair and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparisons, attrition/subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor helps learners hear and express meaning clearly across unfamiliar speakers and contexts, without treating accent difference as failure.**
