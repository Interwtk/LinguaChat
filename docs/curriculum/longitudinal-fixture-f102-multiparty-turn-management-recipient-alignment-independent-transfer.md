## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — Group-addressed question versus one person's answer.** In a source-authored three-person project meeting, Jo asks **everyone**, “Can each of you review the draft by Friday?” Mina replies, “I can.” Ravi remains silent. Learner A tells Jo “We all agreed to review it,” falsely assigning Mina's commitment to Ravi. Learner B independently says Mina agreed and Ravi has not answered, or asks Ravi for confirmation in a culturally appropriate way. In a matched control Ravi also explicitly accepts; group agreement is then supported. Later unseen transfer changes the team, addressee wording, task and response order. Score the recipient's resulting assignment, not name recall alone.

- **B — Attention backchannel is not permission to act.** A source-authored speaker explains a proposed budget while another participant says “Mm-hm, I'm following.” The speaker later asks whether to submit it, but receives no approval. Learner A reports “They said yes; submit it.” Learner B independently distinguishes acknowledgment of listening from an affirmative decision and requests explicit confirmation. A matched source world includes “Yes, submit that budget now,” and the learner should not over-hedge. Validate equivalent acknowledgment forms and local pragmatic norms; do not declare a single particle universally noncommittal.

- **C — Unfinished turn and consequential interruption.** During an accessible source-authored group planning exchange, a participant begins “The hall is available Thursday—” and continues “—but only until noon.” Learner A takes the floor after the first clause and announces a full-day Thursday event. Learner B waits for completion or uses an appropriate brief clarification before recommending a time. A second world deliberately ends the turn after a complete Thursday availability statement; responding without waiting is valid. A third world contains overlapping or truncated speech that makes the qualification genuinely inaccessible; the learner may ask for repetition, and the task must not penalize missing audio. Do not equate all interruptions with rudeness; assess the source-defined communicative consequence.

- **D — Side-thread reply and addressee routing.** In a source-authored four-person exchange, Alex asks Noor for a venue update while Sam separately asks Lee for transport help. A reply “It's confirmed” comes from Noor and refers only to the venue. Learner A tells Sam transport is confirmed because the phrase is nearby. Learner B independently routes the answer to Alex's venue thread and leaves Sam's question open, or asks who/what was confirmed if speaker attribution is inaccessible. A control source explicitly labels “Transport is confirmed, Sam” from Lee; the correct recipient/action changes. Unseen transfer varies participant count, thread order and direct versus group address.

- **E — Immediate annotated mapping versus delayed independent coordination.** In matched early sessions for the **same source-required CEFR multi-party can-do**, `SPEAKER_ADDRESSEE_MAPPING` yields quick correct responses when a tutor-completed turn map is visible; `MULTIPARTY_ROLEPLAY` initially yields slower unaided attempts and some valid repairs. In **later distinct real sessions**, unseen group exchanges change speaker sequence, backchannel form, recipient routing and decision stakes. The synthetic learner with roleplay practice independently avoids more false consensus and wrong-recipient actions, despite lower immediate coached accuracy. Match or disclose baseline proficiency, number of participants/threads, overlap, source ambiguity, feedback, practice dose, language variety/register, modality/accessibility and topic familiarity. Log the **actually rendered** changed next method, keep sampling alternatives, and do not claim roleplay is universally superior.

- **F — Favorite-topic overfit, cue timing, replay and inaccessible speaker identity.** A learner accurately coordinates familiar game-team discussions but mistakes acknowledgments and recipients in neutral classroom and unfamiliar community meetings with equivalent participant/turn complexity. A tutor's speaker map was rendered before the first answer but its telemetry arrived late; another map was selected but never displayed. Two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; an authoritative transcript correction changes the addressee of a short reply; a rubric revision accepts a regional backchannel form; a delayed probe was offered but not completed. In an inaccessible video/caption track, two speakers are indistinguishable and the source fails to identify who agreed. Reconstruct trusted render/source chronology, deduplicate only true session-qualified replays, quarantine incompatible source/ledger/rubric versions, preserve missing follow-up as unknown, do not infer agreement from inaccessible attribution, rotate contexts and require invariant assistance/recipient/next-method verdicts under receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR applicability and bounded multi-party burden.** A–F emit a real versioned descriptor/can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored multi-party task, target language/variety/register, accessible channel, frozen participant/turn/recipient ledger, recipient goal, rubric/evaluator, assistance policy, independent threshold, required skills, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. No source-required multi-party turn/recipient distinction means `NOT_APPLICABLE`; missing mapping or inaccessible identity means `UNSUPPORTED`, not a new graduation requirement.

2. **Addressee and consensus scope.** A/D preserve who was asked, who actually replied, to which thread and with what authority. One participant's acceptance cannot become whole-group consent; a side-thread response cannot silently answer a different participant. A source world with explicit additional acceptance must change the expected verdict.

3. **Acknowledgment force versus authorization.** B/F distinguish source-supported listening/backchannels, tentative alignment, explicit acceptance, refusal and silence. Do not authorize booking, sharing, submitting or promising from a mere attention signal. Accept regional and culturally valid realizations based on the frozen pragmatic rubric, rather than fixed keywords or stereotypes.

4. **Turn completion, overlap and bounded clarification.** C/D distinguish source-defined unfinished turns, completed turns, legitimate overlap and unavailable audio/speaker attribution. Valid waiting, yielding, appropriately timed floor-taking or clarification can pass; indiscriminate silence or unnecessary clarification is not automatically optimal. Do not punish disability-related pacing or inaccessible input.

5. **First independent recipient-facing move and delivered-cue provenance.** A/B/C/F evaluate the first learner-authored move against trusted **actually rendered** tutor/interlocutor chronology. A displayed speaker map, explicit acceptance label, answer-bearing recap or completed reply before that move makes affected evidence assisted; selected-but-unrendered help does not. A source-authored reply under the frozen task protocol is stimulus; out-of-protocol answer revelation is coaching. Late telemetry cannot promote a coached response to independent.

6. **Delayed genuinely unseen independent multi-party transfer.** A–F require distinct real learning sessions, meaningful delay, new source-authored interactions with changed participant roles, recipient scope, turn order, acknowledgment forms and recipient decisions, first unaided responses and source-defined recipient outcomes. Replaying annotated exchanges, assisted repairs, scripted memorization or missing follow-ups cannot establish durable mastery. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.

7. **Comparable methods, accessibility and interest breadth.** C/E/F match or disclose participant/thread counts, turn overlap, baseline proficiency, source ambiguity, response burden, feedback, dose, variety/register, modality/accessibility and preferred/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar topic exposure. Preserve shared CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive skills, due reviews and exit gates. Inaccessible diarization, missing captions, unsupported speech input and cultural-norm mismatch are confounds, not learner deficits.

8. **Reversible outcome-based adaptation and replay invariance.** E/F record feasible candidates, selected/assigned/**actually rendered** instruction, completed later independent recipient outcomes, uncertainty/confounds, exploration and next-choice rationale. Better delayed independent performance may reverse immediate assisted-score preferences without permanent learning-style labels; sparse/conflicted evidence falls back to `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Session-qualified replay deduplication, frozen source/ledger/rubric versions and trusted cue chronology must produce identical recipient/independence/review-debt/next-method verdicts across receipt permutations; unresolved conflicts fail closed.

9. **Assertion-level audit and bounded claims.** A–F emit exact source/task/ledger/rubric/evaluator IDs, participant/turn/addressee ground truth, source-supported acknowledgment force, first/final learner-authored responses, rendered cues, recipient interpretation/action, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay permutations and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI is not executable multi-party dialogue evaluation, verified voice support, a CEFR award or evidence of improved human retention.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and command, deterministic seed/clock, real source-linked CEFR tasks that actually require multi-party interaction, validated multilingual/variety-aware pragmatic rubrics, frozen participant/turn/recipient ledger, accessible speaker attribution, trusted rendered-cue chronology, first unaided recipient-facing moves, source-defined recipient interpretations/actions, completed delayed genuinely unseen multi-party transfer with denominators, comparable method/topic/modality/accessibility exposure, protected required skills/reviews, receipt-order permutations, reversible next-method traces and assertion-level verdicts. Missing real mapping, accessible speaker/turn information, reliable chronology, valid recipient-action adjudication or completed follow-up remains `UNSUPPORTED`/unknown.

Any claim that instructional adaptation **improves durable independent multi-party coordination** additionally requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator, multilingual/variety and accessibility validation, human adjudication, privacy/subgroup-harm review and an ethically reviewed pilot. **A strong tutor helps learners hear who is speaking to whom, distinguish “I'm listening” from “I agree,” and keep a group conversation accurate without inventing consensus.**
