Not a shipped implicature evaluator, universal conversational rule, new CEFR gate or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F158 treats disjunction scope; F180 treats quantifier scope; F182 treats indirect requests; F183 treats presuppositions; F189 treats source attribution; F191 treats question–answer relevance. **Distinct construct:** where a source-linked communicative task requires it, distinguish a proposition **entailed by what was said** from a **plausible but cancellable contextual inference**, recognize explicit cancellation without declaring a contradiction, and communicate the remaining uncertainty faithfully. “Some” does not logically entail “not all”; a listener's plausible default inference is not a fact, commitment, speaker intention or universal language rule. English examples are illustrative only.

A frozen, independently reviewed **literal-content oracle** records the exact utterance, scope of quantifiers/connectives/negation, temporal/modal qualifications, speaker and accessible preceding/following context. Record what the source **asserts or entails**, what is **not established**, and whether a proposed later continuation contradicts that literal content. Do not mechanically equate a phrase with an inference: `some`, `or`, `possible`, `ready` and indirect replies have variety-, genre- and context-sensitive uses.

A candidate inference can be reasonable without being certain; a later explicit continuation can cancel it without falsifying the original statement. ## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — “Some” can coexist with “all”.** In a fictional club, a coordinator says, “Some volunteers have replied.” With no other context, at least some replied; whether all replied is not established. The learner may say, “At least some have replied; I don't know if everyone has.” A continuation then says, “In fact, all six replied.” Accept the update and recognize that the continuation is compatible with the original statement. Reject “Only some replied” as an asserted fact, and reject “The coordinator contradicted herself” solely because “all” followed “some.” The task does not reward pretending that a common conversational inference is impossible; it requires recognizing its defeasibility.

**B — Explicit cancellation versus genuine contradiction.** A club message says, “You may choose tea or coffee; both are fine.” An independently reviewed task permits the learner to preserve the explicit both-allowed continuation rather than forcing exclusive choice from the word “or.” A distinct message says, “Exactly one drink per person; not both.” That is a genuine explicit restriction, not a default inference. Reject “Both are forbidden” in the first version and “Both are allowed” in the second. If the first message only says “Tea or coffee?” without further context, accept an ambiguity-aware response or a targeted question where the communicative goal requires knowing whether both are permitted. This is not a replacement for F158's logical scope objectives.

**C — A context-supported hint is not a confirmed refusal.** Maya asks Theo, “Would you like to stay for another hour?” Theo answers, “I have an early class tomorrow.” In an independently reviewed social context this may plausibly signal reluctance or a polite decline, but the answer does not literally state “No” or a promise to leave now. Accept “He may be declining because he has an early class” or an appropriate clarification when necessary; reject “Theo definitely refused and left.” In a later turn Theo adds, “But I can stay another hour today.” The learner updates the interpretation without marking Theo a liar. If a different context makes the early class mere background, accept that reading; do not universalize indirectness or politeness norms.

**D — An inference can be cancelled, but a fact cannot.** A notice says, “The poster draft is finished.” A learner may infer that printing is the next step but must not report that printed posters are ready. The next line says, “Printing has not started.” This cancels any informal readiness inference while preserving the draft-completion fact. A different continuation says, “Actually, the draft is still unfinished.” That directly retracts or contradicts the earlier asserted draft status, rather than merely cancelling a pragmatic inference. Accept faithful chronological reporting of the correction; reject both “Printing is complete” and “The draft was never reported complete.”

**E — Exposure, missing audio and replay are not independence.** At 09:01 the source audio fails and a readable fallback presents “Some volunteers replied; in fact, all six did.” At 09:02 a tutor model explicitly states “There is no contradiction.” At 09:03 the learner copies that judgment. Record the actual reading route, ANSWER_EXPOSURE and ASSISTED, preserve any earlier eligible unaided answer and award no independent listening. Replaying the same event at 09:04 cannot manufacture another first attempt. An unopened hint does not count as displayed help. Only a distinct later session with a genuinely new reviewed literal/context/cancellation combination and no answer-bearing help can support delayed independent transfer.

**F — Reversible instruction and topic-balanced transfer.** Learner A repeatedly reports default inferences as confirmed facts. Across comparable real sessions, SAID_VS_INFERRED_TWO_COLUMN, CANCELLATION_CONTINUATION_PAIRS and BALANCED_DEFAULT are actually rendered and alternated with matched cue strength, linguistic complexity, help and delay. Later independent unseen recipient paraphrases may cautiously favor a method if they preserve warranted content while handling both genuine contradiction and valid cancellation. Learner B initially benefits from contrast pairs but later calls every explicit contradiction “just an implicature”; the contradictory evidence reduces confidence and restores balanced sampling or targeted entailment contrasts. If one option used simpler utterances, more explicit cancellation, familiar gaming contexts, clearer audio, visible solutions or shorter delays, declare **no causal winner**. Missing follow-up cannot establish retention. Gaming interests may increase example probability, but neutral libraries, adjacent clubs, unfamiliar workshops and all required modalities/objectives remain reachable.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR boundaries.** A–F bind only applicable existing versioned can-dos and independently reviewed target-language/variety-specific literal, pragmatic, recipient and actual-delivery oracles. Preserve prerequisites, turn ceilings, thresholds, rubrics and exit gates; otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE/UNSUPPORTED.
2. **Some versus all.** A preserves the literal minimum and unknown upper bound; an explicit “all” continuation is not falsely graded as a contradiction, and “only some” is not invented.
3. **Alternative-choice context.** B distinguishes an explicit both-allowed continuation from an explicit exactly-one restriction and accepts legitimate ambiguity where the context is underdetermined; do not replace F158 scope requirements.
4. **Contextual indirectness.** C permits a supported tentative inference without promoting it to a completed action, definitive refusal or speaker motive; a later clarification can defeat the earlier inference.
5. **Cancellation versus asserted contradiction.** D differentiates printing-status inference from draft-completion assertion and distinguishes a later cancellation from an explicit correction of asserted content.
6. **Actual delivery and first authorship.** E separates failed audio, accessible text, displayed answer-bearing help, unopened hints, assisted copying and event replay; no phantom listening or independent mastery.
7. **Matched, uncertain, reversible adaptation.** F updates instructional hypotheses only from comparable actually delivered methods and later first-unaided faithful transfer. Sparse, contradictory or confounded evidence lowers confidence; alternatives continue to be sampled.
8. **Delayed unseen transfer and topic diversity.** Require genuinely distinct learning sessions, independently reviewed new literal/context/cancellation combinations, unaided recipient-facing responses and explicit missing follow-up. Interests are nonexclusive and cannot suppress required CEFR objectives or balanced skills.
9. **Evidence and protected scope.** Synthetic prose and generic CI are not executable interaction QA, shipped adaptation or demonstrated human-learning gains. Require exact-head executable stimulus/context/response/provenance checks, observed reversible method changes and ethically reviewed delayed independent transfer against a balanced baseline before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs, auto-merge, Pre-A1 changes, A1–C2 enablement or weakened B2 boundaries.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are synthetic acceptance histories, not observed learners or executable tests. A strong tutor should help a learner distinguish what a speaker actually said from what context suggests, revise a defeasible inference when new information arrives, and independently communicate only warranted conclusions in unfamiliar conversations. Remain UNSUPPORTED until implementation and independent verification.
