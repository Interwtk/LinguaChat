## Six synthetic longitudinal histories

- **A — “Not all” is not “none.”** A source-defined planning task states that not all participants need a badge; the source also specifies exactly which roles do. Learner A interprets this as no one needing a badge; learner B independently identifies the required subset and communicates the correct source-defined decision. If the subset is not supplied, the valid response is to retain uncertainty or ask a source-permitted clarification, not invent the members. A lucky binary choice without a scope-sensitive explanation or outcome is insufficient.
- **B — Exception changes the recipient's action.** In a source-defined service instruction, everyone must bring identification **except** a documented exempt group. Learner A accurately repeats “everyone” but drops the exception, leading to an incorrect instruction for the exempt recipient. Learner B independently preserves the exception and gives audience-appropriate directions. Do not impose an English-specific “except” construction if a target-language equivalent communicates the same scope.
- **C — A tutor supplied the exact branch.** The tutor actually renders “Unless the venue is closed, go to room B; if it is closed, call the coordinator.” Learner A copies the resulting correct conditional decision: task success is assisted for that move. In a later session learner B independently resolves a genuinely new condition with different objects and outcome. A selected but unrendered cue cannot contaminate independence; a scripted interlocutor's permitted question is not a tutor cue.
- **D — Early method advantage reverses on unseen transfer.** For one learner, matched first unaided probes favor `SET_MEMBERSHIP_VISUALIZATION` over `CONTRASTIVE_SCOPE_EXAMPLES`. Later delayed unseen tasks with new quantifier domains, exceptions, modalities and source-equivalent burden reverse the apparent advantage. Match or disclose difficulty, operator frequency, reading load, numeracy demands, language variety, topic familiarity, practice dose, feedback, channel, accessibility and rubric version. Lower confidence, sample alternatives and log the actually rendered next instruction; never claim a permanent “visual” type.
- **E — Favorite topics and access do not narrow mastery.** A learner correctly handles “all but one” in a favorite-game invitation but overgeneralizes “at least” in a neutral clinic-booking dialogue and misses a source-required exception in an unfamiliar community task. Another learner completes a source-equivalent accessible written task, but no listening/speaking evidence is available. A caption that reveals the correct exception is answer-bearing assistance. Rotate contexts, preserve due reviews and all source-required skill evidence, and avoid inferring disability or network latency as a scope deficit.
- **F — Ambiguous wording, offline replay and missing delayed probe.** A source sentence admits two validated readings in the target variety; two offline sessions reuse a local event ID; a tutor's exception highlight was rendered before the learner decision but uploaded later; a rubric revision changes the eligible interpretations; and the scheduled unseen probe is never completed. Use immutable session-qualified IDs, frozen source/rubric hashes and trusted cue-render chronology. Credit either validated interpretation under the correct rubric, quarantine irreconcilable versions, deduplicate true replays, keep follow-up unknown and require identical independence labels, review debt and next-method rationale across receipt-order permutations.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source-defined scope before scoring.** For A–F emit versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint, prerequisites, task and decision goal, target language/variety, source proposition/domain, legitimate readings, rubric, assistance rules, independent threshold, delayed/unseen probes, due reviews and exit gate. Missing mapping, invented formal-logic demands or unsupported source reading yields `UNSUPPORTED`.
2. **Domain, polarity and operator scope.** In A/D/F distinguish `NOT_ALL` from `NONE`, relevant set membership and source-valid alternative readings. Do not equate correct numeric tokens, response length or a lucky choice with independent semantic comprehension.
3. **Exceptions and conditional outcomes.** In B/C/E evaluate whether the learner preserves source-required exclusions, “only/at least/at most” bounds and applicable conditional branches when these are source-valid. Score the actual communicative decision and valid paraphrases, not a single English template or invented calculations.
4. **Ambiguity and language-variety validity.** In A/F retain validated ambiguity and accept source-valid paraphrases, clarification requests and legitimate variety-specific scope realizations. Missing information stays unknown; forced certainty or a culturally/linguistically narrow evaluator fails closed.
5. **Rendered assistance and interlocutor provenance.** In C/E/F anchor first learner-authored scope move to trusted actual tutor-cue render time. A displayed answer-bearing diagram, caption or paraphrase makes that move assisted; an assigned but unseen cue does not. Ordinary frozen task clarification is not coaching unless it leaks the exact solution outside protocol.
6. **Independent delayed unseen transfer.** In C/D/F require meaningful delay, new source propositions/domains, first unaided interpretation, source-defined communicative outcome and completed probe. Rehearsed sentence substitutions, immediate retries, assisted decisions and absent probes cannot prove durable independent mastery; emit eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators.
7. **Comparable adaptation with balanced coverage.** In D/E match or disclose linguistic scope difficulty, modality, numeracy and transcription confounds, topic familiarity, practice dose, feedback, accessibility, variety and rubric. Keep CEFR objectives, noncompensatory receptive/productive evidence, due reviews and favorite/adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar exposure; interests softly influence examples, never remove required objectives.
8. **Reversible hypotheses and replay safety.** In C/D/E/F emit feasible candidates, selected/assigned/actually rendered instruction, later independent evidence, uncertainty, confounds, alternative sampling and next-method rationale. Sparse or confounded histories use `BALANCED_DEFAULT`. Deduplicate session-qualified events, enforce source/rubric hashes and replay-order invariance for assistance, skill/review debt and next choice; irreconcilable provenance is quarantined.
9. **Assertion-level audit without efficacy inflation.** For A–F emit source/task/rubric/evaluator IDs, proposition and scope annotation, first/final learner-authored messages, actual rendered assistance, decision outcome, delayed/unseen denominators, confounds, replay equivalence and `PASS`/`FAIL`/`UNSUPPORTED` per assertion. Documentation CI cannot prove a shipped scope evaluator, CEFR certification or improved human learning.
