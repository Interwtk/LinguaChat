## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Two plausible antecedents, no invented choice.** A fictional volunteer says **“Maya placed the blue folder next to the green folder. Please bring it.”** Without gesture or additional context, **“it”** could refer to either folder. Reporting **“Maya definitely wants the blue one”** fails. **“Do you mean the blue folder or the green folder?”** is a successful targeted clarification. Counterfactual: the volunteer adds **“Bring the green folder; leave the blue one there”**; the green folder is now uniquely grounded. Do not penalize clarification when the original remains ambiguous or grant certainty because a language model favors the most recent noun.

**B — Demonstratives and unavailable shared visual context.** A fictional organizer says **“Put this one over there”** while pointing at two unlabeled boxes and an off-screen shelf. The learner receives audio only, with no view or accessible description of the gesture. Any exact box/shelf selection is unsupported; **“Which box and where should I put it?”** is appropriate. Counterfactual: an actually delivered accessible diagram and text explicitly label **“the red box on the left”** and **“shelf B”**, and the organizer says **“Put the red box on shelf B.”** A faithful handoff is now possible. A screenshot that was never displayed, or alt text that omits the pointing target, cannot be treated as learner evidence.

**C — Ellipsis recoverable from parallel clauses, but no extra event.** A fictional club message reads **“Ana ordered tea; Ben, coffee.”** A reviewed context supports **“Ben ordered coffee”** by parallel ellipsis. It does **not** support **“Ben drank the coffee”** or **“Ana ordered coffee too.”** Counterfactual: the message reads **“Ana ordered tea; Ben, maybe coffee?”**; uncertainty and question force must remain. In a second reviewed source **“Nora can attend Tuesday; Omar, Thursday”** licenses a parallel Thursday attendance interpretation only within that source's grammar and context; it cannot prove that Omar already attended or that both are free on both days.

**D — Multi-turn antecedents and a later correction.** A fictional librarian says **“The study room is beside the office. It closes at six.”** Both the room and office are plausible closing-time referents in the absence of further context. A learner who tells visitors **“The study room definitely closes at six”** fails; a question naming both locations succeeds. Counterfactual: a later reviewed librarian message says **“To clarify: the office closes at six; the study room closes at seven.”** A recipient-facing update must preserve the later clarification and its source/time without retroactively treating the first message as unambiguous. A reply from an unrelated participant is not automatically an authoritative correction.

**E — Delivered-channel drift, first authorship and stable event identity.** The original fictional two-speaker dialogue is **“A: Give the red folder to Lina. B: Should I bring the blue one too? A: No, leave that one here.”** ASR omits **“red”**; captions merge speakers A/B; translation substitutes a pronoun with a different antecedent; screen-reader order presents the final reply before the question. Quarantine construct-changing delivery rather than marking a learner wrong for a missing antecedent or unavailable speaker boundary. At 10:00 the learner independently asks **“Does ‘that one’ mean the blue folder?”**; at 10:01 a tutor renders an answer-bearing arrow; a copied handoff at 10:02 is ASSISTED. The 10:00 question remains FIRST_UNAIDED; queued but unrendered help does not contaminate it. Replayed event IDs cannot create new independent attempts or learning days. Corrected accessible text may support reading but not unheard listening.

**F — Reversible method hypothesis, real unseen transfer and soft interests.** Fictional learner A completes REFERENT_HIGHLIGHT_WITH_FADE examples with hints but repeatedly guesses antecedents in later first-unaided unfamiliar handoffs. Several matched, actually delivered TARGETED_CLARIFICATION_ROLEPLAY opportunities precede stronger delayed independent recipient-facing questions with different speakers, objects, reference distance and ellipsis structures. Only repeated comparable outcomes may cautiously favor clarification practice; alternatives remain sampled. Fictional learner B initially benefits from TURN_BY_TURN_DISCOURSE_MAP but later incorrectly expands omitted predicates into completed events; confidence falls, review becomes due and exploration resumes. If the discourse-map condition has only one candidate while the clarification condition has three, or only one condition supplies an answer-bearing arrow, infer **no method winner**. Replacing a game controller with a musical instrument in an otherwise identical pronoun template is not unseen transfer. Missing follow-up is MISSING, not failure; neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain eligible.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Existing CEFR objective and reviewed oracle.** A–F require a versioned existing can-do, prerequisites, source/recipient/information gap, reviewed candidate-referent/ellipsis oracle, target language/variety/register, accessible actually delivered stimulus, evaluator/rubric, first-unaided threshold, due review, delayed/unseen probes and unchanged exit gate. Otherwise NOT_APPLICABLE or UNSUPPORTED; no invented advanced grammar requirement.
2. **Ambiguity versus unique grounding.** A distinguishes two plausible antecedents from an explicitly resolved one. Accept a targeted clarification under underdetermination; reject unsupported confident guesses and fabricated uniqueness.
3. **Deictic and recipient-access grounding.** B requires actually shared pointing/visual/alt-text context or explicit descriptions for demonstratives and locations. An absent or inaccessible gesture is not evidence of the intended referent.
4. **Licensed ellipsis and event boundaries.** C reconstructs only what reviewed parallelism licenses, preserving modality, question force and uncertainty. Do not promote an order into consumption, a possibility into attendance, or an omitted predicate into unrelated facts.
5. **Multi-turn correction with source/time.** D keeps competing antecedents ambiguous until a valid clarification, then reports the corrected source-world without rewriting the earlier ambiguous message or trusting an unsupported third-party correction.
6. **Actual delivery, first-authorship chronology and deduplication.** E reconciles source versus rendered ASR/captions/translation/screen-reader order, quarantines construct-changing omissions, preserves pre-help authorship, logs actual answer exposure and deduplicates stable events. Text cannot certify unheard listening.
7. **Controlled uncertain reversible instructional adaptation.** F compares feasible actually delivered methods under matched candidate count, antecedent distance, distractor similarity, ellipsis load, language, modality, topic, source completeness, help, spacing and follow-up. Sparse, contradictory, selective or confounded evidence yields no winner; delayed regression lowers confidence and restores exploration.
8. **Delayed first-unaided genuinely unseen transfer and nonexclusive interests.** Hinted pronoun matches, copied maps, same-session repetitions and topic-only swaps cannot earn delayed/unseen mastery. Require a later distinct eligible session, different speaker/recipient/context **and changed reference/ellipsis structure**, reviewed oracle, first-unaided actionable handoff or clarification and explicit missingness. Preserve shared objectives, receptive/productive balance and adjacent/neutral/unfamiliar rotation.
9. **Honest implementation and efficacy boundary.** Green documentation CI is not a shipped referent evaluator, adaptive tutor or proof of improved human retention. Require exact-head executable assertions against real objectives, logged delivered instructional changes, later independent transfer versus a balanced baseline and separately reviewed learner evidence before efficacy claims. No Auth/cloud/infra/production/Supabase mutation, paid Claude, EvoLabs work, threshold weakening, level enablement or auto-merge.

**Fixture verdict:** A–F are fictional design/probe cases, not observed learner histories or executable QA. Preserve justified ambiguity, grounded reference, ellipsis limits, accessible delivery and explicit UNSUPPORTED gates until implementation and learner-evaluation evidence exist.
