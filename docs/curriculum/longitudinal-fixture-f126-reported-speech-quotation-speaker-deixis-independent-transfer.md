# F126 — Reported speech, quotation, speaker perspective, deictic anchors and independent communicative transfer

**Status:** design-only curricular acceptance fixture; **UNSUPPORTED** until executable assertions run against a named exact engine HEAD, real versioned CEFR-linked tasks, independently validated multilingual dialogue sources and accessible equivalents, and delayed first-unaided transfer. This does **not** ship a reported-speech evaluator, transcript processor, speaker diarizer, CEFR mastery or evidence of improved human learning. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F37/F38 protect delivered-help chronology and tutor-origin errors; F49 protects accessibility; F50 keeps interests nonexclusive; F82/F95 cover temporal references; F103 protects consequential confirmation; F104 isolates instructional method from difficulty; F107 covers unreliable tutor feedback; F117 protects register and linguistic varieties; F118 covers negation and scope; F119 covers clarification; F120 protects certainty and evidence attribution; F125 covers calendar occurrence/time-zone provenance. **Distinct construct:** can a learner independently **quote, paraphrase or relay what a specific person communicated** while preserving who said what, to whom, when and where, which words were quoted versus inferred, the original commitment/negation/uncertainty, and the intended listener's perspective? F120 asks whether a claim is warranted; F126 additionally tests **linguistic viewpoint transformations and fidelity across turns and speakers**. It is not a fixed auditory/reading learner type, a requirement to perform one universal English-style tense backshift, or permission to infer a speaker's identity from an unreliable recording. Choose age-appropriate, source-authored dialogue with CEFR-appropriate burden; beginner objectives may involve simple explicit speaker identification without advanced nested indirect speech.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Speaker, addressee and pronoun anchoring.** In a fictional study-group dialogue, Maya tells Omar, “I will send you my notes.” The source-authored task asks a third student to relay Maya's promise to another classmate. Learner A reports “Omar said he would send Maya his notes”; learner B reports “Maya said she would send Omar her notes.” The latter preserves speaker, addressee and possessive anchors; a faithful direct quote can also be valid when clearly attributed as a quote. Counterfactual: change only the authoritative speaker/addressee assignment while keeping the sentence text: the correct indirect report changes. Merely changing avatar colors or bubble positions cannot change speaker identity. If captions lack a validated speaker assignment, speaker-attribution evidence is UNSUPPORTED, not proof of learner confusion.

**B — Temporal and spatial reference from two viewpoints.** On Monday at the library, Lina tells Sam, “I will bring it here tomorrow.” On Wednesday from a café, Sam reports the message to Jo. Learner A says “Lina will bring it to this café tomorrow” without evidence; learner B says “Lina said on Monday that she would bring it to the library on Tuesday.” A source-valid unchanged direct quote must retain its original attribution and Monday context. Counterfactual: shift the original utterance time to Tuesday or the source place to the sports hall; the resolved reference changes. A language/variety may legitimately retain original tense or relative adverbs when its reporting context makes the anchors clear; do not enforce an English-specific mechanical backshift. When the original date/place is not supplied, a source-valid clarification or explicit unresolved reference is better than invented dates.

**C — Nested attribution and preserved uncertainty.** Fictional exchange: Ana tells Bea, “Carlos told me the workshop **might** move, but he has not confirmed it.” Learner A reports “Ana confirmed that the workshop moved”; learner B says “Ana said Carlos had mentioned a possible move, not a confirmed change.” The task must preserve the nested source chain and uncertainty without rewarding vague hedging when the source actually confirms something. Counterfactual: Carlos later provides a source-authoritative confirmed update; the valid current report can reflect that version while historical quotations remain historical. Compare F120's epistemic claim status without collapsing the additional speaker/turn transformations tested here.

**D — Negation, quotation boundaries and repair.** A fictional team dialogue says, “I **didn't** say the event was cancelled; I said it **might be postponed**.” Learner A relays “The event is cancelled”; learner B reports the correction, negation and tentative postponement. Counterfactual: source-authoritative wording changes to “I said it was cancelled”; expected meaning changes. If an automatic transcript drops “didn't”, merges two speakers or moves quotation marks across turns, record the actual delivered caption/audio and quarantine affected semantic-attribution evidence. A learner who independently asks “Did you mean cancelled or postponed?” can receive task-appropriate clarification credit when the delivered stimulus genuinely conflicts. Do not reward an arbitrary denial of a clear source.

**E — Multilingual/accessibility fidelity, help chronology and replay.** A fictional dialogue is independently validated in the target language, its approved varieties, captions and accessible audio/text. In one variety an indirect report requires a different person/tense construction than in another; both preserve the same source meaning. A screen reader announces the second speaker's line before the first; an auto-translation reverses “not yet”; captions incorrectly attribute a line to the wrong speaker. Preserve authoritative and **actually rendered** versions and mark affected comparisons UNSUPPORTED while retaining separately valid partial language evidence. The learner writes an independent accurate report at 10:00; a tutor pronoun table queued at 09:59 but first **rendered** at 10:01 does not contaminate that first attempt. A later copy of the table is ASSISTED. Offline replay of the original response must be deduplicated by immutable session-qualified event ID; identical words in different turns are not the same event.

**F — Longitudinal, reversible methods and unseen communicative transfer.** Fictional learner A correctly copies indirect-speech models immediately but later misattributes pronouns in unaided messages. After **actually delivered** PRONOUN_ANCHOR_MAPPING on comparable CEFR tasks, A later independently relays a new source-authored sports-club dialogue with different speakers, addressees, person references, time anchors and recipient. Learner B improves instead after QUOTE_VERSUS_REPORT_CONTRAST on independently comparable dialogues. Compare baseline proficiency, objective, target language/variety, speaker count, nested-report depth, deictic shifts, source clarity, modality, accessible representation, topic familiarity, assistance exposure, review interval and follow-up opportunity. If A sees simple two-person exchanges and B sees nested four-person dialogues, mark METHOD_DIALOGUE_COMPLEXITY_CONFOUNDED and infer **no method winner**. Renaming the speakers while preserving the same utterances, viewpoint shifts and answer path is not unseen transfer. Record eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing follow-ups; missing is neither success nor failure. Repeated comparable delayed independent outcomes may support different **actually delivered** next instruction, with uncertainty; contradictory evidence lowers confidence and permits reversal. Keep due reviews, productive/receptive balance, alternative exploration and nonexclusive interest-topic rotation.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Real CEFR and source linkage.** A–F resolve versioned descriptor/blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored dialogue/turn/speaker/addressee IDs, target language/variety, rubric/evaluator, first-unaided threshold, accommodations, protected skills, due reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Do not silently impose advanced nested-report grammar on beginner objectives.
2. **Quotation versus indirect reporting.** A/B/E distinguish verbatim attributed quotation from semantically faithful paraphrase. Legitimate variety-specific reporting structures are accepted; a fluent report with reversed roles or fabricated context fails the target construct.
3. **Speaker, recipient and viewpoint binding.** A/B/C bind who originally spoke, to whom, who is now reporting and to whom, plus person/possessive and original-versus-reporting time/place anchors. Cosmetic speaker-bubble changes cannot alter authoritative assignments; source-effective changes can.
4. **Negation, modality and nested attribution fidelity.** C/D preserve source uncertainty, correction, negation scope, nested attribution and commitments; a possible move must not become a confirmed cancellation. Missing or conflicting anchors permit bounded clarification, not hallucinated certainty.
5. **Actual first-unaided provenance and replay.** E records the first authored answer, source-effective version, retries and help **actually rendered before** the attempt. Queued/failed/post-answer help is not retroactive; copied models remain assisted; replay does not double-count.
6. **Multilingual and accessible delivered-stimulus validity.** B/D/E independently validate translated viewpoint, captions, diarization, speaker order, screen-reader order, audio and accessible alternatives against authoritative source meaning. Corrupted or unvalidated delivery quarantines affected construct evidence while preserving independently valid partial language evidence.
7. **Delayed retention and genuinely unseen transfer.** A–F require meaningful separated learning opportunities and new source-authored dialogues with noncosmetic speaker, recipient, deictic, negation or nested-attribution structure; no answer-bearing help. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators. Recoloring or renaming the same exchange is insufficient.
8. **Reversible instruction and soft interests.** F records feasible/assigned/**actually delivered** alternatives, baseline/task comparability, confounds, uncertainty, exploration, contradictory outcomes, protected listening/reading/writing/speaking/free production, due reviews and exit gates. Interests adjust example likelihood only; adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts remain available and required objectives are never skipped. No fixed learner-style label.
9. **Exact-head deterministic reproducibility and bounded claims.** A–F emit deterministic seed/clock, named engine HEAD and runnable command, descriptor/source/turn/speaker/task/rubric/evaluator hashes, source-effective and actually rendered accessible artifacts, first/assisted utterances, help/event ledger, counterfactuals, delayed/unseen denominators and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED per assertion**. Docs-only CI is not an executable reported-speech evaluator, reproducible product defect or evidence of improved human learning.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; real versioned CEFR-linked tasks; independently validated multilingual, multivariety and accessible dialogue sources; authoritative speaker/addressee/utterance/quote/time/place/commitment bindings; source-effective and actually delivered stimuli; first-unaided evidence; meaningful delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen dialogue-to-message transfer; comparable or explicitly confounded instructional alternatives; protected skill/review/exit obligations; complete eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing denominators; and pronoun/deixis/nested-attribution/negation/translation/caption/screen-reader/version/replay counterfactuals with per-assertion verdicts. Unreliable source, missing speaker/anchor, unvalidated accessibility, absent follow-up or unsupported variety-specific rubric leaves affected claims **UNSUPPORTED/unknown** while preserving independently established partial language evidence.

A stronger claim that adaptive reported-speech instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires a separately reviewed, privacy-appropriate, multilingual, multivariety and accessibility-validated real-learner longitudinal study with credible comparison, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis and reproducible delayed outcomes. **A strong tutor teaches the learner to relay what someone actually meant and said, from the correct viewpoint—not merely transform pronouns in a memorized sentence.**
