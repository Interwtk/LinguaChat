This is not shipped multilingual dialogue support, a verified production defect, a CEFR award or proof of human learning gains. Pre-A1 frozen; A1–C2 unavailable.

**Contract:** `docs/curriculum/longitudinal-instructional-adaptation.md`. F33 requires genuinely unseen transfer; F34/F63 cover bilingual *instructional scaffolds*; F37/F38 distinguish assigned from rendered help; F49 protects construct-equivalent accessibility; F53 protects language variety/register; F58 learner initiative; F70 lexical selection; F73 implicit pragmatic intent; F92 quotation/attribution; F104 confound-aware method choice; F105 strategic help; F107 unreliable feedback provenance; F108 natural disfluency and self-repair. **Distinct construct:** can the learner comprehend and participate in **source-authored multilingual communication** where switching language, borrowing a word, quoting another speaker, preserving a name or briefly using a first language has different implications for *communicative success* and *target-language evidence*? Can the tutor avoid both indiscriminate penalties for legitimate switches and false target-language mastery from assisted or other-language production? F109 concerns the **language boundary inside an authentic task**, not merely whether bilingual explanations are effective.

## Six deterministic synthetic longitudinal histories

**A — Valid bilingual request versus monolingual target-language evidence.** A source-authored bilingual service encounter permits “¿Me puede confirmar el appointment para mañana?”; the recipient correctly understands that the speaker requests confirmation of tomorrow's appointment. In this *bilingual communicative task*, the utterance may achieve its pragmatic purpose. A separate source-authored Spanish productive probe requires independently producing the target-language phrase “la cita”; the same switched noun **does not by itself evidence that specific Spanish lexical target**, although the request may still be understood. Another learner independently says “¿Me confirma la cita para mañana?” and satisfies both when the rubric supports it. Do not collapse communicative adequacy and target-form evidence into one score; do not impose the monolingual requirement on the bilingual task retroactively.

**B — Borrowing, names and quotations are different.** In a source-validated variety, a conventional loanword appears in an otherwise target-language sentence; a venue named “Blue Room” appears unchanged; a speaker explicitly quotes “no hay cupos” from a Spanish notice while summarizing in English. Another learner switches to Spanish for an unquoted sentence because the required English expression is unknown. Segment the three source-licensed cases differently from the learner's lexical-gap bridge. Do not “correct” proper names or quotation fidelity into false target-language errors; do not automatically treat every borrowed word as independently demonstrated target-language vocabulary. The task's source and variety-specific rubric, not an English-only dictionary heuristic, decide.

**C — Intentional accommodation versus avoidance and unearned mastery.** A bilingual partner asks for clarification in Spanish; one learner responds in Spanish to help the partner understand, then independently restates the required English instructions when asked. Another learner gives only Spanish instructions during a source-defined English productive probe and receives an answer-bearing English model before repeating it. A third learner asks, without a model, how to express an unknown phrase and later self-produces it in a genuinely separate probe. Score communicative accommodation, English first-unaided production, legitimate help seeking and assisted repetition separately. A code-switch is not automatically an error, a virtue or proof of the required target-language can-do.

**D — Speech recognition, scripts and access boundaries.** A consented, source-authored bilingual audio message contains a Spanish phrase followed by an English time; an ASR version drops the Spanish phrase, while another transliterates a proper name incorrectly. A learner accurately reports the intended time and relevant condition from source-valid audio; another sees only an accessible written transcript. The source adjudicator, not ASR language ID, resolves the original message when trustworthy audio and consent permit. Text may validly support a reading/mediation objective but cannot silently certify source-required listening or speaking unless a validated equivalent route exists. When audio and approved alternatives are unavailable, listening evidence is UNSUPPORTED, not learner failure.

**E — Favorite-topic switching and longitudinal instructional reversal.** Across matched fictional sessions, CONTEXTUAL_SWITCH_INTERPRETATION produces excellent immediate assisted results on a learner's favorite entertainment topic, but the learner later fails first-unaided unfamiliar community-service dialogues that require distinguishing a quoted foreign-language warning from the speaker's own commitment. BORROWING_VS_TARGET_FORM_CONTRAST initially looks harder, yet yields stronger delayed independent transfer in a comparable condition; a second learner shows the reverse pattern. Compare source task, difficulty, language pair/variety, topic familiarity, actual modality/access, rendered support, practice dose, prior exposure and follow-up missingness. Update a **capability-local uncertain hypothesis** rather than assigning an “auditory”, “bilingual” or permanent preferred-method identity. Continue balanced receptive/productive practice and deliberate context rotation.

**F — Offline replay, language-policy update and cue chronology.** Two genuine offline sessions reuse a local event ID; one uploaded packet is a true replay; a tutor's complete target-language translation is rendered before the learner's second attempt but uploaded after it; a source-authorized task update changes a bilingual dialogue from permitting a conventional borrowing to explicitly assessing its target-language equivalent; an evaluator migration adds a validated regional loanword. The learner's first independent bilingual response remains valid under its **original source policy**; the later stricter task may require additional target-form evidence but cannot retroactively erase the earlier communicative outcome. Deduplicate only session-qualified true replays, reconstruct trusted event/render/effective-time order and quarantine irreconcilable rubric/language-policy versions. Network receipt permutations must not change first-unaided labels, assistance, source-valid outcomes, due-review debt or reversible next-method rationale.

## Nine acceptance assertions

1. **Source and CEFR language policy.** A–F require a real versioned CEFR can-do, blueprint/prerequisites, source-authored multilingual or monolingual purpose, permitted language boundaries, variety/register, rubric/evaluator, accessibility, independence threshold, protected skills, reviews, delayed/unseen probes and exit gate. Missing source-valid boundaries are UNSUPPORTED; a task without multilingual relevance is NOT_APPLICABLE. Never invent a blanket prohibition on code-switching.
2. **Separate meaning from target-form evidence.** A/C/E independently score whether the recipient receives the correct source-defined meaning/action and whether the learner produced the **required target-language form unaided**. Communicatively successful multilingual output does not automatically satisfy a monolingual productive target; a form-focused probe cannot retroactively invalidate a distinct bilingual communicative task.
3. **Borrowing, quotation, naming and lexical gaps.** B/C/D distinguish source-authorized loanwords, proper names, faithful quotations, transliterations, accommodation and lexical-gap bridging using task-specific language/variety adjudication. Do not penalize conventional regional usage, silently translate names, count quotation as original target-language production or treat every switch as avoidance.
4. **Assistance and speaker provenance.** C/D/F retain original speaker and learner-authored segments, legitimate partner stimuli, first independent response, actual tutor translation/model render time, retries and repairs. A displayed answer-bearing translation contaminates subsequent target-form independence; an assigned but never rendered translation does not. Reordered server arrival must not fabricate independent mastery.
5. **Speech, scripts, access and uncertainty.** B/D/F preserve original source wording, source-valid audio or accessible alternative, ASR/transliteration hypotheses, language-ID uncertainty and evaluator versions. Text-only evidence cannot be relabeled oral evidence without source-validated construct equivalence; unresolved media or language boundaries are UNSUPPORTED, not failures.
6. **Reversible instruction and valid comparisons.** C/E/F log baseline, candidate feasibility, selected/assigned/actually rendered method, scaffold dose, task/variety/access/topic controls, later first-unaided outcomes, uncertainty, safe fallback and alternative sampling. Short-term translated success cannot determine a fixed learner type or causal method winner; conflicting delayed evidence can reverse the next-method hypothesis.
7. **Time-separated independent transfer and protected CEFR skills.** A–F require genuinely new source-authored language pair/surface/recipient/context where appropriate, meaningful intervening time/material, first-unaided semantic and target-form probes, and source-defined recipient outcome. Report eligible/offered/rendered/completed/missing delayed and unseen follow-up separately. Preserve required listening, reading, writing, speaking, free production, due reviews, prerequisites and exit gates.
8. **Soft interests, policy-version integrity and replay invariance.** E/F rotate preferred, adjacent, neutral and unfamiliar contexts, without excluding required topics or changing objectives/rubrics to favor interests. A later source policy or accepted-variety update applies only at its authorized effective time. Session-qualified replay deduplication and network receipt permutations must preserve evidence and next-method rationale; irreconcilable version conflicts fail closed.
9. **Assertion-level audit and bounded claims.** A–F emit source/task/CEFR/rubric/evaluator hashes, language-policy and speaker/segment annotations, adjudication uncertainty, original utterance, actual delivered modality/cue chronology, independent versus assisted attempts, meaning and target-form verdicts, delayed/unseen denominators, skill/review coverage, candidate-method rationale, replay permutations and **PASS/FAIL/UNSUPPORTED for each assertion**. Documentation CI and synthetic PASS are not proof of shipped multilingual competence, CEFR mastery, causal learning gains or a reproducible production defect.

## Required evidence and release gate

An **executable fixture PASS** requires a named exact engine HEAD and reproducible command; deterministic seed/clock; real versioned CEFR-linked multilingual and monolingual tasks; independently adjudicated language boundaries, borrowings, quotations, named entities, varieties and pragmatic outcomes; consent-respecting trustworthy source media or validated accessible equivalents; actual rendered translation/model chronology; separate first-unaided and assisted responses; delayed independent retrieval and genuinely unseen transfer; matched or explicitly noncomparable task/method/topic/access conditions; protected skill/review/exit requirements; follow-up denominators; replay/version permutations and assertion-level verdicts. Without source-authorized multilingual norms, trustworthy modality/cue provenance or independent follow-up, corresponding claims remain UNSUPPORTED/unknown.

A stronger claim that changing multilingual instruction **improves durable independent communication** requires separately reviewed real-learner longitudinal evidence against a credible balanced comparator or ethically reviewed experiment, multilingual/variety and accessibility adjudication, attrition and subgroup-harm analysis, privacy safeguards and monitoring for unfairly penalized bilingual speakers. **A strong tutor can value authentic multilingual communication while still measuring precisely which target-language abilities the learner has demonstrated independently.**
